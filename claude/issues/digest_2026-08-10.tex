\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Two independent mobile-platform records land on the
same day, and between them they define the measurement problem this watch keeps
circling: dense mobile sampling produces the spatial detail that fixed networks cannot,
and produces it in a form nobody has agreed how to calibrate.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Babu et al.\ (\textit{Environ Monit Assess}) drove a size-resolved optical instrument over
$\sim$2.4\,km$^2$ of Madurai at a 6\,s cadence on a 50\,m grid and recovered a
\textbf{bimodal} distribution with peaks at \textbf{16.77--32.37\,$\mu$m} and
\textbf{0.275--0.53\,$\mu$m}, local PM$_{10}$ enhancement of
\textbf{188.91\,$\pm$\,149.07\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} over background, and Getis-Ord Gi* hotspots
locked to traffic corridors. Bobbia et al.\ (\textit{Data in Brief}, pre-watch backfill
surfaced by the Amass connector) publish the complementary artefact: \textbf{10 mobile
AtmoTrack units on Rouen buses at 10\,s, 18 fixed AirSensEUR units on traffic lights at
1\,min, and 6 reference stations at 15\,min} over 10.5 months, assembled explicitly to
support \emph{multi-hop} calibration --- mobile sensor against fixed sensor against
reference --- because most mobile units never see a reference site.
Read together the implication is uncomfortable and specific: Babu's standard deviation is
\textbf{79\% of the mean enhancement}, and on a moving platform that dispersion is
inseparable from uncalibrated instrument response, sampling-line losses and 6\,s response
time. The Rouen dataset exists because that inseparability is the field's open problem;
the Madurai paper reports hotspots without addressing it. Neither is wrong. The gap
between them is where the calibration-transfer literature of the past fortnight ---
Ginsburg's regional PurpleAir corrections, Zhivkov's distance-dependent uncertainty ---
now has to go: from static co-location to platforms that move.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The connector layer was widened, and it changed what the day looks like.}
This issue is the first run to sweep the full set of connected research surfaces ---
PubMed, Amass BiomedCore, Consensus, Scholar Gateway, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, medRxiv,
Crossref, Exa and Nimble --- alongside the committed harvester. The measurable result is
\textbf{not} more same-day records: PubMed's entry-date window is still the only leg that
reliably resolves a single day. What the wider sweep produced was \textbf{three
high-value records with pre-watch entry dates} (9 July, 29 June, 9 June) that no
date-windowed query could ever have surfaced, and \textbf{seven duplicates} that
\texttt{seen.json} caught on PMID or DOI. Both outcomes are worth having; neither
substitutes for the entry-date spine.

\item \textbf{Instrument-side particle metrics finally outnumber mass metrics.}
Six of sixteen records report absorption coefficient, number size distribution,
heavy-metal content or optical apportionment rather than a regulated mass concentration.
The 9 August run log flagged that \texttt{pmclass()} had no bin for any of these and
collapsed them all into ``PM (unspeciated)''. Four new classes were added in this issue.
The fallback bin did not disappear --- it still holds \textbf{4} records --- but those
four now genuinely have no stated particle metric, and \textbf{5} records that would have
been buried there are separable as optical properties or composition/speciation.

\item \textbf{Source apportionment moves from composition to optics.}
Acton-Bond et al.\ apply \emph{rolling} positive matrix factorisation to aerosol
\emph{optical} apportionment rather than to filter-based chemical speciation. If the
approach holds, it puts source attribution within reach of an aethalometer rather than a
laboratory, which is directly relevant to what a distributed network can be asked to
report. The record is metadata-only, so this is a flag, not a finding.

\item \textbf{The measurement-comparability question is being asked out loud.}
Wang et al.\ title a paper ``operational definitions, method comparability and
environmental interpretation'' for heavy metals in PM. That is the same critique this
watch has been making of low-cost mass measurement, applied to a different quantity:
the number you report is a property of your protocol before it is a property of the air.

\item \textbf{Health-side records are all weak designs today, and one of them is
instructive about why.} Chin et al.\ regress monthly counts of HS clinical encounters on
monthly counts of days above 12\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ and report $\beta=0.57$ at lag 0 and
$\beta=0.62$ at lag 2, with lag 1 and lag 3 null. A non-monotonic lag structure across
four adjacent lags in a single-centre monthly series is a pattern of multiple comparisons,
not a biological latency.

\item \textbf{No forest plot again --- seventh consecutive issue.} Nothing in the corpus
is ratio-scale with an interval. The two quantitative health estimates are a
\textbf{$-$11.8\,g} birth-weight difference per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ second-trimester
PM$_{2.5}$ and a \textbf{$+$23.1\%} preterm-birth risk increase, both quoted without CIs
in a narrative review. \texttt{f5\_forest.png} is intentionally absent.

\item \textbf{Standing gap, now measured over 469 records:} still not one paper couples an
instrument to a health endpoint. The MARTHA device flagged on 9 August
(\texttt{10.1016/j.jaerosci.2026.106882}) remains without an abstract in Crossref or
Europe PMC after 72 hours, so it cannot yet be counted.
\end{itemize}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 6 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Acton-Bond et al.\ 2026}
{Rolling positive matrix factorisation for aerosol optical apportionment}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Scholar Gateway.
Listed, not summarised. Carried because \emph{rolling} PMF applied to optical rather than
chemical data is a specific and consequential claim: conventional PMF source
apportionment requires filter sampling and offline speciation, whereas an optical
implementation with a rolling window could in principle run on an aethalometer or a
multi-wavelength absorption photometer at deployment cadence. That would move source
attribution from a laboratory capability to a network capability, which is the
distinction that decides whether a distributed sensing programme can report anything
beyond mass. Whether the paper supports that reading is unknown until an abstract
appears.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122295}

\paperentry{Akaezuwa et al.\ 2026}
{SFFO-RL intelligent environmental monitoring: smart sensing networks with artificial
intelligence}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Proposes an ``Intelligent Environmental Monitoring Framework'' combining adaptive
filtering, Isolation Forest outlier removal, min--max normalisation, multi-order neighbour
feature fusion and a reinforcement-learning optimiser, reporting \textbf{97.1\,\%
accuracy, 97.8\,\% F1, 97.6\,\% recall and 98.21\,\% precision}. Those figures cannot be
interpreted. The abstract names no pollutant, no dataset, no sensor, no units, no sample
size and no held-out split; ``accuracy'' is quoted for what is elsewhere described as
predicting continuous pollutant concentrations, which is a category error unless the task
was silently discretised. A four-metric cluster in the 97--98\,\% band on environmental
time series with autocorrelation is the signature of evaluating on temporally adjacent
data. Carried as tier C because the framework class (edge-side denoising plus
learned correction) is squarely on this watch's axis and the record should be visible
in the register; the reported numbers should not be cited.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15724-0}

\paperentry{Babu et al.\ 2026}
{Capturing the invisible: hyperlocal variability of size-resolved particulate matter in a
smart city}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Mobile monitoring campaign in Madurai, Tamil Nadu, during the northeast-monsoon retreat:
a vehicle-mounted size-resolved optical instrument sampling every \textbf{6\,s} across
$\sim$\textbf{2.4\,km$^2$} aggregated to a \textbf{50\,m\,$\times$\,50\,m} grid.
Local enhancement above route background was largest for PM$_{10}$ at
\textbf{188.91\,$\pm$\,149.07\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, attributed to road dust and construction;
evening PM$_{1}$ (\textbf{42.10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}) exceeded morning levels; the size
distribution was \textbf{bimodal}, with coarse (16.77--32.37\,$\mu$m) and submicron
(0.275--0.53\,$\mu$m) peaks. Getis-Ord Gi* identified persistent hotspots on traffic
corridors and cold spots in restricted-access zones; SEM-EDX on 22 elements linked size to
composition. The limitation that threatens the inference is that the reported dispersion
(SD 79\,\% of the mean) is not decomposed: on a moving platform, true spatial variance,
uncalibrated optical response, inlet losses and 6\,s instrument response time are
confounded, and no co-location against a reference monitor is described. Directly relevant
to distributed sensing: this is the spatial resolution a mobile network buys, reported
without the calibration chain that would make it transferable.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15746-8}

\paperentry{Bobbia et al.\ 2026}
{Multi-source dataset of NO$_2$ and PM from mobile and fixed sensors in Rouen (France)}
{Data in Brief}
{\textsc{backfill} (PubMed entry 2026-07-09; surfaced by the Amass connector, not by the
date window). Data descriptor for the TIGA ``Smart Mobility for All'' deployment in Rouen:
\textbf{10 mobile AtmoTrack} units on buses at 10\,s, \textbf{18 fixed AirSensEUR} units
on traffic lights at 1\,min, \textbf{6 reference stations} at 15\,min, plus 2 AtmoTrack
units co-located beside a reference station, over \textbf{15 January--30 November 2025}.
Mobile records carry bus speed, GPS position and onboard RH/T/pressure; fixed records add
O$_\mathrm{x}$, NO and CO. The stated purpose is explicitly \textbf{multi-hop
calibration} --- transferring a reference-derived correction through fixed sensors to
mobile units that never see a reference site --- and heterogeneous data fusion. This is
the single most directly usable record of the issue for distributed low-cost PM sensing,
because it is the rare case where the co-location topology needed to test calibration
transfer is published rather than described. Limitation: it is a data descriptor, so it
contains no calibration result, no error metric and no validation; the value is entirely
in the availability of the raw structure.}
{10.1016/j.dib.2026.113025}

\paperentry{Gilardoni et al.\ 2026}
{First continuous aerosol measurements at Testa Grigia at 3480\,m\,a.s.l.: aerosol
populations and dust transport dynamics in the southern European Alps}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{First continuous \textbf{number size distribution and absorption coefficient} record at
1\,h resolution from the Testa Grigia Observatory (3480\,m\,a.s.l., Italian Alps),
spanning \textbf{September 2021--May 2023}. Three aerosol population types are separated
by transport pathway: a coarse population indicating long-range Saharan transport, a fine
population tied to Po Valley and alpine-valley boundary-layer dynamics and broader
continental flow, and low-number periods representing free-tropospheric background. The
record is a baseline dataset rather than a hypothesis test, and the limitation is the one
every single-site campaign carries --- population attribution rests on back-trajectory and
covariance reasoning, not on independent source measurement. Relevant to distributed
sensing for a narrow but useful reason: a high-altitude site with characterised free-
tropospheric periods gives an absolute-zero reference for optical instruments that
low-altitude urban co-location cannot provide, and the absorption-coefficient record is
one of the few instrument-side quantities published at deployment cadence.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11135-2026}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026}
{Measuring heavy metals in atmospheric particulate matter: operational definitions, method
comparability and environmental interpretation}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Scholar Gateway.
Listed, not summarised. Carried because the title states the problem this watch treats as
central, transposed to a different measurand: for heavy metals in PM, the reported
concentration depends on the digestion protocol, the filter substrate, the size cut and
whether total or bioaccessible fraction is meant --- so ``comparability'' is a property of
the method chain, not of the air. The same argument applies to low-cost optical mass
measurement, where the reported $\mu$g/m$^3$ is a function of assumed density, refractive
index and hygroscopic growth. A systematic treatment of operational definitions on the
metals side would be worth reading against the sensor-calibration literature.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122291}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 4 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Fan et al.\ 2026}
{Santa Ana winds control wildfire smoke transport and related health impacts in California
during January 2025}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Scholar Gateway.
Listed, not summarised. Carried because it is the first record in this watch to attach a
health-impact estimate to the January 2025 Los Angeles fires, and because the physical
premise is specific and testable: offshore Santa Ana flow reverses the usual coastal
transport direction, so smoke-attributable exposure is not predicted by proximity to the
burn perimeter. Satellite work on the same event (Li et al., \textit{AGU Advances}, Feb
2026) reports spread rates to \textbf{3.7\,km\,h$^{-1}$}, residential-area fire
intensities of \textbf{72--297\,W\,m$^{-2}$} and a total-PM emission coefficient of
\textbf{10.2--21.1\,g\,MJ$^{-1}$}, with 24\,h mean PM$_{2.5}$ within 7.5\,km reaching
\textbf{300\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} --- context, not this paper's result. The health-impact
methodology is unknown until an abstract appears, and for a wildland--urban-interface fire
burning structures the applicability of vegetation-fire concentration--response functions
is exactly the assumption that needs checking.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122292}

\paperentry{Gong et al.\ 2026}
{Regional analysis of urban physical factors influencing air pollution: evidence from
Seoul's living areas}
{Res Sq (preprint)}
{Constructs a \textbf{100\,m} grid over the Seoul metropolitan area, kriges a continuous
surface from \textbf{193 monitoring stations} for SO$_2$, CO, NO$_2$, O$_3$, PM$_{10}$ and
PM$_{2.5}$ combined into a Comprehensive Air-quality Index, reduces 15 urban-form
variables to four principal components (urban enclosure, low-density buildings,
high-density buildings, development intensity) and fits geographically weighted
regression. CAI rises from \textbf{88--92} in northeastern Seoul to \textbf{96--104} in
the southwest. The headline model comparison --- adjusted \textbf{R$^2$ 0.9928} for GWR
against \textbf{0.0182} for OLS --- should be read as a warning rather than a result. A
54-fold jump in explained variance on a kriged surface means the GWR is largely
reproducing the interpolation, since kriged values at 100\,m between 193 stations are
smooth by construction and locally varying coefficients will fit that smoothness almost
exactly. No spatial cross-validation or effective-degrees-of-freedom correction is
reported. Tier C. Carried because the urban-form-to-exposure framing is the right one for
siting a sensor network, and because it is a clean illustration of how interpolated
exposure surfaces manufacture model fit.}
{10.21203/rs.3.rs-10443285/v1}

\paperentry{Hernandez et al.\ 2026}
{Urbanization and humidity impact atmospheric deposition of anthropogenic microparticles
(including microplastics) in a humid subtropical city}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Scholar Gateway.
Listed, not summarised. Carried at tier C because atmospheric microplastic is a particle
class that optical monitors detect but do not identify: fibres in particular violate the
spherical-equivalent-diameter assumption underlying every optical particle counter, so a
deposition record that quantifies their abundance as a function of urbanisation and
humidity bears on the size and shape distribution a low-cost sensor is actually sampling.
The humidity dependence is the part worth reading if an abstract appears, since RH is
simultaneously the dominant artefact in optical PM measurement and, here, an exposure
variable.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122294}

\paperentry{Lin et al.\ 2026}
{Elevated aerosol layers within the daytime mixed layer over ecologically sensitive areas
of northwest China: diurnal variation, formation mechanisms and regional effects}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Identifies an elevated aerosol layer embedded \emph{within} the daytime mixed layer over
the Hexi Corridor using intensive vertical-profile campaigns in \textbf{2010 and 2012} at
Dunhuang and Minqin. Excluding dust events, elevated layers occurred at
\textbf{0.6--2\,km} on \textbf{82\,\%} of days at Dunhuang and \textbf{44\,\%} at Minqin,
with the paper analysing formation mechanism, air-quality consequence and radiative
effect. The relevance to surface monitoring is direct and under-appreciated: a
concentration maximum aloft but inside the mixing layer breaks the well-mixed assumption
that lets a surface monitor stand for a column, and it is precisely the configuration in
which satellite AOD and ground PM$_{2.5}$ decouple --- which is the failure mode of every
AOD-to-surface-PM scaling relationship. Limitations: two campaigns fourteen years old, two
sites, and an arid region whose boundary-layer behaviour does not generalise to the humid
urban settings where most low-cost networks are deployed.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11111-2026}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 1 record}{Violet}

\paperentry{Cho et al.\ 2026}
{\textit{Lacticaseibacillus paracasei} ATG-E1 alleviates particulate-matter-induced airway
inflammation in association with altered inflammatory signalling, gut microbiota and
faecal metabolites}
{J Microbiol Biotechnol}
{BALB/c mice challenged intranasally with PM$_{10}$D (PM$_{10}$ plus diesel exhaust
particles) on days 0, 3, 6 and 8, then treated orally with ATG-E1 or dexamethasone
\emph{after} inflammation was established --- a therapeutic rather than prophylactic
design, which is the methodological point in its favour. ATG-E1 reduced BALF inflammatory
infiltrate and neutrophilia, lowered collagen deposition and pro-inflammatory mediators,
and attenuated \textbf{I$\kappa$B$\alpha$ and ERK phosphorylation} while leaving JNK and
p38 unchanged; caspase-1 and IL-1$\alpha$ fell, lung RNA-seq showed broad downregulation
of cytokine--cytokine-receptor signalling, and cecal 16S plus faecal metabolomics showed
microbiome remodelling with increased branched-chain fatty acids and decreased
branched-chain amino acids. Two limits: the microbiome and metabolite changes are
correlative, with no germ-free, antibiotic or transfer arm to establish that the gut axis
mediates the airway effect rather than co-occurring with it; and no effect size, n per
group or variance is given in the abstract, so the magnitude is unassessable. All authors
are employed by the company that owns the strain.}
{10.4014/jmb.2604.04029}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 1 record}{Clay}

\paperentry{Zhao et al.\ 2026}
{Occupational dust exposure and thyroid nodule screening: a predictive modelling approach
for early detection in coal mining communities}
{J Int Med Res}
{Cross-sectional analysis of \textbf{1,708} coal miners examined at an occupational-disease
hospital in Anhui in April 2021, combining general, laboratory and imaging data to predict
nodular thyroid disease, using a proposed ``Nonlinear Inverse Nearest Manifold
Projection'' model benchmarked against established risk models. The record is carried at
tier C and should not be cited for a result: the abstract reports that the model
``achieved high scores in both F1 and area under the precision--recall curve'' and
``significantly outperformed'' comparators \textbf{without giving a single numeric value},
confidence interval, or comparator name. Beyond the missing numbers, dust exposure is
never measured --- occupation is the exposure proxy, with no mass concentration, no
respirable fraction, no duration and no job-exposure matrix --- so the paper cannot
support any dose--response claim about particulate exposure and thyroid nodules. Included
because occupational particulate exposure with thyroid endpoints is a thin literature and
the register should show what is in it.}
{10.1177/03000605261473853}

\band{Reproductive \& developmental\hfill 1 record}{Sky}

\paperentry{Hahad et al.\ 2026}
{Air pollution and adverse birth outcomes: a narrative review of epidemiological and
mechanistic findings}
{J Environ Health Sci Eng}
{\textsc{backfill} (PubMed entry 2026-06-29; surfaced by the Amass connector). Narrative
review synthesising epidemiological and mechanistic evidence linking prenatal air
pollution to low birth weight, preterm birth and stillbirth, with searches across PubMed,
Scopus and Web of Science. The two quoted estimates are a \textbf{$-$11.8\,g} birth-weight
difference and a \textbf{$+$23.1\,\%} increase in preterm-birth risk per
\textbf{10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} second-trimester PM$_{2.5}$; mechanisms discussed are oxidative
stress, inflammation, endocrine disruption, vascular dysfunction and epigenetic
modification acting on placental morphology, perfusion and metabolic function. Both
estimates are quoted \textbf{without confidence intervals or provenance to a specific
pooled analysis}, which is why neither enters the forest plot --- a narrative review is
not a meta-analysis, and a point estimate lifted from one without its interval is not an
effect size. The review is honest about what remains unresolved: disentangling individual
pollutants, establishing causality, and identifying critical windows. Relevant to sensing
because trimester-specific windows demand exposure resolution that residential-address
annual means cannot deliver.}
{10.1007/s40201-026-00990-4}

\band{Neuro / mental health\hfill 1 record}{Deep}

\paperentry{Domingo 2026}
{Aging in a highly polluted world: challenges and solutions to prevent Alzheimer's disease}
{Arch Toxicol}
{\textsc{backfill} (PubMed entry 2026-06-09; surfaced by the Amass connector). Mechanistic
