\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A meta-analysis of 19 studies shows that the
\emph{harmonisation step} --- not the exposure --- carries most of the heterogeneity in
the PM--venous-thromboembolism literature.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Pooling 19 observational studies and more than \textbf{4.5 million participants}, the
headline long-term estimates are null and enormously heterogeneous:
\textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ HR 1.42 (95\,\% CI 0.88--2.28), $I^2 = 97.0\,\%$} and
\textbf{PM$_{10}$ HR 1.18 (0.85--1.64), $I^2 = 96.2\,\%$}. Restricting to the subset of
studies that \emph{natively reported} effects per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ --- i.e.\ dropping
every estimate the reviewers had to rescale --- collapses the heterogeneity to
\textbf{$I^2 = 0\,\%$} and yields \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ HR 1.070 (1.010--1.135)} and
\textbf{PM$_{10}$ HR 1.023 (1.000--1.047)}. Two orders of magnitude of $I^2$ removed by a
data-handling decision is not a subgroup finding; it says the rescaling of
non-native contrasts (per-IQR, per-SD, per-quartile) into a common per-10 unit is
importing variance that the underlying studies do not contain. The direct consequence
for distributed sensing: a network that reports its own exposure contrast in a
non-standard unit is not merely inconvenient for meta-analysis, it is
\emph{actively destabilising} to it. Reporting per-10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ natively is the
cheapest methodological contribution a monitoring study can make.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The largest single-day issue this watch has built, and only the fourth
to occupy all ten clusters.} 36 records against a median of roughly 14 --- mechanical,
not scientific: a \textit{Toxics} volume release supplied six, and Crossref's by-ISSN leg
43 deposits of which most were rejected. Only the 26 July founding backfill (164 records
over a month) is larger. \textbf{Four deposits arrived abstract-free}, three of them on
the sensing axis --- the refractive-index, fire/extreme-PM, TRAKER and Yangtze records.

\item \textbf{Low-cost PM sensors used as a biological proxy, with a validated
transfer function.} Li et al.\ treat greenhouse PM sensors not as air-quality
instruments but as continuous physical proxies for airborne \textit{Pseudomonas}
aerosol load, triggering targeted qPCR only when an estimated risk threshold is crossed.
Across \textbf{732 plot-days} the surveillance model reached
\textbf{$R^2 = 0.884$ for pathogen aerosol accumulation} and the downstream forecast
\textbf{$R^2 = 0.874$ for daily disease-index change rate}. The framing is the
interesting part: a sensor with no biological specificity is made useful by pairing it
with a specific but expensive assay that it schedules.

\item \textbf{Condensable PM gets a real-time instrument and an age-resolved dose.}
Kang et al.\ characterised CPM from eight vehicle types with a purpose-built real-time
system (PNU-CPM). CPM accounted for \textbf{63.2\,\% of total PM mass for an untreated
level-4 diesel} against \textbf{8.2\,\% for GDI}; organics were dominated by alkanes
($\sim$53.5\,\%) and the water-soluble fraction by SO$_4^{2-}$ and Na$^+$. MPPD
simulation gives higher mass-per-body-weight lung deposition in younger age groups.
CPM is invisible to hot-filter source testing by construction, and semi-volatile
material of this composition will not obey the scattering and hygroscopic assumptions
baked into optical-sensor corrections.

\item \textbf{Speciation beats mass again, on oxidative potential.} In Xi'an,
XGBoost--SHAP attribution over DTT assays makes \textbf{potassium and manganese} --- a
biomass-burning tracer and a redox-active transition metal --- the dominant contributors
to volume-normalised OP, despite water-soluble metals being a minor mass fraction. Dust
dominates winter OP by loading, traffic dominates summer OP by intrinsic toxicity. Same
conclusion as the 26 August component-IRR result, different measurement chain.

\item \textbf{A well-powered null more informative than most positives.} Instrumenting
municipal PM$_{2.5}$ with wind direction across \textbf{$>$3.5 million French
municipality-weeks}, Morvan et al.\ find \emph{no} effect on COVID-19 hospitalisation or
death among people without pre-existing conditions, and a clear one once comorbidity is
conditioned on (\textbf{+1.5 deaths per 100{,}000 per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, 0.8--2.2;
respiratory 0.9 (0.4--1.4), cardiovascular 0.6 (0.1--1.1)). The 2020--21 literature was
overwhelmingly ecological and positive; this design has an instrument, and it puts the
effect entirely in the susceptible stratum.

\item \textbf{PM$_1$ outperforms PM$_{2.5}$ in two Guangzhou cohorts.} Prenatal PM$_1$
gave the largest per-5\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ odds of a rapid fetal-weight trajectory
(\textbf{OR 1.56, 1.03--2.37}, versus 1.44 for PM$_{2.5}$ and 1.28 for PM$_{10}$), and
the toddler-sleep cohort put the sub-micron fraction on par with PM$_{2.5}$. One city,
one exposure vendor, so independence is partial --- but the ordering is what deposition
predicts, and what mass-only monitoring cannot see.

\end{itemize}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 6 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Li et al.\ 2026c}
{Early warning of cucumber angular leaf spot from airborne pathogen aerosols estimated with PM sensors}
{Plants (Basel)}
{Two-stage greenhouse surveillance in which \textbf{low-cost PM sensors act as continuous
physical proxies} for suspended pathogen aerosol, and qPCR air sampling is triggered only
when an estimated aerosol risk crosses a threshold. Evaluated on \textbf{732 plot-days}
across spatially independent trials: an Extra Trees model predicted pathogen-aerosol
accumulation at \textbf{$R^2 = 0.884$}, and an XGBoost model integrating aerosol load with
microclimate forecast the daily disease-index change rate at \textbf{$R^2 = 0.874$}. SHAP
identified aerosol concentration and vapour pressure deficit as the primary drivers.
The limitation is stated by the authors and is the right one: PM sensors have no
biological specificity, and the design compensates by scheduling a specific assay rather
than by claiming the sensor sees pathogens. Cross-greenhouse rather than cross-site
validation, and no reference-instrument comparison for the PM channel itself.}
{10.3390/plants15162510}

\paperentry{Kang et al.\ 2026b}
{Emission characteristics and health impacts of vehicle-originated condensable PM}
{J Hazard Mater}
{Condensable PM from \textbf{eight vehicle types} characterised with a purpose-built
real-time system (PNU-CPM) plus offline physicochemical analysis, with emission factors
derived from the real-time record. Untreated \textbf{diesel levels 4 and 5 emitted the
most CPM and total PM}; CPM mass fractions were \textbf{63.2\,\% (diesel L4), 8.2\,\%
(GDI) and 58.8\,\% (LPG 2)}. Organic CPM was $\sim$53.5\,\% alkanes and $\sim$5.1\,\%
esters; dominant water-soluble ions were SO$_4^{2-}$ and Na$^+$. MPPD dosimetry gives
higher deposited mass per body weight in younger age groups. Directly relevant to optical
sensing: CPM is ultrafine, semi-volatile and forms after the tailpipe, so it is missed by
hot-filter source testing and will not follow the scattering or hygroscopic-growth
assumptions that low-cost sensor corrections inherit from combustion soot. No on-road
fleet measurement --- these are dynamometer emission factors.}
{10.1016/j.jhazmat.2026.143367}

\paperentry{Lefevre et al. 2026}
{Urban microclimate dataset from low-cost sensor networks, Reunion Island}
{Data Brief}
{A data descriptor for \textbf{25 self-built LoRa weather stations} deployed on
residential buildings across two contrasting neighbourhoods (Saint-Denis, Le Port),
October 2024 to June 2025, at \textbf{10-minute cadence} for air temperature, relative
humidity, wind speed, global horizontal solar radiation and grey-globe temperature.
Station metadata, QC procedures and maintenance logs are published alongside, and
incomplete stations are identifiable. \textbf{There is no PM channel} --- this is a
microclimate network, not an air-quality one --- so its value here is as a deployment
reference: dense neighbourhood-scale LoRa siting, self-built hardware, published QC, and
an explicitly stated reuse path for model calibration (ENVI-met, STEVE). Tropical
hot-humid deployments are exactly the regime in which PM sensor RH correction fails
worst, and this is a rare open record of what such a network's data actually look like.}
{10.1016/j.dib.2026.113166}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026d}
{Perchloric acid drives new particle formation enhanced by dimethylamine, ammonia and sulfuric acid}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Quantum-chemical calculations coupled to the Atmospheric Clusters Dynamic Code compare
the nucleation enhancement of DMA, NH$_3$ and H$_2$SO$_4$ for perchloric-acid-based new
particle formation, motivated by recent Arctic observations of atmospheric HClO$_4$.
Both DMA and NH$_3$ bind PA strongly through hydrogen bonding and proton transfer, and
\textbf{PA nucleates more readily with DMA than sulfuric acid does}; the PA--DMA cluster
formation rate exceeds PA--NH$_3$ even at overwhelming NH$_3$ excess, with 1:1
PA:DMA clusters controlling growth. Purely computational --- no chamber or field
verification, and the claimed NH$_3$ excess of ``10--100 orders of magnitude'' is a
transcription problem in the abstract rather than a physical statement. Relevant as a
candidate nucleation channel in clean marine and polar boundary layers where sulfuric
acid is scarce, which is where number-concentration instruments see unexplained events.}
{10.5194/acp-26-12171-2026}

\paperentry{Moteki et al. 2026}
{Refractive index of fractal-like aggregates from complex forward-scattering amplitudes}
{J Aerosol Sci}
{\textbf{Metadata-only deposit} --- title and DOI without an abstract. The method named in
the title is the single-particle complex forward-scattering amplitude $S(0^\circ)$
approach, previously used to constrain the black-carbon refractive index at
$\lambda = 0.633\,\mu$m; the new element is \textbf{mass classification of the aggregates}
before the optical measurement, which removes the size-distribution term that dominates
the uncertainty in bulk retrievals. If it delivers, this is the most directly useful
sensing record in the window: every optical PM instrument, from a nephelometer to a
\$20 laser scatter sensor, converts scattered intensity to mass through an assumed
refractive index, and for soot-dominated aerosol that assumption is the largest single
error term. Carried at tier C on the title alone and placed at the top of the abstract
retry list.}
{10.1016/j.jaerosci.2026.106884}

\paperentry{Chen et al. 2026e}
{Aerosol and VOC pollution characteristics along the lower Yangtze, from shipborne observation}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textbf{Metadata-only deposit.} Shipborne mobile measurement of aerosol and VOCs along an
inland waterway corridor --- a platform class this watch sees rarely and one with a
specific methodological problem: the measurement frame moves through the plume it is
sampling, so concentrations are Lagrangian-contaminated in a way that fixed-site data are
not, and self-sampling of the vessel's own exhaust is a standing artefact that requires
wind-relative rejection to control. Whether the authors address either is not
determinable from the title. Carried at tier C; the record is retained rather than
rejected because inland-shipping emissions are a genuinely under-monitored PM source and
the corridor is one of the most heavily trafficked in the world.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122331}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 7 records}{Deep}

\paperentry{Rashid et al. 2026}
{Ultrafine particles and mortality: scoping review of evidence and exposure-assessment limits}
{Toxics}
{PRISMA-ScR scoping review of PubMed and Scopus: \textbf{21 of 704 screened articles},
2007--2025, split \textbf{14 short-term} (time-series or case-crossover against central-site
particle number counters) and \textbf{7 long-term} (land-use regression, chemical transport
or machine-learning surfaces). \textbf{10 of 14 short-term and 6 of 7 long-term studies
reported positive associations}, strongest for respiratory mortality. The finding that
matters for this watch is the second-order one: \textbf{the strongest associations came
from the studies with higher-resolution or source-specific exposure assessment}, a pattern
consistent with reduced Berkson-type misclassification rather than with true effect
heterogeneity. A scoping review does not pool, so there is no summary estimate and no
formal heterogeneity statistic; the exposure-method gradient is observed, not tested.
Still the clearest statement to date of why UFP regulation stalls on measurement.}
{10.3390/toxics14080664}

\paperentry{Chen et al. 2026f}
{Source-specific oxidative potential of PM$_{2.5}$: water-soluble metals via DTT and interpretable ML}
{Toxics}
{Winter and summer ambient PM$_{2.5}$ in Xi'an assayed for DTT oxidative potential, with
XGBoost--SHAP attribution over composition and receptor-model source contributions.
\textbf{Volume-normalised OP (DTT$_v$) was markedly higher in winter}, and although
water-soluble metals are a small fraction of PM$_{2.5}$ mass, \textbf{potassium and
manganese dominated the OP attribution} --- a biomass-burning tracer and a redox-active
transition metal. Source apportionment separates the two seasons cleanly: dust drives
winter OP through mass loading, traffic drives summer OP through intrinsic per-mass
toxicity. The abstract reports no numeric DTT$_v$ values, confidence intervals or model
performance, which is a real gap for a paper whose central claim is quantitative
attribution; SHAP importance is also not a causal decomposition and correlated
constituents will trade rank. The qualitative conclusion --- OP tracks composition and
source, not mass --- is the second independent instance of it this week.}
{10.3390/toxics14080646}

\paperentry{Li et al. 2026d}
{Surface PM transport under a closed earth-air model}
{Environ Pollut}
{One year of continuous in-situ monitoring in the semi-enclosed basement of the Mogao
Grottoes conservation department, testing whether \textbf{earth-air vertical movement}
driven by atmospheric-pressure fluctuation controls surface PM independently of wind and
human activity. Structural equation modelling gives \textbf{CO$_2$ as a tracer for
EVM-driven PM dynamics}, with path coefficients of \textbf{0.452 (PM$_{2.5}$), 0.470
(PM$_1$) and 0.386 (PM$_{100}$)}; $^{222}$Rn regulates PM positively but far more weakly,
and relative humidity is strongly inhibitory. PM$_{2.5}$ and PM$_1$ are strongly affected
by EVM, PM$_{100}$ only weakly. Single site, single enclosure, and SEM path coefficients
on autocorrelated one-year time series overstate precision badly. The mechanism claim is
interesting for indoor and heritage monitoring; the effect sizes should not be
transported.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.128983}

\paperentry{Mainka \& Mainka 2026}
{Urban cycling versus PM$_{2.5}$ exposure: does the benefit outweigh the risk?}
{Preprints.org}
{Scenario analysis of \textbf{162 real commuting trips by two cyclists} in Gliwice across
heating and non-heating seasons, with personal PM$_{2.5}$ measured by low-cost sensor and
inhaled dose reconstructed from concentration, trip duration and cycling-specific
ventilation. Median trip 33.0 min/day. The median physical-activity relative risk was
\textbf{0.836} (a 16.4\,\% all-cause mortality reduction) against a median PM$_{2.5}$
relative risk of \textbf{1.00182} (0.182\,\% increase), for a \textbf{net RR of 0.840
(IQR 0.815--0.879)}; \textbf{160 of 162 trips retained net benefit} under sensitivity
analysis. Two hard limits: \textbf{$n = 2$ commuters}, so the exposure distribution is a
convenience sample of two routes, and the risk functions are transported from published
cohorts rather than estimated. Unreviewed preprint. The useful part is the
method --- trip-resolved dose from a low-cost sensor is the right exposure object for
this question, and almost no one computes it.}
{10.20944/preprints202608.1778.v1}

\paperentry{Moryani et al. 2026}
{Bioaccessibility and speciation of heavy metals in PM$_{2.5}$ road dust}
{Toxics}
{\textbf{48 grid-based samples} across four functional zones (residential, school,
hospital, office) in Karachi ($>$27 million) and Shikarpur ($\sim$0.3 million), with the
PM$_{2.5}$ fraction of road dust extracted in artificial lysosomal fluid
(\textbf{pH 4.5, 37\,$^\circ$C, 24 h}) and speciation modelled in PHREEQC and Visual
MINTEQ. \textbf{Cr, As and Pb showed moderate inhalation bioaccessibility}; contamination
and ecological-risk indices were computed alongside. The design addresses a real gap ---
total metal content is a poor predictor of the dose that reaches lung tissue --- but ALF
extraction is a simulation with no in-vivo anchor, and the abstract reports no
bioaccessible fractions, no concentrations and no zone contrasts, so nothing quantitative
can be lifted. Road dust is also resuspended, not directly emitted, which the pollution
indices do not distinguish.}
{10.3390/toxics14080704}

\paperentry{Isworo \& Oetari 2026}
{Risk-integrated industrial air quality: Gaussian dispersion linked to a health-weighted pressure index}
{Environ Sci Pollut Res Int}
{Gaussian-plume dispersion for a Central Java textile facility combined with a
hazard-quotient-normalised Atmospheric Environmental Pressure Index, using an emission
inventory, ERA5 2015--2024 meteorology and six receptor measurements. The headline is a
compliance paradox worth stating: \textbf{modelled concentrations meet Indonesia's
national standards while PM$_{2.5}$ exceeds the WHO 2021 guideline}, and PM$_{2.5}$ and
PM$_{10}$ dominate the index. AEPI values (0.06 baseline, 0.08 predicted) sit in the
authors' own ``very low'' band, which makes the index insensitive at exactly the
concentrations where the health argument bites --- a scaling problem the paper does not
confront. One facility, six receptors, no validation of the plume model against the
measurements. Tier C: the framework is transferable, the result is not evidence.}
{10.1007/s11356-026-38159-z}

\paperentry{Wu et al. 2026b}
{Global fire activity exacerbates extreme PM$_{2.5}$ pollution}
{npj Clim Atmos Sci}
{\textbf{Metadata-only deposit.} The title claim --- that fire activity acts
disproportionately on the \emph{extreme tail} of the PM$_{2.5}$ distribution rather than
on the mean --- is the one that matters, because attainment statistics, exposure-response
functions fitted on annual means and most burden calculations are all mean-anchored and
will understate a tail-loading source. It is also the regime in which low-cost networks
are most useful and least characterised: sensor response at several hundred
$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ of smoke is outside the concentration range of essentially every
published co-location study. Carried at tier C pending the abstract; a Nature-portfolio
deposit with no abstract in Crossref is unusual and may resolve on retry.}
{10.1038/s41612-026-01527-0}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 3 records}{Teal}

\paperentry{Correa Lara et al. 2026}
{Ambient PM and venous thromboembolism: updated systematic review and meta-analysis}
{Thromb Haemost}
{\textbf{19 observational studies, $>$4.5 million participants}, searched across PubMed,
Embase, Web of Science and Cochrane, harmonised to 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ and pooled under
restricted maximum likelihood with Hartung--Knapp adjustment. Overall long-term:
\textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ HR 1.42 (0.88--2.28), $I^2 = 97.0\,\%$}; \textbf{PM$_{10}$ HR 1.18
(0.85--1.64), $I^2 = 96.2\,\%$}. Restricting to studies natively reporting per
