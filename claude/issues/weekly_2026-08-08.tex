\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}The week in one paragraph}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}This was the week the low-cost-sensor calibration
literature stopped asking whether a correction fits and started asking where it stops
working --- and the same week the epidemiology independently concluded that
source-\ and co-exposure-resolved metrics beat total mass.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Twenty-four of the 146 pooled records are low-cost-sensor or calibration papers, and the
strong ones share a single framing. Ginsburg et al.\ tested the US EPA PurpleAir
correction \emph{worldwide} and found it requires regional recalibration; Zhivkov et al.\
quantified calibration-transfer error as a function of \emph{distance} from the reference
node; Chung et al.\ characterised PurpleAir deployment \emph{longevity} across the US
2016--2025; Gäbel \& Hertig examined \emph{recalibration} practice against sensor
ageing. None of these is a new correction equation. All four are about the boundary of an
existing one. On the health side the convergence is independent: Cantuaria et al.\
(\textit{EHP}, \textbf{159{,}769} pooled Danish participants) found that decomposing
PM$_{2.5}$ by source inverts which fraction is harmful relative to UFP and elemental
carbon (total PM$_{2.5}$ \textbf{HR 1.11, 1.05--1.17} per IQR for incident COPD);
Hur et al.\ (\textbf{262{,}288} Kyushu deaths) found the SPM--respiratory-mortality
association separates on pollen load (\textbf{1.021, 1.005--1.037} on high-pollen days
against \textbf{1.003, 0.987--1.018} on low); Sassano et al.\ found household biomass
fuel carried \textbf{HR 1.20 (1.04--1.37)} for kidney-disease mortality with the estimate
splitting on chimney presence. \textbf{For this to be a real convergence rather than a
reading imposed on it, the two literatures would have to be citing each other. They are
not.} Not one of the 146 records validated an instrument against a health endpoint. The
convergence is on the \emph{demand} --- resolved exposure --- and the gap between the two
sides is the finding, not the agreement.
\end{keybox}

\noindent\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\FIGDIR/w1_subtopic_trend.png}
\figcap{Per-issue subtopic counts across the week from \texttt{state/metrics.csv}.
\textbf{Read the denominators first}: the daily totals were 31, 25, 13, 10, 27, 26 and 14,
a factor of three between the largest and smallest day, so every count here is a share of
a moving base (the 8 August column exists only because \texttt{metrics.csv} was rebuilt

that day). The one genuine inflection is 2 August, when the sensing cluster reached

literature: the Crossref-by-ISSN leg was widened that day and immediately surfaced ten
calibration papers that the PubMed legs structurally cannot see. The 4--5 August trough
(13 and 10 records) is a mid-week Elsevier deposit lull, and the 8 August floor of 14 is
a Saturday. \textbf{None of the three is a signal about the field.} The only movement
that survives normalisation is the steady 32--54\,\% measurement-side share, which held
every single day of the week.}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Emerging this week.} \emph{Calibration transferability as an object of study in
its own right} --- absent from the preceding week, and this week carried by four
independent groups (Ginsburg, Zhivkov, Chung, Gäbel \& Hertig) plus Barkjohn et al.'s
Air Sensor Network Analysis Tool, an R-Shiny QA/QC application that is infrastructure
rather than a result. \emph{Network design as an optimisation problem} also appears for
the first time: Jafari et al.\ jointly optimise bus-mounted mobile sensing against fixed
reference placement, and Bhardwaj \& Subramanian pose identifiability-aware source
apportionment for sparse city-scale networks. Both treat siting as a decision variable
rather than a constraint, which is new in this corpus.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Fading.} Neuro and mental-health work collapsed from 22 records in the preceding
week to \textbf{2}, and reproductive/developmental from 24 to 4. \textbf{This is almost
certainly journal-issue timing, not decline.} The preceding week's count was inflated by a
164-record seed backfill on 26 July that included several review clusters; the honest
comparison is 146 records over 7 issues this week against 131 over 6 issues in the
preceding week excluding that backfill --- 20.9 against 21.8 per issue, i.e.\ flat.
Do not read the subtopic deltas against the raw 295.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Flat but load-bearing.} Exposure assessment and modelling (32 records) and
mechanistic toxicology (11) held their share through both the peaks and the troughs.
Chemical-transport modelling, satellite retrieval and source apportionment are the
constant substrate of this corpus; they generate few headlines and most of the methods
everything else depends on.

\footnotesize
\textbf{Recurring author and group clusters.} The naive first-author count is
\textbf{misleading and must not be used}: the top entries are Li (5), Wang (4), Zhang (3),
Liu, Zhao, Guo, Yang and Kim (2 each), which are among the most common surnames in the

\textbf{Orr} appears twice in genuinely different roles --- the \textit{EHP} wildfire-smoke
and influenza analysis across six US states, and a \textit{JMIR} feasibility study of an
in-home air-quality monitor in families of children with asthma --- and those two records

says is missing. \textbf{Kumpika} appears twice because \textbf{the same paper shipped in

\textbf{This is a deduplication failure, one record in 146, and it is stated here rather
than quietly corrected} --- the pooled count is 146 records over 145 unique DOIs.
Beyond that, repeat appearances in this corpus are the exception: 146 records, no
institution with more than two, and no evidence of a single programmatic output stream
inflating apparent consistency.

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Venue concentration.} \textit{Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics} is the largest
single contributor at \textbf{11 records}, and that is a themed concentration rather than
a research surge --- ACP publishes continuously and the deposits cluster by editorial

\vspace{2mm}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Trial registry watch}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}From \texttt{state/trials.json}: registrations
surfaced inside the window. Daily issues accumulate; the rollup is where they surface.
\textbf{21 registrations} entered state during 2--8 August --- 16 from the 2 August sweep,
1 on 4 August, 3 on 7 August and 1 on 8 August. Note a hard limitation of the connector:
it exposes \emph{no} last-update-date filter, so these are relevance sweeps, not true
window queries, and \texttt{first\_seen} means ``first seen by this pipeline'', not
``registered or updated on that date''.\par}

\vspace{1.5mm}
\footnotesize
\textbf{The structural finding first.} \textbf{Of the 21, at least 13 are sham- or
placebo-controlled portable-air-cleaner or filtration trials.} That is the intervention
literature in this field almost in its entirety, and it has a common design problem:
a HEPA unit in a home typically buys a 30--60\,\% reduction in indoor PM$_{2.5}$ over a
few months, which is a small absolute exposure contrast, and the trials are then powered
on \textbf{surrogate endpoints} --- blood pressure, spirometry, biomarkers, cognitive
scores --- rather than events. Where a hard outcome is attempted, enrolment is far too
small for it.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Adequately powered for their stated endpoint (surrogate).}
\textbf{NCT06376994} (JHSPH Center for Clinical Trials, air cleaner vs sham in COPD,
\textbf{n=770}, primary completion 2029-08-31) and \textbf{NCT05874479} (NYU Langone,
active vs sham PAC in NYC public housing, blood pressure, \textbf{n=440}, 2027-09) are the
two that can plausibly detect a several-mmHg or modest spirometric difference.
\textbf{NCT06070428} (Henry Ford, PATHFINDER, PAC at discharge in heart failure,
\textbf{n=400}, 2027-06) is the only one of the three with an event-adjacent population,
and even there the endpoint is not a hard event.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Underpowered or endpoint-mismatched --- flagged.} \textbf{NCT06534385}
(Alaska Native Tribal Health Consortium, HEPA purifiers in rural Alaska homes,
\textbf{n=15}) and \textbf{NCT07118189} (Kaiser Permanente, N95 + indoor air cleaner for
wildfire smoke, \textbf{n=20}) cannot support any inferential health claim and should be
read as feasibility work. \textbf{NCT07536178} (UBC, controlled lodgepole-pine woodsmoke
vs filtered air, n=24) is a mechanistic exposure study, correctly small, and is the
cleanest causal design in the list. \textbf{NCT06197477} (Hasselt, school purifiers,
\textbf{n=900}) is the largest trial in state and carries \textbf{four cognitive
co-primary endpoints with no secondaries and no stated multiplicity control} --- with
status \texttt{UNKNOWN} past a December 2025 primary completion, it is now also a
publication-risk item.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{New on 8 August.} \textbf{NCT05368493} (New York State Psychiatric Institute,
prenatal air pollution and neurodevelopment in Puerto Rican children,
\textbf{n=182}, \texttt{ENROLLING\_BY\_INVITATION}, primary completion 2028-06-01).
\texttt{analyze\_endpoints} returns a \emph{single} primary --- bilateral middle frontal
cortex and corpus callosum volumes, scanned at either 6--36 months or 6--11 years --- and
\textbf{zero secondary endpoints}. Two problems worth recording now rather than at
readout: the registry classifies it \texttt{INTERVENTIONAL} when the only listed
intervention is an MRI task, so it is an observational neuroimaging cohort mislabelled;
and the primary is measured at two non-overlapping developmental windows depending on
enrolment timing, which makes the effective sample for either window well under 182.
Exposure is modelled ambient PM, not measured.

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Standing watch list --- results due or overdue.} \textbf{NCT05994937}
(NYU Langone, PAC glycaemic/cardiometabolic biomarkers, n=150) reached primary completion
\textbf{2026-03-16} with \textbf{no results posted} --- five months overdue.
\textbf{NCT06534385} (2026-04-01) and \textbf{NCT07047430} (U. Washington, airport-adjacent
asthma, 2026-06-01) are also past completion. \textbf{NCT07111208} (MARKOPOLO, noise and
UFP, n=180) had primary completion \textbf{2026-07} and is the only UFP-exposure
interventional registration in state. \textbf{NCT07344987} (Peking Union, ILD crossover,
2026-12) and \textbf{NCT07118189} (2026-12-31) close this year.

% -------------------------------------------------------------- gaps & direction
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Measurement--epidemiology coupling: still zero.} Across 146 records and 24
low-cost-sensor papers, \textbf{not one validated an instrument against a health
endpoint}. The nearest approach is Orr et al.\ (\textit{JMIR}), which measured
feasibility and acceptability of an in-home monitor in families of children with asthma
but reported no exposure--response. The two literatures were adjacent all week and never
touched.

\item \textbf{Ultrafine and speciated metrics.} Five records mention UFP; \textbf{exactly
one measures it as an exposure} (Cantuaria et al., where the local-traffic fraction
dominates for UFP and elemental carbon while the \emph{non}-traffic fraction dominates for
PM$_{2.5}$ --- an inversion with direct siting implications). The rest are a narrative
review, a dosimetry update, a filtration-CFD paper and a mechanistic pathway study.
Against 55 PM$_{2.5}$-only and 18 joint PM$_{2.5}$/PM$_{10}$ records, the deposition
physics remains badly under-served.

\item \textbf{Geographic coverage vs.\ attributable burden.} China 21, North America
$\approx$24, Europe $\approx$10 against \textbf{South Asia $\approx$9 and sub-Saharan
Africa 5}. India and Bangladesh together carry a larger share of global PM-attributable
DALYs than China and North America combined and contributed fewer than ten records in
seven days. The one sub-Saharan primary instrumentation record --- Rehbein et al.\ on
source triangulation from a low-cost network in Ile-Ife --- is exactly the kind of work the
gap needs, and it is one paper.

\item \textbf{Calibration transferability: improving, and now the week's strongest
theme.} Unlike previous weeks, this corpus \emph{does} contain held-out and out-of-region
evaluation: Ginsburg et al.\ (US correction applied worldwide), Zhivkov et al.\
(error as a function of distance from reference), Gomez et al.\ (ML/DL against a T640X
reference), Chung et al.\ (deployment longevity, US 2016--2025). \textbf{What is still
missing is a common benchmark} --- every one of these evaluates on its own network, so the
numbers are not comparable across papers.

\item \textbf{Negative and null results.} Present, which is a good sign. Russo et al.\
report \textbf{protective} point estimates in a spermatogenesis-window analysis
(\textbf{RR 0.71, 0.55--0.93} and 0.73, 0.50--1.05) that are more likely selection or
window misspecification than a real benefit; Orr et al.\ report a protective estimate in
Washington state (\textbf{0.884, 0.842--0.919}) that the paper itself cannot explain
except by outcome misclassification; Cantuaria reports two null source-resolved HRs
(1.02, 1.00--1.05 and 1.05, 0.99--1.11). \textbf{A corpus with no nulls is a
reporting-bias signal; this one has them, and they are informative rather than
decorative.}
\end{itemize}

\vspace{3mm}
\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Where this is heading --- judgement, clearly
marked as such}\\[1.2mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{The methodological demand the field is converging on is a resolved exposure
record, and the convergence is arriving from two directions that are not talking to each
other.} From epidemiology: source apportionment (Cantuaria), co-pollutant interaction
(Hur), microenvironment (Sassano, Tillery) and sub-daily lag structure (Shukla) each
independently showed this week that a scalar mass concentration is not a sufficient
statistic for the health-relevant exposure. From instrumentation: four groups
simultaneously stopped publishing new correction equations and started publishing the
\emph{failure boundaries} of existing ones. Both are asking for the same object. Neither
is building it. Confidence: moderate-to-high on the description, low on any claim that
this week caused it --- seven days is not enough to establish a turn, and 2 August's
calibration cluster was partly an artefact of widening this pipeline's own Crossref leg.

\vspace{1.4mm}
\textbf{What would change the picture.} A single paper that deploys a calibrated low-cost
network and reports an exposure--response against a clinical endpoint would collapse the
empty heatmap cell that has now survived two weeks of accumulation. Nothing in the 21
registered trials will supply it: they are almost all filtration interventions with
surrogate endpoints, and the two that could (NCT06376994, n=770; NCT05874479, n=440) read
out in 2027 and 2029. If the coupling comes, it will come from a cohort retrofitting a
dense sensor network onto existing outcome ascertainment, not from a trial.

\vspace{1.4mm}
\textbf{What a distributed low-cost PM sensing programme should do differently.} Three
concrete things this week's corpus argues for. \textbf{(1)} Report calibration
performance as a function of distance and time from the co-location reference, not as a
single held-out R$^2$ --- Zhivkov et al.\ give the framing and Chung et al.\ give the
longevity data to do it. \textbf{(2)} Instrument the co-exposure, not just the mass:
Hur's pollen interaction and Cantuaria's source split both say the node needs a second
channel (fluorescence, coarse-mode, or a traffic-proxy gas) before its mass output is
epidemiologically interpretable. \textbf{(3)} Resolve below 24 hours and keep it ---
Shukla localises the asthma signal at a 3--6 hour lag, which no filter-based reference
method can see and which is the one regime where a low-cost network is not merely cheaper
but strictly better. \textbf{The week was informative, and unusually so on the
instrumentation axis specifically.}
\end{keybox}
