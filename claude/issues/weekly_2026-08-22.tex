\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}The week in one paragraph}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Six independent groups stopped analysing PM$_{2.5}$
as a mass this week and started analysing it as a list of constituents --- and in the two
endpoints where a single constituent led, it was the same one. Meanwhile a fifth of the
week's corpus arrived with no abstract, which is the price this pipeline is now paying for
its own coverage.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
The methodological through-line is constituent decomposition, and it is unusually
consistent. \textbf{Wu et al.} (16 Aug) put prenatal PM$_{2.5}$ and its components through
a mixture model for childhood overweight; \textbf{Wang et al.} (16 Aug) do the same for
early-life anaemia across China, India and 26 African countries; \textbf{Zhao et al.}
(17 Aug) report spontaneous abortion by constituent, with \textbf{ammonium highest
(OR 1.19) --- above total mass (1.18)}; \textbf{Parmar et al.} (18 Aug) separate black
carbon from bulk PM$_{2.5}$ for neuropsychiatric hospitalisation in Delhi and find the
component term nearly an order of magnitude larger; \textbf{Chen et al.} (21 Aug) run
five constituents against incident depression in 24{,}940 UK Biobank participants and
again land on \emph{ammonium}; and \textbf{Wei et al.} (21 Aug) run all fifteen components
against mortality in 528{,}127 lung-cancer patients by weighted quantile sum.

\textbf{The answers do not agree, and that is the finding.} Ammonium and nitrate --- the
secondary inorganic fraction --- lead in the reproductive and psychiatric endpoints, while
Wei's mortality weights fall on silicon, vanadium, zinc and iron, a crustal, traffic and
heavy-fuel-oil signature. Two different exposures are being called PM$_{2.5}$. The week
also supplies the atmospheric-chemistry half of that argument: \textbf{Chai et al.}
(21 Aug) measure secondary inorganic aerosol at \textbf{49.55\% of PM$_{2.5}$ mass} in
suburban Nanjing and show the system is total-nitrate-limited, so the very fraction that
the epidemiology is now singling out is the one that does \emph{not} respond to ammonia
control. If a component drives the risk, mass-only monitoring cannot see it and
mass-targeted policy cannot move it.

\textbf{Against that, an operational fact that should be read as a cost.} 22 of 112
records (20\%) carry no abstract from any checked source and are classified but unread ---
almost double last week's 15 of 134 (11\%). \textit{Metadata only} is now the single
largest architecture label in the pooled corpus, ahead of every real study design.
This follows directly from widening the Crossref by-ISSN sweep: the leg buys coverage
of journals PubMed indexes late or not at all, and pays for it in publishers that
deposit a title and a DOI and nothing more.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Subtopic trend and inflection}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\noindent\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\FIGDIR/w1_subtopic_trend.png}
\figcap{Per-issue subtopic counts across the week from \texttt{state/metrics.csv}.
\textbf{Read the denominators first}: daily totals were 10, 12, 18, 22, 18, 19 and 13 ---
a range of 2.2$\times$, and a flatter week than 9--15 August (12 to 28). The 16 August
floor of 10 is a Sunday and the smallest issue the watch has recorded; the 22 August
figure of 13 is a Saturday. \textbf{No day in this week carries a publisher batch
artefact of the kind that distorted 11--12 August}, so unlike last week the trend line can
be read more or less at face value.}

\footnotesize
\textbf{The measurement side took the week back.} \textit{Exposure assessment \&
modelling} rose \textbf{26 $\rightarrow$ 36} while \textit{Sensing, forecasting \&
instrumentation} fell \textbf{34 $\rightarrow$ 22}; together they are 58 of 112 records,
a measurement-side share of \textbf{52\% against last week's 45\% and 54\% the week
before}. \textbf{Do not read this as a recovery in instrumentation.} The sensing cluster
shrank; what grew is exposure and deposition work, and four of the eight
\textit{Exposure} records in the 21 August issue alone measure PM by what it lands on ---
pine needles, privet leaves, tree canopies --- rather than by what it does in air. The
oscillation between 54\%, 45\% and 52\% across three weeks is within the range this
pipeline's own harvesting legs can produce, and no claim about the field should be built
on it.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Contracting, and worth watching.} \textit{Occupational \& indoor} fell
\textbf{9 $\rightarrow$ 4}, \textit{Mechanistic toxicology} \textbf{11 $\rightarrow$ 8}
and \textit{Cardiovascular \& metabolic} \textbf{7 $\rightarrow$ 3}. The mechanistic drop
breaks a two-week run at exactly 11 and is the first movement in that cluster since the
watch began keeping the count. Cardiovascular at 3 is the lowest recorded for what is
normally the field's largest health cluster --- against 10 respiratory-endpoint records
this week. One week is not a trend; two would be.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Holding.} \textit{Other clinical endpoints} rose \textbf{11 $\rightarrow$ 13},
extending last week's finding that the clinical tail is now longer than the
cardiorespiratory trunk, and the content stays unfamiliar: cirrhotic hepatic
decompensation, diabetic microvascular complications, systemic lupus, and --- new this
week --- \textbf{the prognosis of an emerging tick-borne viral infection}. All ten
subtopics were active on at least one day.

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Venues, provenance and what the pipeline did}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Venues.} \textit{Environ Sci Technol} led with 11 records, then
\textit{Atmos Chem Phys} (9), \textit{Atmos Environ} (8) and \textit{Environ Pollut} (6);
\textit{Ecotoxicol Environ Saf}, \textit{Atmos Pollut Res}, \textit{Atmosphere} and
\textit{Atmos Res} contributed 4 each. \textbf{Five preprints} entered the week
(Research Square 3, medRxiv 1, Preprints.org 1) --- 4.5\% of the corpus, and three of the
five carried quantitative results substantial enough to lead a daily issue.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Two harvester changes were made inside this period, and both affect what the
figures above show.} On 22 August the supplementary ISSN list trialled on 20 August was
promoted into the standing Crossref sweep --- 15 additions, each ISSN verified against
Crossref's own journal index, with MDPI \textit{Sensors} deliberately excluded after
returning 27 deposits for zero in-scope records. The immediate effect was that the
Crossref leg carried 62\% of the 22 August issue while PubMed returned two records. The
immediate cost is the abstract-free share discussed above. \textbf{The first run with
\textit{Environ Sci Technol} in the list also returned 154 works of which 142 were
mastheads and issue-information stubs} being re-deposited in bulk from volumes 42--52;
those carry DOIs and titles and would have passed every downstream check, so a
title-pattern front-matter filter now drops them at the source.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Three figure defects were closed during the period and one is disclosed
uncorrected.} The forest-plot axis now enforces a minimum 1.6$\times$ span in log space,
after a single near-null estimate on 21 August was auto-scaled onto an axis spanning 1.5\%
and drawn two-thirds of the way to the right edge. Two design labels
(\texttt{In vitro exposure}, \texttt{In vitro organ-on-chip}) and one more
(\texttt{Health impact model}) were silently falling into ``Other / mixed'' on every issue
and rollup that contained them, and \texttt{Literature review} was mapped to
\textit{Tool / software}; all four are corrected, and this rollup is the first output
rendered with the corrections. \textbf{Disclosed and not corrected:} the endpoint-label
canonicalisation gap reported in the 15 August rollup persists, so \texttt{f2b}'s
``no health endpoint'' bar is assembled here by hand in the caption (62 of 112) rather
than being read off the figure.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{OpenAlex has now returned HTTP 429 on twenty consecutive runs} and should be
treated as removed from the pipeline rather than degraded. Europe PMC's
\texttt{CREATION\_DATE} leg continues to work but is increasingly dominated by archival
back-scans --- legacy PMC page images with no title and a publication year decades outside
the window --- which accounted for 14 of 28 unique Europe PMC hits on 21 August and 15 of
23 on 22 August. \textbf{The trial registry has not been re-queried since 8 August};
rollups do not query APIs, and the standing trial watch is therefore stale by two weeks
and is not restated in this issue.

% -------------------------------------------------------------- gaps
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Nobody measured the exposure they analysed.} Of the 20 tier-A human health
records this week, the overwhelming majority inherit a modelled exposure surface ---
satellite, land-use regression, or a spatiotemporal reanalysis product --- and none reports
that model's error at the spatial scale of its own analysis. The single clear exception is
Liu et al.\ (22 Aug), whose exposure is a directly observed household fuel transition.
This is now the fourth consecutive week the watch has recorded this and it remains the
largest methodological opening for an instrumentation group.

\item \textbf{Constituent-resolved epidemiology has arrived without
constituent-resolved monitoring.} Six papers this week analyse PM$_{2.5}$ by component,
and all six take those components from chemical transport or satellite-derived products.
No record this week measured a speciated ambient time series and linked it to a health
outcome in the same study.

\item \textbf{Ultrafine particles: 2 records in 112.} Unchanged in direction and
magnitude from every previous week of this watch.

\item \textbf{One intervention study in 112 records, and it was not randomised.} The
evidence base being built is almost entirely observational, while the policy questions the
same papers pose --- which precursor to cut, which fuel to subsidise, which filter to
deploy --- are interventional.

\item \textbf{Twenty per cent of the week is unread.} 22 records have no abstract from any
checked source. Two of them (aerosol pH and preterm birth; ammonia-driven ROS generation
in secondary organic aerosol) sit directly on the week's main thread and are the highest
priority for full-text retrieval before the monthly rollup.
\end{itemize}

{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Provenance and method}\\[1.2mm]
{\fontsize{7.2}{8.8}\selectfont
This rollup is an \textbf{aggregation only}. It pools \texttt{state/corpus/*.json} and
\texttt{state/metrics.csv} over \PERIODSTART{}--\PERIODEND{} via
\texttt{PMRW\_START}/\texttt{PMRW\_END} and \textbf{re-queries no literature API}, so every
record was screened and deduplicated in the daily issue that first carried it. Per-record
provenance, source-query windows, exclusions and known metadata defects are documented in
those daily issues and are not restated here.
\textbf{Daily issues covered: 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21 and 22 August 2026 --- seven of seven,
no gap in entry-date coverage.} The 21 and 22 August issues were built as a backfill on the
evening of 22 August after an off-schedule 21 August invocation correctly deferred under
the 22:00 rule; the entry windows are contiguous and nothing was skipped.
\textbf{112 records, 112 unique DOIs} --- no duplicate survived the pooling.
\textbf{Known defects carried into this rollup, stated rather than hidden:}
(i) the \texttt{ENDPOINT\_CANON} gap reported on 15 August is unfixed, so \texttt{f2b}
understates its ``no health endpoint'' bar and the caption states the hand-counted figure;
(ii) journal names are free text, so preprint share is a lower bound;
(iii) \textbf{22 records carry no abstract from any checked source and are classified but
unassessed} --- they are counted in every distribution figure and contribute nothing to the
narrative; (iv) the trial registry has not been refreshed since 8 August and no trial
section is included in this issue rather than presenting stale counts as current.
Effect estimates are transcribed from abstracts and use heterogeneous exposure contrasts
--- see the forest-plot caption; \textbf{no meta-analytic summary is computed and none
should be}. Trend and inflection statements are judgements over a seven-day window and are
not statistical tests; where a movement is plausibly an artefact of this pipeline's own
harvesting legs, the text says so. Content derived from publisher metadata; abstracts
remain the copyright of their respective publishers.\par}
