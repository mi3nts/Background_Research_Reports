\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Monitoring capacity is now measurably
\emph{anti}-correlated with need: in eastern Canada, situationally vulnerable populations
had 54\% higher odds of living in a PM$_{2.5}$ monitoring desert after adjusting for the
concentration itself.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Siu et al.\ (\textit{Environ Res Health}) applied \textbf{locally adaptive kernel density
estimation} to all PM$_{2.5}$ monitors --- regulatory and low-cost --- across Ontario,
Quebec and Atlantic Canada, 2020--2024, setting each bandwidth from the local variability
of surface PM$_{2.5}$ and its sensitivity to distinguishing an exceedance. That is the
methodological move worth stealing: coverage is defined against the concentration field's
\emph{own} spatial structure rather than against a fixed radius, so ``monitored'' means
``able to resolve an exceedance here'', not ``within $x$\,km of a device''. In
mixed-effects logistic regression, residential instability carried \textbf{25\% higher
odds} and situationally vulnerable populations \textbf{54\% higher odds} of monitoring
deprivation, \emph{after} controlling for surface PM$_{2.5}$ and emission-facility
density --- so this is not simply monitors following pollution. With Canada's annual
standard tightening to 8\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ in 2030, the exceedance question becomes
harder exactly where the network is thinnest. Read alongside Jafari et al.\ and the
siting papers of 4 August, a coherent strand is forming: \textbf{siting is an
optimisation problem with an objective function}, and the objective is not coverage area
but resolvable exposure contrast in the populations that carry the burden.
\end{keybox}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.1mm}
\item \textbf{Entry window 5 August 2026, contiguous with the 4 August issue; no gap.}
The health axis was the weakest this watch has recorded: PubMed returned 15 health-axis
and 8 sensing-axis records for the 5 August entry date, of which \textbf{11 were rejected
as off-topic} --- diet scores, dementia checklists, a lead-exposure oncology paper, a
correction notice, therapeutic aerosols, poultry bioaerosols. The signal came from the
non-PubMed legs.

\item \textbf{Half the corpus is sensing, and for the second consecutive issue that is
network \emph{design}, not calibration chemistry.} Siu et al.\ quantify where monitoring
is absent; Jafari et al.\ solve fixed-and-mobile placement jointly as a constrained
optimisation over a Rotterdam bus network, maximising spatiotemporal coverage subject to
a budget \emph{and} to a constraint that every mobile sensor passes a reference station
often enough to stay calibrated. That last constraint is the interesting one: it makes
recalibration cadence a term in the siting objective rather than a separate operational
concern.

\item \textbf{One held DOI from 4 August was recovered and shipped.} Jafari et al.\
(IEEE OJ-ITS) had no retrievable abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Consensus on 4
August; a Semantic Scholar Graph query returned it on 5 August. \textbf{Semantic Scholar
is now a fourth abstract source in the retry chain} --- it is the only index that resolved
any of the five held records. The other four, all Elsevier, remain unretrievable after a
second attempt and stay held rather than rejected.

\item \textbf{G\"abel \& Hertig answer the question the calibration literature keeps
deferring: how often.} A yearlong Augsburg co-location across $-$10 to 36\,$^{\circ}$C
and 19--96\,\% RH finds that a single pre-deployment calibration decays, and that
\textbf{monthly in-season recalibration} is what keeps an SPS30 inside the EU/EPA
indicative-measurement data-quality objective. Carried as an out-of-window backfill
(\textit{Atmos.\ Meas.\ Tech.}, created 19 February 2026) --- \textit{AMT} is structurally
invisible to PubMed, which is exactly the gap the Consensus leg exists to close. It must
be excluded from any weekly trend line.

\item \textbf{Sea breeze as a monitoring-representativeness problem, not a meteorology
result.} Yang et al.\ identify 428 diurnal sea-breeze events in a 10-year coastal record
and show that excluding them changes the diurnal amplitude \emph{and} the seasonal cycle
of O$_3$ and CH$_4$ under southwesterly flow. A coastal background site is therefore not
a background site on those days. Aerosol number showed a pronounced diurnal cycle
attributed to new particle formation from accumulated ship SO$_2$ --- a mechanism that
generates ultrafine number with negligible mass, invisible to a mass-based network.

\item \textbf{Only one effect estimate carries an interval, and it is from a preprint.}
Zhang et al.\ (medRxiv, not peer reviewed) estimate 7.1\% (95\% UI 5.4--8.8\%) of
cardiorespiratory ED visits averted by combined EGU, mobile-source and port policies
across NYC, LA and Atlanta. Fourth consecutive issue without a peer-reviewed PM ratio
estimate with a confidence interval --- see the forest-plot caption so a rollup does not
read this as pipeline loss.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 5 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Siu et al.\ 2026}
{Disparities in fine particulate matter (PM$_{2.5}$) monitoring capacity in eastern
Canada}
{Environ Res Health}
{Locally adaptive kernel density estimation of total PM$_{2.5}$ monitor density ---
regulatory plus low-cost --- across Ontario, Quebec and Atlantic Canada, 2020--2024, with
each local bandwidth set from the surrounding variability of surface PM$_{2.5}$ and its
sensitivity to resolving an exceedance. Mixed-effects logistic regression gives
\textbf{25\% higher odds of monitoring deprivation for residential instability and 54\%
higher odds for situationally vulnerable populations}, after adjusting for surface
PM$_{2.5}$ and emission-facility density; suburban Montreal and Toronto, the Windsor and
Niagara border regions, southeast Kitchener and remote northwest Ontario are named.
Limitation: monitor density is a proxy for information yield, and low-cost and regulatory
devices are pooled without weighting by data quality, so a dense but uncalibrated cluster
counts as capacity. The adaptive-bandwidth construction is directly reusable for
designing where to add nodes rather than merely auditing where they are.}
{10.1088/2752-5309/ae87c0}

\paperentry{Jafari et al.\ 2026}
{Joint optimization of bus-based mobile sensing and reference sensor deployment for urban
air quality monitoring}
{IEEE Open J Intell Transp Syst}
{Mathematical-programming formulation selecting simultaneously \textbf{which buses to
instrument with low-cost sensors and where to place reference stations}, maximising
spatiotemporal network coverage under a budget constraint while requiring that every
mobile sensor pass a reference site frequently enough to be recalibrated. Demonstrated on
a Rotterdam case study covering residential and industrial areas. The contribution is
structural rather than empirical: recalibration cadence enters the siting objective as a
constraint, so coverage and data quality are traded off in one problem instead of
sequentially. Limitation: coverage is geometric --- optimised against route and population
geometry, with no validation against a measured concentration field, and no treatment of
the sampling bias that fixed bus routes impose. This is the record held on 4 August and
recovered on 5 August via Semantic Scholar.}
{10.1109/ojits.2026.3706855}

\paperentry{Yang et al.\ 2026}
{Diurnal sea breeze worsens coastal air quality and complicates monitoring of remote
North Atlantic air}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Ten years of observations from Penlee Point Atmospheric Observatory on the northeast
Atlantic coast, from which \textbf{428 diurnal sea-breeze events} were identified, most
frequent in spring and summer under low wind and strong irradiance. Daytime surface
O$_3$, NO$_x$, CH$_4$, CO$_2$, SO$_2$, total aerosol number, PM$_{2.5}$ and PM$_{10}$ were
all elevated during events, with mean daytime O$_x$ near \textbf{45\,ppb} and increased
regulatory exceedance for O$_3$. Accumulated ship-emitted SO$_2$ appears to drive new
particle formation and a pronounced diurnal cycle in aerosol number. The monitoring
payload: \textbf{excluding sea-breeze events reduces the diurnal amplitude and alters the
seasonal cycle of O$_3$ and CH$_4$} under southwesterly flow --- a coastal ``background''
site is conditionally not background. Limitation: one site, so the event classification
and its consequences are not shown to transfer. Number-without-mass is precisely the
regime a mass-based low-cost network cannot see.}
{10.5194/acp-26-10909-2026}

\paperentry{G\"abel \& Hertig 2026}
{Recalibration of low-cost O$_3$ and PM$_{2.5}$ sensors: linking practices to recent air
sensor test protocols}
{Atmos Meas Tech}
{Yearlong urban-background co-location at Augsburg of Alphasense OX-B431 and Sensirion
SPS30 against reference instruments, spanning $-$10 to 36\,$^{\circ}$C, 19--96\,\% RH and
PM$_{2.5}$ to 153\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$, with MLR, ridge, random forest and XGBoost
calibration functions evaluated against the current US EPA and CEN air-sensor test
protocols. A five-month baseline training gives PM$_{2.5}$ $R^2$ 0.93--0.97 in training
and \textbf{0.84--0.93 in the following test period}, RMSE 2.04--3.02\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$
--- the decay is the finding. Testing pairwise recalibration cycles, \textbf{monthly
in-season recalibration} was required to keep uncertainty inside the data-quality
objective for indicative measurements. Limitation: one site, one year, two sensor models,
so the cadence is an existence result rather than a general rule. \textbf{Out-of-window
backfill} (created 19 Feb 2026); \textit{AMT} is not indexed by PubMed. Exclude from
weekly trend lines.}
{10.5194/amt-19-1293-2026}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026}
{Traffic-induced environmental health assessment at intersections using roadside LiDAR}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Roadside LiDAR extracts high-resolution vehicle trajectories at one urban and one
suburban intersection, from which traffic emissions, noise and ground vibration are
estimated and combined into a fuzzy comprehensive assessment score, with weights set by
quadratic programming under ranking constraints, variable-weight grey entropy, and game
theory to reconcile the two. The urban intersection scored \textbf{69.45 by day, falling
to 57.12 at night ($\sim$18\%)}, while the suburban site was stable at 72.80/74.54;
population-dimension scores were consistently below environmental-dimension scores.
Limitation: PM is \emph{inferred} from trajectories through an emission model and never
measured, so this is a modelled exposure surrogate validated against nothing --- the
elaborate weighting machinery sits on top of an unvalidated emissions estimate. Included
because trajectory-derived emission proxies are an increasingly common input to near-road
sensor fusion, and their weak point should be explicit.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15750-y}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 2 records}{Sky}

\paperentry{Pinakana et al.\ 2026}
{Influence of transboundary wildfire smoke on fine particulate matter concentrations at
South Texas border schools}
{NPJ Clean Air}
{Low-cost sensors deployed at schools in Roma and Rio Grande City, Starr County, on the
US--Mexico border for \textbf{146 days, November 2023 to April 2024}, corrected by
collocation-based calibration against a research-grade instrument and read alongside
meteorology, back trajectories and satellite smoke and fire products. Daily mean
PM$_{2.5}$ was significantly higher on smoke-impacted days ($p<0.001$), with
\textbf{site-level increases of 8.61\% to 54.9\%} --- a sixfold spread across sites within
one small county, which is itself the finding: transboundary smoke impact is not
spatially uniform at the scale a single regulatory monitor would represent. Trajectories
attribute the source to Mexican wildfire emissions. Limitation: one season, no indoor
measurement despite the school framing, and attribution rests on trajectory plus
satellite coincidence rather than on composition. A clean template for low-cost sensor
deployment in an under-monitored community.}
{10.1038/s44407-026-00096-z}

\paperentry{Phalen et al.\ 2026}
{Inhaled aerosol dosimetry update}
{Inhal Toxicol}
{Synthesis of the Fourth International Aerosol Dosimetry Conference (Irvine, 2024)
covering computational deposition models, in vitro and in vivo exposure systems, and
deposition variability. Reported advances: integration of model types to span all major
respiratory-tract regions; improved in vitro particle delivery and dose measurement; the
upper airways recognised as a significant entry route. Two findings matter for ambient PM
work --- \textbf{dose variability emerged as the critical cross-cutting issue}, and
\textbf{deposited ultrafine particles may acquire surface coatings after deposition and
translocate into tissue, including fetal tissue}. Limitation: a conference report, not a
systematic review --- coverage reflects who presented, there are no effect sizes and no
pooling, and the fetal-translocation claim is stated without the primary evidence. Useful
mainly as a statement of where dosimetric uncertainty sits, which is the term that
converts a measured ambient concentration into an internal dose.}
{10.1080/08958378.2026.2691193}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 2 records}{Teal}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026 (preprint)}
{Health impacts of national and local air pollution control policies targeting electric
generating units, mobile sources, and port activities in three US cities}
{medRxiv (preprint, not peer reviewed)}
{Counterfactual policy evaluation for New York City, Los Angeles and Atlanta, 2005--2019.
Daily PM$_{2.5}$, criteria gases, 1-in-3-day PM$_{2.5}$ components and PMF-resolved PM
sources at six monitoring sites were used to construct the concentrations that would have
occurred without the selected EGU, mobile-source and port policies; city-site-specific
multi-pollutant Poisson models then linked observed concentrations to respiratory
emergency-department visits and hospitalised cardiovascular ED visits, and the fitted
models were applied to both concentration series. \textbf{Median PM$_{2.5}$ reductions of
27--62\%} across cities, and \textbf{7.1\% (95\% UI 5.4--8.8\%) of cardiorespiratory
visits averted}. Limitation: not peer reviewed; the counterfactual is a modelled construct
whose uncertainty is not propagated into the health interval, and multi-pollutant Poisson
models over highly correlated criteria pollutants cannot cleanly assign the benefit to PM.
The source-resolved PMF layer is the part worth following.}
{10.64898/2026.08.04.26359640}

\paperentry{Goyal et al.\ 2026}
{Quantifying environmental co-benefits of nitrogen-based crop restructuring and its
implications on India's interstate trade network}
{Nat Commun}
{Integrated nutrient, water, greenhouse-gas, economic and interstate-trade accounting for
Indian agriculture, comparing nitrogen-focused against water-focused crop-restructuring
pathways. The nitrogen-focused pathway cuts \textbf{nitrogen surplus by 13.4\%, water use
by 18.6\% and greenhouse gases by 8.7\%} while holding baseline calorie production, and
delivers about \textbf{4.6-fold larger reciprocal co-benefits} than the water-focused
alternative, worth roughly \textbf{USD 1.19\,bn} in avoided nitrogen-pollution
socio-environmental cost. Limitation: PM is entirely implicit --- reactive nitrogen is a
dominant precursor of secondary inorganic aerosol over the Indo-Gangetic Plain, but no
ambient PM$_{2.5}$ response is modelled and no health endpoint is estimated, so the
PM-relevant co-benefit is asserted through the nitrogen accounting rather than computed.
Carried because agricultural NH$_3$ is the least-constrained term in South Asian PM
source apportionment.}
{10.1038/s41467-026-75905-w}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 1 record}{Clay}

\paperentry{Shi et al.\ 2026}
{Ischemic heart disease in G20 countries: forecasted burden and regional disparities
based on GBD 2023 estimates}
{Catheter Cardiovasc Interv}
{GBD 2023 descriptive burden analysis of ischemic heart disease incidence, mortality and
DALYs across the G20, 1990--2023, with EAPC and joinpoint regression, population
attributable fractions for modifiable risks, ARIMA and Bayesian age-period-cohort
projection to 2050, and XGBoost with SHAP for DALY determinants. \textbf{8.52\,M cases
and 5.81\,M deaths in 2023}; age-standardised incidence and mortality fell (EAPC $-$1.61
and $-$1.49) but incidence rose in women aged 15--49, with Russia highest on all three
age-standardised rates and Japan projected to peak again around 2032. High systolic blood
pressure dominated the risk attribution, with \textbf{particulate matter and lead named as
the prominent environmental contributors}. Limitation: GBD population attributable
fractions are modelled from the same exposure--response literature this watch tracks, so
this is not independent evidence of a PM effect --- it is that literature propagated
through a burden model, and the machine-learning layer adds interpretability, not
identification. Tier C: read as context, not as evidence.}
{10.1002/ccd.70783}
