\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}The week in one paragraph}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}The biggest regular week this watch has run, and the
measurement side took it --- but the structural story is indoors. Occupational \& indoor
went 7 $\rightarrow$ 19, and what arrived was not exposure epidemiology but
\emph{engineering}: ventilation rates, filter media, purifier siting and smoke evacuation.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{Scale first, because it conditions everything below.} 150 records over seven
issues, the largest week outside the founding backfill, on daily totals of 14, 16, 33, 15,
\textbf{38}, 27 and \textbf{7} --- a 5.4$\times$ range. The 7 on Saturday is not a thin
harvest but a \textbf{true PubMed zero}: no record entering PubMed on 5 September matched
either axis, confirmed independently by the connector against a day that admitted only 544
records in total. Read every week-over-week movement below against that volatility.

\textbf{The indoor turn is the week's real finding.} \textit{Occupational \& indoor} at 19
ties the founding backfill week and is more than double any regular week this watch has
recorded (previous regular maximum 9). The composition of that cluster is what matters:
Jin et al.\ (31 Aug) optimise air-change rate against purifier placement jointly rather
than separately; Wood et al.\ (31 Aug) and Derwein et al.\ (4 Sep) both put ventilation
rate against filtration and mask blocking in the same model; Jamali et al.\ (3 Sep) and
Lv H et al.\ (2 Sep) report new filter media (all-cellulose HEPA, polyimide
microsphere/nanofibrous). Alongside them sit household solid fuel (Gu et al., Chen Y
et al.), surgical smoke (Ma L et al.), welding fume (Yang et al., Wei J et al.), wood dust
(Srinivasan et al.) and chalk dust (Mbazima et al., 5 Sep). \textbf{This is a control- and
mitigation-side literature, not an exposure--response one}, and it is the part of the week
most directly usable by an instrumentation programme.

\textbf{The measurement side is back at its ceiling.} \textit{Exposure assessment \&
modelling} 24 $\rightarrow$ \textbf{47} --- the highest this cluster has ever recorded, and
level with the all-time single-subtopic weekly high set by \textit{Sensing} in the
2--8 August week --- with \textit{Sensing} itself 21 $\rightarrow$ 34. Together 81 of 150,
a measurement-side share of \textbf{54\%}, tying the highest on record.

\textbf{The health side thinned correspondingly, and the effect estimates thinned with
it.} \textit{Respiratory \& allergic} (9 $\rightarrow$ 5) and \textit{Burden, policy \&
mitigation} (13 $\rightarrow$ 7) both hit their lowest weekly counts recorded.
\textbf{34 pooled effect estimates against 150 records} is the weakest estimate density of
any week this watch has run --- one estimate per 4.4 records, against one per 2.7 last
week. \textbf{Operationally the week was clean}: 150 records, \textbf{150 unique DOIs},
so unlike last week there was no double-publication to repair.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Subtopic trend and inflection}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\noindent\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\FIGDIR/w1_subtopic_trend.png}
\figcap{Per-issue subtopic counts across the week from \texttt{state/metrics.csv}.
\textbf{Read the denominators first}: daily totals were 14, 16, 33, 15, \textbf{38}, 27
and \textbf{7}. The 3 September peak of 38 and the 5 September floor of 7 are both
harvest-side artefacts rather than field signals --- the first a heavy Crossref-by-ISSN
deposit day, the second a weekend on which PubMed admitted no PM record at all. The
Saturday point should not be read as a decline.}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Expanding.} \textit{Occupational \& indoor} \textbf{7 $\rightarrow$ 19} is the
week's largest movement in absolute and relative terms, and its 19 ties the founding
backfill week against a regular-week maximum of 9. \textit{Exposure assessment \&
modelling} \textbf{24 $\rightarrow$ 47} sets a cluster record; \textit{Sensing,
forecasting \& instrumentation} \textbf{21 $\rightarrow$ 34} recovers the ground it lost
in the two preceding weeks. \textit{Reproductive \& developmental}
(\textbf{3 $\rightarrow$ 7}) is at its highest regular-week level, and
\textit{Cardiovascular \& metabolic} (\textbf{5 $\rightarrow$ 7}) continues the slow
recovery the 29 August rollup flagged.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Contracting.} \textit{Burden, policy \& mitigation} \textbf{13 $\rightarrow$ 7}
and \textit{Respiratory \& allergic} \textbf{9 $\rightarrow$ 5} are both the lowest weekly
readings this watch has recorded for those clusters. \textit{Neuro / mental health} fell
\textbf{7 $\rightarrow$ 4}; that is low but not a record (the 2--8 August week returned 2).
\textit{Mechanistic toxicology} was effectively flat at \textbf{12 $\rightarrow$ 11},
holding a three-week band of 11--12.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{The inflection worth naming.} For four weeks the measurement-side share has run
41--54\% with no trend, and the 29 August rollup said explicitly that no claim about the
field should rest on it. That still holds. What is \emph{not} noise is the indoor cluster:
a 7 $\rightarrow$ 19 jump concentrated in ventilation, filtration and source-capture
engineering, arriving in the same week that \textit{Burden, policy \& mitigation} hit its
floor. The mitigation literature did not shrink --- it moved from the policy register into
the engineering register. \textbf{Worth a second week of watching before it is called a
trend}, because a single MDPI or Elsevier volume release can manufacture a cluster of this
size, and the 27 August precedent in the last rollup is exactly that failure.

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Venues, trial watch, and what the pipeline did}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Venues.} \textit{Environ Sci Technol} at \textbf{23 of 150} is the most
concentrated single-venue week this watch has recorded, and it is a harvesting artefact as
much as an editorial one --- ES\&T is on the Crossref by-ISSN list and publishes in daily
batches, so it enters this corpus at full rate while journals reached only through PubMed
enter at PubMed's cadence. \textit{Atmos Chem Phys} (8), \textit{J Environ Sci (China)}
(8) and \textit{Atmosphere} (8) follow, then \textit{Environ Pollut} (7).
\textbf{Research Square supplied 5 preprints}, all labelled as such in the register.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Recurring authors.} Surname clustering is dominated by high-frequency Chinese
surnames --- Wang (7), Chen (6), Li (4), Liu (4) --- which is a naming artefact, not a
research-group signal, and this watch has no author-disambiguation field to resolve it.
\textbf{No genuine repeat-group cluster is claimed for this week.} Adding a first-author
affiliation field remains the open item that would make this section meaningful; it has
been on the list since 3 September.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Trial watch --- one new interventional registration in the period.}
\textbf{NCT07805824} (first posted 4 September) is a cluster-randomised trial of a
workplace heat and air-pollution mitigation package across \textbf{20 restaurants and 200
oven workers} in Najaf, Iraq, with a delayed-intervention comparator over 24 weeks. Its
primary endpoint is an objective gingival melanin index; its \textbf{sole secondary
endpoint is change in personal PM$_{2.5}$ exposure, measured both gravimetrically and in
real time}. \texttt{analyze\_endpoints} was run: 1 primary, 1 secondary, 0 other.
\textbf{Registry-hygiene flags are recorded on the record and should be read before
citing it}: it is labelled PHASE4 with no drug; \texttt{start\_date} equals
\texttt{primary\_completion\_date} equals 2026-01-01 while status is COMPLETED; and the
public \texttt{detailed\_description} still carries the submitter's own pre-submission
checklist. The endpoint design is usable; the timeline fields are not.
Two further records were \emph{updated} rather than registered in the period ---
\textbf{NCT04153539}, the Fudan busy-road versus traffic-free-park crossover in 69 healthy
young adults, and \textbf{NCT05016271} --- and \textbf{NCT07797413} (Savannah
port-adjacent nutrition feasibility trial, $n=20$) was carried in the August monthly.
\texttt{trials.json} now holds \textbf{26 trials across 11 windows}.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{A weekend zero in the registry is structural.} ClinicalTrials.gov posted
\textbf{no updates registry-wide} on 5 September, nor on 29 or 30 August, against
973--1{,}001 on each intervening weekday. Every Saturday and Sunday daily issue will
therefore report a trial zero for calendar reasons alone. This was verified this week
across six dates and should stop the weekend zeros being mistaken for an absence of PM
trial activity --- a misreading the daily issues were previously set up to invite.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{What the harvesting legs did.} PubMed carried five of seven days and returned a
verified zero on the sixth-largest\footnote{Ordering by daily count: 38, 33, 27, 16, 15,
14, 7 --- Saturday 5 September is the smallest.} --- on the \emph{smallest} day.
Crossref-by-ISSN was the week's workhorse and the reason ES\&T dominates the venue table.
\textbf{OpenAlex returned HTTP 429 on every day of the week}, unchanged since 28 July, and
is now best described as a dead leg rather than a degraded one. \textbf{arXiv returned
HTTP 200 every day but its newest submission has been frozen at 2026-08-20 all week}, so
it contributed nothing to any issue in this period --- worth a look at the query before
next week. \textbf{Consensus returned a nil for the seventh consecutive run}, but the
4 September check confirmed why: its hits are the right literature with the wrong dates,
and one of them was already in this corpus. Post-filtering Consensus on Crossref
\texttt{created} inside the window remains the recommended fix and is still not
implemented.

% -------------------------------------------------------------- gaps
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ultrafine remains 4 of 150} while two of the week's strongest records ---
Sawan et al.\ on airport-attributable UFP and Shi S et al.\ on ship-plume number modes ---
are ultrafine-resolved. The metric that carries the source signal is still the metric
almost nobody measures.

\item \textbf{One interventional record in 150.} The evidence base moving into the
engineering register this week is almost entirely observational or bench-scale; the
ventilation and filtration papers optimise against models and chambers, not against
health outcomes in occupied buildings.

\item \textbf{No health endpoint in 57 of 150 records}, and the sensing/exposure rows of
the heatmap remain empty of endpoint linkage for the sixth week. This is now the
longest-standing structural finding in the series and it has not moved.

\item \textbf{Estimate density fell to one per 4.4 records.} Whether that is a real
thinning of quantitative epidemiology or an artefact of a measurement-heavy week is not
decidable from one week; the four-week series is 3.3, 2.7, 2.9, 4.4.

\item \textbf{Pipeline defects carried forward}: unnormalised geography labels (which
understate western concentration in f3), the \texttt{Cancer}/\texttt{Oncologic} split in
\texttt{ENDPOINT\_CANON}, no first-author affiliation field, and a 6-element
\texttt{LIFECOURSE} array in the 2026-08-03 corpus where the schema requires 5 --- outside
this window, but it will corrupt any rollup that spans it.
\end{itemize}

{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Provenance and method}\\[1.2mm]
{\fontsize{7.2}{8.8}\selectfont
Period \textbf{2026-08-30 to 2026-09-05}, seven daily issues pooled.
\textbf{This rollup queried no API.} It aggregates \texttt{state/corpus/*.json},
\texttt{state/metrics.csv} and \texttt{state/trials.json} over
\texttt{PMRW\_START}--\texttt{PMRW\_END}; every record was screened, summarised and
DOI-gated at daily-issue time and is reproduced verbatim. \textbf{150 records, 150 unique
DOIs} --- no duplicate publication this period, unlike the two repaired last week. Daily
totals: 14, 16, 33, 15, 38, 27, 7. \textbf{Week-over-week comparisons} are against the
23--29 August rollup (109 records), with the 16--22 August (112), 9--15 August (134) and
2--8 August (146) rollups used where a longer run is cited; the 26 July--1 August week
(295) is excluded from "record" claims because it contains the founding retrospective
back-scan. \textbf{Effect estimates} are pooled as recorded, on heterogeneous contrasts,
with no meta-analysis and no heterogeneity statistic. \textbf{Figures} are regenerated
over the pooled range, not composited from the daily images. \textbf{Labels} --- subtopic,
design, particle-metric, geography --- are single-label by dominant contribution, assigned
at daily-issue time, and are a judgement rather than a controlled vocabulary.\par}
