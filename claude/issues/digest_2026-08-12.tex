\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}The Parkinson's--PM$_{2.5}$ association is carried by
exposure at least four years before the event --- which makes the exposure-history
requirement, not the concentration, the binding constraint on the design.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Delaney et al.\ (\textit{Environ Epidemiol}) fitted distributed-lag discrete-time survival
models to \textbf{10{,}366{,}083 Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries} aged 65+ across the
contiguous US, 2000--2016, with 10-year exposure histories for PM$_{2.5}$, NO$_2$ and
summer ozone. Ten years of continuous PM$_{2.5}$ at the 90th versus the 0.5th percentile
(11.8 vs.\ 3.0\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) gave \textbf{OR 1.634 (95\% CI 1.489--1.792)} for first
hospitalisation with PD; NO$_2$ at 31.7 vs.\ 3.7\,ppb gave \textbf{1.474 (1.379--1.575)}.
Ozone was equivocal. The load-bearing detail is the lag structure: associations were
carried by exposure \emph{at least four years} before hospitalisation, with the
near-event years contributing little.

Two consequences follow for instrumentation. First, the exposure contrast that matters
here is well below current US design values --- 11.8\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ is an unremarkable
annual mean --- so the measurement problem is long-run bias and drift, not peak capture.
A network whose calibration wanders by 1--2\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ over a multi-year deployment
degrades exactly the contrast this model exploits. Second, hospitalisation with PD is a
proxy for onset that is displaced by an unknown and probably differential interval; the
four-year lag should be read as a lower bound on latency, not an estimate of it.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Emission-factor choice is invisible in total mass and decisive in source
attribution.} Park et al.\ ran EMEP/EEA and KIMM non-exhaust factors through the same
3-D CFD--chemistry model over a dense Seoul district. Total non-exhaust PM$_{10}$ was
similar under both; the partitioning was not --- brake wear dominant under EMEP/EEA,
road wear dominant under KIMM. Non-exhaust reached \textbf{up to 70\% of total
PM$_{10}$} in near-road cells. A monitor reporting mass alone cannot distinguish the two
worlds, and they imply opposite mitigation priorities.

\item \textbf{Two independent mediation results converge on oxidative/inflammatory
metabolism.} Chow et al.\ (\textit{Nat Commun}) profiled $>$1{,}100 pre-diagnostic plasma
metabolites in 1{,}357 Cancer Prevention Study participants and found eight metabolites
associated with both exposure and subsequent lung cancer; four
($\gamma$-glutamylglutamine, phenylacetylglutamate, N-(2-furoyl)glycine,
4-vinylguaiacol glucuronide) mediated the PM and ozone associations at $q<0.2$. On the
same day, a correspondence in \textit{JACC Adv} pushes back on the statistical fragility
of exactly this class of metabolomic mediation analysis --- worth reading as a pair.

\item \textbf{Renal burden is now quantified with a pooled exposure--response function.}
Li et al.\ pooled 38 CKD and 16 eGFR studies: \textbf{RR 1.32 (1.21--1.43) per
10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, predominantly log-linear, with a saturating eGFR response at high
concentration. Attributed 2020 burden is 485{,}000 deaths and 243 million prevalent
cases; PM$_{2.5}$ ranks second among level-2 CKD mortality risk factors, up from first in
1990 only because metabolic risks grew faster.

\item \textbf{Instrumentation: a non-flammable CPC working fluid reaches parity.}
Kirchhoff et al.\ ran a DMSO-based condensation particle counter against a butanol
counterpart for six months. Mean cutoff \textbf{5.8$\pm$0.9\,nm} (DMSO) vs.\
\textbf{5.6$\pm$0.5\,nm} (butanol), with substantially lower fluid consumption. The
practical unlock is airborne and unattended remote deployment, where butanol's
flammability is the blocking constraint.

\item \textbf{Open-source exposure tooling.} Chillrud et al.\ released \texttt{pcpr},
an R implementation of principal component pursuit with three environmental-health
extensions (non-negativity, missingness, below-LOD penalty). Applied to 26 PM$_{2.5}$
constituents in Queens 2015--2021 it recovered four patterns and 211 outlying events
including July Fourth firework spikes. Directly usable on speciated network output.

\item \textbf{The abstract famine continues --- fifth consecutive issue.} Six records
carried today have no abstract at the entry date: four Elsevier
\textit{Atmos.\ Environ.} articles, one Elsevier correspondence, one Lancet editorial.
The four \textit{Atmos.\ Environ.} papers are squarely in scope (mobile monitoring,
S\~ao Paulo PM$_{10}$ speciation, Australian biomass-burning fingerprints, absorbing
aerosol index) and carry no extractable content. They are queued for the Saturday
weekly sweep.
\end{itemize}

\band{Neuro, cognition \& mental health\hfill 1 record}{Deep}

\paperentry{Delaney et al.\ 2026}
{Cumulative annual air pollution exposure and first hospitalisation with Parkinson's disease}
{Environ Epidemiol}
{Discrete-time survival with distributed lag models over 10{,}366{,}083 Medicare FFS
beneficiaries 65+, contiguous US 2000--2016; 10-year exposure histories for PM$_{2.5}$,
NO$_2$ and summer O$_3$; non-linear exposure--response. \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ OR 1.634
(1.489--1.792)} at 11.8 vs.\ 3.0\,$\mu$g/m$^3$; \textbf{NO$_2$ OR 1.474 (1.379--1.575)}
at 31.7 vs.\ 3.7\,ppb; O$_3$ limited. Associations carried by exposure $\geq$4 years
pre-event. The endpoint is hospitalisation, not incidence --- diagnosis-to-hospitalisation
lag is unmeasured and plausibly differential by exposure via healthcare access, which is
the main threat to the latency claim. PM$_{2.5}$ and NO$_2$ are strongly correlated in
Medicare-scale data and the paper does not resolve which is doing the work.}
{10.1097/EE9.0000000000000519}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 1 record}{Teal}

\paperentry{Kakhaia et al.\ 2026}
{Exposome approach to causal effects of time-varying air pollution mixtures on CVD incidence}
{Environ Int}
{UK Biobank, annual PM$_{2.5}$ and NO$_2$ from a Europe-wide LUR model, 10-year
cumulative CVD incidence under modified treatment policies. Confounders selected by a
causal structure discovery algorithm with prior knowledge; sequential doubly robust
non-parametric estimation with ensemble learning and censoring adjustment. A 25\%
exposure reduction over 2005--2019 gave $-0.62$ per 100 (95\% CI $-0.70$ to $-0.55$),
RR\,$\approx$\,0.90; the projected net-zero scenario reached $-1.05$ per 100 and
RR\,$\approx$\,0.85. \textbf{The stated sensitivity is the finding}: dropping
assessment-centre region from the adjustment set more than doubled the estimate, so the
headline number is contingent on a spatial-confounding choice the authors cannot
adjudicate. Method-forward, estimate-fragile.}
{10.1016/j.envint.2026.110441}

\band{Other clinical endpoints\hfill 5 records}{Slate}

\paperentry{Chow et al.\ 2026}
{Blood metabolomic signatures linking air pollution to lung cancer}
{Nat Commun}
{Prospective nested case-control across CPS-II and CPS-3, 1{,}357 participants,
$>$1{,}100 metabolites in pre-diagnostic plasma, residential concentrations of six
pollutants at blood draw. Eight metabolites associated with both exposure and subsequent
lung cancer; four ($\gamma$-glutamylglutamine, phenylacetylglutamate,
N-(2-furoyl)glycine, 4-vinylguaiacol glucuronide) significantly mediated PM and ozone
associations at adjusted $q<0.2$. Single-timepoint metabolomics cannot separate exposure
response from early disease, and $q<0.2$ is a permissive threshold over 1{,}100 features
--- treat the four as candidates. Smoking adjustment in a lung cancer metabolome study is
the obvious residual-confounding exposure.}
{10.1038/s41467-026-75116-3}

\paperentry{Li et al.\ 2026}
{Global and national CKD burden attributable to fine particles: updated exposure--response meta-analysis}
{Innovation (Camb)}
{Three-stage mixed-effects meta-analysis over 38 CKD and 16 eGFR studies, fitting a
non-linear ERF and propagating it to 1990--2020 burden above a 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$
counterfactual. \textbf{RR 1.32 (1.21--1.43) per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, predominantly
log-linear; \textbf{eGFR $-2.115$ (0.452--3.777)\,mL/min/1.73\,m$^2$} with saturation at
high exposure. 2020 burden: 485{,}000 deaths, 5.77 million incident and 243 million
prevalent cases. The counterfactual floor is a choice, not a threshold, and the burden
figures scale with it; the primary studies are also concentrated in high-exposure Asian
settings, so the low-concentration arm of the ERF is thin.}
{10.1016/j.xinn.2026.101429}

\paperentry{Jain et al.\ 2026}
{Ambient air pollution and glaucoma: systematic review and meta-analysis}
{J Curr Glaucoma Pract}
{PRISMA review, PubMed and Embase to 30 December 2025; 18 observational studies, 8
pooled by generic inverse variance. \textbf{Pooled RR 1.12 (1.06--1.18)} for long-term
PM$_{2.5}$, $I^2=78.7\%$. NO$_2$, NO$_x$, PM$_{10}$ and CO directionally positive but
synthesised narratively. RNFL and GCIPL thinning and modest IOP elevation also reported.
Heterogeneity at 79\% with eight studies makes the pooled point estimate a weak summary;
the authors say causality cannot be established and they are right. Two databases only
is a real recall limitation for this literature.}
{10.5005/jp-journals-10078-1522}

\paperentry{Schlaepfer et al.\ 2026}
{Environmental hazards and the risk of idiopathic urethral stricture}
{Transl Androl Urol}
{Utah Population Database, 2{,}048 cases born 1996--2021 matched 1:1 on birth year and
location to 1{,}939 controls; historical air pollution and toxics exposure assessed
prenatally, in the first year of life, and in the five years before diagnosis.
\textbf{Only the first-year window was associated: RR 1.21 (1.07--1.36).} Univariate
signals for ethylbenzene, tetrachloroethylene and molybdenum trioxide. Three exposure
windows tested with one positive is a multiplicity problem the paper does not address,
and the univariate toxicant hits are unadjusted. Included as a hypothesis, not a result.}
{10.21037/tau-2026-0338}

\paperentry{Liu et al.\ 2026}
{Reply: statistical fragility and methodological constraints in metabolomic mediation}
{JACC Adv}
{Correspondence from the Emory group (Liu, Chang, Jones, Quyyumi, Liang) responding to a
critique of metabolomic mediation between air pollution and cardiovascular risk.
\textbf{Abstract not indexed} --- carried as metadata only because it sits directly
against Chow et al.\ (same senior author, same method class) in today's harvest and the
methodological dispute is the reason to read both. Full text required.}
{10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103149}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 4 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{Feng \& Chao 2026}
{Lower respiratory infection burden attributable to PM$_{2.5}$ in China and the G20, 1990--2023}
{Int J Biometeorol}
{GBD 2023 deaths and DALYs from LRI attributable to ambient and household PM$_{2.5}$,
Joinpoint regression on age-standardised DALY rates with sex subgroups and loess against
SDI. Long decline reversed upward in 2021--2023 (APC \textbf{China 12.76--14.23\%}, G20

\paperentry{Yang et al.\ 2026}
{COPD and LRI burden attributable to household air pollution and secondhand smoke, 1990--2021}
{J Thorac Dis}
{GBD 2021 across 204 countries with BAPC and ARIMA projection. Age-standardised COPD

every assumption in the parent estimates.}
{10.21037/jtd-2026-0284}

\paperentry{Wu et al.\ 2026}
{Air pollution and COPD-related health outcomes: bibliometric and visualisation analysis}
{J Thorac Dis}

{10.21037/jtd-2026-0784}

\paperentry{The Lancet Respiratory Medicine 2026}
{The far-reaching effects of wildfire smoke}
{Lancet Respir Med}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 2 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Choi et al.\ 2026}
{Degradable porous PLGA/PCL membrane enabling a lung alveoli-on-a-chip for particulate injury}
{Biofabrication}
{Porogen-assisted phase-separation membranes with tunable degradation ($\sim$40\% surface
porosity gain, $\sim$3\,$\mu$m thickness loss over culture) carrying primary human
alveolar epithelium and microvascular endothelium at an air--liquid interface under
breathing-mimetic cyclic strain. Apical diesel PM produced dose-dependent cytotoxicity,
barrier permeability increase, elevated ROS and DNA damage \emph{in both layers} ---
trans-barrier propagation, which an insert-only model cannot show. Roflumilast-N-oxide
(PDE4 inhibitor) selectively attenuated oxidative and inflammatory readouts without
restoring barrier integrity. Deposited dose is delivered apically in suspension and is
not equivalent to inhaled dosimetry; no dose--response anchoring to ambient
concentrations is attempted. Relevant here as a bench receptor for source-resolved
particle samples.}
{10.1088/1758-5090/ae9402}

\paperentry{Yuan et al.\ 2026}
{Hallmarks of lung cancer driven by inhalable particulate matter}
{Innovation (Camb)}
{Critical review proposing a 12-feature ``hallmarks of PM-associated lung cancer''
framework spanning cell-intrinsic traits (genetic alteration, epigenetic reprogramming,
metabolic remodelling, stemness) and microenvironment remodelling (inflammation, immune
suppression, angiogenesis, pre-metastatic niche). Argues for integrating PM exposure into
screening and risk stratification. A framework paper: the hallmark taxonomy is an
organising device, not evidence, and the underlying studies are heterogeneous in particle
source and dose. Useful as a citation scaffold for a mechanism section.}
{10.1016/j.xinn.2026.101347}

\paperentry{Tian et al.\ 2026}
{Bioinspired honeycomb-structured nanofibrous membranes for high-efficiency PM capture}
{Nanomaterials (Basel)}
{Template-assisted electrospinning onto a mesh receiver establishes a directional
electric field, producing concentrated ordered stacking plus sparse random orientation of

{10.3390/nano16150941}

\paperentry{Xie et al.\ 2026}
{Thirty-year change and projection of Asian tracheal, bronchus and lung cancer burden}
{J Thorac Dis}

{10.21037/jtd-2026-0740}

\paperentry{Liu et al.\ 2026b}
{Lung cancer incidence and mortality trends with age-period-cohort analysis, Zibo 2014--2024}
{Front Oncol}
{Registry data for a heavily industrial Shandong city. Age-standardised incidence rose
(AAPC \textbf{+4.34\%}, 95\% CI 2.14--6.59) while mortality fell (AAPC \textbf{$-$2.13\%},
$-$3.53 to $-$0.72); male rates exceeded female throughout. Period effect increasing,
cohort effect decreasing across successive birth cohorts. Rising incidence with falling
mortality in a screening-active decade is the classic overdiagnosis signature and the
paper does not test it; air pollution appears as narrative context with no exposure

{10.3389/fonc.2026.1873592}

\paperentry{Suryanarayanareddy et al.\ 2026}
{Low-cost bio-based particle-capturing device for vehicular emissions: pilot study}
{Environ Sci Pollut Res Int}
{Cylindrical coconut-husk and sugarcane-pulp enclosure tapering to a conical outlet and

(PM$_{10}$) for halted vehicles; 20--65\% and 15--65\% for moving vehicles. Efficiency

simulation of an outdoor near-road environment, no replication count given, and no
material-degradation testing on a biodegradable enclosure meant to sit at a roadside.}
{10.1007/s11356-026-38118-8}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 6 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Park et al.\ 2026}
{City-scale non-exhaust PM$_{10}$ from emissions to exposure with a coupled chemistry--CFD framework}
{Environ Pollut}
{Two non-exhaust emission-factor frameworks (EMEP/EEA Guidebook and KIMM) implemented in
the Fine-scale Air Quality Simulation Unit, a 3-D coupled CFD--chemistry model, over a
densely built Seoul district, combined with the 2022 Seoul Time-Activity Profile.
\textbf{Total non-exhaust PM$_{10}$ was similar under both frameworks; source
partitioning was not} --- brake wear dominant under EMEP/EEA, road wear under KIMM.
Non-exhaust reached \textbf{up to 70\% of total PM$_{10}$} in near-road and dense cells;
outdoor activity-weighted contributions of 9--10\% in commercial and traffic zones
exceeded concentration-based estimates because peak traffic coincides with peak outdoor
presence. No measurement validation of the partitioning is presented, which is the
obvious next step and the reason a receptor-model or tracer campaign would be
complementary.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.128941}

\paperentry{Chillrud et al.\ 2026}
{\texttt{pcpr}: principal component pursuit for exposure pattern recognition and outlier detection}
{Environ Epidemiol}
{R package implementing PCP with three environmental-health extensions: a non-negativity
constraint on the low-rank matrix, missingness handling, and a specialised penalty for
below-LOD observations. Demonstrated on 26 PM$_{2.5}$ constituents measured every few
days in Queens, NYC, 2015--2021: four patterns recovered (secondary/tailpipe, traffic,
crustal dust, salt) plus \textbf{211 outlying events} including July Fourth firework
spikes. PCP's sparse component is an outlier detector, not a source; calling the 211
events ``exposure events'' presumes an interpretation the decomposition does not supply.
Still the most immediately usable artefact in today's corpus for anyone holding speciated
network data.}
{10.1097/EE9.0000000000000515}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026}
{National-scale heavy metal contamination in urban road dust across 82 Chinese cities}
{Environ Geochem Health}
{13{,}269 samples pooled from 169 studies into an urban-agglomeration framework;
geo-accumulation index for anthropogenic enrichment, potential ecological risk index,
multivariate source deconvolution. Cd and Hg are the priority pollutants, jointly
\textbf{$>$85\% of total ecological risk} in every agglomeration despite low absolute
concentrations relative to Mn and Zn; a Cd/Hg $I_{\text{geo}}$ ratio is proposed as a
source fingerprint. Pooling 169 studies with heterogeneous sampling depth, particle-size
cut and digestion protocol is the central weakness --- the fingerprint may partly index
methodology. Road dust is the resuspension reservoir behind non-exhaust PM, so this pairs
with Park et al.\ above.}
{10.1007/s10653-026-03422-2}

\paperentry{Flor\^encio et al.\ 2026}
{Comprehensive analysis of PM$_{10}$ in the Metropolitan Area of S\~ao Paulo, Part I:
chemical characterisation and sources}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textbf{Abstract not deposited at Crossref on the entry date.} Title indicates
chemical speciation and source apportionment for a megacity with almost no
component-resolved PM literature in this watch to date. Queued for the Saturday sweep.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122286}
