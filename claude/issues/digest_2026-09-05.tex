\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Aviation and roadway leave separable fingerprints on
the same neighbourhood --- but only if the network measures particle \emph{number} and
absorption, not mass.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Sawan et al.\ (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}) ran a hybrid mobile and fixed-site campaign in
a marginalized northwest Toronto neighbourhood sitting downwind of Toronto Pearson and a
freight corridor, and showed the two dominant combustion sources decouple spatially.
Ultrafine particle number tracked flight activity (\textbf{$r=0.58$, $p<0.05$ at 3.5\,km})
with \textbf{1.7--3.3-fold enrichment} under downwind conditions, still
\textbf{3.2-fold at residential sites $\sim$8\,km} from the airport; black carbon instead
followed roadway proximity and freight, correlating weakly with flights. The diagnostic
detail is that BC--UFP correlation collapses from $r=0.62$ under northerly winds to
$r=0.1$ downwind of the airport --- shared sources versus independent ones, visible only
because both pollutants were measured together. Random Forest models
($R^2=0.79$ UFP, $0.87$ BC) separated airport proximity as a UFP-specific driver from
freight trucking as a BC-specific one. For distributed low-cost networks the implication
is unwelcome and concrete: a PM$_{2.5}$-mass node sited here would have reported an
essentially null aviation signal, because the aviation contribution lives in a size range
that contributes negligible mass. Source separation of this kind needs a CPC-class number
channel and an absorption channel at the same node, which is precisely the pairing most
low-cost deployments omit.
\end{keybox}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PubMed returned a true zero, verified two ways.} No record entering PubMed on
5 September matched either the health axis or the sensing axis. This is not a harvester
fault: 544 records entered PubMed that day against 4{,}765 on Friday 4 September, and the
independent PubMed connector run with looser field tags also returned 0/0. Saturday
deposits are thin and, this week, contained no PM work at all.

\item \textbf{The issue was carried entirely by the non-PubMed legs.} Crossref-by-ISSN
supplied four of seven records and Europe PMC three. Europe PMC returned 11 hits but
\textbf{seven were already shipped in the 4 September issue} --- its
\texttt{CREATION\_DATE} runs about a day behind PubMed \texttt{[EDAT]}, so cross-source
deduplication, not recall, is what that leg mostly exercises.

\item \textbf{Instrumentation dominates a health-thin day.} Five of seven records sit on
the sensing or exposure-assessment axis (71\%, second only to 20 August's 78\%), and only
\textbf{two records carry a human health endpoint at all}. No record in the window reports
an effect estimate with a confidence interval, so there is no forest plot today --- the
seventh such issue of 42.

\item \textbf{Two source-attribution results worth reading together.} Sawan et al.\ separate
aviation UFP from roadway BC by wind sector; Shi S et al.\ show that vanadium, the
canonical ship-plume tracer, is carried by only \textbf{35.8\% of plume particles} and that
coverage moves with vessel speed (29.5\% at 4.9 knots vs.\ 39.7\% at 13.4). Both are
warnings that a single-species or single-metric tracer under-identifies its own source.

\item \textbf{A speed-reduction policy may trade CO$_2$ for composition.} In Shi S et al.'s
matched-vessel pair the CO$_2$ benefit of slowing coincided with a carbon-rich particle
shift and lower detectable-vanadium coverage. They also find \textbf{$\sim$20\% of analysed
particles meet single-particle biomass-burning criteria}, i.e.\ ship plumes generate false
positives in exactly the source-apportionment schemes used to police them.

\item \textbf{Trial watch is a structural zero, not a topical one.} ClinicalTrials.gov
posted \emph{no} updates registry-wide on 5 September --- nor on 29/30 August --- against
973--1{,}001 on each weekday. Every Saturday and Sunday issue will carry this zero for
calendar reasons, and it should not be read as an absence of PM trial activity.

\item \textbf{Two records ship without abstracts.} The \textit{Atmos Pollut Res} tire-wear
tracer review and the \textit{Atmos Res} dust-aging lidar paper are indexed by Crossref
with no abstract and no Europe PMC record; both are titled informatively enough to
place but not to assess, and both go on the retry list.
\end{itemize}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 3 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Shi S et al.\ 2026}
{Vessel speed reduction trades CO$_2$ against plume composition --- and against the tracer
that identifies the plume}
{npj Clim Atmos Sci}
{UAV interception of 14 fresh ship plumes with single-particle mass spectrometry and
population-balance inversion to reconstruct ultrafine and coarse modes. Two marker gaps
emerge. \textbf{Only 35.8\% of the reconstructed particle-number population carried
detectable vanadium} (29.5\% at 4.9 knots vs.\ 39.7\% at 13.4 knots in a matched-vessel
pair; 0--70.8\% across a speed-blind manifold), so the canonical ship tracer is
operation-dependent rather than conserved. Separately, \textbf{$\sim$20\% of analysed
particles met biomass-burning criteria}, a false-positive channel in ship-influenced
samples. Sulfate-related ion signals ran 2.3--10.7-fold higher in V/VO-bearing particles.
The authors are careful that laser desorption/ionization matrix effects may contribute to
that contrast, which is the right caveat and limits how far the V--S co-location can be
pushed mechanistically. Single campaign, 14 plumes, one matched pair --- the speed
comparison rests on very few vessels.}
{10.1038/s41612-026-01532-3}

\paperentry{Wang S et al.\ 2026}
{Smoldering chaparral emits 10--70$\times$ the particulate of flaming, and fuel moisture
sets the multiplier}
{Fire Saf J}
{FTIR quantification of gas and particulate emissions from controlled flaming and
smoldering combustion of four dominant California chaparral species at varying fuel
moisture. \textbf{Smoldering produced 10--70-fold higher PM emission factors than flaming
across all species}, with the amplification ratio itself varying by fuel type and
moisture; higher moisture enhanced incomplete-combustion products, principally CO and
hydrocarbons, with live eucalyptus highest at
\textbf{37.18 $\pm$ 0.65\,g/kg} hydrocarbons under smoldering. Cross-ecosystem comparison
found chaparral broadly similar to western shrubland and grassland fuels except in
nitrogen-containing species. The limitation that matters is scale: these are
bench-scale burns, and the authors note combustion scale and mixing conditions shift
emission profiles, so transfer to landscape fire inventories is an assumption rather than
a result. Directly useful for anyone weighting wildfire smoke exposure by combustion
phase rather than by total burned area.}
{10.1016/j.firesaf.2026.104687}

\paperentry{Ji et al.\ 2026}
{Dust aerosol aging tracked by combined Mie, polarization and fluorescence lidar}
{Atmos Res}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Crossref carries the title, author list (8) and 5 September
registration date but no abstract, and there is no Europe PMC record; the publisher's
full text was not retrievable on this run. From the title the study links optical
evolution during dust aging to chemical transformation using a three-channel lidar ---
a combination relevant to anyone attempting composition inference from optical
instruments rather than mass. \textbf{No quantities, site, campaign length or retrieval
performance are asserted here, because none could be verified.} Added to the abstract
retry list.}
{10.1016/j.atmosres.2026.109312}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 2 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Sawan et al.\ 2026}
{Aviation drives ultrafine number, freight drives black carbon, in the same
neighbourhood}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Hybrid mobile and fixed-site campaign in a marginalized northwest Toronto neighbourhood
downwind of Toronto Pearson and a freight corridor. UFP correlated with flight activity
(\textbf{$r=0.58$, $p<0.05$ at 3.5\,km}) and was enriched \textbf{1.7--3.3-fold} downwind
of the airport, measured as the ratio of interquartile ranges under downwind versus other
wind conditions, reaching \textbf{3.2-fold at residential sites $\sim$8\,km away}. BC
instead tracked roadway proximity and freight and correlated weakly with flight counts.
BC--UFP correlation was strong under northerly winds (mean $r=0.62$) but collapsed to
$r=0.1$ downwind of the airport. Random Forest models ($R^2=0.79$ UFP, $0.87$ BC)
identified industrial land use and a highway as shared drivers, airport proximity as
UFP-specific and freight trucking as BC-specific. The inference is observational and
wind-sector stratified, so airport attribution rests on a co-location argument rather than
on any tracer unique to jet exhaust. The lesson for network design is that mass-based
siting would have missed the aviation signal entirely.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c06666}

\paperentry{Scalabrin et al.\ 2026}
{Chemical tracers for tire wear particles in atmospheric aerosol: analytical approaches
and challenges}
{Atmos Pollut Res}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Crossref registers the title, 11 authors and a 5 September
creation date with no abstract, and the record has no Europe PMC counterpart. The title
places it as a review of tracer chemistry for apportioning non-exhaust tire wear ---
a live problem, since tire wear is a growing share of traffic PM precisely as exhaust
controls tighten, and the standard markers each fail differently. \textbf{No findings,
recommended tracer or study count are asserted here.} A targeted Scholar Gateway
full-text query returned only adjacent June 2026 reviews, not this paper. Added to the
abstract retry list.}
{10.1016/j.apr.2026.103194}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 1 record}{Clay}

\paperentry{Mbazima et al.\ 2026}
{Chalk dust puts teachers over a deposition threshold while staying under the
occupational limit}
{Int J Environ Health Res}
{Sixty personal breathing-zone samples from 12 Johannesburg secondary-school teachers
(7 female, 5 male), collected Monday--Friday 08:00--14:35 with GilAir-3 pumps, a 4\,$\mu$m
cut-point cyclone and 25\,mm PVC filters at 1.85\,L/min, then run through a multiple-path
particle dosimetry model at low, moderate and high exertion for nasal and oronasal
breathing. \textbf{PM$_4$ ranged 0.019--0.761\,mg/m$^3$, mean 0.126 $\pm$
0.134\,mg/m$^3$}; chalk use ($\beta=0.468$) and sampling duration ($\beta=0.305$, both
$p<0.001$) predicted concentration. Breathing route dominated deposition, and alveolar
clearance was slower than tracheobronchial in both sexes. The authors' conclusion --- that
risk persists below the occupational exposure limit --- rests on modelled rather than
measured dose, and 12 teachers in one region is a small and non-random base. Note the
standard deviation exceeds the mean, so the distribution is heavily right-skewed and the
mean is a poor summary.}
{10.1080/09603123.2026.2726420}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 1 record}{Violet}

\paperentry{Han G et al.\ 2026 (preprint)}
{Picropodophyllotoxin attenuates PM$_{2.5}$ injury in pulmonary endothelial cells via
mTOR-dependent autophagy}
{Research Square (preprint)}
{Human pulmonary artery endothelial cells exposed to PM$_{2.5}$ at 25--100\,$\mu$g/mL for
24--48\,h with picropodophyllotoxin (2--50\,$\mu$M) or dexamethasone (50\,$\mu$M).
PM$_{2.5}$ reduced viability dose- and time-dependently with increased LDH leakage and ROS
and suppressed SOD/CAT and SGK1; PPT restored viability, SGK1 and mTOR phosphorylation and
attenuated TLR4, MyD88, LC3II and Beclin 1 upregulation. \textbf{Not peer reviewed, single
cell line, no animal or human data, and no dose--response statistics reported in the
abstract.} The exposure concentrations are far above realistic alveolar deposition, which
is the standard weakness of this design and limits the finding to pathway plausibility
rather than therapeutic inference. Included for completeness of the mechanistic map,
not as evidence of effect.}
{10.21203/rs.3.rs-10446885/v1}
