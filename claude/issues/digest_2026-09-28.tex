\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A year after the Maui wildland--urban interface
fire, 1,400 adults carry urinary metal burdens far above US reference levels, and the mixture
index tracks abnormal spirometry --- a hazard that PM$_{2.5}$ mass monitoring would not have
registered.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Maunakea and colleagues (\textit{PNAS}, tier A) enrolled \textbf{1,400 adults 6--18 months} after the
August 2023 Maui fires and measured \textbf{24 urinary metals} and spirometry at the same visit.
Relative to US reference values, antimony was \textbf{40-fold}, manganese \textbf{3.8-fold}, barium
\textbf{2.3-fold} and arsenic \textbf{2.2-fold} higher, clustering in the most affected areas. Per
quantile of the WQS positive-mixture index (driven by As, Cd, Cu), the odds of FVC, FEV$_1$,
FEV$_1$/FVC and FEF$_{25-75}$ below the lower limit of normal were \textbf{1.71, 1.53, 1.83 and 1.84}.
\textbf{Why it is the signal:} a WUI fire burns buildings, vehicles and treated materials, and what
it leaves behind is a metal-rich ash that resuspends for months; mass-based PM$_{2.5}$ sensors
record smoke days, not this. The inference has real gaps --- cross-sectional, no pre-fire baseline,
no CIs in the abstract, arsenic partly dietary and cadmium partly from smoking, and \textbf{no air
or dust measurement} ties the burden to the fire. \textbf{The operational rule:} after structure
fires, add filter-based metal speciation (or XRF on sensor-co-located filters) and resuspension
monitoring for months, rather than standing down when the optical PM$_{2.5}$ series returns to
baseline.\par
\end{keybox}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Indoor and occupational records dominated: six of fifteen.} Coal-mine dust and NO$_2$ led
a BKMR/Qgcomp mixture associated with metabolic syndrome in Chinese miners; Benin City classrooms
ran \textbf{11.2--81.4\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} PM$_{2.5}$ with cough in \textbf{58.2\,\%} of pupils while

inverse SO$_2$ association in males passed FDR, with address at registration standing in for
gestational weeks 5--9.

\paperentry{Maunakea et al. 2026}
{Climate-amplified wildland-urban interface disasters create persistent internal metal mixtures associated with pulmonary impairment.}
{Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A}
{1,400 adults enrolled 6-18 months after the August 2023 Maui wildland-urban interface fires had 24 urinary metals and spirometry at the same visit. Burdens exceeded US reference levels - antimony 40-fold, manganese 3.8, barium 2.3, arsenic 2.2 - highest in the most affected areas. \paperentry{Bae et al. 2026}
{Exposure to ambient air pollutants during early gestation and phenotypic variation in non-syndromic microtia.}
{J Plast Reconstr Aesthet Surg}
{Single-centre case-only study of 589 patients (625 ears) with non-syndromic microtia, relating spatiotemporally modelled exposure in gestational weeks 5-9 to bilateral involvement and anatomical severity. \band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 2 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Kamyabi et al. 2026}
{A novel controlled wood smoke exposure model to evaluate physiological responses to smoke-derived air pollution in dairy calves.}
{J Dairy Sci}
{24 pre-weaned Holstein bull calves were randomised to 6 h in purpose-built enclosures with wood smoke (PM$_{2.5}$ 238.44 +/- 62.97 $\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$) or clean air (9.94 +/- 4.50), with CO$_2$, CO, temperature and RH similar. Smoke-exposed calves had lower respiratory rate (17.50 vs 21.52 breaths/min) and higher tidal volume (0.90 vs 0.75 L), unchanged minute ventilation; BALF showed more granulocytes, CD68+ macrophages and NK cells, and circulating neutrophils fell. A single 6-h exposure with necropsy immediately after cannot address recovery or repeated smoke days; fresh wood smoke differs from aged wildfire plumes. Relevance: the enclosure PM$_{2.5}$ record gives a controlled dose, rare in agricultural wildfire work, and the design transfers to sensor-guided barn ventilation trials.}
{10.3168/jds.2026-29100}

\paperentry{Driencourt et al. 2026}

{Rev Mal Respir}
{French-language short review framing the experimental programme on how PM$_{2.5}$ alters pulmonary immunity to Streptococcus pneumoniae, with IL-20-family cytokines as the candidate mediator. Relevance: low; flags IL-20 cytokines as a mechanistic target for PM-infection susceptibility studies.}
