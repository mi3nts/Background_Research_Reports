\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A national STEMI register and a Mendelian-randomisation
study land on the same day at opposite ends of the effect-size scale --- and the gap
between them is the whole methodological problem of PM epidemiology in one figure.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
De Potter et al.\ analysed \textbf{every ST-elevation myocardial infarction admission in
Belgium} over nine years ($n$=51{,}544) in a time-stratified case-crossover design with
residential exposure from high-resolution models. Per IQR, same-day PM$_{2.5}$ gave
\textbf{OR 1.022 (1.008--1.035)} --- a 2\% acute excess, tight CI, and the kind of number
a design that self-matches on person and season is built to produce. Li H et al.\ instead
instrument lifetime PM$_{2.5}$ genetically and report \textbf{OR 1.897 (1.063--3.388)}
for childhood asthma, with \textbf{FUT10 mediating 19.3\%} of the effect and 70
TWAS-implicated genes enriched in lysosomal and leukocyte-mediated immunity.
Both are defensible; they are not measuring the same thing. The case-crossover isolates a
trigger effect over hours with the chronic exposure held constant by design; the MR
estimates a lifetime contrast with the acute component invisible. Reading either as
``the'' PM effect is a category error, and any sensor network that claims to serve
epidemiology has to declare which of the two it is resolving --- minutes-to-days for the
first, cumulative annual burden for the second. They imply completely different siting,
duty cycle and calibration requirements.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Six of fifteen records ship on metadata only} --- the worst abstract
availability this watch has recorded. Elsevier deposited five \textit{Atmos.\ Environ.},
\textit{Environ.\ Pollut.}, \textit{Sci.\ Total Environ.} and \textit{Build.\ Environ.}
records to Crossref with no abstract, none is yet in PubMed or Europe PMC, and Semantic
Scholar returns 404 or a null abstract for all of them. They are counted and listed;
\textbf{no number is attributed to any of them}, and four are on-topic enough
(PM$_{2.5}$-bound metals, solid-fuel PAH biomarkers, heritage-site particle sensing,
landfill receptor monitoring) that they belong on the abstract retry list. All six are
scored \textit{Metadata only} in the architecture figure rather than being given a design
guessed from the title.

\item \textbf{Two effect estimates on the same acute contrast, in the same paper,
disagree by pollutant.} De Potter et al.\ report same-day \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ OR 1.022
(1.008--1.035)} against \textbf{NO$_2$ OR 1.044 (1.025--1.063)} per IQR, with O$_3$
strongest at lag 0--2 (\textbf{1.063, 1.034--1.090}). NO$_2$ doubling the PM$_{2.5}$
coefficient in a single-pollutant comparison is the traffic-proximity confounding this
literature never resolves --- and in-hospital mortality (11.8\%) was driven not by any
pollutant but by individual and area income and retirement status.

\item \textbf{A filtration material with a number worth writing down.} Lv H et al.'s
single-component polyimide gradient aerogel reaches \textbf{99.996\% PM$_{0.3}$ efficiency
at 45.08\,Pa initial pressure drop} with \textbf{127\,g\,m$^{-2}$ dust-holding capacity},
holding 99.91\% at \textbf{300\,\textdegree C}. The gradient architecture buys depth
filtration rather than surface caking, which is why the dust-holding number is the
interesting one, not the efficiency.

\item \textbf{Personal exposure during exercise, measured rather than modelled.} Engeroff
et al.\ ran 60 instrumented 2\,km runs across a park, a residential street and a major
road at two times of day. Lowest PM across all fractions: \textbf{midday in the park};
highest PM$_{10}$: \textbf{morning on the same park route}. Route choice and rush-hour
avoidance both work, but the meteorological covariates (humidity, pressure, wind,
temperature) carried real signal --- which is a warning about drawing route conclusions
from a single campaign.

\item \textbf{Sulfate aerosol water turns out to be photochemically active on its own.}
Petersen et al.\ show the sulfate anion absorbs surface sunlight and ejects an electron to
form SO$_4^{\bullet-}$ on a sub-microsecond timescale, at rates \textbf{comparable to
measured hydroxyl-radical production in aerosols}. Biomass-fire chromophores photosensitise
the pathway, raising radical formation \textbf{up to five-fold} and more strongly at low
pH. The consequence is not marginal: secondary brown carbon from this chemistry
\textbf{flips the direct radiative forcing of sulfate-containing aerosol from negative to
positive}.

\item \textbf{No sensing-side calibration or drift work today.} \textit{Sensing,
forecasting \& instrumentation} holds two records, both metadata-only. OpenAlex was 403
and arXiv 429 again, Consensus was not re-queried after returning three
already-seen records on the 1 September run, and PubMed's instrumentation axis returned
\textbf{zero} hits for this entry date.
\end{itemize}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 1 record}{Teal}

\paperentry{De Potter et al.\ 2026}
{Socioeconomic and environmental determinants of STEMI incidence and in-hospital mortality}
{Eur Heart J Acute Cardiovasc Care}
{Nine years of Belgian national hospital discharge data, \textbf{51{,}544 STEMI
admissions} (51.4 per 100{,}000 per year), with residential PM$_{2.5}$, NO$_2$, O$_3$,
temperature, noise and greenspace from high-resolution models. A time-stratified
case-crossover handled the acute question and multivariable logistic regression the
mortality question. Per IQR, same-day: \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ OR 1.022 (1.008--1.035)},
\textbf{NO$_2$ OR 1.044 (1.025--1.063)}; O$_3$ peaked over lag 0--2 at
\textbf{1.063 (1.034--1.090)}. In-hospital mortality was 11.8\% and tracked
\emph{socioeconomic} variables --- lower individual and area income, retirement status ---
rather than any pollutant. Two readings matter: NO$_2$ outperforming PM$_{2.5}$ in
single-pollutant models is the standard traffic-proximity confounding, and the
incidence/mortality split says exposure and outcome severity are governed by different
mechanisms.}
{10.1093/ehjacc/zuag118}

\paperentry{Zanini et al.\ 2026}
{Maternal air-pollution exposure and fetoplacental Doppler markers}
{Environ Pollut}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 1 record}{Sage}

\paperentry{Li H et al.\ 2026}
{Multi-omics Mendelian randomisation of PM$_{2.5}$ and childhood asthma}
{ERJ Open Res}
{Two-sample MR on large-scale GWAS summary data gives \textbf{OR 1.897 (1.063--3.388),
$p$=0.030} for genetically instrumented PM$_{2.5}$ on childhood asthma. A TWAS identified
\textbf{70 genes} co-expressed with both, enriched in lysosome- and leukocyte-mediated
immunity, with \textbf{MEAF6} validated as protective and \textbf{RNF40} as a risk gene.
Two-step MR put \textbf{FUT10 as a positive mediator carrying 19.3\%} of the effect, with
CD200 and MANBA negative mediators; a phenome-wide MR flagged associations of these
targets with other diseases, which the authors correctly present as a caution against
treating them as drug targets. The limits are the usual MR limits and they are severe
here: the PM$_{2.5}$ instrument is a genetic proxy for a residentially correlated
environmental exposure, so horizontal pleiotropy through socioeconomic position is not
excluded, and the interval spans a factor of three.}
{10.1183/23120541.01444-2025}

\band{Occupational \& indoor\hfill 4 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Lv H et al.\ 2026}
{Polyimide microsphere/nanofibre gradient aerogels for high-temperature filtration}
{ACS Appl Mater Interfaces}
{A single-component polyimide gradient aerogel built to beat the dust-holding limit of
uniform nanofibre mats, which cake at the surface and choke. Dual-layer structure:
$\approx$730\,nm coarse fibres under $\approx$300\,nm medium fibres, with a composite
functional layer of $\approx$3.34\,$\mu$m microspheres and $\approx$60\,nm ultrafine
fibres on top. Performance: \textbf{99.996\% PM$_{0.3}$ efficiency at 45.08\,Pa} initial
pressure drop, \textbf{127\,g\,m$^{-2}$ dust-holding capacity}, and \textbf{99.91\%
retained at 300\,\textdegree C}. Single-component construction is claimed for
recyclability and uniform thermal expansion. What is not reported is loaded pressure-drop
evolution or efficiency after thermal cycling, which is what actually determines service
life in a stack.}
{10.1021/acsami.6c10042}

\paperentry{Ma L et al.\ 2026}
{Surgical smoke from laser laparoscopic partial nephrectomy with an aspirating handle}
{Can J Urol}
{Ex vivo porcine kidney comparison of electrocoagulation, ultrasonic, 980\,nm diode and
532\,nm KTP laser resection, with and without a novel aspirator-integrated handle. The
KTP green laser produced the most smoke; coagulation-layer thickness ran diode
(\textbf{4.16 $\pm$ 0.21\,mm}) $>$ green (1.55 $\pm$ 0.21) $>$ electrocoagulation
(0.35 $\pm$ 0.08) $>$ ultrasonic (0.52 $\pm$ 0.08). With the aspirator, diode-laser
formaldehyde fell \textbf{4.95 $\to$ 1.69\,mg\,m$^{-3}$}, TVOC \textbf{8.34 $\to$
1.11\,mg\,m$^{-3}$} and CO \textbf{124 $\to$ 24\,ppm}. Gases only --- the study reports no
particle-size distribution or number concentration, which is the fraction that reaches the
alveoli and the reason surgical smoke is an occupational aerosol problem rather than a
gas one. In vitro, no personal sampling.}
{10.32604/cju.2026.070096}

\paperentry{Chen Y et al.\ 2026}
{Solid-fuel PAH exposure, phenanthrene metabolites, DNA damage and inflammation}
{Atmos Environ}
{Ships on metadata only --- no abstract in the Crossref deposit and no PubMed, Europe PMC
or Semantic Scholar record on this run. Title and authorship indicate urinary
phenanthrene metabolites as the exposure index in a rural northern Chinese solid-fuel
population, with DNA-damage and systemic-inflammation biomarkers as outcomes. Counted and
listed; no numbers claimed. High priority for the retry list: household solid-fuel
combustion is the single largest PM exposure contrast available in observational work and
this watch carries very little of it.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122339}

\paperentry{Micallef \& Scerri 2026}
{A flow-conditioned infiltration metric for traffic-related pollution ingress}
{Build Environ}
{Ships on metadata only --- Crossref carries no abstract and the record is not otherwise
indexed on this run. The title indicates an outdoor-to-indoor infiltration metric for
traffic-related air pollution in naturally ventilated buildings, conditioned on flow
regime rather than assumed constant. Counted and listed; no numbers claimed. Worth

{10.1016/j.buildenv.2026.115195}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 6 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Petersen et al.\ 2026}
{Sulfate ions in aerosol water are their own source of radical oxidants}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Direct spectroscopic evidence that the sulfate anion absorbs surface-level sunlight and
ejects an electron, forming \textbf{SO$_4^{\bullet-}$ on a sub-microsecond timescale} in a
concentration-dependent manner; long-timescale production rates quantified by radical
trapping with LC-HRMS. Compounds emitted from biomass fires photosensitise the pathway,
raising radical formation \textbf{up to five-fold} over direct photolysis across
pH 1.6--5.5, more strongly at low pH. Estimated SO$_4^{\bullet-}$ production is
\textbf{comparable to measured hydroxyl-radical production in aerosols} --- i.e.\ a second
oxidant budget nobody was counting. The downstream claim is the striking one: secondary
brown carbon from this chemistry \textbf{turns the direct radiative forcing of
sulfate-containing aerosol from negative to positive}, and the organosulfates formed are
cloud-active. Bulk solutions at controlled ionic strength; extrapolation to real
aerosol water is the open step.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c05704}

\paperentry{Engeroff et al.\ 2026}
{PM exposure while running: park, residential and roadside routes by time of day}
{Research Square (preprint)}
{Mobile personal PM$_{10}$, PM$_{2.5}$ and PM$_1$ over \textbf{60 instrumented 2\,km runs}
(20 each on a major road, a residential street and an urban park; 30 morning, 30 midday;
pace 8:02--4:31\,min/km) with local urban background as reference and linear mixed-effects
models adjusting for meteorology. Lowest concentrations of every fraction:
\textbf{midday in the park}. Highest PM$_{10}$ anywhere: \textbf{morning on that same park
route}. Exposure across all routes was lower at midday, tracking urban background, and
humidity, pressure, wind speed and temperature all carried signal. The conclusion --- park
routes and rush-hour avoidance are sufficient --- is reasonable but rests on 20 runs per
route in one city, and the fact that the park produced both the minimum and the maximum
should temper any general route advice. Preprint, not peer reviewed.}
{10.21203/rs.3.rs-10016764/v1}

\paperentry{Zhu et al.\ 2026}
{Stabilized Criegee intermediates and the sensitivity of sulfuric acid formation}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Criegee reactions in MCM v3.3.1 updated to current IUPAC recommendations, driven through
a large AtChem box-model ensemble, with interpretable XGBoost surrogates used to attribute
the sCI-mediated SO$_2$ loss rate and its fractional share to controlling factors. The
sCI loss rate is governed chiefly by \textbf{relative humidity and isoprene}; the drivers
of the sCI \emph{fraction} split diurnally --- alkene speciation, O$_3$ and alkene share of
VOCs by day, alkene speciation, RH and NO$_x$ by night. Under high-sCI-fraction regimes,
the positive response of total SO$_2$ oxidation to O$_3$, VOCs and alkene fraction is
\emph{amplified}, which a summer episode at Wuhai supports. Policy consequence: where
alkene ozonolysis is active, \textbf{cutting VOCs controls sulfuric acid formation more
effectively than cutting SO$_2$ alone} --- and sulfuric acid is the new-particle-formation
precursor. Box model with a machine-learned surrogate; the surrogate inherits every
mechanistic assumption underneath it.}
{10.5194/acp-26-12479-2026}

\paperentry{Zhao H et al.\ 2026}
{Lead isotopes resolve dustfall sources across urban functional areas}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Ten trace elements (V, Mn, Co, Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, Pb) in dustfall from
\textbf{16 sites} across residential, busy-road, park and industrial areas of Baoding,
with enrichment factors, geo-accumulation index and Pb isotope fingerprinting. Cu, Pb, Zn
and Cd reach considerable pollution levels; the spatial ranking is
\textbf{residential $>$ busy road $>$ industrial $>$ park}, which is not the ordering most
source-apportionment intuition predicts and the paper does not fully explain. Total
non-carcinogenic risk stayed below threshold but \textbf{total carcinogenic risk exceeded
the acceptable threshold} in several areas, worst on busy roads and in residential zones.
Pb isotopes attribute \textbf{over 50\%} to a combined coal-combustion plus
traffic-emission group in most areas --- combined, i.e.\ the isotope system does not
separate the two here, which is the honest limit of the method in a city that burns coal
and drives cars.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15859-0}

\paperentry{Lei et al.\ 2026}
{Source transition in PM$_{2.5}$-bound metals in a coastal megacity}
{Atmos Environ}
{Ships on metadata only --- no abstract in the Crossref deposit and no other index carries
the record on this run. The title indicates a multi-year, high-time-resolution
observational record of PM$_{2.5}$-bound metals with a source-transition analysis and a
health-implication component. Counted and listed; no numbers claimed. High retry priority:
multi-year hourly metals is exactly the dataset class against which a low-cost network's
composition proxies would have to be validated, and there are very few of them.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122336}

\paperentry{Mutic et al.\ 2026}
{Paired silicone wristbands across home and early-childhood-education settings}
{medRxiv (preprint)}
{A \emph{protocol}, not results. Prospective repeated-measures pilot targeting
\textbf{44 preschool children and 8 staff} across two sociodemographically distinct Atlanta
early-childhood centres, with home- and centre-designated silicone wristbands exchanged
between settings over three consecutive days and nights, one spot urine per child, and
continuous indoor air-quality monitoring in two classrooms per site. Eight pre-specified
feasibility indicators. The design premise is sound and underexploited --- children spend
$>$90\% of time indoors and early-childhood centres are high-occupant-density
microenvironments almost absent from the exposure literature --- but wristbands integrate
semi-volatile organics, not particle mass, so the PM component rests entirely on the
fixed classroom monitors. Listed for the design; nothing to conclude from it yet.}
{10.64898/2026.08.27.26361470}
