\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Two very large paediatric studies land on the same day
and both report that the constituent, not the mass, carries the effect --- and both
identify \emph{organic matter} as the dominant contributor.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Wu et al.\ (\textit{Ecotoxicol Environ Saf}) run quantile g-computation on
\textbf{339{,}435 mother--child pairs} in Liaoning and find prenatal PM$_{2.5}$ total mass
at \textbf{OR 1.050 (95\% CI 1.036--1.064) per IQR} for overweight/obesity by age two,
while the constituent mixture reaches \textbf{1.063 (1.050--1.076)} with
\textbf{organic matter contributing 54.1\%} of the joint effect, ammonium 34.4\% and
nitrate 11.5\%. Wang et al.\ (same journal, \textbf{449{,}894 children under five} in
China, India and 26 African countries) report cumulative PM$_{2.5}$ at
\textbf{OR 1.019 (1.009--1.028) in China, 1.009 (1.008--1.009) in India and
1.001 (1.000--1.002) in Africa} for anaemia, with organic carbon and dust the leading
components and population attributable fractions of \textbf{28.5\%, 10.6\% and 0.69\%}
respectively. The two papers disagree on almost everything else --- design, outcome,
exposure product (Chinese reanalysis vs.\ MERRA-2), continent --- and converge on the same
methodological demand: \emph{a mass-only instrument cannot measure the quantity that
carries the association}. For a distributed low-cost network this is the recurring problem
in its sharpest form, because optical scattering is precisely the technique least able to
separate organic matter from ammonium nitrate. Note also that the PAF ordering is inverted
relative to the exposure ordering --- Africa has the highest anaemia prevalence (64.5\%)
and the lowest attributable fraction --- which is a statement about model-assigned exposure
contrast, not about African air.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Effect modification, not main effect, is where the COVID--PM$_{2.5}$ signal
now sits.} Kannoth et al.\ (\textit{JESEE}) find short-term PM$_{2.5}$ alone essentially
null for COVID-19 pneumonia (\textbf{RR 1.03, 95\% CI 0.95--1.12}) and prior myocardial
infarction alone at \textbf{1.34 (1.14--1.58)}, but the joint contrast at
\textbf{1.67 (1.41--1.99)}, with upper-quartile PM$_{2.5}$ raising pneumonia risk
\emph{only} in those with cardiovascular disease (ATE 0.05, 0.02--0.08). A study powered on
the main effect would have concluded nothing.

\item \textbf{Non-exhaust PM gets a validated inorganic workflow.} Diana et al.\
(\textit{Talanta}) put 32 WLTP brake-dynamometer PM$_{10}$ samples through a dual digestion
(HNO$_3$/H$_2$O$_2$ and HNO$_3$/HF/H$_3$BO$_3$) validated against NIST SRM 1648a,
quantifying \textbf{27 elements at $>$75\% recovery}. Low-steel pads on grey cast iron give
the highest emission factors and Fe-dominated PM$_{10}$; non-asbestos organic pads give the
lowest and are marked by Ba, Ti, K and Zr. That is a usable source-marker set for
apportionment as tailpipe emissions keep falling and brake wear does not.

\item \textbf{Two independent attacks on the same instrumentation problem: extracting
structure from sparse regulatory data.} Peri\v{s}i\'c et al.\ (\textit{Sci Total Environ})
cluster SHAP profiles from a single station into ten recurrent
meteorology--pollutant settings ($R^2 = 0.913$), showing that identical PM$_{10}$
concentrations correspond to different physical configurations. Band \& Mohammadi (arXiv)
pretrain on 10 US stations and adapt to 77 in Taiwan, reaching \textbf{$R^2 = 0.8739$,
MSE 21.66, MAE 3.14} with an adaptive source encoder. Both are aimed at deployments that
cannot afford speciation or dense reference coverage.

\item \textbf{The day's health records are all exposure-modelled, none exposure-measured.}
Every one of the four human-endpoint records assigns exposure from a reanalysis or CMAQ
product at ZIP-code, district or 1\,km resolution. No record this issue measured particles
and outcomes on the same subjects --- the third consecutive issue with that property.

\item \textbf{Weak record worth flagging rather than hiding.} Erickson-DiRenzo et al.\
(\textit{J Voice}) report paired laryngeal biopsies from \textbf{one} participant during a
117\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ wildfire episode and at $<$10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, with a $\approx$2-fold
MMP-1 increase. $n=1$, no control for time, phonatory load or infection; carried for the
paired-biopsy design, not the result.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 3 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Peri\v{s}i\'c et al.\ 2026}
{Explainable AI resolves ten recurrent environmental settings behind urban PM$_{10}$ variability}
{Sci Total Environ}
{Four years (2020--2023) of routine regulatory data from a \emph{single} station ---
PM$_{10}$, PM$_{2.5}$, NO$_2$, SO$_2$, O$_3$ and meteorology --- fed to ensemble
machine-learning models with metaheuristic hyperparameter optimisation, reaching
\textbf{$R^2 = 0.913$}, then clustered on SHAP-derived predictor-impact profiles rather
than on concentrations. \textbf{Ten recurrent settings emerge.} The strongest positive
contributions are cold-season accumulation regimes: E0 is frequent and predominantly
nocturnal at a mean impact of \textbf{50.9\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$}; E7 is rarer but reaches
\textbf{82.9\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} and persists day and night. Warm, well-mixed settings run
\textbf{26--29\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ below} the model baseline. The substantive claim --- that
equal concentrations can encode different pollutant--meteorology configurations --- is well
supported and directly useful where speciation is unaffordable. The limitation is
structural and the authors say so: one station, one climate, no external validation, and
SHAP attributions are model-conditional, so the ``settings'' are properties of a fitted
ensemble before they are properties of the atmosphere. For distributed low-cost networks
the appeal is that the entire input set is what a modest node plus a met sensor already
produces.}
{10.1016/j.scitotenv.2026.182195}

\paperentry{Diana et al.\ 2026}
{Validated multi-technique workflow for inorganic characterisation of brake-wear PM$_{10}$}
{Talanta}
{Thirty-two PM$_{10}$ samples from WLTP-based brake-dynamometer tests on four tribological
couples --- low-steel (LS) and non-asbestos organic (NAO) pads against grey cast-iron (GCI)
or coated discs. A combined acid digestion (HNO$_3$/H$_2$O$_2$ plus
HNO$_3$/HF/H$_3$BO$_3$) was validated against \textbf{NIST SRM 1648a} and quantified by
ICP-OES and ICP-MS, giving \textbf{27 elements with recoveries above 75\% for most
analytes}; FESEM-EDS confirmed the trends at particle scale and PCA/HCA separated the
tribological families cleanly. \textbf{LS-GCI produced the highest PM$_{10}$ emission
factors and NAO-GCI the lowest}; Fe dominates LS-derived material while Ba, Ti, K and Zr
mark NAO. An Mn-based enrichment factor improved discrimination of rotor- versus
pad-derived contributions. The value here is a defensible marker set for non-exhaust source
apportionment, which matters more each year as tailpipe PM falls. Caveats are the usual
dynamometer ones: WLTP braking is not real-world braking duty, only four couples were
tested, and no on-road or roadside validation is attempted.}
{10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130448}

\paperentry{Band \& Mohammadi 2026 (preprint)}
{Shift-aware dual-encoder transfer learning for short-horizon PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting}
{arXiv}
{A source encoder pretrained on hourly PM$_{2.5}$ from 10 US monitoring locations is fused
with a target-specific encoder and adapted to \textbf{77 Taiwanese stations} over two years
under a chronological train/validation/test split --- the correct protocol, and one many
comparable papers do not use. Against four baselines the frozen-source dual encoder gives
\textbf{MSE 21.8960, MAE 3.1597, $R^2 = 0.8725$} (a 7.1\% and 4.1\% MSE reduction over two
transfer-learning variants); allowing the source encoder to adapt improves this to
\textbf{MSE 21.6575, MAE 3.1383, $R^2 = 0.8739$}. Ablation shows the Taiwan-specific branch
matters most, and SHAP attributes predictions mainly to recent PM$_{2.5}$ and meteorology.
The framing --- explicit handling of source/target distribution shift to avoid negative
transfer --- is the right one for anyone standing up a network in a region with no long
record. Two reservations: the absolute gain over the better baselines is a few percent of
MSE and no significance or repeated-seed variance is reported, and both domains are
reference-grade regulatory data, so transfer onto low-cost nodes (a far larger shift) is
untested. Preprint, not peer reviewed.}
{10.48550/arXiv.2608.14456}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 2 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Fan et al.\ 2026}
{Multiple amides synergistically enhance sulfuric acid--ammonia nucleation}
{iScience}
{Quantum-chemical cluster thermodynamics plus kinetic simulation of new particle formation
in binary, ternary and quaternary systems containing sulfuric acid (SA), ammonia,
methylacetamide (MAA) and methylpropionamide (MPA). \textbf{(SA)$_1\cdot$(MPA)$_1$ and
(SA)$_1\cdot$(MAA)$_1$ are more stable than (SA)$_1\cdot$(NH$_3$)$_1$ and even than
(SA)$_1\cdot$(DMA)$_1$}, and the two amides together enhance nucleation by
\textbf{an order of magnitude more than either alone}, most strongly at low SA
concentration and low temperature. A displacement hierarchy is identified: MPA replaces
NH$_3$, MAA replaces MPA. Carried because secondary particle number --- not mass --- is
what an optical low-cost sensor is blindest to, and because amide sources (industrial,
agricultural, carbon-capture solvent degradation) are rising and largely unmonitored. It is
a purely computational result with no chamber or ambient confirmation, the amide
concentrations assumed are not tied to measured ambient values, and cluster-level
thermodynamics has a poor track record of quantitative transfer to observed formation
rates.}
{10.1016/j.isci.2026.117013}

\paperentry{Alshehri \& El-Hamid 2026 (preprint)}
{Sentinel-5P and MODIS land-surface coupling of pollutant fields over Riyadh, 2018--2025}
{Research Square}
{Eight years of TROPOMI NO$_2$, SO$_2$, CO, O$_3$ and Absorbing Aerosol Index combined with
MODIS land-surface temperature and NDVI and Sentinel-2 land cover in Google Earth Engine,
analysed by correlation, PCA and multiple linear regression. NO$_2$ and CO concentrate over
urban and industrial areas, O$_3$ peaks downwind, LST falls from 43.10\,$^\circ$C (2021) to
37.07\,$^\circ$C (2025) while NDVI rises after 2022. \textbf{The regression is weak:
$R^2$ ranges from 0.124 (O$_3$) to 0.231 (SO$_2$)}, and PCA explains 55.8\% of variance;
the authors correctly conclude that land-surface descriptors explain only a minor share and
that emissions and transport dominate. Carried as a negative methodological result for an
arid-region setting where ground networks are sparse --- and as a caution, because
\textbf{no surface PM product appears anywhere in the study} despite the framing as air
pollution monitoring; AAI is a column absorption index, not a PM concentration. Preprint,
not peer reviewed, and the LST trend is reported without any test for its significance
against interannual variability.}
{10.21203/rs.3.rs-10538140/v1}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 2 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{Kannoth et al.\ 2026}
{Short-term PM$_{2.5}$ and COVID-19 pneumonia in New York City, amplified by pre-existing cardiovascular disease}
{J Expo Sci Environ Epidemiol}
{\textbf{15{,}361 NYC COVID-19 hospitalisations} (March--December 2020) with short-term
PM$_{2.5}$ defined as the 7-day residential ZIP-code average from EPA CMAQ, and 15
ICD-coded circulatory conditions as the modifier. Modified Poisson regression for ARDS,
pneumonia, ventilation and dialysis; Cox models for fatality and length of stay; RERI for
additive interaction; TMLE for average treatment effects. \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ alone was null
(RR 1.03, 95\% CI 0.95--1.12) and prior myocardial infarction alone 1.34 (1.14--1.58), but
jointly 1.67 (1.41--1.99)}; upper-quartile PM$_{2.5}$ raised pneumonia risk only among
those with cardiovascular conditions (\textbf{ATE 0.05, 0.02--0.08}). The interaction is
the finding and it is the sort a main-effect analysis destroys. Weaknesses are real:
ZIP-code CMAQ is a coarse surrogate for personal exposure and its error is spatially
structured with deprivation, the effect appears only from July onward with no mechanism
offered for the seasonal split, and the cohort is hospitalised patients only, so selection
on admission is unaddressed. For network design the implication is concrete --- the
susceptible stratum is identifiable in advance, which is an argument for siting density by
cardiovascular prevalence rather than by population alone.}
{10.1038/s41370-026-00957-5}

\paperentry{Erickson-DiRenzo et al.\ 2026}
{Wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ and the human larynx: paired in-event and clean-air assessment in one adult}
{J Voice}
{A single adult assessed twice --- during a wildfire episode with PM$_{2.5}$ reaching
\textbf{117\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} and again at \textbf{$<$10\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} --- with
acoustic and aerodynamic voice measures, laryngeal endoscopy with stroboscopy, and
\emph{ventricular fold biopsies at both time points} analysed by qPCR for extracellular
matrix and inflammatory markers. Speaking fundamental frequency fell relative to baseline,
endoscopy showed visibly increased laryngeal mucus, and \textbf{MMP-1 expression roughly
doubled}; other ECM and inflammatory genes moved inconsistently. \textbf{$n = 1$.} There is
no control participant, no adjustment for phonatory load, hydration, intercurrent
infection or the ordinary diurnal variation in fundamental frequency, and a two-point
comparison cannot separate exposure from time. The reason to carry it is the design, not
the result: paired human airway tissue across a natural exposure contrast is rare, and it
establishes feasibility for a properly powered version. Treat the MMP-1 figure as a
hypothesis.}
{10.1016/j.jvoice.2026.07.048}

\band{Reproductive \& developmental\hfill 1 record}{Sky}

\paperentry{Wu et al.\ 2026}
{Prenatal PM$_{2.5}$ constituents and early childhood obesity: organic matter dominates the mixture}
{Ecotoxicol Environ Saf}
{\textbf{339{,}435 mother--child pairs} in Liaoning Province with prenatal PM$_{2.5}$ and
five constituents (organic matter, black carbon, ammonium, nitrate, sulfate) assigned from
high-resolution atmospheric reanalysis, and overweight/obesity ascertained across the first
two years. Single-pollutant models give \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ mass OR 1.050 (95\% CI
1.036--1.064) per IQR}, with \textbf{OM 1.058 (1.043--1.072)}, \textbf{NH$_4^+$ 1.048
(1.035--1.062)}, \textbf{NO$_3^-$ 1.048 (1.033--1.064)} and \textbf{BC 1.033
(1.020--1.047)}; sulfate is null. Quantile g-computation puts the mixture at \textbf{1.063
(1.050--1.076)} per quartile increase with \textbf{OM contributing 54.1\%}, NH$_4^+$ 34.4\%
and NO$_3^-$ 11.5\%. The sample size makes the intervals tight, but tight intervals on
reanalysis-assigned exposure are a precision claim about the model, not the exposure:
constituent fields are far more uncertain than total mass and their errors are correlated
with each other, which is exactly the condition under which QGC weights become unstable.
Residential address at delivery stands in for the whole gestation, and no gestational
windows are resolved. The direction is credible; the 54\% attribution to OM is not
something the exposure product can really support.}
{10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120670}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 1 record}{Coral}

\paperentry{Alamsyah et al.\ 2026}
{Industrial intensity and traffic congestion propagate air-pollution health vulnerability across district borders in Java}
{Environ Sci Pollut Res Int}
{A health vulnerability index (HVI) for Java built by PCA over satellite pollutant fields,
official statistics and OpenStreetMap congestion data, then regressed on industrial
activity intensity and congestion in a spatial Durbin model to separate direct from
spillover effects. \textbf{HVI ranges 29.2--62.3 on a 0--100 scale}, highest in West Java,
Jakarta and Banten and lowest in Central Java, Yogyakarta and East Java; the exposure
dimension varies most (13.18) against adaptive capacity (9.40) and sensitivity (5.17), and
both industrial intensity and congestion act through \textbf{direct and indirect
(spillover) channels}. The spillover result is the useful one --- it says district-level
mitigation will leak, which is a real argument for regional rather than municipal air
management. But the index is a construct: PCA weights are data-driven rather than
health-anchored, no health outcome is used to validate the HVI against observed morbidity,
and OpenStreetMap congestion is an availability-biased proxy that is denser precisely in
the wealthier districts. Uncertainty and sensitivity analysis is reported, which is more
than most index papers do.}
{10.1007/s11356-026-38148-2}

\band{Other clinical endpoints\hfill 1 record}{Slate}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026}
{Early-life PM$_{2.5}$ mass and components and childhood anaemia across China, India and Africa}
{Ecotoxicol Environ Saf}
{Cross-sectional analysis of \textbf{449{,}894 children under five} from China, India and 26
African countries, with prenatal, postnatal and cumulative PM$_{2.5}$ and five components
(black carbon, organic carbon, sulfate, dust, sea salt) from NASA MERRA-2 reanalysis.
Anaemia prevalence is \textbf{26.9\% in China, 62.6\% in India, 64.5\% in Africa}. Per
1\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ of cumulative PM$_{2.5}$: \textbf{OR 1.019 (95\% CI 1.009--1.028) in
China, 1.009 (1.008--1.009) in India, 1.001 (1.000--1.002) in Africa}, with organic carbon
and dust positive in all three. Population attributable fractions are \textbf{28.46\%,
10.64\% and 0.69\%}. The regional ordering of effect size and PAF runs \emph{opposite} to
the ordering of anaemia burden, and the paper does not resolve this: the most economical
explanation is that MERRA-2 at 0.5$^\circ\times$0.625$^\circ$ has little within-country
exposure contrast in Africa and correspondingly attenuated coefficients, not that African
children are less susceptible. Cross-sectional design cannot order exposure and outcome,
and anaemia in these settings is dominated by iron deficiency, malaria and haemoglobinopathy
--- confounders that co-vary strongly with dust exposure. Read the component ranking, not
the PAFs.}
{10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120658}
