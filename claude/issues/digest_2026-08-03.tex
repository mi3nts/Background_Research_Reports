\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Low-cost networks have acquired the deployment
longevity that multi-year exposure epidemiology requires --- and, in the same window, the
evidence that their drift over that horizon is uncharacterised.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Chung et al.\ (\textit{Sensors}) profiled every publicly shared outdoor PurpleAir unit in
the United States, 2016--2025, and find that \textbf{most remain deployed for more than
three years}, densest and longest-lived in the western states. That is the first national
statement that these networks are temporally continuous enough to support multi-year
exposure assignment rather than campaign-scale snapshots. Read against it, Kumpika et al.\
(\textit{Environ Pollut Bioavailab}) show that over exactly that horizon a PMS7003 needs a
\emph{joint} humidity-and-aging correction, not the humidity correction the literature
usually stops at --- and report the uncomfortable operational finding that
\textbf{limiting duty cycle to 20\,min/day reduces drift relative to continuous
operation}, which is precisely the opposite of how a continuous exposure network is run.
Silva et al.\ close the triangle: of 2{,}736 screened indoor-air studies only 24 met
quality criteria, most devices fail regulatory compliance requirements, and sensor aging
is named as an unaddressed confounder. The three papers were not written together, but
they define the gap cleanly: \emph{the sensors now last long enough to matter, and nobody
has published the drift model that would let a five-year record be trusted end to end.}
For a distributed-sensing programme that is the tractable hole in the field --- not the
month-one calibration problem, which is solved.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Source apportionment gained an identifiability theory.} Bhardwaj \&
Subramanian (arXiv) stop treating sparse-network apportionment as a fitting problem and
ask when the answer is \emph{recoverable at all}: exact identifiability requires full
column rank of the projected lagged response matrix, and noise robustness is governed by
its singular values, coefficient visibility, background absorption, pairwise coherence
and ray distance. This is the first framing in this watch that tells you whether a given
sensor \emph{placement} can distinguish two candidate sources before any data is
collected --- directly actionable for network siting.

\item \textbf{Two independent groups did source apportionment from low-cost networks
without a reference monitor.} Rehbein et al.\ triangulate six source clusters in Ile-Ife,
Nigeria from particle-number profiles alone; G\'omez et al.\ show in Bogot\'a that FastDTW
alignment plus Random Forest / XGBoost with RH, temperature and absorption flow is what
makes an LCS node usable against a T640X. Together these are the strongest current
argument that LMIC networks need not wait for reference infrastructure.

\item \textbf{The atmospheric-measurement literature reappears in force --- 5 records from
ACP/AMT, none of them visible to PubMed.} The Crossref by-journal leg is now carrying the
part of the corpus the biomedical spine structurally cannot see, including B\"uttner
et al.'s PIA v3.0.0: a released, versioned QA/QC pipeline for continuous INP measurement,
and a template worth studying for anyone writing acceptance criteria for a sensor network.

\item \textbf{Mechanistic work converged on reversibility.} Asplund et al.\
(\textit{Toxicol Sci}) is the day's best-designed toxicology: concentrated ambient PM
suppressed erythropoietin, reticulocytes and bone-marrow erythroid precursors and broadly
depressed splenic proresolving lipid mediators (lipoxin A$_4$ most consistently), and
\emph{return to filtered air normalised all of it}. A recovery arm is rare in this
literature and is what separates an adaptive response from damage.

\item \textbf{Composition, not mass, again carries the health signal.} The Shanghai
fever-clinic preprint finds cadmium the strongest single constituent, with nitrate and
ammonium acting at delayed lags consistent with secondary inorganic aerosol, while Xu
et al.\ show secondary processes dominate wintertime nitroaromatic formation across 11
Chinese cities.

\item \textbf{Negative-space observation.} \emph{No record in this window reports a
ratio-metric effect estimate with a confidence interval} --- Shin et al.\ publish no
numbers in the indexed abstract --- so the forest plot is omitted rather than assembled
from non-comparable quantities.
\end{itemize}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 10 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Chung et al.\ 2026}
{PurpleAir deployment trends, longevity and uncertainty across the United States}
{Sensors}
{Every publicly shared outdoor PurpleAir unit, 2016--2025, with deployment duration taken
as the span between first and last public observation, summarised nationally, by Census
region and by state. \textbf{Most sensors persist beyond three years}; density and
longevity are highest in the western US. Descriptive PM$_{2.5}$ summaries for California,
Minnesota, Pennsylvania and Texas by deployment duration and urban--rural class. The
limitation is that it characterises \emph{availability}, not accuracy --- persistence of a
data stream is not persistence of calibration, and the paper explicitly does not address
drift. That is exactly the join left open against Kumpika et al.\ below.}
{10.3390/s26061789}

\paperentry{Kumpika et al.\ 2026}
{Joint humidity and sensor-aging correction for low-cost light-scattering PM$_{2.5}$ sensors}
{Environ Pollut Bioavailab}
{PMS7003 units in Chiang Mai under varying RH, validated against a government BAM with
additional dry-air verification through a diffusion dryer. Polynomial corrections to third
order; \textbf{R$^2$~=~0.9737} against BAM under elevated humidity. The operational finding
is the more interesting one: limiting operation to \textbf{20\,min/day materially reduces
performance drift} versus continuous running. Single site, single sensor model, and the
aging arm is short relative to the multi-year deployments Chung et al.\ document --- so the
correction is demonstrated rather than validated at the horizon where it matters.}
{10.1080/26395940.2026.2616098}

\paperentry{Silva et al.\ 2026}
{Accuracy, applications and limitations of low-cost sensors for indoor air quality}
{J Air Waste Manag Assoc}
{PRISMA 2020 review of PubMed, Scopus and ScienceDirect to March 2025;
\textbf{24 of 2{,}736 screened studies} met inclusion and quality criteria, appraised with
the Critical Review Form for Quantitative Studies. Optical particle counters and NDIR
dominate; correlations against reference instruments are strong under controlled
conditions and degrade with humidity, temperature and source type. Named unaddressed
confounders: sensor aging and environmental cross-sensitivity. Conclusion for the
programme --- most devices do not currently meet regulatory compliance requirements, and
standardised validation protocols do not exist.}
{10.1080/10962247.2026.2624795}

\paperentry{G\'omez et al.\ 2026}
{Machine- and deep-learning calibration of low-cost PM$_{10}$/PM$_{2.5}$ nodes}
{Sensors}
{LCS nodes co-located with a T640X reference at Universidad Distrital, Bogot\'a, with RH,
temperature and absorption flow from an Automet station. Pipeline: filtering,
segmentation, \textbf{FastDTW} alignment, then six calibration models (two statistical,
three ML, one DL). Random Forest and XGBoost performed best; the authors argue that the
FastDTW preprocessing step and the inclusion of absorption flow --- not the model
choice --- carry the accuracy gain. Single-site co-location with no transfer test to an
uncalibrated node, so generalisation is unestablished.}
{10.3390/s26030796}

\paperentry{Rehbein et al.\ 2026}
{Source identification and triangulation from a low-cost sensor network in Ile-Ife}
{Clean Air J}
{Reanalysis of a published Nigerian LCS dataset using $k$-means over particle-number
concentration profiles, resolving \textbf{six clusters} that separate day from night and
local from regional influence. Stagnant conditions near industrial activity gave PM
roughly $10\times$ the daily mean (PM$_{2.5}$ 30.6$\pm$40.7, PM$_{10}$
83.4$\pm$259.2\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$); cleanest conditions were daytime south-westerlies
(PM$_{2.5}$ 11.5$\pm$4.7), still above WHO annual guidance. Standard deviations exceeding
the means signal a heavily skewed distribution that $k$-means handles poorly --- the
triangulation is indicative, not quantitative apportionment.}
{10.17159/caj/2026/36/1.24186}

\paperentry{B\"uttner et al.\ 2026}
{PIA v3.0.0: automated quality assurance for PINE ice-nucleating-particle data}
{Atmos Meas Tech}
{Software description for the analysis chain behind the Portable Ice Nucleation
Experiment. Two components documented in detail: automated data quality control, and the
algorithm separating aerosols and droplets from ice crystals by optical size. The second
half gives quality-assurance recommendations for PINE operation. Not a PM$_{2.5}$ paper,
but the most directly transferable artefact in the issue --- a published, versioned QA/QC
specification for a continuously operating particle instrument is precisely what
distributed sensing networks lack.}
{10.5194/amt-19-5007-2026}

\paperentry{Nimmala et al.\ 2026}
{Drone and ground sampling of transgenic switchgrass pollen with Lagrangian dispersal modelling}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Release--recapture with 200 GE switchgrass plants expressing orange fluorescent protein
under a constitutive (PvUBI1) and a pollen-specific (Zm13) promoter, sampled to
\textbf{1\,km} by fixed and drone-borne collectors, with Lagrangian stochastic simulation
driven by high-resolution wind. Emission rate estimated by combining simulated and
measured concentrations; a strong diurnal trend correlates positively with wind speed,
temperature and vapour pressure deficit and negatively with RH. Incorporating changing
wind direction improved estimates under low-wind meandering --- the transferable result for
anyone inverting a dispersion model against sparse fixed sensors. Held back from the
2 August issue on its per-record \texttt{[EDAT]}; it is not a gap.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15632-3}

\paperentry{Tsai et al.\ 2026}
{Vision-based vertical PM$_{2.5}$ retrieval from a 5G-connected UAV}
{IEEE Sens J}
{Dark-channel-prior atmospheric transmittance extracted from UAV imagery, fused with
meteorology into a random-forest regression with 7-D polynomial feature engineering hosted
on a 5G-core virtual host; Gemini used for semantic interpretation of the vertical
profile. Reported \textbf{R$^2$~=~0.954, RMSE 1.27\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$}. Treat with caution:
no held-out spatial or temporal validation is described, the DCP proxy is
illumination-dependent, and the generative-AI layer contributes interpretation, not
measurement. Carried for the vertical-profiling concept, not the reported accuracy.}
{10.1109/jsen.2026.3695609}

\paperentry{Rubio et al.\ 2026}
{LoRa-connected micromobility pollution monitor for the Internet of Vehicles}
{IEEE Consum Electron Mag}
{On-board PM$_x$/CO$_2$/meteorology unit for personal mobility vehicles, evaluated across
Cartagena's three-gateway LoRa network. The engineering contribution is compact low-power
design plus LoRa in a genuinely mobile setting, where duty-cycle limits and Doppler
normally break LPWAN links. No reference co-location and no uncertainty quantification are
reported, so the pollution data quality is unestablished --- the communications and
positioning results are the defensible part.}
{10.1109/mce.2026.3684542}

\paperentry{Suryawanshi et al.\ 2026}
{Spatiotemporal variation and hotspot identification of PM$_{2.5}$ from low-cost sensors}
{Int J Environ Res}
{Abstract not indexed at the time of harvest; carried as a metadata-only record so the
register is complete and the next rollup does not re-surface it as new. No claims are made
about its content. Full text required.}
{10.1007/s41742-026-01169-3}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 7 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Bhardwaj \& Subramanian 2026}
{Identifiability-aware source apportionment for sparse city-scale sensor networks}
{arXiv (preprint)}
{Reframes inventory-constrained apportionment as a wind-conditioned lagged inverse
problem: time-varying source activity is represented on a low-dimensional nonnegative
temporal basis, each source--basis coefficient produces a sensor--time fingerprint, and a
separate low-dimensional background space is projected out. \textbf{Exact identifiability
requires full column rank of the projected lagged response matrix}; noise-robust
attribution is governed by its singular values, coefficient visibility, background
absorption, pairwise coherence and ray distance. The practical payoff is diagnostic --- you
can test before deployment whether a proposed sensor geometry can separate two inventory
groups at all. Preprint, not peer reviewed.}
{10.48550/arXiv.2608.00050}

\paperentry{Pavlovic et al.\ 2026}
{Aligning satellite Earth-observation capability, use case and capacity for air-quality health}
{J Air Waste Manag Assoc}
{Twenty-three-author synthesis from the GEO Health Community of Practice Air Quality and
Respiratory Health Work Group. Argues satellite EO translates into public health benefit
only where three things align: technical capability of the retrieval, a defined use case,
and the human, computational, operational and governance capacity to sustain a solution.
Surveys applications from long-term trend tracking and epidemiological exposure assignment
to short-term emissions detection and early warning, then names gaps in each dimension. A
consensus document rather than new evidence --- useful as a citation anchor for why a given
satellite product is or is not fit for a stated purpose.}
{10.1080/10962247.2026.2698602}

\paperentry{Xu et al.\ 2026}
{Multi-factor control of aerosol nitroaromatic compounds across 11 Chinese cities}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Simultaneous winter measurement of PM$_{2.5}$-bound nitroaromatics in 11 cities.
Nitrophenols and nitrocatechols dominate, with city-varying relative abundance; totals are
higher in northern cities, attributed to biomass and coal combustion. Secondary processes
dominate formation everywhere, but the paper's actual target is the claim --- built from
chamber and single-site work --- that relative humidity or aerosol liquid water
\emph{drives} that formation. At large-scale field level the relationship is conditioned
by multi-factor interactions rather than being a clean RH dependence. A useful corrective
to chamber-derived parameterisations.}
{10.5194/acp-26-10751-2026}

\paperentry{Baldo et al.\ 2026}
{Chemical and mineralogical composition of Southern African mineral dust}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{First comprehensive characterisation of dust from a major and under-sampled source
region. Laboratory-generated aerosol from soils at natural and emerging anthropogenic
source areas, characterised by Si/Al, (Ca+Mg)/Al and K/Al ratios plus clay content. Those
ratios separate arid western coastal sources from humid eastern inland sources and encode
source-area weathering regime. The K-feldspar finding matters beyond composition ---
K-feldspar is the dominant mineral ice-nucleating agent, which links this record directly
to the PINE/PIA instrumentation above. Laboratory-generated, so size distribution and
mineralogy need not match what is actually emitted and transported.}
{10.5194/acp-26-10727-2026}

\paperentry{Wandji Nyamsi et al.\ 2026}
{Broadband aerosol optical depth reconstructed from sunshine-duration records since 1900}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Uses sunshine duration as a proxy for broadband AOD together with cloud-cover
observations from \textbf{2{,}700 sites}, producing the first historical BAOD estimates for
several regions back to the late 19th century. The motivation is the persistent spread in
modelled anthropogenic aerosol radiative forcing. The proxy is indirect and confounded by
cloud regime and 120 years of instrument-network change; the authors' own framing --- a
constraint on century-scale trend, not a concentration product --- is the right reading.}
{10.5194/acp-26-10767-2026}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026}
{Direct thermal versus emission-mediated pathways in heatwave-driven O$_3$ and SOA increases}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Explicitly decomposes the two routes by which heatwaves raise ozone and secondary organic
aerosol: direct acceleration of photochemistry, versus temperature-driven biogenic VOC and
soil-NO emissions. \textbf{Direct thermal and meteorological pathways account for over
80\% (normalised)} of the national increases; ozone--temperature sensitivity peaks in the
Yangtze River Delta (0.66\,ppb\,$^{\circ}$C$^{-1}$) and Pearl River Delta (0.95). Model-based
attribution, so the decomposition inherits the chemical mechanism's biases --- but the
direction of the result argues against the common assumption that BVOC enhancement is the
dominant heatwave pathway.}
{10.5194/acp-26-10789-2026}

\paperentry{Li et al.\ 2026}
{Multi-media, multi-pathway PAH exposure and carcinogenic risk under three scenarios}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{PAH concentrations in air, water, soil, dust and food compiled from Chinese studies
published 2010--2024; average daily doses computed for diet, inhalation, ingestion and
dermal contact; risk expressed as benzo[a]pyrene equivalents and incremental lifetime
cancer risk, with Monte Carlo and sensitivity analysis. Industrial areas dominate air and
water, urban areas dust and soil. \textbf{Dietary intake dominates both total exposure and
carcinogenic risk}, and the industrial--urban--rural ranking is stable across dietary
percentile scenarios even though absolute ILCR is not. Compiled from heterogeneous
published measurements spanning 14 years --- exposure misclassification is structural, not
incidental.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15753-9}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 3 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Asplund et al.\ 2026}
{Concentrated ambient PM$_{2.5}$ reversibly suppresses erythropoiesis and proresolving lipids}
{Toxicol Sci}
{Whole-body concentrated ambient particle exposure in mice with targeted splenic
lipidomics. CAP suppressed erythropoietin, circulating reticulocytes and bone-marrow
erythroid precursors, and reduced splenic iron and heme despite preserved splenic
architecture --- diminished erythrocyte turnover, not structural damage. Proresolving lipid
mediators were broadly suppressed with \textbf{lipoxin A$_4$} most consistent, and its
receptor ALX/FPR2 downregulated under prolonged exposure. \textbf{Return to filtered air
normalised the lipid profile, LXA$_4$ and the erythroid parameters.} The recovery arm is
what makes this the strongest design in the issue; the open question is whether the same
reversibility holds after chronic rather than sub-chronic exposure.}
{10.1093/toxsci/kfag092}

\paperentry{Gao et al.\ 2026}
{SQSTM1/p62-mediated mitophagy in PM$_{2.5}$-aggravated asthma, and dusquetide rescue}
{Zhongguo Yi Xue Ke Xue Yuan Xue Bao}
{House-dust-mite-sensitised C57BL/6 mice exposed to PM$_{2.5}$, assessed by whole-body
plethysmography, HE/PAS histology, TOMM20--LC3B colocalisation and LC-MS/MS mitochondrial
proteomics. PM$_{2.5}$ raised airway hyperresponsiveness, inflammation and mucus and
increased mitophagy signal; Mdivi-1 inhibition reduced inflammation; proteomics identified
SQSTM1/p62 as the hub, and dusquetide suppressed both p62 and mitophagy while relieving
hyperresponsiveness. Mdivi-1 is a poorly selective DRP1 inhibitor with known off-target
effects on complex I, so the mitophagy-specificity of the rescue is not secured by that
arm alone.}
{10.3881/j.issn.1000-503X.16948}

\paperentry{Deng et al.\ 2026}
{Baicalin rescue of PM$_{2.5}$-induced testicular injury via DKK1--Wnt/$\beta$-catenin}
{J Biochem Mol Toxicol}
{Oral-nasal PM$_{2.5}$ exposure at \textbf{750\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$, 4\,h/day for 45 days},
with baicalin gavage 1\,h before exposure at 50 and 100\,mg/kg. PM$_{2.5}$ lowered
testicular weight and organ coefficient, sperm count and serum testosterone and
downregulated StAR, CYP11A1 and CYP17A1; baicalin restored these dose-dependently alongside
DKK1--Wnt/$\beta$-catenin pathway markers. The exposure concentration sits one to two
orders of magnitude above ambient even in heavily polluted settings, which is the standing
weakness of this whole sub-literature --- the pathway may be real, the dose is not.}
{10.1002/jbt.71057}

\band{Occupational \& indoor\hfill 2 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{Seyedrezaei et al.\ 2026}
{Ecological momentary assessment plus continuous IEQ sensing across home and office}
{J Occup Environ Med}
{Nineteen office workers followed four months contributing \textbf{4{,}559 EMAs} of
productivity, mood, stress and eustress--distress appraisal, against continuous CO$_2$,
TVOC, PM$_{2.5}$, temperature, humidity and noise, analysed with mixed-effects and
multilevel mediation. Office work associated with higher productivity and greater
eustress--distress coexistence, home with higher boredom and modestly lower stress;
\textbf{home workspaces had poorer air quality}, and CO$_2$ and TVOC indirectly explained
part of the mood and stress difference. $n=19$ is very small for multilevel mediation, and
the within-person design cannot separate the environment from everything else that differs
between home and office --- read the IEQ mediation as hypothesis-generating. The
methodological interest is the pairing of dense subjective sampling with dense
environmental sampling, which is the design template for sensor-linked wellbeing work.}
{10.1097/JOM.0000000000003833}

\paperentry{Niari et al.\ 2026}
{Microplastic burden in beauty-salon indoor air and worker nasal lavage fluid}
{J Environ Health Sci Eng}
{Thirty-six indoor air and 18 nasal lavage samples across salons and homes in Ardabil.
Salon air carried \textbf{99.4$\pm$4.1 versus 36.6$\pm$3.1\,MPs\,m$^{-3}$} at home, with
nasal lavage 12.3--23.8 versus 9.3--20.1\,MP\,mL$^{-1}$ and estimated daily exposure
