\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Water is the day's unifying artefact --- and one paper
puts it inside the \emph{reference} method, not just the low-cost sensor.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Six records this issue are about particle-bound water, and they stack into an argument.
Chyzhykov et al.\ (\textit{J Hazard Mater}) measured retained water directly by
Karl~Fischer coulometry with stepwise heating across 35 materials and an artificial-PM
mixture: deliquescent inorganic salts retain most of it, water-to-solute ratio
\emph{falls} as filter loading rises, and PM-bound water releases across a thermal
continuum rather than at a threshold. \textbf{Gravimetric PM mass --- the quantity every
low-cost sensor is calibrated against --- is therefore composition-dependently biased by
an operational definition of ``dry''.} Against that, Guo et al.\ (\textit{ACS ES\&T Air})
attack the sensor side mechanistically, separating aerosol liquid water with EPA CSN
speciation and correcting optics by Mie theory rather than fitting an RH polynomial;
Kumpika et al.\ do fit the polynomial (PMS7003, $R^2 = 0.974$ against BAM) and find that
\textbf{restricting duty cycle to 20\,min/day materially slows drift}; Oybek et al.\
bound the raw penalty in a chamber --- PM$_{2.5}$ error rising from
\textbf{1.2 to $\sim$9\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} between 30\% and 85\% RH. Ginsburg et al.\ then show
the consequence at scale: across 1{,}013 co-located PurpleAir units on six continents,
US-derived corrections \emph{do not transfer}, and the authors attribute this specifically
to RH sensitivity and aerosol-composition variability. The practical reading for a
distributed network is uncomfortable: an RH correction fitted against a gravimetric
reference is partly fitting the reference's own water artefact, and that artefact is
composition-specific --- which is exactly what changes when you move the network.
\end{keybox}

% -------------------------------------------------------------- exec brief
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Calibration transferability now has numbers attached to distance, not just
to geography.} Zhivkov et al.\ derive pollutant-specific uncertainty growth rates of
\textbf{3.84--5.62\% per km} from a reference station by leave-one-station-out spatial
CV over 24 months in Sofia, and convert them into a siting rule: co-location sites
\textbf{6\,km apart} keep the midpoint penalty under 30\%. This is the first record in
this watch that gives a defensible answer to ``how far apart can the anchors be'' rather
than asserting that co-location is necessary.

\item \textbf{The high-exposure metro cohort came back close to null, and it matters.}
Thies et al.\ estimate most London Underground commuters encounter hourly PM$_{2.5}$
above 100\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ --- 8--10$\times$ roadside --- yet find only a
\textbf{1.93\% (0.58--3.66) survival deficit} versus above-ground rail users, negligible
cancer difference, \textbf{no dose--response}, and no meaningful benefit from a
hypothetical 50\% reduction. Either iron-rich mechanical-abrasion PM is substantially
less toxic per unit mass than the ambient mixture, or the modelled cumulative exposure
metric is too noisy to carry a gradient. Both readings are damaging to mass-only
exposure surrogates.

\item \textbf{Two independent groups attack the same station-density bias in
satellite-derived PM$_{2.5}$.} Ding et al.\ show that models trained on urban-skewed
monitor networks systematically overestimate rural concentrations --- correcting it drops
clean-site RMSE from \textbf{18.18 to 8.31\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} and national mean PM$_{2.5}$ by
$\sim$55\%, with the attributable-mortality burden in low-density areas revised down
accordingly. Hu et al.\ approach the gap differently, filling AOD with WRF-Chem and
injecting physics-derived features to reach hourly 1\,km ($R^2 = 0.87$,
RMSE 13.42\,$\mu$g/m$^3$). Both are arguments that the training-set geography, not the
algorithm, is the binding constraint.

\item \textbf{Near-source attribution improved on both the statistical and the
deterministic side.} Burke et al.\ re-grid nonparametric trajectory analysis onto polar
coordinates, raising estimated maximum near-road contributions by \textbf{22\% for BC and
39\% for NO$_x$} --- the previous Cartesian grid was smoothing away the near-field. Yu
et al.\ resolve the street-canyon increment citywide for Hong Kong and find hotspot
segments are only \textbf{12.2--18.8\%} of roads per district but expose $\sim$184{,}000
residents in winter, with $\Delta$C rising 19--26\% from summer to winter.

\item \textbf{EPA shipped tooling, not a paper.} Barkjohn et al.\ describe an R-Shiny
application for sensor-versus-reference comparison and baseline QA/QC --- explicitly not a
correction method, but the kind of artefact that changes what a small group can operate.

\item \textbf{Retraction to note.} \textit{Sci Total Environ} retracted ``Abundance and
origin of fine particulate chloride in continental China'' (2018;624:1041--1051),
\texttt{10.1016/\allowbreak j.scitotenv.\allowbreak 2026.182121}. Check bibliographies.
\end{itemize}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 13 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Chyzhykov et al.\ 2026}
{Particle-bound water and the composition dependence of gravimetric PM mass}
{J Hazard Mater}
{Karl Fischer coulometric titration with stepwise heating on 35 single-component
chemicals and CRMs at two filter loadings (0.6 and 2.0\,mg, summer- and winter-like),
plus a compositionally realistic artificial PM mixture built both by sequential addition
and in bulk. Deliquescent inorganic salts retain the most water; metals, metal oxides,
PAHs and graphitic carbons sit near background. Water-to-solute molar ratio falls as
absolute loading rises --- compacted powder is less vapour-accessible --- and
temperature-resolved release clusters into three material groups differing mainly in
strongly bound (250\,$^\circ$C) water. Bulk artificial-PM release profiles matched
ambient PM$_1$ filters. \textbf{Limitation: powders are not ambient particles}; mixing
state, morphology and coating history are not reproduced, so the transfer to real filters
is argued from profile similarity, not demonstrated. For distributed sensing this is the
uncomfortable upstream result --- the mass reference itself carries a
composition-dependent water term, so an empirical RH correction fitted against it is
partly correcting the reference.}
{10.1016/j.jhazmat.2026.143142}

\paperentry{Ginsburg et al.\ 2026}
{Do US PurpleAir corrections travel? 1{,}013 co-located sensors on six continents}
{Aerosol Sci Technol}
{Global evaluation dataset, January 2023--March 2025, of PurpleAir units co-located
within 500\,m of reference-grade monitors, assessed at 24-h and 1-h averaging on
$R^2$, RMSE, nRMSE, precision (CV) and AQI category correctness (24-h and NowCast).
Raw PurpleAir overestimates PM$_{2.5}$ in most regions and fails EPA sensor performance
targets outright. The three widely used US correction equations improve accuracy but
\textbf{transfer poorly outside North America}; a global quadratic helps in Australia and
New Zealand and hurts elsewhere; \textbf{regional calibrations consistently win}, meeting
EPA 24-h thresholds in several regions and lifting 24-h AQI correctness to
$\geq$77\%. \textbf{Limitation: 500\,m is a generous co-location radius}, so some of the
residual regional error is spatial mismatch rather than sensor bias, and the regional
strata are unevenly populated. Directly actionable --- it says region-specific correction
is mandatory wherever a co-location exists, and quantifies what is lost when it does not.}
{10.1080/02786826.2026.2676293}

\paperentry{Zhivkov et al.\ 2026}
{How fast does a calibration decay with distance from its reference station?}
{Atmosphere}
{Twenty-four months of hourly data (Aug 2023--Jul 2025) from Sofia: five official
reference stations and 22 AirThings low-cost units, four co-located. Random Forest
calibration achieved $R^2$ 0.53--0.75 across PM$_{2.5}$, PM$_{10}$, NO$_2$ and O$_3$,
improving on multiple linear regression by 40\% (O$_3$) to 408\% (PM$_{2.5}$).
Leave-one-station-out spatial cross-validation yields pollutant-specific uncertainty
growth rates $\alpha$ of \textbf{3.84--5.62\% per km} (significant for PM$_{10}$ and
O$_3$ only, $p<0.05$), applied to 18 non-co-located sensors to generate 1.2\,M calibrated
hourly values with 95\% prediction intervals. \textbf{Limitation: $\alpha$ is estimated
from five stations in one city}, so its significance for PM$_{2.5}$ is not established
and the linear-in-distance form is an assumption. Still the most directly usable network
design result in the window: 6\,km anchor spacing keeps the midpoint uncertainty increase
below 30\%.}
{10.3390/atmos17040335}

\paperentry{Guo et al.\ 2026}
{Mechanistic RH correction by separating aerosol liquid water from dry mass}
{ACS ES\&T Air}
{Two-stage framework for a nephelometer plus optical particle counter: (i) Mie-theory
optical calibration accounting for size distribution, refractive index and hygroscopic
growth; (ii) quantification of aerosol liquid water mass from EPA Chemical Speciation
Network composition to recover dry-equivalent PM$_{2.5}$ at high RH. Corrected values
agree closely with EPA reference measurements, and the recovered ALW is itself useful for
aqueous-phase aerosol chemistry. Practical guidance is given for sites without co-located
CSN speciation. \textbf{Limitation: the method is only as good as the speciation
surrogate} --- outside the CSN footprint the ALW term becomes an assumption, and the paper
does not report how much accuracy is lost when the surrogate is wrong. This is the
right-shaped answer to the humidity problem for anyone unwilling to refit an empirical
curve at every new site.}
{10.1021/acsestair.5c00225}

\paperentry{Yaqoob et al.\ 2026}
{Calibration that survives a change of city and a change of reference instrument}
{IEEE Access}
{PurpleAir and Alphasense OPC-N3 in Antwerp, Oslo and Zagreb, hourly PM$_{2.5}$ and
PM$_{10}$, calibrated with Random Forest, a compact feedforward network, and HybriCal-AQ
(a hybrid NN taking a standardised out-of-fold RF hint). Models are evaluated separately
against TEOM, FIDAS and generic reference grades to mirror real reference availability,
with a train-first split/impute/encode pipeline to avoid leakage. HybriCal-AQ wins on
PM$_{2.5}$ (Oslo-FIDAS/PurpleAir RMSE 1.79\,$\to$\,1.18, $R^2$ 0.94\,$\to$\,0.97) while
\textbf{plain RF usually remains better for PM$_{10}$} (Antwerp 4.76 vs 6.58). A
sparse-reference test withholding 25\% of reference observations probes robustness.
\textbf{Limitation: three cities is not a transferability test} --- every model is still
trained and evaluated within-city, so this bounds instrument sensitivity, not spatial
generalisation. The explicit reference-instrument stratification is the transferable
methodological idea.}
{10.1109/access.2026.3656120}

\paperentry{Kumpika et al.\ 2026}
{Humidity and ageing corrected together, and duty cycle as a drift control}
{Environ Pollut Bioavail}
{PMS7003 units in Chiang Mai across varying RH, validated against a government BAM and
verified in dry air with a diffusion dryer. Polynomial corrections up to third order gave
strong agreement at elevated humidity (\textbf{$R^2 = 0.9737$}). The distinctive result is
operational rather than statistical: \textbf{limiting operation to 20\,minutes/day
measurably reduced long-term performance drift} relative to continuous running.
\textbf{Limitations: single site, single sensor model, single aerosol regime}
(Chiang Mai's biomass-burning season is compositionally atypical), and the duty-cycle
finding is confounded with total particle throughput --- a sensor that samples less sees
less mass through its optics, so ``ageing'' and ``fouling'' are not separated. Worth
replicating; if it holds, duty cycling is a nearly free lifetime extension for
battery-constrained nodes.}
{10.1080/26395940.2026.2616098}

\paperentry{Qian et al.\ 2026}
{AutoML calibration staged through an intermediate drift-reference sensor}
{Atmos Meas Tech}
{Multi-stage indoor calibration framework linking deployed low-cost sensors to
intermediate drift-correction reference sensors and finally to a reference-grade
instrument, with separate models fitted for clean and pollution-event concentration
ranges. Evaluated in a controlled chamber on two sensor models under diverse indoor
sources and uncontrolled ambient conditions: \textbf{$R^2 > 0.90$}, NRMSE and sMAPE
roughly halved against uncalibrated data, bias effectively removed. \textbf{Limitation:
chamber conditions and an indoor concentration range}; the two-regime split is fitted, not
physically motivated, and nothing establishes that the intermediate-reference tier is
worth its cost against direct periodic co-location. The tiered-reference architecture is
nonetheless the interesting part --- it is a plausible answer to maintaining a large
network with few reference-grade instruments.}
{10.5194/amt-19-603-2026}

\paperentry{Oybek et al.\ 2026}
{Chamber bounds on the temperature and humidity penalty, by sensor chemistry}
{Am J Appl Sci Technol}
{Controlled-chamber factorial over six temperature (15--35\,$^\circ$C) and relative
humidity (30--90\%) combinations for an optical PM$_{2.5}$ counter, a metal-oxide NO$_2$
sensor and an electrochemical CO$_2$ sensor. PM$_{2.5}$ error rises from
\textbf{1.2\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ at 30\% RH to $\sim$9\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ at 85\% RH}; a simple
linear correction recovers up to 74\% of it. \textbf{Limitations are substantial}: one
unit per chemistry, no replication, an unstated test aerosol, a venue with no visible
peer-review record, and a linear correction applied to a process (hygroscopic growth) that
is not linear. Carried as a magnitude anchor only --- the number is consistent with better
studies and useful for sizing an error budget, but nothing here should be cited as a
correction method.}
{10.37547/ajast/volume06issue05-25}

\paperentry{Yan 2026}
{What 90+ ML calibration studies actually agree on}
{Sensor Review}
{Structured review of more than 90 peer-reviewed studies (2013--early 2024) across
optical, electrochemical, metal-oxide and NDIR sensors and across PM, O$_3$ and NO$_2$.
Consensus findings: ML calibration cuts error by $>$50\% and lifts $R^2$ above 0.8--0.9;
\textbf{optical PM sensors post-calibration cluster at $R^2$ 0.8--0.95 with slopes near
unity under long-term co-location}, while electrochemical NO$_2$/O$_3$ sit at 0.6--0.9
with much larger site-to-site spread. Neural networks and gradient boosting beat simple
regression when data are plentiful; regression is more stable when they are not.
Persistent problems named: drift, absent standard protocols, poor cross-site
generalisation, and regulator resistance to black-box models. \textbf{Limitation: the
search ends in early 2024} and the review is narrative in its weighting, with no
quantitative pooling. Useful as a citation anchor for the claim that transferability, not
fit, is the open problem.}
{10.1108/sr-10-2025-0761}

\paperentry{Burke et al.\ 2026}
{Polar-grid nonparametric trajectory analysis recovers near-field source structure}
{Atmos Environ}
{Nonparametric trajectory analysis combines continuous concentrations with local-scale
back trajectories from wind speed and direction to map source-area impact. The regular
Cartesian grid plus kernel smoothing used previously suppresses spatial variability
exactly where a near-source monitor needs it; re-gridding onto polar coordinates gives
finer resolution close to the site. Tested on one year of continuous data from a
near-road network site in St.\ Louis. Areas nearest the roadway contributed most
(1.4\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ BC, 39\,ppb NO$_x$), and \textbf{polar NTA raised maximum estimated
contributions by 22\% (BC) and 39\% (NO$_x$)}. Background-subtracted PM$_{2.5}$ tracked
the traffic-related species but also revealed nearby point-source contributions.
\textbf{Limitation: one site, one year, no independent validation} of the recovered source
field --- the improvement is demonstrated as internal consistency, not against known
emissions. Immediately relevant to siting and to interpreting single-node data in a
sparse network.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.121951}

\paperentry{Barkjohn et al.\ 2026}
{An R-Shiny application for sensor-versus-reference QA/QC}
{Atmosphere}
{Tool paper describing an application that lets air-quality agencies (i) assess sensor
data quality against nearby regulatory reference monitors, (ii) apply baseline QA/QC and
(iii) characterise local conditions, with reproducibility as an explicit design goal.
Demonstrated on two cases: PM$_{2.5}$ sensor performance under Midwestern wildfire smoke,
and a year of O$_3$ sensor performance. The authors are explicit that advanced correction
remains out of scope. \textbf{Limitation: no new method and no quantitative evaluation of
the tool itself} --- the case studies illustrate rather than validate. Carried because
availability, not novelty, is what determines whether a small group's sensor data ever
gets screened at all; this lowers that barrier and standardises the first pass.}
{10.3390/atmos16111270}

\paperentry{Reis 2026}
{A low-power node that does its own inference and stops talking so much}
{Sensors}
{Design and system-level optimisation of an embedded node integrating PM, gas,
temperature and humidity sensing on a low-power microcontroller with a neural inference
accelerator, using adaptive duty-cycling, hierarchical power domains and edge-level data
reduction. On-device quantised fixed-point models perform three-class normal/anomalous/
critical classification. Reported: \textbf{94\% inference accuracy, 0.87\,ms latency,
$\sim$2.9\,mWh average power}, and an adaptive LoRaWAN strategy cutting transmissions by
$\approx$88\% versus periodic reporting, enabling solar-battery autonomy.
\textbf{Critical limitation: every number comes from MATLAB/Simulink simulation}, not a
deployed node --- so the accuracy figure describes a synthetic label distribution and the
power figure omits the real cost of sensor warm-up, radio retries and thermal effects.
Read as an architecture proposal, not a measurement.}
{10.3390/s26020703}

\paperentry{Nimmala et al.\ 2026}
{Drone plus ground sampling with Lagrangian inversion for emission rate}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Release--recapture field studies with 200 transgenic switchgrass plants (two promoter
constructs, constitutive and pollen-specific) at the centre of a clear-cut field. Pollen
was sampled with fixed ground and drone-mounted devices at several distances while
Lagrangian stochastic dispersal simulations were driven by high-resolution wind
measurements; emission rate was inferred by combining simulated and measured
concentrations. Emission showed strong diurnal structure, positively correlated with wind
speed, temperature and vapour pressure deficit and negatively with RH. \textbf{In
low-wind meandering conditions, letting wind direction vary within the dispersal model
materially improved emission-rate recovery} --- the standard steady-direction assumption
fails exactly when dispersion is weakest and concentrations highest. \textbf{Limitation:
bioaerosol, one site, one season}; transfer to combustion PM is by analogy. The
meandering result is the portable one for mobile and UAV-based PM sampling.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15632-3}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 8 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Ding et al.\ 2026}
{Station density is the bias: urban--rural balanced PM$_{2.5}$ estimation}
{Geo-spatial Inf Sci}
{Satellite AOD-based PM$_{2.5}$ retrieval is trained on ground networks that are heavily
urban, and the resulting models overestimate rural concentrations. This framework
integrates AOD, reanalysis fields and ground measurements inside an ML model guided by
data-assimilation principles, adaptively constraining non-urban estimates with
low-weighted reanalysis samples while preserving urban fidelity, then ensemble-averaging
over dynamically weighted background--observation settings. Applied to China 2015--2021 it
cut clean-site RMSE from \textbf{18.18 to 8.31\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, lowered the national mean
PM$_{2.5}$ by \textbf{$\sim$55\%}, and substantially corrected overestimated
PM$_{2.5}$-attributable mortality in low-density areas, revealing a sharper urban--rural
decline gradient than previously reported. \textbf{Limitation: the rural ``truth'' is
partly reanalysis}, so the correction is validated against a small number of genuinely
clean sites and the 55\% national revision rests on that thin anchor. If it survives
replication it is a large downward correction to a widely cited burden estimate.}
{10.1080/10095020.2026.2624335}

\paperentry{Hu et al.\ 2026}
{Hourly 1\,km seamless PM$_{2.5}$ by filling AOD gaps with a chemical transport model}
{Geo-spatial Inf Sci}
{Rather than stacking ground, satellite and CTM outputs, this framework first builds
seamless AOD by merging WRF-Chem AOD with satellite retrievals, then injects
physics-informed features --- the temporal component of PM$_{2.5}$ and the vertical
structure of AOD from WRF-Chem --- into a geospatially aware LightGBM that also exploits
monitoring-site spatial structure. Site-based cross-validation gives \textbf{$R^2$ 0.87,
RMSE 13.42\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, MAE 8.68\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, with day-and-night 1\,km hourly
coverage demonstrated through a pollution episode. \textbf{Limitation: site-based CV
overstates performance for locations far from any monitor}, which is precisely where
seamless products are used; no leave-region-out test is reported. The gap-filling-first
ordering is the transferable design choice --- it removes the missing-AOD selection effect
that biases most fusion products toward clear-sky, low-pollution conditions.}
{10.1080/10095020.2026.2680855}

\paperentry{Yu et al.\ 2026}
{Street-canyon increment to near-road NO$_2$, resolved for a whole city}

\paperentry{Kaur et al.\ 2026}
