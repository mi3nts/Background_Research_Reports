\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A three-year, ten-node PurpleAir network anchored to
collocated reference monitors is now the pollution baseline for a capital city ---
the distributed low-cost architecture doing the job a regulatory network was never
built to do.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Shlipak et al.\ (\textit{ACS EST Air}) report April 2022--March 2025 of continuous PM$_{2.5}$
from \textbf{10 PurpleAir sensors across Addis Ababa}, calibrated against Beta Attenuation
Monitors collocated at two contrasting U.S.\ Embassy sites, plus black carbon from
AethLabs MA350 microaethalometers at those same two sites. Network-mean PM$_{2.5}$ was
\textbf{30\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, exceeding the WHO 24-hour guideline of 15\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ on
\textbf{99\% of days}; BC was 3.7 and 7.7\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ at the two BC sites, an
\emph{intra-urban} factor of two that a single-site regulatory monitor would have erased.
Aethalometer-model apportionment put \textbf{94\% of BC on fossil fuel} and 6\% on biomass
burning --- inverting the assumption that a sub-Saharan African capital's carbon is
predominantly biomass. Two design choices are worth stealing: the two-site BAM anchor
(rather than one) constrains the calibration across contrasting source regimes, and
tying the deployment to the forthcoming MAIA satellite gives the surface network a
defined validation role rather than leaving it as a standalone dataset. For distributed
low-cost PM work this is the strongest existing-practice reference published this window.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{This is an atmospheric-science issue, not an epidemiology one.} 14 of 18
records measure particles rather than people, and \textbf{exactly one} carries a human
health endpoint. \textbf{No record in the window published a confidence-interval-bearing
risk estimate}, so the forest plot is omitted rather than populated with chamber
fold-changes and model fit statistics --- see \textit{Corpus analytics}.

\item \textbf{Two independent papers attack the same measurement gap: source resolution
without a mass-only monitor.} Shlipak et al.\ separate fossil from biomass BC by
aethalometer model in Addis Ababa; Saarikoski et al.\ (\textit{ACP}) show at a Helsinki
traffic site that relying on hydrocarbon-like OA alone to estimate traffic primary OA
\textbf{underestimates it by $\sim$50\%}, because a second, more oxygenated
traffic-related factor (distinct C$_2$H$_4$O$_2^+$, C$_2$H$_5$O$_2^+$, C$_3$H$_5$O$_2^+$
fractions) also peaks with the morning rush. Any source-apportionment product built on
an HOA-only convention inherits that bias.

\item \textbf{Wildfire smoke is being reframed as a joint-pollutant problem.}
Yu et al.\ (\textit{ES\&T}) fit a spatiotemporal Bayesian neural field over 2004--2023
CONUS at 5\,km to estimate daily probabilities of \emph{co-occurring} PM$_{2.5}$ and ozone
extremes, and find the amplified co-occurrence risk propagating into the Midwest and
Northeast on long-range transport. Two monitors, not one, is the implied network
requirement.

\item \textbf{Personal exposure and neighbourhood proxies diverge sharply, and the
divergence is quantified.} Zundel et al.\ (medRxiv preprint) report personal PM$_{2.5}$
of 28.2$\pm$20.0 vs 5.1$\pm$4.7\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ on smoke vs non-smoke days against
neighbourhood PurpleAir estimates of 87.6$\pm$80.2 vs 12.3$\pm$7.0 --- personal
concentrations were \emph{lower} than the outdoor proxy but rose fivefold, with a
within-person slope of only \textbf{2.3\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} of
neighbourhood PM$_{2.5}$. Four participants; treat the slope as an order of magnitude,
not an estimate.

\item \textbf{Aerosol phase state is becoming a tractable field measurement.}
Do et al.\ (\textit{ES\&T}) measured RH-dependent viscosity of ambient PM$_{2.5}$ by
poke-and-flow (6.2$\times$10$^4$--1.3$\times$10$^7$\,Pa\,s at 20--40\% RH) and propagated
it to nighttime N$_2$O$_5$ uptake coefficients of 0.01--0.07 --- up to an order of magnitude
below the liquid-particle assumption of 0.1 that chemical transport models still use.

\item \textbf{Metadata-only deposits are now a fixed 20--25\% tax.} 4 of 18 today
(Elsevier \textit{Atmos Environ} $\times$2, \textit{Atmos Res}, Springer Nature
\textit{JESEE}). Semantic Scholar and Europe PMC returned nothing for all four; they are
too new. They ship with an explicit \textsc{metadata only} marker and no inferred
quantity.

\item \textbf{Screening-side note.} 27 of the 49 exclusions came from a single journal
(MDPI \textit{Sensors}) whose ISSN was added to the Crossref sweep this run and returned
\textbf{zero} in-scope records --- it indexes sensing hardware of every kind, not airborne
particle instrumentation. Recorded for the next sweep-list revision.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 4 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Shlipak et al.\ 2026}
{Spatial and temporal trends of ambient PM$_{2.5}$ and source-apportioned black carbon in Addis Ababa}
{ACS EST Air}
{Three continuous years (Apr 2022--Mar 2025) from the MAIA surface network: 10 PurpleAir
units across the city calibrated against collocated Beta Attenuation Monitors at two
contrasting U.S.\ Embassy sites (Central, Jacros), plus AethLabs MA350 microaethalometers
at those two sites. Network mean PM$_{2.5}$ \textbf{30\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}; BC 3.7 and
7.7\,$\mu$g/m$^3$; the WHO 24\,h guideline of 15\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ was exceeded on
\textbf{99\% of days}. Diurnal peaks at morning traffic and under nighttime inversion,
strongest in the wet season; highest concentrations west of the city centre.
Aethalometer-model apportionment attributes \textbf{94\% of BC to fossil combustion},
6\% to biomass --- the opposite of the usual prior for the region. Directional and
back-trajectory analysis raises long-range shipping transport from the Gulf of Aden as a
regional contributor. Limitation: only two sites carry a reference anchor and BC, so the
calibration transfer across the other eight nodes is assumed rather than demonstrated.
The single most directly transferable deployment design in this window.}
{10.1021/acsestair.6c00140}

\paperentry{Zundel et al.\ 2026 (preprint)}
{Wearable personal PM$_{2.5}$ monitoring during wildfire smoke episodes in Metro Detroit}
{medRxiv}
{AirBeam3 wearables on four participants (one adolescent, three adults) during southeast
Michigan smoke episodes, with neighbourhood outdoor PM$_{2.5}$ taken as the mean of the
three nearest PurpleAir units and smoke days identified from state advisories.
\textbf{Personal 28.2$\pm$20.0 vs 5.1$\pm$4.7\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} on smoke vs non-smoke days;
neighbourhood \textbf{87.6$\pm$80.2 vs 12.3$\pm$7.0}. Within participants, each
10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ of neighbourhood PM$_{2.5}$ mapped to only \textbf{2.3\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}
personal --- personal exposure is both attenuated and poorly tracked by the outdoor proxy,
yet still put $>$25\% of participant-days over the EPA 24\,h standard. $n=4$ and no
formal uncertainty on the slope: this is a feasibility demonstration, and the attenuation
factor should not be reused as a calibration constant. Preprint, not peer reviewed.}
{10.64898/2026.07.30.26359253}

\paperentry{Clark \& Bashar 2026}
{How IoT enables the Protective Action Decision Model during a wildfire event}
{Risk Anal}
{Survey of 1{,}200 Oregon residents (Jun--Aug 2021) about their responses to the 2020
fire season, framed against the Protective Action Decision Model and the availability of
hyper-local real-time PM$_{2.5}$ from IoT devices. The finding that matters for network
designers is negative: residents were \textbf{overwhelmed by the data and reported
inadequate guidance} on how to act on it. Retrospective self-report a year after the
event, and the survey does not establish who actually had sensor access --- so this
constrains interface and messaging design rather than measuring sensor efficacy. Still
the rare paper in this watch that treats the last mile of a dense network as the
problem.}
{10.1111/risa.70319}

\paperentry{Wei et al.\ 2026}
{Stacked Transformer-XGBoost model with SHAP interpretability for AQI prediction in Linfen}
{iScience}
{Transformer temporal encoder stacked with XGBoost through temporally consistent
out-of-fold stacking, on 2013--2024 air quality and meteorological series from a heavily
industrialised Chinese city. Reported \textbf{$R^2=0.955$, RMSE 9.05, MAE 2.91}; SHAP
attributes the variance to PM$_{2.5}$, NO$_2$, lagged AQI and seasonal terms. The
performance claim is against conventional ML and DL baselines on the same series, with
no external site held out, so it is an in-sample-city result. The out-of-fold stacking
discipline is the part worth borrowing --- naive stacking on autocorrelated air quality
series leaks badly.}
{10.1016/j.isci.2026.116396}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 10 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Yu et al.\ 2026}
{Probabilistic assessment of PM$_{2.5}$ and ozone co-occurring extremes under wildfire smoke}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Spatiotemporal Bayesian neural field (ST-BayesNF) integrating satellite smoke-coverage
products, ground PM$_{2.5}$ and ozone, and meteorological reanalysis to give daily
probabilities of \emph{joint} extremes at 5\,km over the contiguous US, 2004--2023. The
model discriminates co-extreme from non-co-extreme days and is most sensitive to smoke
density interacting with boundary-layer height and near-surface temperature; predicted
co-occurrence risk rises during smoke events not only in the western US but increasingly
in the Midwest and Northeast under long-range transport. No health outcome is modelled ---
the health premise (co-occurrence raises risk beyond either pollutant alone) is imported
from prior literature and not tested here.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c04403}

\paperentry{Saarikoski et al.\ 2026}
{Role of hydrocarbons and oxygenated organic species in traffic-derived aerosol}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Four Aerodyne AMS campaigns (2018--2024) at a Helsinki traffic site with PMF source
apportionment. Beyond hydrocarbon-like OA peaking at the morning rush, a second
traffic-related factor (TrOA) peaks in the morning but stays elevated longer, with mass
spectra between HOA and biomass-burning OA and distinct C$_2$H$_4$O$_2^+$ ($m/z$ 60),
C$_2$H$_5$O$_2^+$ (61) and C$_3$H$_5$O$_2^+$ (73) fractions. \textbf{Using HOA alone to
estimate traffic primary OA underestimates it by $\sim$50\%.} The authors are explicit
that TrOA's origin --- atmospheric processing versus direct emission from modern
vehicles --- is unresolved, which is exactly the ambiguity that makes the 50\% figure a
bound rather than a correction factor. Directly relevant to any speciated or
source-resolved sensing product.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11817-2026}

\paperentry{Do et al.\ 2026}
{Field-constrained diurnal viscosity of urban PM$_{2.5}$ and implications for N$_2$O$_5$ uptake}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Poke-and-flow measurement with fluid-dynamics inversion on PM$_{2.5}$ collected in Ansan,
South Korea (summer 2024): \textbf{6.2$\times$10$^4$--1.3$\times$10$^7$\,Pa\,s at
20--40\% RH}, exceeding 10$^8$\,Pa\,s below $\sim$10\% RH. Pooled with Seoul and Beijing
data into a Vogel--Tammann--Fulcher temperature--RH parametrisation and pushed through an
ML surrogate over hourly meteorology for 14 megacities. Predicted viscosity is
systematically lower at night everywhere (RH plasticisation beating nocturnal cooling),
giving nighttime N$_2$O$_5$ uptake coefficients of \textbf{0.01--0.07} against the
conventional liquid assumption of 0.1. Weakness stated by the authors: the
parametrisation rests on 24\,h composition, so no time-resolved chemistry enters the
diurnal prediction it is being used to make.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c04811}

\paperentry{Wang Q et al.\ 2026}
{Quantitative analysis of aerosol acidity and its driving factors in the Guanzhong area}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{One year of water-soluble inorganic ions in PM$_{2.5}$ on the semi-arid Guanzhong Plain,
with thermodynamic pH reconstruction and sensitivity-based driver attribution. Annual
mean pH \textbf{3.8$\pm$1.0} (winter $>$ spring $>$ summer $>$ autumn), shifting from 2--5
on clean days toward 3--6 on polluted days. Temperature contributes 22.3--33.8\% of pH
variation across all seasons, NH$_x$ 11.4--44.9\% (dominant in autumn/winter), sulfate
8.5--10.8\%; Ca$^{2+}$ is a spring-specific driver. RH shows a non-monotonic effect with
an inflection at 60--85\% RH, attributed to deliquescence-driven liquid water content.
Attribution rests on a thermodynamic model rather than measured pH, which is the standing
caveat for this whole literature.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11785-2026}

\paperentry{Kommula et al.\ 2026}
{Comparable organic aerosol mass enhancement during photochemical and dark oxidation of biomass burning emissions}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Chamber aging of emissions from savannah grass, savannah wood and boreal forest surface
fuel under varied combustion conditions, comparing daytime photochemical with nighttime
dark oxidation. OA is 82--99\% of PM$_1$ in the fresh emissions. Both aging routes yield
\textbf{comparable net OA mass}, which PMF traces to more efficient primary-OA loss during
daytime offsetting more efficient gas-phase oxidation. The resulting mass enhancement of
\textbf{0.75--1.3$\times$} matches field observations and closes a long-standing
lab--field discrepancy. Applies to low-NO$_x$ remote conditions; the authors say so, and
that is where the proposed model simplification is and is not safe.}
{10.1021/acs.est.5c15841}

\paperentry{Albugami 2026}
{Dust event characteristics, transport pathways and source regions over Riyadh}
{Atmosphere}
{34 dust events over 2024--2025 identified from AERONET by a dual optical criterion
(AOD$_{500}\geq$0.50 and 440--870\,nm {\AA}ngstr\"om exponent $\leq$0.50), cross-checked
against NOAA ISD present-weather and visibility and traced with HYSPLIT back-trajectories,
PSCF and CWT. \textbf{82\% fell in March--May}; mean {\AA}ngstr\"om exponent 0.28, mean
fine-mode fraction 0.26. Surface present-weather codes confirmed 26 of 34; the other
8 (24\%) were classified as elevated dust invisible at the surface station --- a clean
demonstration that column and surface instruments disagree by design, not by error.
Source--receptor analysis points to the Mesopotamian Basin, with the northern
Rub' al-Khali secondary. Single-author, single-station, two years.}
{10.3390/atmos17080802}

\paperentry{Ashta et al.\ 2026}
{Wet and dry atmospheric deposition of microplastics across a Swiss site gradient}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Four-weekly wet and dry deposition sampling over one year (May 2024--May 2025) at urban
(Zurich), suburban (D\"ubendorf), rural (Magadino, Payerne) and mountainous (Chaumont)
sites, with focal-plane-array $\mu$-FTIR over the 20--215\,$\mu$m range and an explicit
measurement-uncertainty treatment. Urban deposition \textbf{881 MPs\,m$^{-2}$\,d$^{-1}$
(95\% CI 562--1199)} and \textbf{53\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-2}$\,d$^{-1}$ (17--107)}; the other four
sites are similar to each other at 249--331 MPs\,m$^{-2}$\,d$^{-1}$ (140--478). Scaled to
land use, 219\,t or 3.8$\times$10$^{14}$ particles per year below 2000\,m nationally,
excluding tyre wear. The 20\,$\mu$m lower cut is the binding limitation: the
inhalable fraction sits mostly below it.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11803-2026}

\paperentry{Wang T et al.\ 2026}
{Chlorine radical cycling on photoactive particulate matter amplifies photolytic NO$_x$ release}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Bench photochemistry showing chloride on photoactive PM surfaces oxidised by
photogenerated holes to Cl$\bullet$, which accelerates conversion of nitrite intermediates
to gaseous NO$_x$ while chloride is regenerated. \textbf{$\sim$40-fold NO$_x$ enhancement}
under the model conditions, robust across humidity, chloride salt, substrate (including
soot) and nitrogen source (ammonium, 4-nitrophenol). Rate scales with photoactive-matter
loading and PM composition. Extrapolated to \textbf{0.7--6.2\% of NO$_2$ variability} in
the study region --- a small number derived from a large laboratory enhancement, and the
gap between the two is the result's main uncertainty.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c06768}

\paperentry{Naghizade et al.\ 2026}
{Multi-class organic marker profiling of urban PM$_{10}$ in Strasbourg}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- seasonal distribution of exhaust, non-exhaust and
biomass-burning tracers in northeastern France. No abstract in the Crossref deposit;
Semantic Scholar and Europe PMC both returned nothing on 20 August. No quantity is
inferred here. Flagged for the next issue that can reach the full record.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122306}

\paperentry{Chuang et al.\ 2026}
{Simulating PM$_{2.5}$ in southern Taiwan under high-pressure return flow (Kao-Ping Experiment)}
{Atmos Res}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- a KPEx modelling study of a synoptic regime that traps
PM$_{2.5}$ over the Kao-Ping region. No abstract in the deposit and no indexed full text
on 20 August. No quantity inferred.}
{10.1016/j.atmosres.2026.109286}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 1 record}{Sage}

\paperentry{Zhang S et al.\ 2026}
{Respiratory and cardiovascular effects of ozone modified by PM$_{2.5}$}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Two complementary designs testing one hypothesis: that 30-day mean PM$_{2.5}$
\emph{modifies} the acute cardiorespiratory response to ozone, and that unmodelled
modification is part of why the ozone literature is inconsistent. In a healthy-adult
panel, short-term ozone was associated with significantly larger decrements in FEV$_1$,
FEF$_{25-75}$ and PEF, and larger rises in systolic and diastolic blood pressure, under
\textbf{PM$_{2.5}$ above 22\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}. A meta-regression across published studies
agreed: study areas with higher annual PM$_{2.5}$ showed larger ozone effects on FEV$_1$
and blood pressure. \textbf{The abstract reports direction and significance but no
interval estimates}, which is why this record contributes nothing to the forest plot.
Panel size is not stated in the abstract either. Mechanistically the claim is priming
rather than co-exposure confounding, and the meta-regression cannot separate the two ---
areas with high PM$_{2.5}$ differ in many ways. Still the day's one health record, and
the modification framing is directly relevant to designing multi-pollutant sensor nodes.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c05604}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 1 record}{Clay}

\paperentry{Zhao et al.\ 2026}
{CFD-based prediction of air change rate and PM in a dynamically occupied operating room}
{Indoor Air}
{Class 10{,}000 operating room in Dalian (Chinese GB 50333-2013), combining field
measurement with CFD across 45 orthogonal scenarios, Sobol sensitivity, and three
optimisation routes (Bayesian, multi-objective, response surface) to calibrate a
non-uniformity coefficient and fit ACH--occupancy--PM. Response surface gave the lowest
prediction error (\textbf{RMSE 0.032, MAE 0.024}); multi-objective optimisation was the
most stable across runs ($C_v$ 0.005). The premise is sound --- fixed as-built ACH
standards ignore occupancy dynamics --- but the model is calibrated on one room in one
climate, and the PM metric is cleanroom particle count rather than a health-relevant
mass fraction.}
{10.1155/ina/5838379}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 1 record}{Coral}

\paperentry{Anastasopolos et al.\ 2026}
{Mortality attributed to ambient air pollution by anthropogenic emission sector in Canada}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- a comparative health-burden analysis run at multiple
