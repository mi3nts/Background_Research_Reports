\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Four independent records today attack the same
assumption from four directions: that a surface-sited, mass-reporting monitor measures the
exposure that matters. Source, height, composition and speciated dose each break it.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{Source.} Zhang et al.\ (\textit{Nat Commun}) separated wildfire-specific from
non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ across \textbf{20 US states, 2006--19}, in a self-controlled
design. Per 1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ raised cardiopulmonary hospitalisation
risk \textbf{10\% (heart failure) to 16\% (asthma)}; non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ raised it
\textbf{4.7\% (COPD) to 8.5\% (hypertension)}. Equal mass, unequal hazard, by roughly a
factor of two.
\textbf{Height.} Bai et al.\ (\textit{Atmos Meas Tech}) used Doppler wind lidar over
Hefei to show two dust episodes with opposite surface signatures: in one, a descending
low-level jet drove aerosol down and surface PM$_{10}$ peaked at
\textbf{410\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}; in the other, a stable jet core held the layer aloft and the
surface peak arrived \textbf{$\sim$8\,h} after frontal onset. A surface network reads
clean air while a loaded layer sits overhead.
\textbf{Composition.} Yu et al.\ (\textit{Atmos Chem Phys}) found marine organic-aerosol
light absorption over the Yellow and Bohai Seas rising with both organic-nitrogen
concentration \emph{and} ON/OC ratio --- absorption capability per unit carbon changes
with continental outflow, which is precisely the term a fixed optical calibration assumes
constant.
\textbf{Speciated dose.} Morrel et al.\ (\textit{Biol Psychiatry Glob Open Sci}), the
largest human study in the issue at \textbf{6,949 ABCD preadolescents}, finds the signal
in \emph{components} and not in mass: zinc, ammonium and bromine predicted perivascular-%
space count; calcium, zinc and ammonium predicted poorer cognition (FDR $p<0.01$); and
frontal-lobe perivascular-space count \textbf{mediated} the zinc--cognition association.
Total PM$_{2.5}$ entered only as a weighted-quantile-sum mixture, and never as the
quantity a monitor reports.
Only one of the four is a sensing paper. Together they are the strongest argument this
watch has carried that the next increment in exposure assessment is source-, height- and
composition-resolved, not denser mass.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A three-year reference-grade absorption record at a rural South Asian site.}
Raju et al.\ (\textit{J Environ Sci Health A}) report seven-wavelength AE33 aethalometry at
Panchgaon, Haryana, \textbf{January 2020 -- December 2022}: eBC \textbf{4.06\,$\pm$\,2.15},
fossil-fuel \textbf{3.10\,$\pm$\,2.40} and biomass-burning \textbf{1.42\,$\pm$\,1.19}\,%
$\mu$g/m$^3$, with eBCff carrying \textbf{68\%} of absorption at 880\,nm and winter maxima
roughly double the monsoon minima. This is the co-location anchor low-cost networks in the
region do not have. The apportionment itself is shakier than the record: the
eBCff/eBCbb ratio is \textbf{3.02\,$\pm$\,3.80} --- a standard deviation larger than the
mean --- and the assumed absorption \AA ngstr\"om exponents are never stated.
\item \textbf{A community-designed monitoring campaign that is a template, not a result.}
Frueh et al.\ (\textit{J Expo Sci Environ Epidemiol}) ran \textbf{38 VOCs at 20 sites for
12 months} in a South Philadelphia fenceline community with Philly Thrive, around a former
refinery. Benzene --- the pollutant the community named --- came in low
(\textbf{median 1.21\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}), while (\textit{m,p})-xylenes emerged in industrial
areas, and PMF apportioned sources. One-week integrated passive samplers resolve none of
the short plumes residents actually report, which is the design's own limit. The siting
logic --- community-set locations, a declared pollutant of concern, apportionment rather
than compliance --- is what distributed PM networks should copy.
\item \textbf{A humidity threshold that lands squarely on the sensor-correction problem.}
Sun et al.\ (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}) split wintertime basin nitrate formation almost
evenly between daytime OH+NO$_2$ (\textbf{52\%}) and nighttime N$_2$O$_5$ hydrolysis
(\textbf{46\%}), and locate abrupt efficiency thresholds at \textbf{RH 87\%} and
\textbf{ALWC 76.5\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}. That is the same humidity band in which optical low-cost
sensors diverge from reference monitors --- the aerosol is physically changing where the
instrument is least trustworthy. One site, one winter, no out-of-sample validation.
\item \textbf{A cardiovascular case-crossover in which every PM term is null.}
Chiang et al.\ (\textit{J Am Heart Assoc}) found no same-day association for any pollutant
across \textbf{798 acute aortic dissections}; only CO at 2-day lag survived
(\textbf{OR 2.13 per IQR, 95\% CI 1.075--4.239}). PM$_{2.5}$, PM$_{10}$, NO$_2$, O$_3$ and
SO$_2$ showed nothing at any lag. With six pollutants across four lags and no multiplicity
correction, the surviving association is fragile --- but the PM nulls are themselves
informative: exposure was assigned from \textbf{24 fixed stations}, a density at which
within-city contrast is largely erased.
\item \textbf{Eight of thirty records assert no finding at all.} Metadata-only is the
largest single design bucket in this issue, ahead of measurement campaigns at seven.
Same-day deposits from Elsevier, ACS, BMC and now Taylor \& Francis carry a title, authors
and a DOI and nothing else. A new recovery route was tested and \textbf{failed}: the
OpenAlex per-work endpoint (\texttt{/works/doi:}), which is free even though the
date-range filter is not, returns \texttt{abstract\_inverted\_index} only where the
publisher deposited an abstract to Crossref in the first place --- so it recovers nothing
these publishers withhold.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase. Records
marked \textit{metadata only} carry no recoverable abstract and assert no finding.\par}

\band{Neuro, cognition \& mental health\hfill 2 records}{Deep}

\paperentry{Temporao et al.\ 2026}
{Daily associations between outdoor air pollution and sleep quality}
{medRxiv (preprint)}
{Budapest Sleep, Traits and Experiences Study --- seven days per participant of daily
activity and sleep-quality reports plus home EEG giving sleep efficiency, \%REM, onset
latency and total sleep time. Linear mixed models with participant random intercepts,
adjusted for age, sex, BMI, education, weekday, season, temperature, physical activity,
screen exposure and travel. \textbf{Day-1 ozone predicted lower day-2 sleep efficiency
and poorer perceived quality}, surviving multiple-comparison correction, multi-pollutant
models and sensitivity analyses for depression, insomnia and chronotype;
\textbf{no robust association for PM$_{2.5}$ or NO$_2$}. The null is confounded with
exposure-model quality --- city-aggregated station data give near-zero within-city
contrast, and misclassification is worse for PM$_{2.5}$ than for the more spatially
homogeneous ozone. Sample size is not stated in the abstract.}
{10.64898/2026.09.15.26363130}

\paperentry{Morrel et al.\ 2026}
{Outdoor air pollution, perivascular space morphology, and cognition in preadolescence}
{Biol Psychiatry Glob Open Sci}
{Cross-sectional analysis of \textbf{6,949 ABCD participants aged 9--10}, with annual-average
PM$_{2.5}$, O$_3$, NO$_2$ and \textbf{15 PM$_{2.5}$ components} assigned to residential
addresses by spatiotemporal models, perivascular-space (PVS) count and volume from T1- and
T2-weighted MRI, and cognition from the NIH Toolbox. \textbf{Zinc, ammonium and bromine}
were positively associated with regional PVS count; higher count in five regions tracked
poorer cognition; \textbf{calcium, Zn and NH$_4^+$} were associated with poorer cognition
(FDR-corrected $p<0.01$); frontal-lobe PVS count \textbf{mediated} the Zn--total-cognition
association ($p<0.01$); and weighted-quantile-sum PM$_{2.5}$ mixtures predicted higher
temporal and cingulate PVS with poorer working memory and crystallised intelligence. The
design cannot order exposure, PVS and cognition, and the abstract reports significance
without a single effect size. The component-specific pattern is the measurement claim:
speciation, not mass, carries the signal.}
{10.1016/j.bpsgos.2026.100790}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 3 records}{Teal}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026}
{Cardiopulmonary hospitalisation risks from wildfire-specific and non-wildfire
PM$_{2.5}$ in 20 US states}
{Nat Commun}
{Self-controlled design over 20 states, 2006--19, contrasting two-year average
wildfire-specific against non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$. Per 1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, wildfire
PM$_{2.5}$ carried \textbf{10\% (heart failure) to 16\% (asthma)} higher hospitalisation
risk; non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ \textbf{4.7\% (COPD) to 8.5\% (hypertension)}. Associations
were stronger among minorities, metropolitan residents, the less educated and more
deprived communities. The toxicity contrast at equal mass is the contribution, and it
undercuts the mass-only metric every regulatory network and low-cost sensor reports. Two
limits: no confidence interval is given for any of the eight headline risks, so precision
cannot be judged; and wildfire-specific PM$_{2.5}$ is itself modelled, so the toxicity
difference is conditional on a smoke-attribution step whose error is not propagated.}
{10.1038/s41467-026-76974-7}

\paperentry{Chiang et al.\ 2026}
{Ambient carbon monoxide as a delayed trigger of acute aortic dissection: a time-stratified
case-crossover study in a tropical industrial metropolis}
{J Am Heart Assoc}
{Time-stratified case-crossover of \textbf{798 emergency-department-confirmed acute aortic
dissections}, 2018--2021, exposures assigned from \textbf{24 monitoring stations},
conditional logistic regression across lags 0--3 adjusted for meteorology. \textbf{No
pollutant was associated on the event day.} At 2-day lag each 1\,ppm CO increment carried a
2.67-fold risk (95\% CI 1.207--5.900) and each IQR increment of 0.19\,ppm raised risk
\textbf{113\% (95\% CI 1.075--4.239)}; \textbf{PM$_{2.5}$, PM$_{10}$, NO$_2$, O$_3$ and
SO$_2$ showed nothing at any lag}. Two problems: a 1\,ppm contrast is roughly five IQRs, so
the headline number extrapolates past the observed range; and with six pollutants across
four lags the one surviving association is not multiplicity-corrected. The PM nulls are
the relevant part here --- station-assigned exposure at this network density returns
nulls that a denser deployment might resolve.}
{10.1161/jaha.126.049936}

\paperentry{Fleming \& Fleming 2026 \textnormal{---} metadata only}
{Tire wear pollution and cardiovascular risk}
{Am J Med}
{Elsevier deposited title and authorship on 18 September with no abstract in Crossref,
Europe PMC core, Europe PMC \texttt{fullTextXML} or the OpenAlex per-work record. The title
places it as a commentary linking tire-wear particle pollution to cardiovascular risk; it
asserts no finding here. Logged because the particle class matters for measurement:
\textbf{non-exhaust emissions now dominate traffic PM in fleets with modern
aftertreatment}, and tire wear differs from combustion PM in both size distribution and
composition --- a combination that mass-only optical sensors and exhaust-derived emission
factors each mis-handle. Carried forward for pickup when an abstract appears.}
{10.1016/j.amjmed.2026.09.003}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 2 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{San Juan-Reyes et al.\ 2026}
{Short-term ambient air pollution and respiratory hospitalisation in children under five}
{Environ Sci Pollut Res Int}
{266 children under five admitted to one regional IMSS hospital in the Toluca Valley
Metropolitan Zone during 2023; matched case-crossover with conditional logistic
regression, primary window lag 1--7, adjusted for temperature and humidity. PM$_{2.5}$
was associated with admission for viral pneumonia, bacterial pneumonia and asthma, CO
with bronchiolitis, with heterogeneous municipality-level associations. \textbf{The
abstract reports direction only --- not one odds ratio, interval or unit appears} --- so
the effect size is unverifiable from the published summary. 266 events split across four
diagnoses, five pollutants, several lag windows and three municipalities is thin, and
exposure is entirely fixed-station with no personal or indoor component in a setting
where indoor sources plausibly dominate a young child's dose.}
{10.1007/s11356-026-38237-2}

\paperentry{Chen et al.\ 2026}
{Residential green and blue space, genetic susceptibility and incident COPD}
{Int J Chron Obstruct Pulmon Dis}
{385{,}502 UK Biobank participants, Cox models, greenness at 300\,m and 1000\,m buffers
against a COPD polygenic risk score. At 1000\,m: \textbf{green space HR 0.87
(95\% CI 0.83--0.92)}, \textbf{natural environment HR 0.83 (0.78--0.87)}; blue space weak
and inconsistent. No multiplicative or additive interaction with genetic risk, and the
natural-environment association held in every genetic stratum --- so the protective signal
is not running through a susceptible subgroup. The inferential problem is what is absent:
\textbf{no air-pollution term is reported}. Residential greenness is inversely correlated
with traffic PM$_{2.5}$ and NO$_2$ nearly everywhere, so an unadjusted greenness--COPD
association may simply restate a pollution--COPD association.}
{10.2147/copd.s627458}



\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 5 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Gao et al.\ 2026}
{CCL22-associated immune signatures in air pollution-related atopic dermatitis}
{Front Public Health}
{Shared pollutant--atopic dermatitis targets enriched in cytokine and chemokine
signalling, pattern-recognition receptor activity, IL-17 and Toll-like receptor pathways.
A framework over 15 algorithms and 175 combinations selected plsRglm\,+\,AdaBoost, with
AUC 0.963 in training and 1.000, 0.909 and 0.966 across three validation cohorts, giving
a ten-gene signature (CCL22, CCL5, CSF2, F2RL1, HRH4, ICAM1, IFNG, IL10, IL17A, IL18).
CCL22 was upregulated in lesional samples, localised to dendritic cells and macrophages,
and confirmed in DNFB-induced murine dermatitis. \textbf{The exposure link is the weak
joint: no particulate matter was measured or administered anywhere}, so air pollution
enters only as a database annotation. An AUC of exactly 1.000 indicates leakage or
overfitting rather than performance, and DNFB is a contact hapten, not a particle.}
{10.3389/fpubh.2026.1906298}

\paperentry{Vera-Col\'on et al.\ 2026 \textnormal{---} metadata only}
{Airborne fine particulate matter and human embryonic stem cell osteogenic
differentiation}
{Part Fibre Toxicol}
{BMC deposited this DOI on 18 September with no abstract; Crossref, Europe PMC core,
Europe PMC \texttt{fullTextXML} and the publisher landing page all returned nothing on
per-DOI retry --- \textbf{the first time this gap has appeared on a BMC rather than an
Elsevier deposit}. Carried on title because skeletal development is an endpoint the PM
literature has barely touched, and a human-embryonic-stem-cell osteogenic model would be
a new mechanistic line rather than another airway or endothelial assay. No finding is
asserted: neither particle source, dose, duration nor the direction of the reported
disruption is verifiable from metadata. On the retry list.}
{10.1186/s12989-026-00701-9}

\paperentry{Dulong et al.\ 2026}
{Effects of fine particulate matter on neuroblastoma cell line: metabolic and lipid
deregulations and their consequences on the onset of neurological diseases}
{Biochimie}
{Narrative review built around the \textbf{SH-SY5Y} human neuroblastoma line as an in vitro
neurodegeneration model. The proposed cascade runs mitochondrial damage
$\rightarrow$ ER stress $\rightarrow$ oxidative stress $\rightarrow$ calcium dyshomeostasis
$\rightarrow$ lipid dysregulation, converging on apoptosis and autophagy-dependent
\textbf{ferroptosis}, with lipid peroxidation advanced as the emerging driver. No pooled
estimate, no exposure--response and no dose metric: the review does not harmonise
PM$_{2.5}$ mass, source or exposure duration across the studies it assembles, and the in
vitro doses it draws on generally sit far above ambient, so the chain is qualitative. It
concedes its own in-vitro-to-human translation gap. Every mechanism it privileges is
\textbf{composition-dependent} --- redox-active metals, organic carbon --- which a
mass-only network cannot resolve.}
{10.1016/j.biochi.2026.09.011}

\paperentry{Wu et al.\ 2026}
{$\beta$-carotene alleviates PM$_{2.5}$-induced pulmonary fibrosis via suppression of the
Frizzled5-mediated Wnt5a/Ca$^{2+}$ pathway}
{Ecotoxicol Environ Saf}
{C57BL/6 mice given \textbf{intratracheal PM$_{2.5}$}, paired with BEAS-2B human bronchial
epithelial cells carrying Fzd5 overexpression. Instillation produced fibrosis, oxidative
stress and calcium dyshomeostasis --- more mitochondria-associated ER membranes, altered
IP$_3$R, STIM1, ORAI1 and SERCA2 --- with activation of a \textbf{Wnt5a/Fzd5/Ca$^{2+}$}
axis. Dietary $\beta$-carotene reversed these changes, and Fzd5 overexpression abolished
the protection, identifying Fzd5 as the mediating receptor. \textbf{No instilled dose,
particle source, treatment concentration, effect size or CI} is reported for any endpoint,
so potency cannot be placed against ambient exposure. Intratracheal instillation also
bypasses the deposition physics that a sensor-derived exposure metric is meant to
approximate.}
{10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120816}

\paperentry{Kou et al.\ 2026}
{Pollutants regulate changes in pathological markers of neurodegenerative diseases: a new
perspective in environmental toxicology}
{Front Toxicol}
{Review organised around core neurodegenerative pathological markers ---
\textbf{amyloid-$\beta$, tau, $\alpha$-synuclein, TDP-43} --- rather than around individual
pollutants, detailing how air pollutants, metals and persistent organic pollutants modulate
the generation, aggregation, propagation and toxicity of each, under a proposed
``pollutants--pathogenic links--neural network failure'' framework. No quantitative
synthesis, no effect estimates, no exposure metric and no study-quality appraisal; it
reorganises existing mechanistic claims rather than adding evidence, and \textbf{air
pollutants are treated as a single undifferentiated class} alongside metals and POPs. The
one useful implication for measurement is that marker-level convergence makes pollutant
class, not PM mass, the exposure variable worth resolving.}
{10.3389/ftox.2026.1814795}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 3 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Khan et al.\ 2026}
{Cement industry emissions and respiratory health: global systematic review and
meta-analysis, 2000--2025}
{Front Public Health}
{Eighteen observational studies, PROSPERO CRD420251268913. Random-effects pooling gave
standardised mean differences of \textbf{$-$0.71 for FEV$_1$, $-$0.56 for FVC and $-$0.57
for FEV$_1$/FVC} in exposed groups; chronic cough was significantly more common while
other symptoms were elevated but not significant. Occupational exposure produced greater
FEV$_1$ impairment than residential --- the expected dose ordering and the most reassuring
internal check available. Reporting is the weakness: \textbf{no confidence interval, no
$I^2$ and no prediction interval accompanies any pooled estimate}, so neither precision
nor heterogeneity can be assessed, and SMDs pooled across studies using different
spirometry reference equations with no common dust metric cannot be read as a
dose--response.}
{10.3389/fpubh.2026.1887654}

\paperentry{Assan et al.\ 2026}
{Ambient particulate matter, urinary arsenic and resident health in two Ghanaian mining
communities}
