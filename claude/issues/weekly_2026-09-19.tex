\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}The week in one paragraph}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Last week's two open questions both resolved, and
both in the same direction: the collapse in exposure modelling was an artefact, and the
abstract-free problem was fixable. \textit{Exposure} went 20 $\rightarrow$ 38 and
\textsc{metadata only} went 16\% $\rightarrow$ 10\%. The pipeline change that did the
second is the more consequential of the two.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{Scale.} \textbf{148 records} over seven issues on daily totals of 21, 16, 22, 21,
18, \textbf{30} and 20 --- a 1.9$\times$ range, the flattest week this watch has recorded
and flatter again than last week's 2.6$\times$. Against recent rollups: 148 after 126,
150, 109 and 112, so the level is high-normal. The flatness matters more than the level,
because every cluster movement below is being read against a stable denominator rather
than through a single deposit spike. All \textbf{ten} subtopics are populated.

\textbf{\textit{Exposure assessment \& modelling} rebounded 20 $\rightarrow$ 38 and
overshot.} The 12 September rollup recorded a halving from 47 to 20, declined to call it
a turn in the field, and set an explicit test: whether \textit{Exposure} returns to the
mid-20s. It did not return to the mid-20s --- it went past the pre-collapse level to
\textbf{38}, the second-highest reading in the series. \textbf{The test is answered and
the answer is unambiguous}: the 20 was a one-week sampling floor, not a contraction.
\textit{Sensing} moved only 31 $\rightarrow$ 27, so the two measurement clusters again
moved in opposite directions, and the measurement-side share reads 47, 54, 40,
\textbf{44\%} across four weeks --- still oscillating, still not trending, and the 29
August warning against building a claim on it still stands.

\textbf{The abstract-free problem was halved by a pipeline change, not by the
publishers.} \textbf{15 of 148 (10\%)} shipped as \textsc{metadata only}, against 20 of
126 (16\%) last week, and inside \textit{Sensing} the count fell \textbf{10
$\rightarrow$ 4}. Last week's rollup named an abstract-retry pass as the highest-value
outstanding pipeline work. What actually delivered the improvement was something else:
the PMC and AGRICOLA retro-index gate moved into the screening script on 18 September, so
records that used to enter abstract-less and be carried are now rejected at source as
out-of-window artefacts. \textbf{That is a different mechanism with the same visible
effect}, and the distinction matters --- the retry pass is still not built, and the five
same-day Elsevier, ACS and Springer deposits in the 19 September issue confirm the
underlying publisher behaviour is unchanged.

\textbf{Estimate density returned to the series norm and precision improved sharply.}
\textbf{47 pooled estimates against 148 records}, one per 3.1, from a four-week series of
2.9, 4.4, 2.7, \textbf{3.1}. More useful than the density: \textbf{42 of 47 carry a
confidence interval (89\%)}, against 40 of 47 last week and far above the series floor.
\textbf{Four of the five bare points come from a single paper} --- the wildfire versus
non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ hospitalisation analysis of 18 September, whose abstract reports
eight headline risks and not one interval.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Subtopic trend and inflection}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\noindent\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\FIGDIR/w1_subtopic_trend.png}
\figcap{Per-issue subtopic counts across the week from \texttt{state/metrics.csv}, seven
issues. Daily totals 21, 16, 22, 21, 18, 30 and 20 --- a \textbf{1.9$\times$ range, the
flattest week in the series}, against 2.6$\times$ last week and 5.4$\times$ the week
before. The weekend suppression that dominated the last two rollups is largely absent
here: Sunday 13 September returned 21 records, not the collapse seen on 6 and 12
September, because the Crossref-by-ISSN and Europe PMC legs now carry the weekend
entirely. The 30-record Friday is the 18 September issue, which was built twice --- a
partial morning pass and a full-window re-harvest at 23:0x --- and is a complete day
rather than a deposit anomaly.}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Expanding.} \textit{Exposure assessment \& modelling} \textbf{20 $\rightarrow$
38} is the week's dominant movement and is discussed above. \textit{Other clinical
endpoints} \textbf{4 $\rightarrow$ 8} doubles, driven almost entirely by the 19 September
issue, in which six of twenty records landed in that residual band --- frailty, acute-care
utilisation, renal disease, paediatric respiratory infection, ocular surface and
psoriasis. \textit{Mechanistic toxicology} \textbf{9 $\rightarrow$ 14} and
\textit{Cardiovascular \& metabolic} \textbf{8 $\rightarrow$ 11} both continue the
recovery begun last week. \textit{Respiratory \& allergic} \textbf{12 $\rightarrow$ 13}
holds.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Contracting.} \textit{Occupational \& indoor} \textbf{15 $\rightarrow$ 12}
eases for a second consecutive week off the 19-record surge of 5 September, but
\textbf{remains above the pre-surge regular-week maximum of 9 for a third straight week}.
The 5 September rollup declined to call the indoor turn a trend and the 12 September
rollup set a second week as the test; it has now held a third. \textbf{On this watch's own
stated criteria the indoor turn is now a trend and not a fluctuation.}
\textit{Sensing} \textbf{31 $\rightarrow$ 27} and \textit{Burden, policy \& mitigation}
\textbf{14 $\rightarrow$ 13} are mild. \textit{Reproductive \& developmental}
\textbf{6 $\rightarrow$ 5} stays at the bottom of the table, where it has sat every week
of this series.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{The inflection worth naming.} It is not a subtopic movement at all --- it is that
\textbf{two consecutive weekly rollups set explicit falsifiable tests and both were
answered this week}. \textit{Exposure} was tested against a mid-20s return and overshot
to 38; the indoor turn was tested against a third week and held. A watch that only
reported counts could not have done either. The concrete thing to watch next week is
\textit{Occupational \& indoor}: having now qualified as a trend, the interesting question
is no longer whether it persists but whether any of it moves from chambers and models
into occupied buildings with health outcomes, which so far \textbf{none} of it has.

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Venues, trial watch, and what the pipeline did}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Venues.} \textit{Environ Sci Technol} \textbf{11 of 148} leads, followed by
\textit{Environ Pollut} (9), \textit{Front Public Health} (8), \textit{Atmos Chem Phys}
(6) and \textit{Atmosphere} (5), then a four-way tie at 4 between \textit{Atmos Meas
Tech}, \textit{Aerosol Air Qual Res}, \textit{Environ Monit Assess} and
\textit{Zhonghua Liu Xing Bing Xue Za Zhi}. Concentration is low --- the leading venue
holds 7\% of the week --- and the distribution is a harvesting artefact as much as an
editorial one: titles on the Crossref by-ISSN list enter at their own publication rate,
those reachable only through PubMed enter at PubMed's. The presence of \textit{AMT} and
\textit{ACP} at all is entirely due to the by-ISSN leg, since PubMed does not index them.
\textbf{Preprints supplied 2 records}, down from 8, both labelled in the register.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Recurring authors.} Unchanged and still unresolvable. Surname frequency is led by
Zhang, Li, Huang and Chen at two or three each, which is a naming artefact of a corpus
that is 26\% Chinese-affiliated and not a research-group signal. \textbf{No repeat-group
cluster is claimed for this week.} Adding a first-author affiliation field remains the
open schema item that would make this section mean anything; it has been on the list since
3 September and is now the oldest unaddressed gap in the series.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Trial watch --- five in-window records and, for the first time, nothing new to
state.} ClinicalTrials.gov returned \textbf{5 PM or air-pollution registrations or updates
across 13--19 September}, and \textbf{all five were already in
\texttt{state/trials.json}}, captured by the daily sweeps during the week. That is a
first: on every previous weekly reconciliation the weekly sweep found records the daily
leg had missed. \textbf{The daily trial leg is now ahead of the weekly sweep rather than
behind it}, which is the result the daily sweeps were added to produce. The four topical
records are \textbf{NCT07540000} (INTERLUNG, Johns Hopkins, Bhaktapur, Nepal, $n=800$, a
PHASE4 community-health-worker respiratory intervention with a spirometric primary
endpoint --- still the strongest trial in the accumulated set), \textbf{NCT07793123}
(Robertet SA, oral antioxidant versus placebo on skin radiance, $n=60$),
\textbf{NCT07713264} (VISIONS, Stanford, outdoor-worker education, $n=50$) and
\textbf{NCT07817836} (Pavia, completed observational, DNA-methylation biological age,
$n=151$). The fifth, \textbf{NCT02820402} (Project Viva), is a 1999 birth cohort whose
air-pollution work is secondary and is deliberately not stored. \textbf{Three of the four
topical records measure no air-pollution variable at all} --- exposure is inferred from
urban residence or occupation --- which is the fourth consecutive week in which the
registry's air-pollution trials are mostly trials about something else.
\texttt{trials.json} now holds \textbf{12 trials across 14 windows}.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{What the harvesting legs did.} \textbf{Crossref-by-ISSN was again the workhorse},
highest-yield leg on every day it ran and the only route to the instrumentation journals.
\textbf{Europe PMC} was the second leg and the one that made the weekend survivable ---
Sunday 13 September returned 21 records where the two previous Sundays collapsed.
\textbf{PubMed} ran as two separate queries on the health and instrumentation axes all
week; \textbf{the instrumentation query returned zero on every single day}, which after
seven consecutive days is no longer a sampling observation but a confirmation that PubMed
structurally does not index this literature. \textbf{OpenAlex returned HTTP 429 every
day}, unchanged since 28 July. \textbf{arXiv returned HTTP 406 every day} and contributed
no record to any issue this week --- third consecutive week of that, and the query is
overdue for inspection. \textbf{Consensus returned a nil every day} for the fourth
consecutive week, its hits consistently the right literature with Crossref
\texttt{created} dates years outside the window. \textbf{One new abstract-recovery route
was tested and failed}: Semantic Scholar, queried per DOI on 19 September, returned
\textbf{404 on all five} same-day deposits, having not yet indexed them. Together with the
OpenAlex per-work failure logged on 18 September, that closes the two cheapest remaining
recovery options, and leaves the deferred-retry pass as the only mechanism that can work.

% -------------------------------------------------------------- gaps
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The abstract-retry pass is still not built, and is now the only thing that
can work.} The 10\% metadata-only rate is an improvement produced by \emph{rejecting}
retro-indexed records, not by \emph{recovering} withheld ones. Two recovery routes were
tested and failed this week and last --- OpenAlex per-work, and Semantic Scholar per DOI
--- both for the same reason: they cannot return an abstract the publisher never deposited
or that no index has yet ingested. \textbf{The only route left is time}: re-query Crossref,
Europe PMC and PubMed for the standing held list a week after first sight. That list now
holds \textbf{18 records}.

\item \textbf{Four of 65 measurement-side records carry a human health endpoint, and only
three observe one.} Eighth consecutive week, now on the largest single-week base yet
recorded. This remains the most durable structural finding in the series.

\item \textbf{Ultrafine and size-resolved number: 3 of 148.} Unchanged as a share from
last week. The one instrumentation record in the 19 September issue that would speak
directly to this --- a sub-10\,nm unipolar aerosol charger in \textit{J Aerosol Sci} ---
shipped without an abstract, which is the gap and the pipeline failure landing on the
same record.

\item \textbf{One interventional record in 148}, and it does not test a monitoring
technology. The \textit{Occupational \& indoor} cluster now qualifies as a trend on this
watch's own criteria, and \textbf{none} of it has yet been evaluated against health
outcomes in occupied buildings rather than against chambers and models.

\item \textbf{Pipeline defects carried forward}: no first-author affiliation field (oldest
open item); the \texttt{Cancer}/\texttt{Oncologic} split in \texttt{ENDPOINT\_CANON}; a
6-element \texttt{LIFECOURSE} array in the 2026-08-03 corpus where the schema requires 5
--- outside this window but it \textbf{will} corrupt the September monthly; and
\texttt{.git} lock and temp debris that the delete-restricted mount cannot remove, wanting
manual cleanup on a local checkout. \textbf{Resolved this week}: \texttt{metrics.csv} has
been verified unquoted-safe for thirteen consecutive issues (428 rows, 0 malformed), so
the ``known defect'' note in the task specification is stale and should be struck.
\end{itemize}
