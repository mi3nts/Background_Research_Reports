\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A Sunday issue with no forest plot and an unusually
direct message for the sensing side: the quantity being regulated and the quantity that
drives inflammation are coming apart, and today's instrument paper is measuring the wrong
one.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Zhong et al.\ (\textit{Environ Int}) ran three years of Fuzhou observations against an
animal-derived surface-area/inflammation dose--response and found that \textbf{6.6\% of
hours that satisfied the PM$_{2.5}$ standard ($<$35\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) carried
lung-deposited surface area 7.5$\times$ above what the local LDSA/PM$_{2.5}$ ratio
predicts}, so that \textbf{82\% of the predicted inflammatory burden was invisible to
mass-based assessment}. In those hours median PM$_{2.5}$ was \textbf{3.0\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}
--- an order of magnitude under the standard --- while the dose--response projects 27\%
lung neutrophil infiltration. Their ML attribution puts the mechanism on summer--autumn
afternoon photochemistry producing 50--100\,nm particles, i.e.\ a regime that
\emph{grows} as primary emissions fall and radiation load rises. Every optical low-cost
sensor in the field, including the four characterised in today's other instrument paper
(Werderman et al., \textit{Atmos Pollut Res}), is a light-scattering mass proxy with
essentially no response below $\sim$300\,nm. If Zhong et al.\ are right about the
regime, the distributed-sensing literature is building dense networks for a metric that
is becoming progressively less informative about the endpoint it is meant to proxy, and
the missing instrument --- a cheap, deployable surface-area or number-below-100\,nm
sensor --- is not on anyone's roadmap in this corpus.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The first four-way laboratory characterisation of the current low-cost
sensor generation --- and it arrived without an abstract.} Werderman et al.\ evaluate
\textbf{Sensirion SEN66, Piera IPS-7100, PurpleAir PIXEL and Alphasense OPC-N3} across
aerosol types and concentration ranges. Three of the four are units this watch has not
seen characterised before (the PIXEL is PurpleAir's move away from Plantower; the
IPS-7100 is one of the few consumer-priced units advertising particle-number output).
The publisher deposited title, authorship and DOI only, so it ships as
\textit{Metadata only} and is the highest-priority abstract retry on the queue. Last
author Sousan ran the 2021 OPC-N3/SPS30/AirBeam2/PMS-A003 evaluation that established the
environmental-vs-occupational calibration split, which is the lineage but not a finding.

\item \textbf{A biomonitoring result strong enough to be a sensor.} Giannetti et al.\
sampled \textit{Crematogaster scutellaris} at 30 traffic sites plus 4 controls in Parma
and found magnetic susceptibility of particles on the ant exoskeleton tracking traffic
intensity at \textbf{Spearman $\rho = 0.825$, $p < 0.001$}, with each additional
\textbf{1{,}000 vehicles/day associated with a 1.41-fold increase} and roughly
\textbf{700-fold across the sampled gradient}. Co-located soil showed nothing
($\rho = 0.167$, $p = 0.346$), which is the control that makes the result interesting:
the ant is integrating the airborne fraction, not the deposited one. SEM-EDS puts Fe-oxide
particles doped with Zn, Cu and Ti on the thorax, morphologically consistent with brake
and tyre wear. No calibration to $\mu$g/m$^3$ and no CI on the slope --- but as a
zero-power, zero-maintenance spatial sampler it is a genuinely different point in the
network-design space.

\item \textbf{Mediation of the air-pollution/cognition link is still not identified.}
Sakhvidi et al.\ find only \textbf{16 studies and 72 exposure--mediator--outcome
associations} across six pollutants for the whole literature, with cardio- and
cerebrovascular disease the most consistent candidate mediator and mental health, sleep
and lung function unresolved. Reporting quality was judged good; risk of bias was not,
particularly for outcome ascertainment and confounding. The honest reading is that the
mediation question is under-powered rather than answered.

\item \textbf{UK Biobank puts PM next to plasma neurodegeneration markers and finds the
interaction mostly absent.} In 19{,}645 participants aged $\geq$50 with complete
proteomic, metabolomic and environmental data, \textbf{GFAP was associated with dementia
at HR 2.65 (95\% CI 2.17--3.25)} and NfL with transitions to Parkinson's disease,
dementia and death. \textbf{PM $\times$ NfL interacted with mortality only at the nominal
level, and there was little evidence of PM interaction with either marker for dementia};
NO$_2$/NO$_x$ $\times$ GFAP was \emph{negative}. This is largely a null for the PM term
and should be cited that way, not as support for a PM--neurodegeneration pathway.

\item \textbf{No forest plot today.} The only CI-bearing estimate in the corpus has a
plasma protein on its left-hand side, not a particulate contrast, and Zhong's and
Giannetti's numbers carry no intervals. f5 is omitted --- the same call as 20 August.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 3 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Werderman et al.\ 2026}
{Laboratory evaluation of Sensirion SEN66, Piera IPS-7100, PurpleAir PIXEL and OPC-N3 low-cost aerosol sensors across aerosol types and concentration ranges}
{Atmos Pollut Res}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Crossref by-ISSN sweep; title, authorship (Werderman, Richards,
Berkuta, Sousan) and DOI verified, no abstract deposited and no numbers extractable. It is
nevertheless the single most on-topic record of the window: a chamber characterisation of
\emph{four} current-generation units across aerosol type and concentration range, three of
which --- SEN66, IPS-7100, PurpleAir PIXEL --- have no prior evaluation in this corpus,
against the OPC-N3 as the established reference point. The same last author's 2021
four-sensor evaluation established that environmental and occupational settings need
separate calibration factors, so the plausible contribution here is the aerosol-type
dependence of that factor for the newer optics. \textbf{Highest-priority full-text
retrieval on the next run}; nothing is asserted about its findings until then.}
{10.1016/j.apr.2026.103185}

\paperentry{Giannetti et al.\ 2026}
{Ants as bioindicators of urban traffic pollution}
{Environ Pollut}
{Thirty traffic-graded sites plus four controls in Parma; magnetic susceptibility and
SEM-EDS on \textit{Crematogaster scutellaris} exoskeleton and on co-located soil at 5\,cm.
\textbf{Ant magnetic susceptibility tracked traffic intensity at $\rho = 0.825$
($p < 0.001$); soil did not ($\rho = 0.167$, $p = 0.346$)}. On a log scale the relation is
linear ($R = 0.66$, $p < 0.001$) with \textbf{1.41-fold per additional 1{,}000
vehicles/day}, $\sim$700-fold across the gradient. SEM-EDS found Fe-oxide particles doped
with Zn, Cu and Ti concentrated on the thorax, morphologically consistent with
traffic-derived ultrafine PM. The weaknesses are that no slope CI is given, magnetic
susceptibility is a proxy for the ferrous fraction only (it says nothing about organic or
secondary mass), and nest-density confounding with traffic corridors is not addressed. For
distributed sensing the interest is orthogonal: a passive, unpowered integrator with
month-scale averaging at effectively zero unit cost, usable as a siting prior for where to
put real instruments rather than as a replacement for them.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.129010}

\paperentry{Roska et al.\ 2026}
{Beyond targeted calibrations: comprehensive quantification of oxygenated organic compounds in urban atmospheres}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{A collision-induced-dissociation voltage-scanning method to derive sensitivities for an
ammonium-adduct CIMS, so OVOCs without an authentic standard can still be quantified.
Validated in the laboratory, then applied to the \textbf{2023 AEROMMA airborne campaign
and the 2024 SAPHIR-CHANEL chamber campaign}: coverage rose to \textbf{98\% of total
signal in chamber and 78\% in the field}, and adding the newly quantified species raised
estimated \textbf{OH reactivity by $\sim$5\% and SOA formation potential by $\sim$62\%}.
Coverage degrades with molecular size and oxidation state, and chamber-derived
sensitivities are being transferred to ambient air on the strength of trend agreement
rather than field standards --- the paper says so. The 62\% figure is the one that matters
downstream: SOA yields used in chemical-transport models that feed exposure surfaces are
calibrated against measurements that were missing most of this signal.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c04149}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 3 records}{Deep}

\paperentry{Zhong et al.\ 2026}
{Photochemical ultrafine particles pose potential inflammatory risk despite low PM$_{2.5}$ mass}
{Environ Int}
{Three years of hourly Fuzhou observations; lung-deposited surface area compared against
what the local LDSA/PM$_{2.5}$ ratio predicts, with predicted inflammation from an
animal-derived surface-area dose--response. \textbf{6.6\% of PM$_{2.5}$-attainment hours
($<$35\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) carried 7.5$\times$ the expected surface-area exposure, and 82\% of
the predicted inflammatory risk in those hours was undetected by mass}. Median
PM$_{2.5}$ during them was \textbf{3.0\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, with the dose--response projecting
27\% lung neutrophil infiltration. Machine learning attributes the events to photochemical
production of 50--100\,nm particles on warm, high-radiation summer--autumn afternoons
(O$_3$ and secondary organic carbon dominate importance). \textbf{The limitation that
threatens the inference is the dose--response itself}: an animal-derived surface-area
relation extrapolated to a human population gives a predicted, not an observed,
inflammatory response, and no human endpoint is measured here. Even so, the decoupling of
LDSA from mass is measured, not modelled, and it is a direct challenge to the assumption
that a dense network of scattering-based mass sensors indexes health-relevant exposure.}
{10.1016/j.envint.2026.110469}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026b}
{Photoinduced intraparticle chemistry drives the rapid atmospheric oxidation of biomass burning organic aerosol}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Chamber isolation of biomass-burning aerosol aging pathways --- UV-A irradiation,
OH-driven SOA formation, heterogeneous OH oxidation, ozonolysis and evaporation ---
benchmarked against aircraft measurements of wildfire and prescribed-burn plumes.
\textbf{UV-A irradiation drove intraparticle aging an order of magnitude faster than
gas-phase oxidation}, at an estimated quantum yield of $\sim$0.4\% (four in a thousand
absorbed photons generate particle-phase radicals). The authors estimate that
\textbf{over half of oxygenated OA in biomass-burning-influenced regions may originate
from photo-oxidised primary OA within the first day}. This inverts the standing
assumption that gas-phase oxidation dominates BBOA aging, and it matters for exposure
work because oxidation state governs both the oxidative potential of the inhaled particle
and the optical properties that low-cost scattering sensors respond to --- a smoke plume's
sensor bias is not constant along its trajectory.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c02532}

\paperentry{Mohammed 2026}
{Spatiotemporal dynamics of atmospheric vulnerability in hot arid cities: introducing the Urban Microclimate Air Stress Index (UMASI)}
{Build Environ}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Crossref sweep, no abstract deposited, no study city named in the
metadata. A composite index combining microclimate and air-quality stress is exactly the
kind of construct that needs its weighting scheme inspected before it is used, so nothing
is characterised here. Flagged for retry; low priority relative to Werderman et al.}
{10.1016/j.buildenv.2026.115166}

\band{Neuro / mental health\hfill 2 records}{Deep}

\paperentry{Beydoun et al.\ 2026}
{The interplay of air pollution with plasma neurodegenerative markers and metabolome for dementia, Parkinson's disease and all-cause mortality}
{Ecotoxicol Environ Saf}
{UK Biobank, \textbf{19{,}645 participants aged $\geq$50} with complete proteomic,
metabolomic and environmental data; Cox models plus multistate Weibull models for
transitions from healthy to dementia, Parkinson's disease and death. Higher NfL predicted
all three transitions; \textbf{GFAP was specific to dementia, HR 2.65 (95\% CI
2.17--3.25)}. On the exposure side the result is largely negative: \textbf{PM $\times$
NfL interacted with mortality only at the nominal level, NO$_2$/NO$_x$ $\times$ GFAP
interacted \emph{negatively}, and there was little evidence of PM interaction with either
marker for dementia}. Metabolomic principal components (lipid, amino-acid, energy) did
modify the biomarker--outcome relations and interacted with PM for PD and dementia; a
branched-chain-amino-acid component attenuated the GFAP--dementia association. The
interaction tests are nominal and unadjusted for multiplicity across a large grid, so the
positive ones should be treated as hypothesis-generating. Cite this for the biomarker
result and for the \emph{absence} of a PM interaction, not for a PM effect.}
{10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120657}

\paperentry{Sakhvidi et al.\ 2026}
{Ambient air pollution exposure and cognitive aging; mediators and possible pathways: a systematic review on the epidemiological studies}
{Ageing Res Rev}
{\textbf{16 studies, 72 unique exposure--mediator--outcome associations}, six pollutants
(PM$_{2.5}$, PM$_{10}$, NO$_2$, NO$_x$, black carbon, PM$_1$ components), with PM$_{2.5}$
in 65\% of analyses. Outcomes span memory, processing speed and diagnosed dementia;
candidate mediators span depression/anxiety/stress, FEV$_1$/FVC/PEF, stroke and
hypertension, CRP, type 2 diabetes, sleep and neuroanatomical change. \textbf{Cardio- and
cerebrovascular disease emerged as the most consistent mediator}; mental health, sleep and
lung function showed potential but unconfirmed mediation. Reporting quality was judged
generally good, but most studies carried some risk of bias in outcome assessment and
confounding adjustment. No pooled estimate, and 16 studies across six pollutants and this
many candidate mediators is a thin base --- the useful output is the map of which pathways
have been tested at all, and the observation that almost none have been tested with a
formal causal-mediation design.}
{10.1016/j.arr.2026.103330}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 1 record}{Coral}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026}
{Trends, inequalities, and 2030 projections of women's tracheal, bronchial, and lung cancer attributable to air pollution, radon, and tobacco exposure}
{Front Glob Womens Health}
{GBD 2023, female tracheal/bronchial/lung cancer 1990--2023, five inhalation-related risk
factors, with SDI-gradient smoothing, concentration indices for inequality, decomposition
by demographic vs epidemiological change, and projection to 2030. Smoking remains the
leading contributor, \textbf{ambient particulate matter second, secondhand smoke third}.
Over 1990--2023 \textbf{household solid-fuel-attributable mortality fell most sharply
while particulate-matter-attributable mortality rose slightly}, with China the standout
for the PM-attributable share. Along the SDI gradient, PM shows a \textbf{rising burden
with diminishing inequality} --- the opposite pattern to radon and tobacco --- and
decomposition attributes the PM increase to population growth and aging rather than to
rising risk. Projected to keep rising to 2030. Standard GBD caveats bind hard here:
the exposure is a modelled global surface, the attributable fraction rests on assumed
counterfactual and RR functions, and the 2030 projection is an extrapolation of fitted
trend, not a scenario.}
{10.3389/fgwh.2026.1662451}
