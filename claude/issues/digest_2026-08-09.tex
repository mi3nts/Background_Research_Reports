\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A hardware day. Three of twelve records are new
instruments, and one of them is the first paper in 158 records to build a device whose
stated purpose is to put an aerosol and a biological endpoint in the same
apparatus.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
The standing gap this watch has logged since 26 July --- restated in the 2--8 August
weekly over 146 records --- is that \textbf{not one paper coupled an instrument to a
health endpoint}. Shirkhani et al.\ (\textit{J Aerosol Sci}) describe MARTHA, a
high-throughput multipattern aerosol deposition device built explicitly ``for respiratory
toxicity and health-risk assessment''; the author list (Amanatidis, Eiguren-Fernandez,
Spielman) is an aerosol-instrumentation group, not a toxicology group. The record carries
\textbf{no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar}, so it is listed and not
summarised, and the claim above rests on a title and an author list. That is exactly the
right amount of weight to put on it --- but it is the first time the empty cell in the
f4 cross-tabulation has had a candidate at all.
Alongside it, Li et al.\ (\textit{Photoacoustics}) close the split-instrument problem the
other way: two integrating spheres serving simultaneously as scattering cavities and as a
low-frequency differential Helmholtz resonator, with the connecting tube carrying a
high-frequency longitudinal mode, so absorption and scattering at \textbf{450 and
532\,nm} are measured \emph{in the same volume on one sampling path}
(LOD \textbf{1.2} and \textbf{1.32\,Mm$^{-1}$} at 1\,s, 3$\sigma$). Laffolley et al.\
(\textit{ACS Omega}) go the opposite direction on cost: a stereolithography-printed
two-stage virtual impactor, disposable by incineration, CFD-optimised to
\textbf{9.0} and \textbf{1.3\,$\mu$m} cutoffs. Three different answers to the same
question --- what does a size- or wavelength-resolved measurement have to cost?
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Zero effect estimates. The forest plot is absent from this issue for the
first time since the watch began.} This is a reporting-format fact, not an absence of
quantitative content. Three records carry intervals --- Urso et al.'s
\textbf{41\,\% (24.4--57.4)} of EU-27 dementia DALYs attributable to modifiable risk
factors, and their \textbf{7.76\,M (6.69--8.72)} and \textbf{12.28\,M (10.44--13.90)}
prevalence counts --- but a population-attributable fraction and a case count are not
ratios and have no defensible position on a log axis whose null is 1.0. The same rule
excluded mmHg mean differences on 8 August and an OR spanning a factor of fifteen on
7 August. Ni et al.'s $\rho=0.28$ and AUC 0.607 are likewise not ratio-scale. Rather than
render one figure dishonestly, \texttt{f5\_forest} is not generated and the numbers are
carried in the digest text and the register.

\item \textbf{Sunday, and the thinnest window on record after yesterday's: 12 records
against 14 on Saturday and 26 on Friday.} The PubMed connector returned \textbf{6 on the
health axis and 4 on the instrumentation axis}, ending three consecutive empty sensing
queries from that leg; a third broad MeSH sweep returned 15 for the whole day, of which

off-topic. The local harvester returned 5 and 1. \textbf{Crossref by ISSN across

\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase. Records

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 3 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Li et al.\ 2026}
{Spatiotemporally synchronised dual-wavelength photoacoustic--scattering measurement of aerosol optical properties}
{Photoacoustics}
{A sphere--tube coupled resonator that removes the transport, response-time and
microenvironment mismatch inherent to split absorption/scattering instruments. Two
integrating spheres act simultaneously as scattered-light collection cavities and as
acoustic buffers forming a low-frequency \textbf{differential Helmholtz} resonator, while
the central connecting tube supports a high-frequency \textbf{second-order longitudinal}
mode; 450 and 532\,nm lasers are modulated at \textbf{2572} and \textbf{169\,Hz} and
demodulated by an FPGA digital lock-in, so both wavelengths and both quantities are
recovered from one sampling path. Absorption channels calibrated against NO$_2$,
scattering against N$_2$/CO$_2$ Rayleigh scattering; at 1\,s integration and 3$\sigma$
the 532\,nm limits of detection are \textbf{1.2\,Mm$^{-1}$} (absorption) and
\textbf{1.32\,Mm$^{-1}$} (scattering). The validation is the weak part: water mist,
controlled combustion and a field check establish channel isolation and feasibility but
not accuracy against a reference absorption method, and no Allan-variance drift figure is
given, so the 1\,s LOD does not tell you the useful averaging time. For distributed
sensing the transferable idea is architectural rather than economic --- this is a
laboratory instrument, but co-locating absorption and scattering in a shared volume is
what would let a node report single-scattering albedo instead of a mass proxy.}
{10.1016/j.pacs.2026.100864}

\paperentry{Laffolley et al.\ 2026}
{A 3D-printed two-stage virtual impactor ($\mu$SPLIT) for radioactive aerosol size classification and direct analysis}
{ACS Omega}
{Stereolithography-printed virtual impactor separating aerosol into \textbf{$>$10,
1--10 and $<$1\,$\mu$m} aerodynamic classes onto integrated filters for direct
post-collection $\alpha$ counting, designed for Fukushima Daiichi decommissioning where
the instrument must be cheap enough to incinerate rather than decontaminate. Flow path
optimised by CFD with Lagrangian particle tracking; predicted cutoffs \textbf{9.0} and
\textbf{1.3\,$\mu$m}. The paper is unusually candid about its own failure modes:
simulations identify small-particle contamination in the second-stage minor flow and
large-particle contamination in the major flow, and dimensional metrology on the printed
parts found channel contraction and corner rounding --- i.e.\ the as-built geometry is
not the as-designed geometry, which for an impactor moves the cutoff directly.
Experimental validation is a proof of concept only: incense smoke, and Rn-progeny-bearing
NaCl at 1.70\,$\mu$m GMAD putting peak $\alpha$ activity on the middle filter. No
standardised-aerosol calibration and no quantitative wall-loss budget, both of which the
authors name as required next steps. Relevant well beyond radiological work --- additive
manufacture of size-selective inlets is the cheapest available route to giving a
low-cost node a real aerodynamic size cut instead of an optical inference.}
{10.1021/acsomega.6c04521}

\paperentry{Shirkhani et al.\ 2026}
{Design and development of a high-throughput multipattern aerosol deposition device (MARTHA) for respiratory toxicity and health-risk assessment}
{J Aerosol Sci}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar at the
time of this issue (S2 returns 404 on the DOI). Listed rather than summarised. It is
flagged as the day's highest-priority re-check for a specific reason: the title states an
aerosol-delivery instrument built for respiratory toxicity assessment, which is the exact
instrument\,$\times$\,health-endpoint coupling that the 2--8 August weekly identified as
structurally absent across 146 records. Nothing beyond the title and the author list
(Shirkhani, Salana, Amanatidis, Washeem, Eiguren-Fernandez, Spielman, Nannu Shankar,
Lewis) supports that reading yet.}
{10.1016/j.jaerosci.2026.106882}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 3 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Adeniji et al.\ 2026}
{Long-term air quality and meteorological coupling dynamics in Abeokuta, Nigeria}
{PLOS Glob Public Health}
{Twelve years of hourly PM$_{2.5}$, PM$_{10}$, NO$_2$, SO$_2$, CO and O$_3$ (2012--2023)
against six meteorological variables at a single West African site. Particulate matter
dominates and \textbf{consistently exceeds WHO guideline values}, peaking in the
December--March Harmattan; regression against temperature gives \textbf{R$^2$
0.48--0.52}, with negative associations for rainfall and humidity consistent with thermal
stagnation and wet scavenging. The aggregate AQI stays in the ``moderate'' band (39--51)
while the health-risk-adjusted AQI exceeds \textbf{90} seasonally --- the paper's real
contribution, since it demonstrates that an index which averages across pollutants
conceals a PM-driven risk that a health-weighted index exposes. Two load-bearing
limitations: the data provenance for a 12-year hourly record in Abeokuta is not
established in the abstract, and reanalysis or satellite-derived series are common
substitutes at such sites; and single-site, single-city inference cannot separate the
meteorological coupling from local source seasonality (biomass burning peaks in the same
months as the Harmattan). Sub-Saharan Africa remains the most under-monitored region in
this watch and this is the first record from it in six issues.}
{10.1371/journal.pgph.0006683}

\paperentry{Ray et al.\ 2026}
{Evaluating CCN activity through size-resolved hygroscopicity: synergistic observations and simulations in a tropical montane region}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar.
Listed, not summarised. Size-resolved hygroscopicity ($\kappa$) is the physical quantity
underneath every relative-humidity correction applied to an optical PM sensor, and
tropical montane conditions are the high-RH regime where those corrections fail worst, so
this is a priority re-check even though the paper's own framing is cloud-condensation
nuclei rather than air quality.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122284}

\paperentry{de la Torre et al.\ 2026}
{A 15-year atmospheric record of intentionally produced persistent organic pollutants in Spain}
{J Hazard Mater}
{Polyurethane-foam passive samplers at \textbf{23 sites, 2009--2023}, evaluating
Stockholm Convention effectiveness. Hexachlorobenzene dominates at a median
\textbf{41\,\%} of $\sum$POPs with a remote-enriched spatial pattern; legacy pesticides
and indicator PCBs show temperature-dependent secondary volatilisation from contaminated
soils by Clausius--Clapeyron analysis, and an \emph{urban} enrichment plausibly driven by
heat-island effects; PBDEs show a \textbf{35-fold} urban-to-remote gradient from
late-regulated BDE-209. Early-banned compounds decline up to \textbf{$-$33\,\%/yr} while
PeCB, iPCBs and BDE-209 are \textbf{stalled or rising to $+$20\,\%}. This is semi-volatile
organic chemistry, not particulate mass, and enters on method rather than substance: a
passive sampler is the cheapest long-record instrument in atmospheric science and this is
a clean demonstration of what a 15-year, 23-site, zero-power network buys --- an
altitudinal profile on Tenerife separating local secondary emission from free-troposphere
clearance. The tradeoff is equally clear: no time resolution, no size information, and
partitioning between gas and particle phases is unresolved throughout.}
{10.1016/j.jhazmat.2026.143155}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 2 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Salcines et al.\ 2026}
{Monitoring and risk assessment of airborne graphene oxide released during 3D printing with polylactic acid filaments}
{J Environ Health Sci Eng}
{Tiered exposure assessment to EN 17058:2018 and OECD guidance around fused-deposition
printing of graphene-oxide-loaded PLA, combining \textbf{near-field and far-field particle
counters} with SEM and Raman speciation plus VOC measurement against Spanish and Finnish
2022 occupational limits, with ARIMA used to test whether the observed excursions are
distinguishable from background drift. Nanoparticles and agglomerates were present at low
average number concentration and \textbf{GO was detected in the respirable fraction},
below proposed limits but demonstrably released --- the finding is qualitative
(release occurred) rather than quantitative (how much). Weaknesses are structural: a
pilot with no stated replicate count, number-concentration monitors that cannot speciate,
so the GO attribution rests entirely on offline SEM/Raman of collected material, and
occupational limits for GO that do not formally exist. The methodological point
transfers directly --- paired near-field/far-field counters with an offline speciation
leg is the minimum viable design for attributing a number-concentration excursion to a
source, and it is the design a distributed indoor network would need.}
{10.1007/s40201-026-00998-w}

\paperentry{Shadmani et al.\ 2026}
{A comprehensive health profile and exposure-risk analysis in an iron ore mining workforce}
{J Environ Health Sci Eng}
{Cross-sectional study of \textbf{1,193} Iranian iron-ore mine employees, stratified into
high, moderate and low exposure \emph{by job role}, with physical examination, clinical
chemistry and haematology compared by ANOVA and $\chi^2$ and modelled by linear and
logistic regression. The workforce is young (mean \textbf{34.9\,$\pm$\,7.6\,y}) with low
chronic-disease prevalence; the burden is localised --- hearing \textbf{23.8\,\%}, vision
\textbf{17.8\,\%}, dermatological \textbf{31.3\,\%} --- while most haematological and
metabolic markers did not differ by exposure group. Platelet count, LDL and blood
pressure were \emph{lower} in the high-exposure group, which the authors do not resolve
and which has an obvious competing explanation the paper does not exclude: the
healthy-worker survivor effect plus role-based confounding by age and seniority. The
central methodological weakness for this watch is that \textbf{exposure is a job-role
proxy with no measured dust concentration at all} --- no gravimetric respirable sampling,
no personal monitoring, no size fractionation. A three-level ordinal surrogate cannot
support an exposure--response claim, and the paper's own conclusion (localised sensory and
dermatological effects, not systemic metabolic ones) is a statement about a young cohort
with short cumulative exposure rather than about the hazard.}
{10.1007/s40201-026-01000-3}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 1 record}{Deep}

\paperentry{Wei et al.\ 2026}
{Seasonal source shifts drive oxidative potential of size-resolved particulate matter in coastal southeastern China}
{J Hazard Mater}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar.
Listed, not summarised. Noted because size-resolved oxidative potential is one of the few
routinely measured quantities that is both a particle property and a plausible biological
effect metric, and because a source-driven \emph{seasonal} shift in OP at fixed mass
concentration would be direct evidence against mass as a sufficient exposure metric ---
which is the substantive question behind this watch's instrumentation axis. Whether the
paper supports that reading is unknown until an abstract appears.}
{10.1016/j.jhazmat.2026.143237}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 2 records}{Coral}

\paperentry{Urso et al.\ 2026}
{Burden of dementia in Europe, 1990--2023: a systematic analysis from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023}
{Lancet Reg Health Eur}
{GBD 2023 secondary analysis for the EU-27 and the WHO European Region, with non-fatal
outcomes from DisMod-MR 2.1 and dementia-attributable mortality from an excess-mortality
framework. \textbf{7.76\,M (95\,\% UI 6.69--8.72)} prevalent cases in the EU-27 and
\textbf{12.28\,M (10.44--13.90)} across the WHO European Region, both roughly
\textbf{+90\,\% since 1990} despite modest declines in age-standardised prevalence;
dementia moved from the eighth to the \textbf{third} leading cause of death in the EU-27.
\textbf{41\,\% (24.4--57.4)} of EU-27 dementia DALYs are attributed to six modifiable risk
factors, with \textbf{ambient particulate matter pollution named first}, ahead of high
fasting plasma glucose and high BMI, and concentrated in socioeconomically disadvantaged
populations. The limitation is the one every GBD risk-factor attribution carries and this
paper does not resolve: the PM$_{2.5}$--dementia relative risk is imported from a
comparative-risk-assessment function fitted to a thin and heterogeneous cohort literature,
and the \textbf{24.4--57.4} interval is a modelling uncertainty, not a sampling one --- it
does not propagate uncertainty in the exposure surface, which for a 1990--2023 European
back-cast is substantial. Treated here as a burden estimate, not as neurological evidence.
The interval is deliberately absent from the forest plot: an attributable fraction has no
position on a ratio axis.}
{10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101796}

\paperentry{Sathasivam \& Veluchamy 2026}
{Visual characteristics of PM from \textit{Halymenia dilatata} microalgae biodiesel compared to low-sulfur diesel}
{Environ Sci Pollut Res}
{Single-cylinder Kirloskar CI engine, four microalgae biodiesel blends (B10--B25) against
S10 low-sulfur diesel, PM collected on a textile element in a deceleration chamber,
quantified gravimetrically and characterised by SEM for morphology, deposition and
agglomeration. \textbf{PM mass reduction 10.44--69.79\,\%} across blends. The result does
not vary monotonically with blend ratio --- SEM showed heavier deposition for S10, B15 and
B25 but lighter for B10 and B20 --- which the paper reports but does not explain, and a
non-monotonic dose--response across four points with no stated replication is more likely
measurement noise than combustion chemistry. Three further limits matter: a textile
collection element in a deceleration chamber is not a dilution tunnel and has no
characterised collection efficiency as a function of size, so the gravimetric numbers are
not comparable with any regulated emissions measurement; no size distribution or number
concentration was measured, so a mass reduction is fully compatible with an increase in
ultrafine number; and no engine load or speed conditions are given in the abstract.
Included as a tier-C source-characterisation record.}
{10.1007/s11356-026-38134-8}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 1 record}{Sage}

\paperentry{Ni et al.\ 2026}
{Socioeconomic and regional influences on eosinophilic inflammation in chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps}
{World Allergy Organ J}
{Multicentre retrospective series, \textbf{1,047} CRSwNP patients undergoing endoscopic
sinus surgery at three tertiary hospitals in Jiangsu, with paired blood and nasal-mucosa
eosinophil counts at 2008, 2012 and 2016, analysed by correlation, ROC, linear regression
and linear mixed-effects models. Blood eosinophils are a poor surrogate for tissue
