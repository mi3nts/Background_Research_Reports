\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the month}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Coral}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Low-cost PM sensor calibration stopped being a
collection of one-site case studies in August. Eight independent groups published work
whose common subject is not \emph{whether} a correction works but \emph{how far it
travels} --- across cities, across seasons, across distance from the nearest
reference.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
The clearest statement is Ginsburg et al.\ (\textit{Aerosol Sci Technol}), who tested
whether the US EPA PurpleAir correction transfers worldwide and found it does not
without regional recalibration. Zhivkov et al.\ (\textit{Atmosphere}) put a number on the
spatial half of the same problem: using 24 months from Sofia with four co-located and
eighteen remote sensors, calibration uncertainty grows \textbf{3.84--5.62\% per kilometre}
from the nearest reference station, so co-location sites 6\,km apart hold the network
midpoint inside a 30\% uncertainty increase --- a design rule, derived empirically, that a
network planner can actually use. Wang et al.\ (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}) extended
correction to a nationwide dense network without local references at all; Balasubramanian
et al.\ (\textit{Environ Pollut}) built the US-wide PurpleAir model; Blaga et al.\
(\textit{PLOS Clim}) took a city-scale network to regulatory-compliant output; Chung
et al.\ (\textit{Sensors}) characterised PurpleAir \emph{longevity} and uncertainty across
2016--2025 deployments, which is the failure mode nobody calibrates for; Barkjohn et al.\
(\textit{Atmosphere}) shipped ASNAT, an R-Shiny QA/QC and reference-comparison tool, i.e.\
the tooling layer arriving after the methods; and G\"abel \& Hertig (\textit{AMT}) tied
recalibration practice to the current sensor-technology literature.
Two constraints published alongside them matter more than any of the corrections.
Chyzhykov et al.\ (\textit{J Hazard Mater}) quantified \textbf{particle-bound water as a
bias on gravimetric PM mass} --- that is the \emph{reference} being wrong, not the sensor
--- and Benjamin et al.\ (\textit{Sensors}) showed environmental extrapolation failing
outside the training envelope for both CO and PM$_{2.5}$ nodes. \textbf{The month's
verdict: transferability, drift and reference validity are now the three named open
problems, and the field has stopped pretending a single regression solves any of them.}
\end{keybox}

\vspace{3mm}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed in August}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Coral}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The pipeline gap July's rollup named was closed, and it is the largest
single change in this corpus.} July's monthly stated that there was ``no date-windowed
channel into \textit{Atmos.\ Meas.\ Tech.} or \textit{Atmos.\ Chem.\ Phys.}'' and
proposed a Crossref by-journal feed keyed on \texttt{created}. That feed was built and
widened through the month. August's result: \textbf{Atmos Chem Phys is the single largest
venue in the corpus at 37 records, with Atmos Meas Tech at 11} --- 48 together, ahead of
\textit{Environ Sci Technol} (25) and \textit{Environ Pollut} (22). The pure
atmospheric-measurement literature that was structurally invisible in July is now the
backbone of the sensing cluster.

\item \textbf{Sensing is now the largest subtopic, at 140/556 (25\%).} July recorded 23.
Most of that difference is the AMT/ACP channel plus a full 31-issue cadence rather than
five issues, so it is \textbf{not} evidence that the field grew --- it is evidence of how
badly the July corpus under-sampled instrumentation. Exposure assessment \& modelling is
second at 129 (23\%). Together the two non-clinical clusters hold 48\% of the month.

\item \textbf{Ultrafine particles are still the metric nobody measures.} Twelve records
(2\%) use an ultrafine or nanoscale metric, \emph{down} from July's 5\% share. Yet Zhong
et al.\ (\textit{Environ Int}) showed photochemical ultrafine particles posing an
inflammatory risk \emph{at low PM$_{2.5}$ mass}; Rashid et al.\ (\textit{Toxics})
published a scoping review of UFP mortality evidence and exposure assessment; Blanco
et al.\ (\textit{JESEE}) showed mobile-monitoring design choices propagating into UFP
exposure models; and Zhang et al.\ (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}) built a wearable that
measures particle deposition in the human respiratory tract directly. The prediction
made in July's rollup --- that a field calibration of a low-cost particle-number or
black-carbon sensor against a CPC or aethalometer reference would appear within two
monthly issues --- \textbf{did not come true in August}. The nearest approaches are
Kirchhoff et al.'s CPC working-fluid evaluation (\textit{AMT}) and Shlipak et al.'s
source-apportioned black carbon in Addis Ababa, neither of which is a low-cost-instrument
validation. That prediction now carries into September, and one more miss should be read
as a hardware-cost bottleneck rather than a research-attention one.

\item \textbf{Effect estimates: 148 from 63 sources, and 110 of them exclude the null in
the harmful direction.} Only six were protective, and every one of those has a benign
explanation (an ozone term, a wildfire-season influenza-like-illness contrast, thyroxine
and menopause-timing shifts). Thirty-two cross the null. The largest ratios are all
extreme-contrast designs --- Xu et al.\ report OR $\approx$ 4.3--4.4 for cerebral
hypoperfusion against PM$_{2.5}$ and black carbon, and Li et al.\ OR 5.08 (3.45--7.47)
for paediatric allergic-rhinitis exacerbation comparing PM$_{2.5}$ $>$75 against
$<$35\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ --- and are not comparable to per-10\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ estimates.

\item \textbf{Compound and multi-stressor designs consolidated.} Mandalapu et al.\
(medRxiv) mapped joint heat and PM$_{2.5}$ exposure across US metropolitan areas against
historical HOLC redlining grades (\textbf{OR 3.97, 2.79--5.66} for hotspot residence in
the worst-graded areas); Yu et al.\ (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}) gave a probabilistic
treatment of PM$_{2.5}$--ozone co-occurring extremes under wildfire smoke; Wang L et al.\
closed the month with a lipid-fraction $\times$ PM$_{2.5}$ interaction on stroke. Single
-pollutant, single-endpoint time-series work continues to read as the low-information
tail.

\item \textbf{Sub-Saharan Africa quadrupled, from 4 records in July to 16, and it still
is not enough.} Shlipak et al.\ (Addis Ababa source apportionment), Mushore et al.\
(road-edge effect across three campaigns), Rehbein et al.\ (low-cost network source
triangulation in Ile-Ife) and Makacha et al.\ (personal PM$_{2.5}$ exposure determinants)
are all methodologically serious. Against 121 records from China, the imbalance remains
the corpus's most persistent structural feature.

\item \textbf{A dedup defect was found by this rollup and has been gated.} See the
provenance box: twelve records were shipped twice.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Emerging, fading, and recurring}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Coral}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Emerging.} \textbf{(i) Calibration transferability as a named problem} --- see
the signal box; the shift is from ``here is a correction for our site'' to ``here is how
far a correction travels, and here is the uncertainty as a function of distance,
season and instrument age''. \textbf{(ii) The reference itself under scrutiny} ---
Chyzhykov et al.\ on particle-bound water biasing gravimetric mass, Anon et al.\ on a
gravimetric-method assessment campaign for PM$_{10}$ and PM$_{2.5}$, and Alexa et al.\ on
separating gaseous nitric acid from particulate nitrate. When the calibration target is
itself biased, every downstream $R^2$ inherits it. \textbf{(iii) Toxicity- and
source-weighted exposure} --- Chen et al.\ on source-specific oxidative potential of
PM$_{2.5}$ in Xi'an, Kang et al.\ on vehicle-origin condensable PM, and Song et al.'s
closing perspective on shifting control from mass to toxicity. \textbf{(iv) Citizen
science formalised} --- Marcon et al.\ (\textit{Curr Environ Health Rep}) reviewed
methodological approaches and effectiveness, and Cho et al.\ ran a community-scientist-led
mobile campaign; this had been anecdotal in July.

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Fading.} With 31 genuine daily windows this month, a decline claim is finally
defensible in principle --- but one month against one partly-backfilled month is still
not a trend, so nothing is asserted here as fading. The weakest-signal observation, and
it is only an observation: \textbf{single-city, single-pollutant time-series studies
continued to appear and continued to add nothing}, generally reporting PM as the weakest
term in their own multi-pollutant models. That was July's observation too, unchanged.

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Recurring groups and venues.} \textbf{No author-cluster analysis is possible in
this corpus and none is offered.} Records carry a first-author surname but no
affiliation field, so apparent repeats (Li, Zhang, Wang, Chen, Liu) are surname frequency
in a China-heavy corpus, not the same investigators. This has been an open defect since
15 August and remains one. Venue concentration is the analysis that \emph{is} supportable:
\textit{Atmos Chem Phys} 37, \textit{Atmos Environ} 34, \textit{Environ Sci Technol} 25,
\textit{Environ Pollut} 22, \textit{Environ Monit Assess} 17, \textit{Atmos Pollut Res}
14, \textit{Atmos Meas Tech} 11, \textit{Atmosphere} 11, \textit{J Hazard Mater} 10,
\textit{Environ Int} 8. Preprints contributed 31 records --- Research Square 21 under
\emph{three} spellings, medRxiv 6 under two, arXiv 4 --- which is a venue-normalisation
defect, logged below. Citation counts on records days to weeks old are uninformative and were not used
to rank anything here; ``most-cited work of the period'' is therefore not reported,
because any such ranking would be an artefact of indexing lag.

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Trial registry watch}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Coral}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
Seven registry sweeps ran across August. The accumulated watch list now holds
\textbf{24 trials}, of which \textbf{21} were registered, updated or first seen inside
August. Two new interventional registrations appeared in the month:

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{NCT07111208} (MARKOPOLO/METSGREEN, $n$=180, recruiting), added 4 August, and
\textbf{NCT07797413} --- the \textbf{Savannah Environmental Nutrition Study}, registered
31 August by Georgia Southern University. SENS is a community-based
antioxidant- and nutrient-rich food intervention in residents near the Port of Savannah,
$n$=20, not yet recruiting, starting 1 September 2026. \texttt{analyze\_endpoints} was
run: the \emph{primary} endpoint is \textbf{participant retention} ($\geq$80\% target
through week 12) --- this is a feasibility study, and no efficacy claim should be
attached to it. The secondary endpoints are the interesting part: change from baseline in
\textbf{fractional exhaled nitric oxide} (NIOX Mini, ppb) and \textbf{hydrogen peroxide in
exhaled breath condensate} (RTube collection, Amplex Red fluorescence, $\mu$M), plus
blood pressure, resting heart rate and HbA1c. \textbf{No PM exposure measurement is
registered at all} --- proximity to the port is the entire exposure contrast. That is the
recurring weakness of this registry segment: the intervention is characterised in detail
and the exposure is not characterised at all.

\vspace{1.5mm}
The rest of the August watch list is dominated by \textbf{portable air cleaner and
filtration trials} --- PATHFINDER in heart failure ($n$=400), a multi-centre clean-air RCT
in COPD ($n$=770), NCT05874479 on blood pressure in New York ($n$=440), HEPA in rural
Alaska ($n$=15), ERV plus air cleaner for mould and asthma in low-income housing
($n$=80), and cost-effective filtration with PM monitoring in low-income housing
($n$=55) --- plus three MEDiterranean climate-health resilience arms ($n$=102 each,
outdoor workers, hypertensive 65+, and pregnant women) and INTERLUNG in Bhaktapur
($n$=800). One completed cluster-randomised comparison of HEPA against ionisation/plasma
air cleaners ($n$=180) is the most directly instrument-relevant result to watch for
publication. \textbf{The interventional evidence base for personal and household PM
reduction is thickening in the registry but has not yet reached the literature: four
trial-class records in 556 published papers this month.}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps and read on direction}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Coral}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Gaps in the literature}\\[1.2mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{(i) Ultrafine measurement still does not exist at network scale.} Twelve
ultrafine records in 556, no low-cost particle-number instrument with a published field
calibration against a CPC, and a scoping review (Rashid et al.) that documents the
epidemiological interest without closing the measurement gap. July's falsifiable
prediction on this point failed; it is re-issued for September.
\textbf{(ii) The calibration reference is itself unvalidated.} Particle-bound water
biases gravimetric mass (Chyzhykov et al.); a full assessment campaign of the gravimetric
method was needed and published (Anon et al.). Every sensor $R^2$ in this corpus is
conditional on a reference nobody has re-audited.
\textbf{(iii) Four trial-class records in 556.} Every causal claim in the month rests on
observational designs with modelled exposure.
\textbf{(iv) Geographic imbalance persists.} 121 China against 16 sub-Saharan Africa and
13 Latin America --- better than July's 44 against 4, still not proportionate to burden,
and Song et al.'s toxicity-weighting perspective puts the highest toxicity-adjusted
exposure precisely in the regions this corpus covers least.
\textbf{(v) Drift and ageing are measured but not modelled.} Chung et al.\ characterise
PurpleAir longevity; Kumpika et al.\ correct sensor ageing; no record in the month puts
an ageing term inside an operational network's correction pipeline.
\end{keybox}

\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Gaps and defects this pipeline creates}\\[1.2mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{(i) DUPLICATES SHIPPED --- found by this rollup.} Pooling August returned
\textbf{556 records over 548 unique DOIs}. A full-store audit found \textbf{12 records
published twice}: seven on 2 August (four first shipped 1 August, three first shipped
29 July), two on 3 August, one on 9 August (first shipped 7 August), and the two
\texttt{10.3779/j.issn.1009-3419} records already disclosed in the W35 weekly. In every
case the DOI was \emph{already correctly recorded} in \texttt{state/seen.json} under the
first issue date --- the index was being written and not read, and the duplicate shipped
under a reworded title that defeated the title-signature leg. \textbf{Fixed forward on
1 September}: \texttt{check\_dois.py}, which already gates every \texttt{--post} build,
now hard-fails on \texttt{DUPLICATE-DOI} by scanning the shipped corpus itself rather
than \texttt{seen.json}, so it cannot be defeated by a write-side bug either. Shipped
issues have \emph{not} been retro-edited --- rewriting published issues is worse than
disclosing the error --- so \textbf{every count in this issue is over 556 records
containing 8 within-August duplicates, and subtopic counts are inflated accordingly by
roughly 1.4\%.}
\textbf{(ii) 65 records (12\%) are classification-only.} No abstract was deposited
anywhere reachable at issue time. They are counted but carry no assessed content, and the
absence of criticism on those records is not approval.
\textbf{(iii) No first-author affiliation field}, so no author- or institution-cluster
analysis is possible. Open since 15 August.
\textbf{(iv) Venue names are not normalised} --- Research Square appears as ``Res Sq
(preprint)'' (9), ``Research Square (preprint)'' (9) and ``Research Square'' (3), and
medRxiv as two spellings (5 and 1). Any venue table in this series under-reports split
spellings; the top-ten list above is correct only for venues with a single spelling.
\textbf{(v) OpenAlex has been dead all month} (HTTP 403 paid-plan block). Its coverage is
routed through Consensus, which has no entry-date filter, so that leg contributes dated
backfill rather than windowed harvest.
\end{keybox}

\vspace{3mm}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Read on direction.} July's read was that the field is moving from mass to
composition and number while the instrumentation base stands still. August refines it:
the instrumentation base is moving, but along the \emph{transferability} axis rather than
the \emph{new-quantity} axis. Eight calibration papers, a QA/QC tool, a longevity study
and a distance-uncertainty law all improve how well we can trust a PM$_{2.5}$ mass
network --- and not one of them measures anything a PM$_{2.5}$ mass network does not
already measure. Meanwhile Zhong et al.\ demonstrated inflammatory risk from
photochemical ultrafine particles \emph{at low mass}, and Song et al.\ argued that
toxicity, not mass, should be the control variable. \textbf{The two halves of the field
are optimising different objective functions, and the gap widened in August rather than
narrowing.}
\textbf{Falsifiable predictions for September, both re-issued and sharpened:}
(1) a field calibration of a low-cost particle-number or black-carbon sensor against a
CPC or aethalometer reference appears --- this failed in August and a second miss should
be read as a hardware-cost bottleneck, not a research-attention one; and
(2) at least one paper explicitly incorporates a sensor-\emph{ageing} term into an
operational network correction, rather than characterising ageing and correcting it
offline. If (2) also fails, the practical conclusion for a distributed-sensing
programme is that drift management remains an unclaimed contribution.

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest --- the month by subtopic}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Coral}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2mm}

\footnotesize
