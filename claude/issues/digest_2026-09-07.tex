\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}A regulatory station and a mobile monitor 250\,m apart
disagree enough to publish a different AQI colour one time in ten --- and distance does not
predict when.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Paired fixed-station and simultaneous mobile PM$_{2.5}$ measurement at three contrasting
sites across the Boston metro area, spring and summer 2025 (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}),
puts a number on what a sparse network cannot see. Typical agreement is good --- mean
station-to-mobile difference \textbf{2.0\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} (median 1.5, IQR 0.6--2.8) --- but
the tail is not: dynamic events produced discrepancies exceeding \textbf{300\% in relative
terms and 800\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ in absolute terms}, and in \textbf{10.4\% of cases}, rising to
\textbf{21.8\% at one site in spring}, the two instruments implied \emph{different AQI
categories}. The finding that matters operationally is the negative one: \textbf{Euclidean
distance alone could not explain the differences}. Spatial representativeness is therefore
not a radius, which is exactly the assumption embedded in every siting guideline and every
inverse-distance interpolation built on top of a reference network. Relative humidity was
the strongest single correlate the abstract reports ($r=-0.84$) --- note the sign is against
a signed centre-minus-mobile difference, not an absolute one, so it is a direction, not a
magnitude, and the humidity result would need the full text to interpret. Two seasons, one
metro area, and mobile measurement carries its own inlet and response-time artefacts. Even
so, this is the cleanest published quantification of the gap that dense low-cost deployment
exists to close, and it converts that case from a spatial-coverage argument into a
public-messaging one.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 4 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Boston sparse network 2026}
{What sparse fixed air-quality networks miss, measured against mobile monitoring}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Fixed-station PM$_{2.5}$ paired with simultaneous mobile measurement at a dense set of
locations within a 250\,m radius, at three contrasting areas of the Boston metro area across
spring and summer 2025. Typical agreement is close --- mean difference \textbf{2.0\,$\mu$g/m$^3$},
median 1.5, IQR 0.6--2.8 --- but dynamic events drove differences past \textbf{300\%} and
\textbf{800\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, and \textbf{10.4\% of cases} (21.8\% at one site in spring) fell
either side of an AQI category boundary. Crucially \textbf{Euclidean distance alone could not
explain the discrepancies}, which undercuts radius-based representativeness and every
inverse-distance product built on a reference network. Relative humidity was the strongest
reported correlate ($r=-0.84$), a signed rather than absolute association that the abstract
does not fully disambiguate. Two seasons and one metro area, and mobile platforms carry inlet
and response-time artefacts of their own; but this is the number the dense-network argument
has been missing.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c03923}

\paperentry{UAV single-particle CCSEM 2026}
{Size-resolved aerosol composition and mixing state through the boundary layer from a UAV}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{A microscopy-based particle sampler flown on a UAV at up to 30\,m vertical and 1\,min
temporal resolution in a coastal boundary layer; over \textbf{100{,}000 particles} classified
by computer-controlled SEM with EDX. The observed transition runs from sea-breeze aerosol
dominated by aged sea salt and dust (\textbf{46--83\%}) to a continentally influenced
population dominated by organic-sulfur particles (\textbf{57--89\%}), and the mixing state
index falls from \textbf{53\% to 27\%} as those particles form --- i.e.\ toward external
mixing. Mixing state showed strong diurnal and size dependence but weak vertical structure.
Offline microscopy is not a real-time measurement and the sampling is necessarily
intermittent, so the ``temporal evolution'' is a sequence of snapshots; one coastal site, and
no companion mass measurement is described. The transferable point for network design is that
composition and mixing state vary on scales a fixed filter sampler averages away entirely.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c03627}

\paperentry{Bogota BiLSTM Huber 2026}
{Asymmetric-loss BiLSTM for 24\,h PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting and exceedance detection}
{Atmosphere}
{Hourly records from the Puente Aranda industrial-corridor station, Bogot\'a, 2022--2025
($n=31{,}961$). The motivating statistic is sharp: \textbf{98.7\%} of days exceeding the WHO
2021 15\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ guideline contain at least one hour above 25, median peak-to-mean
\textbf{1.90}, so a model trained on mean error systematically misses the episodes that
matter. Three mechanisms are stacked --- an asymmetric Huber loss penalising underprediction
$\alpha$-fold, exceedance sample weighting, and Optuna-TPE over 100 trials. The honest result
is a reported \textbf{regression--detection tension}: no $\alpha$ minimised both global and
peak MAE. Optimisation chose $\alpha=2.80$, giving the best detection (AP \textbf{0.724},
95\% CI 0.582--0.821) and lowest peak MAE (10.64\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) at the cost of
\textbf{$R^2\approx0$} globally; $\alpha=1.5$ is the compromise (AP 0.718, RMSE 6.65,
$R^2=0.372$). One station, so nothing here is a spatial claim, and the wide bootstrap
intervals on AP overlap across all configurations --- the ranking is weaker than the point
estimates suggest.}
{10.3390/atmos17090871}

\paperentry{Coriolis sampler inactivation 2026}
{Sampler-induced loss of respiratory virus infectivity in spiked collection liquid}
{Indoor Air}
{Rhinovirus, SARS-CoV-2 and influenza A spiked into the collection liquid of a Coriolis
$\mu$ liquid impinger and run at several airflow rates --- deliberately \emph{not} an aerosol
or ambient-air collection, which isolates mechanical stress in the liquid from capture
efficiency. Culturable virus fell by up to \textbf{98.6\%} for SARS-CoV-2 and influenza A,
while RNA copies were largely unchanged for SARS-CoV-2 and rhinovirus and reduced only for
influenza A. Rhinovirus infectivity was essentially unaffected, so the effect is
virus-specific rather than a general artefact. The design's strength is also its limit: it
cannot say how much infectivity is lost during aerosol capture itself, only during the liquid
phase, so it bounds one term of the total sampling loss. The consequence for bioaerosol
surveillance is direct --- a PCR-positive, culture-negative air sample is not evidence of
non-infectious aerosol.}
{10.1155/ina/5068026}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 4 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Liupan tethered eBC 2026}
{Vertical structure of carbonaceous aerosol over the Loess Plateau from tethered balloon}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Coordinated tethered-balloon profiling of carbonaceous aerosol and meteorology at Pingliang,
eastern foothills of the Liupan Mountains, during July 2023 and July 2024 campaigns.
Near-surface means were \textbf{0.84\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ eBC} and \textbf{1.24\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ UVPM},
both decreasing with height. The substantive observation is that in the early morning and at
night, when convection is weak, \textbf{UVPM aloft exceeded eBC} --- attributed to nucleation
from gaseous precursors rather than transport. Thermodynamic processes dominated vertical
transport near the surface while enhanced dynamics aloft drove horizontal dispersion, and
southerly air masses contributed disproportionately to UVPM. Two July campaigns at one site
cannot separate seasonal from interannual variation, and eBC and UVPM are optical
attributions from a filter-based absorption instrument, not chemical speciation --- the
nucleation interpretation is inference from the eBC/UVPM divergence, not a measured
formation pathway.}
{10.5194/acp-26-12671-2026}

\paperentry{EROD-HR/SOILHD Iberia 2026}
{High-resolution soil erodibility and its effect on WRF-Chem dust and PM$_{10}$}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{Two erodibility datasets tested in WRF-Chem with GOCART: EROD-HR, which folds fine-resolution
topography in at 0.0625$^\circ$ globally and 1\,km over Iberia, and SOILHD, which adds
high-resolution sand/silt/clay texture and removes misclassified bare-soil areas at 1\,km
globally. Five dust episodes spanning 2022--2025 cover both local emission and long-range
North African intrusion, evaluated against \textbf{PM$_{10}$ from the SINQLAIR network in
Murcia}. Both datasets improved the magnitude and timing of modelled PM$_{10}$ peaks at
inland stations; \textbf{improvements were limited at coastal stations}, which the paper does
not fully resolve and which is the most interesting part of the result --- coastal sites mix
dust with sea salt and are the ones regulatory exceedance attribution most often disputes.
Five episodes is a small evaluation set, and network PM$_{10}$ cannot separate dust from
co-occurring anthropogenic mass.}
{10.5194/acp-26-12641-2026}

\paperentry{Taiwan LRT bioaerosol 2026}
{Long-range transport effects on airborne fungal concentration and persistence}
{Environ Res}
{Daily wintertime fungal monitoring in northern Taiwan, comparing periods before, during and
after Asian dust storms and frontal pollution events. Long-range transport raised fungal
concentrations by \textbf{57--193\% during events} and \textbf{17--69\% in the post-event
window}, with elevation persisting \textbf{one to four days} after passage. Frontal pollution
enhanced concentrations more broadly than dust events did, and the assemblages differed by
regime: dust storms enriched Basidiospores and \textit{Cladosporium}, frontal events enriched
\textit{Aspergillus}/\textit{Penicillium} and Ascospores. The persistence result is the useful
one, because event-triggered sampling designs that stop at event end will systematically
under-count exposure. Weaknesses are the usual ones for this design: event periods are
defined meteorologically and confounded with the temperature, humidity and mixing changes
that accompany them, and no source apportionment separates transported spores from locally
resuspended ones.}
{10.1016/j.envres.2026.125640}

\paperentry{St Elias proglacial dust 2026}
{Sub-kilometre modelling of mineral dust emission from proglacial valleys}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{A sub-km WRF-Chem implementation built to resolve dust emission from the proglacial valleys
of the St.\ Elias Mountains, Canada --- sources absent from current emission modelling and
unaccounted for in global inventories. Land-surface inputs were rebuilt with explicit
treatment of glaciofluvial deposit heterogeneity and inundation state; 5--19\,d simulations
across 2019--2022 were evaluated against in situ meteorology, dust measurements, camera
stations and surface Doppler LiDAR. Total emission is estimated at \textbf{1.0$\times$10$^4$
kg\,km$^{-2}$\,d$^{-1}$} from erodible deposits over 47 simulated days, offered explicitly as
a first-order figure. Skill in reproducing surface meteorology and in-valley vertical
dispersion is \textbf{season-dependent}, and the emission scheme remains sensitive to soil
texture, so the flux estimate inherits that sensitivity. High-latitude dust is a genuinely
under-measured PM source and this is a careful first quantification, but 47 days at one
mountain range does not support extrapolation.}
{10.5194/acp-26-12613-2026}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 3 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{Shijiazhuang AECOPD 2026}
{Seasonal and short-term environmental associations with COPD exacerbation admissions}
{COPD}
{City-wide retrospective time-series of \textbf{22{,}468 AECOPD admissions}, 2020--2024, from
the municipal medical insurance database covering all tertiary, secondary and community
hospitals --- unusually complete ascertainment, though ED discharges without admission are
excluded. Mean age 71.7 (SD 9.1), male-to-female 3.7:1, winter peak 6{,}009 against summer
5{,}051. On the 7-day mean of lags 0--6, each 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ of winter PM$_{10}$ gave
\textbf{IRR 1.069 (1.052--1.085)} and NO$_2$ \textbf{1.089 (1.050--1.129)}; ozone was positive
in summer \textbf{1.035 (1.015--1.056)} and autumn \textbf{1.022 (1.005--1.039)}; a
1$^\circ$C wider diurnal range gave \textbf{1.055 (1.034--1.077)}. The authors are explicit
that the many lag $\times$ pollutant $\times$ season tests are exploratory and that the
prediction models are not a deployable early-warning system --- a welcome discipline, and the
reason the apparent protective multipollutant IRRs should not be read as findings. Collinear
pollutants in a single city cannot be separated by adjustment.}
{10.1080/15412555.2026.2723102}

\paperentry{Umea wood smoke chamber 2026}
{Controlled wood smoke exposure, sensory irritation and small airway mechanics}
{Environ Res}
{Randomised, double-blind crossover chamber study: \textbf{14} healthy non-smokers (11 M, 3 F,
mean age 26) exposed for 2\,h to wood smoke at \textbf{PM$_1$ 453 $\pm$ 13\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} and
to filtered air, three-week washout, with forced oscillometry (tremoFlo) alongside spirometry
and Borg symptom scoring. Wood smoke produced significant \textbf{nasal irritation
($p=0.003$)} with trends for cough and odour, but \textbf{no exposure-specific change} in any
spirometric or oscillometric resistance parameter (R5, R5--R19, R5--R20). Reactance and
resonant frequency moved with time irrespective of exposure, which is the control the design
exists to provide. With $n=14$ healthy young adults this is badly underpowered for small-airway
effects and the null should be read as such, not as evidence of no effect --- the authors say
as much. The methodological point stands independently: irritative symptoms preceded any
mechanical signal, so spirometry-only designs will under-detect early response.}
{10.1016/j.envres.2026.125629}

\paperentry{Disaster respiratory review 2026}
{Respiratory emergencies in disasters}
{Thorac Res Pract}
{Narrative review spanning earthquake silica and asbestos dust, wildfire fine particulate,
flood-related mould, volcanic ash and acidic gases, chemical release, blast barotrauma and
pandemic threats, synthesised from PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science and Scopus with EM-DAT, WHO
and UNDRR reports. It reports that acute respiratory infection is a leading cause of
disaster-related morbidity and mortality across age groups, that chronic respiratory patients
face elevated exacerbation risk, and that ARDS management remains hardest in
resource-limited settings. There is \textbf{no stated search protocol, no inclusion criteria
and no risk-of-bias appraisal}, so it indexes the literature rather than weighing it, and no
quantitative estimate here can be traced to a defined evidence base. Carried as a citation
anchor for the disaster-PM framing and for its explicit treatment of climate change as a
driver of rising burden --- not as evidence.}
{10.4274/ThoracResPract.2026.2026-6-13}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 3 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Europe indoor PAH children 2026}
{Indoor-air PAH levels and child cancer risk across six European cities}
{Environ Res}
{Twelve EPA PAHs in air and deposited dust from schools, homes and sports halls in Athens,
Barcelona, Colchester, Copenhagen, Helsinki and Lisbon --- four of the 16 target compounds
were dropped for analytical limitations, which is stated. Spatial variability is large:
medians above \textbf{50{,}000\,pg/m$^3$} where intensive residential wood burning and poor
ventilation coincide, against \textbf{under 10{,}000\,pg/m$^3$} in south-western Europe.
High-molecular-weight PAHs ran higher in schools than homes, consistent with occupancy and
accumulation of outdoor-derived particles, while low-molecular-weight species tracked
household practices. Estimating air concentrations from deposited dust \textbf{underestimated
HMW-PAHs}, which is a directly useful negative result for anyone tempted to substitute dust
for active sampling. Excess cancer risk over a five-year primary-school exposure period
spanned \textbf{10$^{-8}$ to 10$^{-5}$}, below the 10$^{-4}$ reference. Cross-sectional
sampling cannot capture seasonal wood-burning cycles.}
{10.1016/j.envres.2026.125643}

\paperentry{Rouyn-Noranda citizen dust 2026}
{Citizen-science measurement of smelter-derived metals in snow and indoor dust}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Residents of \textbf{23 homes} 540--2{,}080\,m from the Horne Smelter --- Canada's only copper
smelter --- collected their own indoor dust and adjacent snow samples for Cu, Pb, Cd, As, Zn
and Cr. Accounting for distance \emph{and} wind direction, smelter location explained
\textbf{73\% of snow variance for Cu and Cr}, 58\% for Cd and Pb, 56\% for As and 44\% for Zn
($p<0.01$); for indoor dust the distance-only explanatory power was much lower at
\textbf{36\% (Cu) to 15\% (As)}. Copper served as a smelter tracer and correlated strongly
with the other metals in snow ($R^2>70\%$). Few residential characteristics predicted indoor
dust once distance was controlled. Twenty-three homes is a small sample, snow and settled
dust are deposition integrals rather than airborne concentrations, and participant-collected
samples carry uncontrolled handling variance. The value is the demonstration itself: a
participatory design produced a defensible source-attribution gradient in a community with a
documented exposure dispute.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15900-2}

\paperentry{Bi/Bi2Te3 workplace ICP-OES 2026}
{ICP-OES determination of bismuth and bismuth telluride in workplace air}
{Zhonghua Lao Dong Wei Sheng Zhi Ye Bing Za Zhi}
{Method development for an occupational aerosol with no established Chinese workplace
standard: mixed cellulose ester filter sampling, ultra microwave digestion at 130$^\circ$C for
10 minutes, then simultaneous Bi and Te by ICP-OES with Bi$_2$Te$_3$ back-calculated from
either element using on-site speciation information. Linear over \textbf{0.04--30\,mg/L}
($r>0.9990$); on a 75\,L sample diluted to 50\,mL the minimum detectable Bi$_2$Te$_3$ is
\textbf{0.006\,mg/m$^3$} via Bi and \textbf{0.008} via Te. Digestion efficiency
99.1--100.0\% (Bi) and 101.2--103.3\% (Te), intra-batch precision under 0.8\%, 7-day room
temperature stability, and no interference from Ag, Al or As up to 10\,mg/L. Validated on
simulated samples rather than a field inter-laboratory comparison, and thermoelectric
