\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}The Crossref by-ISSN leg finally caught
\textit{Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics}, and the first four records it delivered
include the cleanest quantification this watch has seen of the error a light-scattering
sensor makes when it cannot tell a swollen aerosol particle from an activated droplet.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Neuberger et al.\ (\textit{Atmos Chem Phys}) analysed the FAIRARI 2021/2022 fog campaign
at San Pietro Capofiume in the Po Valley with $\kappa$--K\"ohler theory and the MIMICA
large-eddy simulation. \textbf{Failing to account for hydrated, non-activated particles
inflated the inferred effective diameter by 81\,\% (11.6 to 21.0\,$\mu$m) and cut the
inferred cloud droplet number concentration by 87\,\% (97.4 to 12.4\,cm$^{-3}$)}, while
hydrated particles supplied \textbf{21\,\% of liquid water content} and accounted for
\textbf{36\,\% of sub-kilometre visibility events with no droplet activation at all}.
Median $\kappa$ separated cleanly by population --- fog residuals 0.45, interstitial
0.40, out-of-fog aerosol 0.34. This is a fog-microphysics paper, but the mechanism is
exactly the one that governs low-cost optical PM sensors at high relative humidity: the
instrument sizes a water-coated particle and reports the mass of a dry one. The
literature's usual answer is an empirical RH correction fitted to a co-location period,
which implicitly assumes a fixed $\kappa$; Neuberger et al.\ measure $\kappa$ varying by
a third \emph{within a single site and campaign} depending on which population the
particle belongs to. A humidity correction estimated during one aerosol regime should
therefore not be expected to transfer to another, and the residual after correction is
not noise --- it is composition.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A structural coverage gap closed, at least for one day.} The sweep caught
\textbf{four} \textit{Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics} deposits --- the first ACP
records it has ever returned --- of which \textbf{three shipped}; the fourth, a
Jungfraujoch radiocarbon CH$_4$/CO$_2$ study, is gas-phase only and was screened out.
ACP is one of the two journals the source audit named as
structurally invisible to PubMed, so until now the watch has been blind to it except
through Consensus, which is relevance-ranked rather than date-windowed and therefore
resurfaces old work. \textbf{This is a harvest-architecture result, not a literature
result}, and it should be read as such: the by-ISSN Crossref leg is now demonstrably
delivering the instrumentation-side coverage it was added for. Whether it does so
reliably needs several more days before it can be claimed.

\item \textbf{Consensus returned six on-topic sensor and exposure-modelling papers and
every one of them was a duplicate.} Low-cost sensor calibration, recalibration cadence,
calibration-strategy review, satellite--surface fusion in North-East India, an
urban--rural balanced estimation framework for China, and a WRF-Chem/satellite/ground
fusion scheme --- all six were already in \texttt{seen.json}, shipped between 26 July and
5 August. The dedupe key caught all six before a single word was written about them. This
is the expected behaviour of a relevance-ranked source against a date-windowed pipeline,
and it is worth stating plainly that on a thin day Consensus adds nothing to recall; its
value is in the topics PubMed cannot index, not in the recency of what it returns.

\item \textbf{Two mechanistic lung-cancer records from the same Chinese journal issue,
pointing at the same exposure from opposite ends.} Li \& Li review GPER as the receptor
through which PM$_{2.5}$'s estrogen-like organics and metal ions drive MAPK/ERK and
PI3K/AKT signalling; the Wumeng Mountain synthesis assembles the exposure--component--
effect--molecular chain for household smoky-coal lung cancer in the Yunnan--Guizhou
Plateau, where the disease concentrates in \textbf{never-smoking women} with a
\textbf{distinctive driver-gene spectrum} (low classical EGFR 19del/L858R, elevated
G719X, S768I, KRAS G12C, ALK and ROS1 fusions). Both are narrative reviews with no
pooled estimate and no formal search protocol reported in the deposited abstract, so
neither carries evidential weight on its own. What they carry jointly is a testable
claim: that a specific source-resolved PM composition maps to a specific somatic
mutation spectrum. That is the kind of claim source-apportionment-capable distributed
sensing could eventually address and mass-only networks cannot.

\item \textbf{The only intervals in the window are on self-reported exposure
categories.} Aulia Anwar et al.'s North Sumatra case-control (240/240) reports
\textbf{OR 2.53 (95\,\% CI 1.45--4.40)} for an unhealthy indoor environment and
\textbf{OR 1.91 (1.13--3.25)} for occupational exposure. Neither is a measured
particulate concentration --- both are questionnaire categories --- so the forest plot
names the exposure metric on its own axis rather than implying a PM contrast. The
same model's age ORs are demographic covariates and are excluded from the figure for
the reason recorded on 20 and 23 August.

\item \textbf{Two abstract-free Elsevier deposits again, both from \textit{Atmospheric
Environment}.} A machine-learning wind-correction comparison for coupled WRF--CMAQ
(dynamic assimilation vs static replacement) and a particulate PAH dry-deposition study
in the Bohai Sea Strait. Both ship as \textit{Metadata only}. Scholar Gateway was queried
for the wind-correction paper and cannot help --- its corpus ends May 2026 --- and
Consensus has not indexed either. The Elsevier abstract backlog first noted on 8 August
is therefore still open and now spans three weeks.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 2 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Neuberger et al.\ 2026}
{Importance of hydrated aerosol particles for aerosol--fog relationships in the Italian Po Valley}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{FAIRARI 2021/2022 aerosol--fog observations at San Pietro Capofiume, analysed with
$\kappa$--K\"ohler theory and the MIMICA large-eddy simulation. Median hygroscopicity
separated by population: \textbf{fog residuals $\kappa = 0.45$, interstitial particles
0.40, out-of-fog aerosol 0.34}, the gradient reflecting inorganic enrichment in activated
droplets. Excluding hydrated but non-activated particles from the analysis
\textbf{inflated effective diameter by 81\,\% (11.6 $\rightarrow$ 21.0\,$\mu$m) and
depressed droplet number concentration by 87\,\% (97.4 $\rightarrow$ 12.4\,cm$^{-3}$)};
hydrated particles supplied \textbf{21\,\% of liquid water content} and drove
\textbf{36\,\% of sub-kilometre visibility events without any activation}. The LES leg
shows fog prediction hinging on the largest dry particles. The limitation that binds is
site specificity --- one heavily polluted valley over two winters, with a $\kappa$
distribution shaped by Po Valley nitrate that will not transfer to a marine or biomass-
burning regime. For distributed low-cost sensing the transferable point is that the
hydrated/activated distinction is a \emph{composition} problem, so an RH correction
fitted at one site during one season inherits that site's $\kappa$ and no more.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11991-2026}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026c}
{Buffering of atmospheric nanoparticle growth by temperature-dependent shifts in molecular composition, volatility and diffusivity}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{A kinetic multiphase treatment applied to previously unreconciled field and chamber
nanoparticle growth-rate data. The standing puzzle is that observed growth rates are
smaller than theory predicts and \emph{less} sensitive to condensable vapour
concentration than theory predicts. The resolution offered is two antagonistic
temperature dependencies: \textbf{slow surface-to-bulk transport limits vapour uptake by
semi-solid, low-diffusivity particles, while Clausius--Clapeyron shifts in the volatility
distribution enhance growth at low temperature and low concentration}. Netted out, they
buffer the vapour-concentration dependence, which is why measured growth rates converge
on a few nm/h across widely varying conditions. No new measurement is reported --- this
is a reinterpretation of existing datasets, and its persuasiveness rests on the
volatility-distribution parameterisation rather than on independent validation. The
relevance here is upstream of sensing: the 50--100\,nm photochemical mode that Zhong
et al.\ flagged yesterday as invisible to mass-based assessment is produced by exactly
this growth process, and a mechanism that makes its rate insensitive to precursor
loading also makes it insensitive to the emission controls that reduce PM$_{2.5}$ mass.}
{10.5194/acp-26-12037-2026}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 3 records}{Deep}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026d}
{Improved representation of isoprene-derived secondary organic aerosol in CAM6-Chem reveals regional contrasts in its long-term changes over China}
{Atmos Chem Phys}
{An explicit isoprene-SOA formation scheme implemented in CAM6-Chem with high-NO$_x$
gas-phase epoxide precursors and branch-resolved heterogeneous uptake from both low- and
high-NO$_x$ pathways. Against ground observations the update \textbf{improves the SOA
normalised mean bias over China from $-76.7\,\%$ to $-51.6\,\%$} and reproduces the
compositional structure of four explicit ISOA subspecies. Over 2000--2019 the national
mean ISOA trend is weak because regional changes cancel: \textbf{the sharpest increase is
in Southwest China, where rising biogenic isoprene emission is the leading statistical
predictor at a 58.3\,\% regression-based contribution}, with the strongest decrease in
Shaanxi--Gansu--Ningxia. The honest reading of the headline number is that a $-51.6\,\%$
bias is still a factor-of-two underestimate --- the scheme narrows the gap without
closing it, and a regression-based attribution is not a causal decomposition. For
exposure work the consequence is directional: any health study using a CTM-derived
organic-aerosol surface over China is working with a field that is known-low by roughly
half, and non-uniformly so by region.}
{10.5194/acp-26-11967-2026}

\paperentry{Kim et al.\ 2026}
{Application of machine learning-based wind corrections to coupled WRF--CMAQ simulations: a comparison of dynamic assimilation and static replacement}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Crossref by-ISSN sweep; title, authorship and DOI verified, no
abstract deposited. The comparison it names --- injecting an ML-corrected wind field into
CMAQ dynamically during the run versus statically replacing the WRF field beforehand ---
is a live methodological question for anyone producing exposure surfaces, because wind
error is the dominant transport error in urban CTM output and the two strategies have
very different mass-conservation properties. Nothing is asserted about which won. Scholar
Gateway cannot reach it (corpus ends May 2026) and Consensus has not indexed it.
Second-priority abstract retry behind Werderman et al.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122324}

\paperentry{Kang et al.\ 2026}
{Characteristics and dry deposition fluxes of particulate parent, alkyl, and nitro-polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in the Bohai Sea Strait, China}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Crossref sweep, no abstract deposited. Nitro-PAHs are among the
more directly mutagenic particle-bound species and are under-measured relative to the
parent compounds, and a marine-strait dry-deposition budget is a receptor-side
measurement rather than an emission inventory --- but no flux, no concentration and no
sampling period are extractable from the deposited record. Flagged for retry, low
priority relative to the wind-correction paper.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122322}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 2 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Li \& Li 2026}
{Research advances in the pulmonary carcinogenic effects of atmospheric fine particulate matter mediated by GPER}
{Zhongguo Fei Ai Za Zhi}
{Narrative review positioning the G protein-coupled estrogen receptor as the molecular
hub linking PM$_{2.5}$ to lung carcinogenesis. The argument: environmental estrogen-like
organics and metal ions carried on PM$_{2.5}$ activate GPER, which is overexpressed in
lung tumour tissue, and the activated receptor drives \textbf{MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT}
signalling into inflammation, oxidative stress and ferroptosis, with downstream
proliferation, EMT and tumour-microenvironment remodelling. GPER inhibition is proposed
as a target. \textbf{The weaknesses are structural to the format}: no search protocol,
no risk-of-bias assessment, no quantitative synthesis, and a receptor-centric frame that
cannot by construction weigh GPER against the AhR and oxidative-stress pathways it does
not review. Read it as a hypothesis map, not as evidence of pathway dominance. The one
genuinely actionable implication for exposure science is that it assigns the causal role
to specific \emph{components} --- estrogenic organics and transition metals --- rather
than to mass, which is another argument for composition-resolved rather than
gravimetric-equivalent monitoring.}
{10.3779/j.issn.1009-3419.2026.101.13}

\paperentry{Qin et al.\ 2026}
{AlCl$_3$ aerosol exposure is associated with pulmonary lactate accumulation, neutrophil dysfunction, and fibrosis-like remodeling}
{Biofactors}
{Repeated AlCl$_3$ aerosol exposure in rats, with and without the MCT1 inhibitor AZD3965,
plus bone-marrow-derived neutrophils treated with sodium L-lactate, plus a supportive
human cohort of aluminium-exposure-related ILD, non-aluminium ILD and healthy controls
characterised by quantitative CT, spirometry and blood transcriptomics. Exposure produced
pulmonary aluminium deposition, \textbf{lactate accumulation}, oxidative stress, DNA
damage response signalling and collagen-rich fibrosis-like remodelling; in vitro,
high lactate impaired neutrophil chemotaxis, oxidative burst, mitochondrial membrane
potential, calcium signalling and NET responses. AZD3965 partially reversed both.
Al-ILD patients showed greater CT parenchymal abnormality and lower FVC and DLCO than
non-Al-ILD, with innate-immune, cytokine, ECM and NET pathways enriched in blood.
\textbf{No exposure concentration, particle size distribution or duration appears in the
deposited abstract}, so the animal arm cannot be placed on any dose axis, and the human
arm is cross-sectional with no exposure measurement at all --- it supports the mechanism
narratively and cannot test it. Relevant here as occupational-aerosol toxicology and as
a reminder that metal-bearing particulate has organ-level effects that mass concentration
does not index.}
{10.1002/biof.70146}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 2 records}{Teal}

\paperentry{Zhongguo Fei Ai Za Zhi 2026}
{China Wumeng Mountain lung cancer: evidence chain, clinical and molecular characteristics of regional lung cancer related to coal pollution in the Yunnan--Guizhou Plateau}
{Zhongguo Fei Ai Za Zhi}
{Narrative synthesis assembling an
exposure\,$\rightarrow$\,component\,$\rightarrow$\,biological-effect\,$\rightarrow$\,%
clinical-molecular\,$\rightarrow$\,prevention chain for household smoky-coal lung cancer
in Xuanwei/Fuyuan, searching PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, CNKI and Wanfang to May 2026.
Carcinogenic components named: \textbf{PAHs and methylated PAHs, PM$_{2.5}$/black carbon,
inorganic minerals and metals, and carbonyls}, as a mixture. The clinical phenotype is
distinctive --- predominantly \textbf{never-smoking women}, high adenocarcinoma fraction,
young onset, early metastasis, multifocal disease and family aggregation. So is the
molecular one: total EGFR mutation is high but classical 19del and L858R are
\emph{under}-represented, while \textbf{G719X, S768I, KRAS G12C and ALK/ROS1 fusions are
elevated} relative to non-Wumeng lung cancer; ARHGEF5, MYO18B and MUC16 are proposed
germline susceptibility genes interacting with POT1/TERT/TERF2. Sputum microbiome shifts
are reported alongside. \textbf{It is a narrative review by an unnamed author group with
no formal inclusion criteria or bias assessment}, drawing on the same group's prior work,
so the evidence chain is an argument rather than a synthesis. Its value is as the clearest
statement in this corpus of a testable source-composition-to-mutation-spectrum hypothesis.}
{10.3779/j.issn.1009-3419.2026.102.16}

\paperentry{Aulia Anwar et al.\ 2026}
{Smoking, environmental exposures, and chronic lung disease as key risk factors for lung cancer in North Sumatra}
{Exp Oncol}
{Hospital-based case-control across three referral hospitals in North Sumatra,
August--November 2024: \textbf{240 histologically confirmed lung cancers and 240 controls}
without malignancy or chronic respiratory disease, matched on sex and residential area
with age adjusted in the model. Independent predictors: \textbf{unhealthy indoor
environment (poor ventilation, household pollutants) OR 2.53 (95\,\% CI 1.45--4.40)} and
\textbf{occupational exposure OR 1.91 (1.13--3.25)}; tuberculosis and COPD were more
prevalent among cases. \textbf{Three weaknesses threaten the inference and should be
stated first.} The exposure is a self-reported composite category with no measured
particulate concentration, so it is subject to recall bias in a design where cases know
their diagnosis. Controls were selected to be free of chronic respiratory disease, which
is a collider on the pathway from household smoke to both COPD and lung cancer and biases
the household-environment OR upward. And smoking differed between groups at $p = 0.023$
yet does not appear among the adjusted independent predictors --- the deposited abstract
does not explain that discrepancy. Treat 2.53 as an upper bound on a poorly specified
exposure, not as a household-PM effect estimate.}
{10.15407/exp-oncology.2026.02.152}
