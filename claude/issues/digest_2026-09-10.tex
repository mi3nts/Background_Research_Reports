\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Brake dust is not inert ballast --- it is a catalyst,
and that moves non-exhaust PM from a mass-inventory problem to a chemistry problem.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Qi et al.\ (\textit{Nat Commun}) measured SO$_2$ uptake on collected vehicular brake wear
particles and found a coefficient reaching \textbf{2.87$\times$10$^{-5}$} under dark
ambient conditions --- \textbf{orders of magnitude above mineral dust or soot}. The
mechanism they propose is a self-sustaining pairing: oxygen vacancies in
$\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$ activate O$_2$ and H$_2$O into reactive oxygen species and Fe--OH,
while the co-emitted organics and elemental carbon abstract protons and hold SO$_2$ at
carbon defects, so the surface keeps turning over instead of deactivating. Every regulated
fleet is now shifting its vehicular primary PM from tailpipe to tyre and brake, and the
implicit assumption in that transition is that the replacement mass is chemically dull
mineral abrasion. If brake wear catalyses sulfate formation at these rates, then roadside
sulfate is partly made \emph{at} the roadside rather than advected to it, and a
source-apportionment scheme that assigns sulfate to regional secondary inorganic aerosol
will systematically under-credit traffic. The limitation is real and the authors do not
hide it: these are collected particles, handled in a laboratory, probed dry and dark. What
that establishes is a rate, not an ambient flux. But it is a rate large enough that the
burden of proof has moved --- the ambient-irrelevance case now has to be argued rather
than assumed. For distributed sensing the consequence is unwelcome in a familiar way: a
network that reports non-exhaust PM as a mass increment is reporting the least interesting
thing about it.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Indoor and occupational work is the day's largest cluster at 4 records, and
between them they price the mitigable fraction.} A nationwide Chinese survey of 280 gyms
across 32 provinces put natural ventilation at \textbf{OR 0.36} and high-efficiency
filters at \textbf{0.42} against self-reported respiratory symptoms, with air-return air
conditioning at 2.85 and absent equipment disinfection at 3.30 in the other direction. In
rural Tanzania, a redesigned house --- chimney, envelope permeability, raised bedrooms ---
ran \textbf{2.8$\times$ lower indoor PM$_{2.5}$} and 2.4$\times$ lower PM$_{10}$ than local
controls over 77 days. Neither study is strong on its own terms (cross-sectional and
self-reported in the first, seven unrandomised houses in the second), but the engineering
levers they point at are cheap, and the effect sizes are not marginal.

\item \textbf{A chamber study makes the PM$_1$ point that mass-based monitoring keeps
burying.} Burning eleven Turkish and four German cigarette brands in a 2.88\,m$^3$ chamber
gave PM$_{10}$ of 1044--1677\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ and PM$_{2.5}$ of
1043--1675\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ --- the two fractions are the same number to within a
percent, because \textbf{PM$_1$ (994--1548) carries essentially all of it}. Brand
differences did not correlate with tobacco weight, filling density, filter length or rod
length, so whatever drives them was not among the physical parameters measured.

\item \textbf{The day's largest epidemiological signal points the wrong way, and that is
the finding.} In 29{,}603 Chinese health examinees, white-cell counts \emph{fell} with
rising particulate exposure --- $-$0.503$\times$10$^9$/L (95\% CI 0.454--0.552) per IQR of
PM$_{2.5}$ at the six-month window --- against the inflammatory-recruitment model the
authors themselves invoke. A single-hospital population selected on health-seeking is a
plausible source of that inversion, and the paper does not resolve it. Logged with the
direction stated plainly rather than smoothed.

\item \textbf{An index that beats its benchmark on the data it was fitted to.} The
temperature-sensitive AQHI reports excess mortality risk of 22.9 (95\% CI 21.1--24.7)
against the conventional AQHI's 15.0 (13.4--16.7) in Ningbo, and 22.2 (20.7--23.8) against
7.7 (6.3--9.0) in London. Weights are estimated and validated on the same 1{,}461-day
series in each city, with no out-of-sample or forward hold-out described --- so the margin
is an in-sample fit advantage until someone tests it prospectively. Two cities on
different continents is nonetheless a genuine test of the \emph{framework}.

\item \textbf{Two abstract-free same-day deposits again, and one of them matters.} A
12-million-student cohort of PM$_{2.5}$ constituents against incident visual impairment
(\textit{Environ Int}) had no abstract in Crossref per-DOI, Europe PMC, PubMed
\texttt{efetch} or Semantic Scholar on the entry date. It ships as \textsc{metadata only},
asserting nothing, and is flagged for re-reading. The other is a correction notice to a
long-term low-cost-sensor field-calibration paper --- no content of its own, logged so a
later reader of that record knows the coefficients moved.

\end{itemize}

\paperentry{4HAIE hair cortisol 2026}
{Intensity-dependent associations of physical activity and cardiorespiratory fitness with hair cortisol concentrations in the context of lifetime air pollution exposure}
{Stress}
{133 adults (87\% women) from the 4HAIE industrial-environment study. \band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 1 record}{Teal}

\paperentry{China WBC homeostasis 2026}
{Long-term exposure to ambient air pollution and circulating white blood cell homeostasis among adults in China: Time-window-specific associations and the mediating role of fasting blood glucose}
{Environ Pollut}
{29,603 adults with routine health examinations at Liangshan General Hospital, 2016-2024, pollutants assigned to geocoded residences. Associations were strongest at the six-month window and were negative for every particulate pollutant: per IQR, WBC fell 0.503 x10\textasciicircum{}9/L (95\% CI 0.454-0.552) for PM$_{2.5}$ and 0.349 (0.307-0.391) for PM$_{10}$, while O$_3$ was positively associated. \band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 3 records}{Sky}

\paperentry{Korea paediatric severity IV 2026}
{Air pollution drives concentrated clinical severity of respiratory illness in early childhood}
{medRxiv}
{PREPRINT. Nationwide claims for 3,210,381 children, 99,261,945 child-months, 2015-2019, with thermal inversions used as a quasi-random instrument for monthly PM$_{2.5}$. A 1-SD rise (7.23 $\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$) adds 0.29 respiratory visits per child-month at age 1, significant through age 6; expenditure rises 46.7\%, 43.0\% and 35.4\% at ages 0, 1 and 2 and is indistinguishable from zero past age 4, giving a 32.2\% increase in annual respiratory spending under age 5. \paperentry{Brake wear SO$_2$ uptake 2026}
{Unexpectedly rapid SO$_2$ heterogeneous reaction on the surface of vehicular brake wear particles}
{Nat Commun}
