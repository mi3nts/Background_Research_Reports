\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Two Sensirion modules, calibrated where they are used, held to $\le$22\,\% error against gravimetry indoors and out and $\le$15\,\% expanded uncertainty outdoors --- and stayed stable through repeated kitchen-smoke events above 1000\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, the manufacturer's ceiling.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Arba Minch University and Lund University, \textit{Atmospheric Measurement Techniques}; SPS30 and SEN55 co-located indoors and outdoors in Arba Minch (Ethiopia) against gravimetry, plus a Swedish outdoor set against a Palas FIDAS for humidity. SEN55 read 6--10\,\% above SPS30; with that offset removed the two were interchangeable (CV $\le$7.7\,\%, between-sampler uncertainty $\le$1.7\,$\mu$g/m$^3$). SEN55 truncates digitally at 6553.4\,$\mu$g/m$^3$; readings between 1000 and that limit still carried mass information. The RH effect was small for both.
\textbf{Where it is weak:} the abstract gives neither the number of gravimetric samples nor the RH range, and ``calibrated under circumstances of use'' means the accuracy figures are post-calibration, not out of the box.
\textbf{Why it matters here:} the 11 November issue carried 923 Ethiopian kitchens measured on uncorrected PurpleAir units. This is the missing local calibration evidence for the same setting --- with a different sensor family.\par
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Network QA for smoke.} University of Washington: an entropy-based rule and a gradient-boosted classifier flag faulty PM$_{2.5}$ sensors across mixed-vendor North American networks during 11 smoke events (2024--2025), when real swings mimic faults. Faults were simulated, and the abstract gives no detection rates.

\item \textbf{Ghana at 1\,km, validated on sensors.} Lamont--Doherty/Columbia: daily PM$_{2.5}$ 2005--2025 from XGBoost on monitors, satellite and reanalysis, evaluated against reference monitors and a calibrated low-cost network; the north--south gap widens by about 0.15\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ per year. A companion all-hour 5\,km AOD product for China falls from R\,=\,0.95 in cross-validation to 0.61 against MODIS.

\item \textbf{A new short-term endpoint.} Beijing, 39,015 endometriosis outpatient visits: PM$_{2.5}$ lag 0--7 RR 1.109 (1.044--1.177), PM$_{10}$ lag 0--6 RR 1.070 (1.014--1.128), increments unstated; the additive interaction with heat ran through ozone, not PM. Indoor PM$_{10}$ and adolescent asthma in REPRO\_PL: OR 2.22 (0.88--5.57) on one 24-h sample in 172 homes.

\item \textbf{Exhaust is no longer the truck problem.} Southern California: brake and tyre wear has dominated heavy-truck PM$_{2.5}$ emissions since 2022 and reaches 92\,\% by 2045 under the zero-emission rules. Separately, mice inhaling PM$_{2.5}$ at 5 or 35\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ for 180 days developed arterial stiffening and calcification --- an ambient-level dose, unusual in this literature.

\end{itemize}

\paperentry{Aberdeen 324 older adults random forest 457 features blood imaging pollution poor cognition MCH}
{Machine learning identifies routine blood tests as accurate predictive measures of pollution-dependent poor cognitive function.}
{Sci Rep}
{Random forest over 457 imaging, blood, socioeconomic and pollutant features in 324 neurologically healthy adults (61.6 +/- 4.8 years) classified general-cognition tertiles with 0.70 accuracy. Pollution measures, particularly lead, CO and particulate matter, consistently improved model performance; low mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration was associated with high pollutant levels and both extremes of MCHC with poor immediate recall (p=0.025). Feature importance is not an effect estimate, the exposure assignment and particulate metric are not described, and with 457 features on 324 people overfitting is a live risk despite cross-validation. Relevance: indirect - hypothesis-generating link between pollution, anaemia markers and cognition; no PM increment or measurement detail.}
{10.1038/s41598-026-64389-9}

\paperentry{Duke systematic review 27 studies environmental exposures brain MRI cognitively healthy older adults PM$_{2.5}$ NO$_2$ greenspace}
{Environmental Exposures and Brain Magnetic Resonance Imaging Phenotypes in Cognitively Healthy Ageing: A Systematic Review}
{PsyArXiv}
{Preprint systematic review (MEDLINE, Web of Science to 23 Feb 2026): 27 studies, more than 84,000 cognitively healthy adults aged 65+ in ten countries; 13 cross-sectional, 14 cohort, of which only five longitudinal. Higher PM$_{2.5}$, NO$_2$ and PM$_{10}$ were associated with smaller total, grey and white matter volumes, more white-matter hyperintensities, cortical thinning and smaller medial temporal and hippocampal volumes; greenspace findings were favourable but inconsistent. Synthesis was by effect direction, without pooling. Outcome assessment was biased in 12 of 27 studies and four of five longitudinal studies had inadequate follow-up, so vote-counting over mostly cross-sectional designs cannot establish direction of effect. Relevance: indirect - neuroimaging endpoints with no exposure-method stratification; measurement error is not assessed.}
{10.31234/osf.io/yj7xw}

\paperentry{Hohhot 508077 CVD visits 2016-2024 temperature x six pollutants polynomial ridge quasi-Poisson random forest}
{Short-Term Interactive Effects of Temperature and Air Pollution on Cardiovascular Visits: A Machine-Learning Study in Hohhot, China.}
{Cardiol Res Pract}
{508,077 cardiovascular visits to tertiary hospitals in Hohhot (2016-2024) linked to daily temperature and six pollutants; polynomial ridge regression for interactions, quasi-Poisson for lags, random forest for prediction (cross-validated R2 0.741). {J Phys Act Health}
{Network meta-analysis of 26 controlled trials (344 healthy adults) comparing acute exercise under heat, cold, hypoxia and air pollution against matched controls.
