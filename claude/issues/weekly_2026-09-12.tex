\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}The week in one paragraph}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Sensing led a week for the first time since
mid-August, the quantitative epidemiology came back hard after last week's floor --- and
one in six records this week has no abstract in existence anywhere. The pipeline's
best-performing leg and its worst failure mode are now the same leg.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{Scale.} 126 records over seven issues on daily totals of 15, 20, 18, 26, 16, 10
and \textbf{21}, a far flatter week than the 5.4$\times$ range of the last one. Against
recent rollups: 126 after 150, 109, 112 and 134 --- squarely mid-range, and the smoothness
matters more than the level, because week-over-week cluster movements below are not being
read through a single 38-record deposit day.

\textbf{\textit{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation} at 31 is the largest cluster, and
that is the week's structural news.} For three consecutive weeks \textit{Exposure
assessment \& modelling} had led; it has now halved, \textbf{47 $\rightarrow$ 20}, the
sharpest single-cluster contraction this watch has recorded, while Sensing fell only
34 $\rightarrow$ 31. The measurement side as a whole went \textbf{54\% $\rightarrow$
40\%}, so the two clusters did not move together: modelling-side exposure work
collapsed and instrumentation held.

\textbf{Estimate density recovered to one per 2.7 records.} 47 pooled estimates against
126 records, from a four-week series of 2.7, 2.9, 4.4, \textbf{2.7}. Last week's rollup
flagged 4.4 as the weakest on record and declined to say whether it was a real thinning
or a measurement-heavy artefact. One week later it is back to the series norm, which
settles it: \textbf{it was the artefact}. The week's quantitative spine is cardiometabolic
--- CHARLS coexposure, the Zhongshan AMI case-crossover, the Shijiazhuang AECOPD series,
the Hangzhou aortic-dissection component analysis, the Korean 8.25-million asthma cohort
and the Kadoorie fracture cohort.

\textbf{The abstract-free problem is now large enough to be a finding about the corpus.}
\textbf{20 of 126 records (16\%)} shipped as \textsc{metadata only}, up from 11\% last
week, and \textbf{10 of those 20 are in \textit{Sensing}} --- the pooled heatmap's densest
cell is \textit{Sensing} $\times$ \textit{Metadata only}. Crossref-by-ISSN is the only leg
that reaches \textit{J. Aerosol Sci.}, \textit{Atmos. Res.}, \textit{AMT} and \textit{ACP},
and it indexes a DOI days before the publisher deposits the abstract. So the leg that makes
the instrumentation literature visible at all is the same leg that delivers it unreadable.
\textbf{An abstract-retry pass over the standing list is the highest-value pipeline work
outstanding} --- it would convert roughly a sixth of the week from title-only to summarised.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Subtopic trend and inflection}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\noindent\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{\FIGDIR/w1_subtopic_trend.png}
\figcap{Per-issue subtopic counts across the week from \texttt{state/metrics.csv}, seven
issues. Daily totals were 15, 20, 18, 26, 16, 10 and 21 --- a 2.6$\times$ range against
5.4$\times$ last week, so the per-cluster lines are readable as movement rather than as
denominator noise. Two of the seven days are weekends (6 and 12 September) and both are
PubMed-suppressed: 12 September admitted \textbf{26 records registry-wide} against 1{,}135
on 8 September. Those two days were carried almost entirely by Europe PMC and
Crossref-by-ISSN, which is why the \textit{Sensing} line does not dip at the weekend the
way the health clusters do.}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Expanding.} \textit{Burden, policy \& mitigation} \textbf{7 $\rightarrow$ 14}
doubles off last week's recorded floor and is the week's largest proportional gain.
\textit{Respiratory \& allergic} \textbf{5 $\rightarrow$ 12} likewise more than doubles
from a floor. \textit{Neuro / mental health} \textbf{4 $\rightarrow$ 7} and
\textit{Cardiovascular \& metabolic} \textbf{7 $\rightarrow$ 8} both continue upward.
Read together, the health side recovered as a bloc from a single unusually thin week ---
which is the strongest evidence available that last week's health-side floor was a
sampling artefact and not a turn in the field.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{Contracting.} \textit{Exposure assessment \& modelling} \textbf{47 $\rightarrow$
20} is a halving from a cluster record, and no interpretation of it should outrun that
fact: a record high is the most likely thing to be followed by a fall. \textit{Occupational
\& indoor} \textbf{19 $\rightarrow$ 15} eases off last week's surge but stays well above
the pre-surge regular-week maximum of 9 --- \textbf{the indoor turn the 5 September rollup
declined to call a trend has now held for a second week}, which was the stated test.
\textit{Mechanistic toxicology} \textbf{11 $\rightarrow$ 9} and \textit{Sensing}
\textbf{34 $\rightarrow$ 31} are both mild.

\vspace{1.2mm}
\textbf{The inflection worth naming.} The four-week measurement-side share now reads
41, 47, 54, \textbf{40\%} --- it oscillates, it does not trend, and the 29 August rollup's
warning against building a claim on it stands. What has changed is \emph{within} the
measurement side: \textit{Exposure} and \textit{Sensing} moved in opposite directions
relative to each other for the first time in four weeks. One week is not a pattern. The
concrete thing to watch next week is whether \textit{Exposure} returns to the mid-20s,
which would confirm this week as the correction of a record rather than a shift.

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Venues, trial watch, and what the pipeline did}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\textbf{Venues.} Far less concentrated than last week: \textit{Environ Pollut}
\textbf{10 of 126} leads, against \textit{Environ Sci Technol}'s 23 of 150 a week ago.
\textit{Front Public Health} (7), \textit{Environ Res} (6) and \textit{Atmos Chem Phys}
(6) follow, then \textit{Atmosphere} (5) and \textit{J Aerosol Sci} (4). The venue table
is a harvesting artefact as much as an editorial one --- titles on the Crossref by-ISSN
list enter at their own publication rate, while journals reached only through PubMed enter
at PubMed's cadence, and this week PubMed's cadence included two near-dead weekend days.
\textbf{Preprints supplied 8 records} --- 4 Research Square, 4 Preprints.org --- all
labelled as such in the register.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Recurring authors.} Unchanged and unresolvable: surname clustering is dominated by
high-frequency Chinese surnames, which is a naming artefact rather than a research-group
signal, and this watch still has no author-disambiguation or affiliation field.
\textbf{No repeat-group cluster is claimed for this week.} Adding a first-author
affiliation field remains the open item that would make this section mean anything; it has
been on the list since 3 September and is now the oldest unaddressed schema gap.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{Trial watch --- a genuine zero week, the first this watch has recorded.}
ClinicalTrials.gov returned \textbf{0 PM or air-pollution registrations or updates across
6--12 September}, against \textbf{4{,}413 studies updated registry-wide} in the same
window. That control matters because the 5 September rollup established that weekend zeros
are \emph{structural} --- the registry posts nothing on Saturdays and Sundays --- and could
not, on weekend data alone, distinguish a quiet registry from a quiet field. A seven-day
window containing five active weekdays and 4{,}413 registry-wide updates removes that
ambiguity: \textbf{this is a topical zero}. Against one new interventional registration
and two updates last week, it is a real if unremarkable fluctuation, and one week of it
means nothing on its own. \texttt{trials.json} still holds \textbf{26 trials}, now across
\textbf{12 windows}. \texttt{analyze\_endpoints} was not run --- there was nothing to run
it on.

\vspace{1.5mm}
\textbf{What the harvesting legs did.} \textbf{Crossref-by-ISSN was the week's workhorse
and its liability}, carrying the instrumentation literature PubMed cannot see and
delivering 10 of its \textit{Sensing} records without abstracts. \textbf{PubMed} carried
five of seven days and was effectively absent on both weekend days --- 26 records
registry-wide on 12 September is the lowest single-day figure this watch has measured, and
the 6 September Sunday behaved the same way. \textbf{Europe PMC} was the quiet success,
returning 17 on 12 September alone including two Research Square preprints, and it is now
the leg that keeps weekend issues from collapsing. \textbf{OpenAlex returned HTTP 429 on
every day of the week}, unchanged since 28 July, and remains a dead leg. \textbf{arXiv}
returned 429 or nothing in window all week and contributed no record to any issue in this
period --- second consecutive week of that, and the query is now overdue for inspection.
\textbf{Consensus returned a nil every day}, and from 9 September onward the nil has been
verified the correct way, on Crossref \texttt{created} date rather than publication date;
its hits are consistently the right literature with dates years outside the window.

% -------------------------------------------------------------- gaps
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Twenty records this week assert no finding.} 16\% of the corpus is
title-only, half of it in \textit{Sensing}. This is the single largest recoverable loss in
the pipeline and the fix is mechanical: a scheduled retry pass over the standing
abstract-retry list, re-querying Crossref per DOI, Europe PMC and PubMed a week after
first sight, when the publisher deposit has landed. Nothing about it requires new sources.

\item \textbf{Five of 51 measurement-side records carry a health endpoint}, for the
seventh consecutive week. This remains the longest-standing structural finding in the
series, and it is now supported by 51 records in a single week rather than by a handful.

\item \textbf{Ultrafine and size-resolved number: 3 of 126.} The strongest sensing record
of the week --- a community CPC matching a research-grade reference at $R^2>0.99$ with a
3\% inter-unit coefficient of variation --- exists precisely to make that metric
affordable. The instrument is arriving faster than the studies that would use it.

\item \textbf{Two interventional records in 126}, and neither tests a monitoring
technology. The engineering turn in the indoor cluster is still being evaluated against
chambers and models rather than against health outcomes in occupied buildings.

\item \textbf{Pipeline defects carried forward}: unnormalised geography labels (which
inflate the \textit{Global / multi-region} bar in f3), the \texttt{Cancer}/\texttt{Oncologic}
split in \texttt{ENDPOINT\_CANON}, no first-author affiliation field, and a 6-element
\texttt{LIFECOURSE} array in the 2026-08-03 corpus where the schema requires 5 --- outside
this window, but it will corrupt any rollup that spans it, including the September monthly.
\end{itemize}

{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Provenance and method}\\[1.2mm]
{\fontsize{7.2}{8.8}\selectfont
Period \textbf{2026-09-06 to 2026-09-12}, seven daily issues pooled.
\textbf{This rollup queried no literature API.} It aggregates \texttt{state/corpus/*.json},
\texttt{state/metrics.csv} and \texttt{state/trials.json} over
\texttt{PMRW\_START}--\texttt{PMRW\_END}; every record was screened, summarised and
DOI-gated at daily-issue time and is reproduced verbatim. The single exception is the
trial watch, which was queried once for the whole window against ClinicalTrials.gov with a
no-term control, because no daily window in this period had logged one.
\textbf{126 records, 126 unique DOIs} --- no duplicate publication this period. Daily
totals: 15, 20, 18, 26, 16, 10, 21. \textbf{Week-over-week comparisons} are against the
30 August--5 September rollup (150 records), with 23--29 August (109), 16--22 August (112)
and 9--15 August (134) used where a longer run is cited; the 26 July--1 August week is
excluded from "record" claims because it contains the founding retrospective back-scan.
\textbf{Effect estimates} are pooled as recorded, on heterogeneous contrasts, with no
meta-analysis and no heterogeneity statistic; several are co-pollutant rather than PM and
are labelled as such on the plot. \textbf{Figures} are regenerated over the pooled range,
not composited from the daily images. \textbf{Labels} --- subtopic, design, particle-metric,
geography --- are single-label by dominant contribution, assigned at daily-issue time, and
are a judgement rather than a controlled vocabulary.\par}
\end{keybox}
