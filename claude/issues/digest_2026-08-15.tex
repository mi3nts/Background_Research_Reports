\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Two large cohorts extend PM$_{2.5}$ dose--response
into South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa on the same day, and both rest entirely on modelled
exposure in the regions where the models are least validated.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Mandal et al.\ (\textit{Kidney Int Rep}) follow 12{,}271 adults in Chennai and Delhi across
three eGFR measurements and report \textbf{$-$0.32 (95\% CI $-$0.49 to $-$0.15) and
$-$0.42 ($-$0.57 to $-$0.27)\,ml/min/1.73\,m$^2$ per 5\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} of annual PM$_{2.5}$.
Moro et al.\ (\textit{Environ Res}) find \textbf{OR 1.06 (1.02--1.11) per
1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ for hypertension} in 1{,}833 rural Ghanaian adults, PAF 18.6\%, with no
association for metabolic syndrome or any other component.

Both are worth having --- renal and sub-Saharan cardiometabolic endpoints are genuine
evidence gaps. But the exposure in Mandal is a 1\,km national spatiotemporal model at
Delhi annual means of \textbf{118--130\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, and in Moro a satellite-derived
model over rural Ghana. Neither region has the reference-monitor density that calibrated
the modelling approaches these estimates inherit, and neither paper reports out-of-sample
model error at the exposure levels it is used at. A per-unit odds ratio of 1.06 is
recoverable only if the exposure contrast is measured to better than a unit. That is a
measurement claim, not an epidemiological one, and it is unaddressed in both papers ---
which is precisely the argument for dense low-cost networks in exactly these settings.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A community-run mobile LCS campaign reports its own year-to-year model
instability.} Cho et al.\ ran two summers of community-scientist mobile PM$_{2.5}$
monitoring in Norwood, Ohio, then fitted street-level land-use regression. Cross-validated
$R^2$ went from \textbf{0.17--0.25 in 2020 to 0.55--0.61 in 2021} on the same streets with
the same protocol. The paper reads the difference as pandemic-phase source recovery; it is
equally consistent with 2020 having too little exposure contrast for LUR to resolve.
Either way, publishing both years rather than the good one is the right call.

\item \textbf{Combined cold-spell and heavy-pollution exposure is supra-additive for
circulatory mortality.} Zhang et al.\ pooled 70 districts across the Beijing--Tianjin--Hebei
region, cold seasons 2013--2018. \textbf{CRR 1.35 (1.14--1.60)} for the overlapping event
against \textbf{1.14 (1.09--1.20)} for cold spell alone and \textbf{1.08 (1.00--1.16)} for
heavy pollution alone --- the last of which only touches unity. Two lagged
single-exposure warnings would each have under-called the combined day.

\item \textbf{A continuous HNO$_3$/particulate-nitrate method at sub-0.15\,$\mu$g/m$^3$
detection limits.} Alexa et al.\ pair a wet diffusion denuder with a continuous aerosol
sampler and chemiluminescence detection, recording at 1\,s intervals, with LODs of
\textbf{0.13\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ for HNO$_3$ and 0.10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ for NO$_3^-$}, validated
against filter/denuder reference sampling across Brno winter and summer campaigns. Nitrate
partitioning is exactly the fast process filter methods average away and low-cost optical
sensors cannot see at all.

\item \textbf{Twelve French sites, seven years, one harmonised PMF --- and the CTM is
systematically biased.} Chebaicheb et al.\ apply harmonised rolling PMF to ACSM organic
aerosol at 12 (sub)urban sites. Primary OA factors are $\approx$33\% of OA mass; oxygenated
OA dominates everywhere; wintertime LO-OOA tracks BBOA, implicating residential biomass
burning in secondary formation. Comparison against CHIMERE shows systematic component-level
bias. This is the reference dataset any speciated-PM epidemiology in France should be built
against.

\item \textbf{Nine of twenty-one records carry no health endpoint.} At 43\% that share is
flat against 12--14 August (46\%, 41\%, 43\%) and the split is now a stable property of the
corpus rather than a daily fluctuation. Every \textit{Sensing} and \textit{Exposure} record
measures particles without measuring a person; every cohort record models exposure without
measuring particles. Unlike 14 August, no record today measured both on the same subjects.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 4 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Cho et al.\ 2026}
{Greenspace, land use and PM$_{2.5}$ across pandemic phases: a community scientist-led mobile sensing campaign}
{Sci Total Environ}
{Community scientists ran low-cost mobile PM$_{2.5}$ monitoring across Norwood, Ohio, for
two summer periods --- the initial pandemic phase (2020) and the reopening phase (2021) ---
with a meteorology-adjusted difference-in-differences framework for the year contrast and
street-level linear land-use regression for spatial structure. \textbf{Higher 2021
concentrations were not fully explained by meteorology.} In the 2021 afternoon model,
PM$_{2.5}$ was elevated within 50\,m of office and manufacturing land use and inversely
associated with NDVI within 100\,m. \textbf{Cross-validated $R^2$ 0.17--0.25 (2020) rising
to 0.55--0.61 (2021).} The paper attributes the gap to source recovery, but a
low-contrast summer is an equally good explanation and is not tested. Health-survey scores
by ward are reported alongside but are not linked to measured exposure at the individual
level. The transferable part is the QC and sampling protocol for neighbourhoods with no
regulatory coverage, which is stated explicitly as the deliverable.}
{10.1016/j.scitotenv.2026.182124}

\paperentry{Alexa et al.\ 2026}
{Simultaneous continuous determination of gaseous HNO$_3$ and particulate nitrate by chemiluminescence}
{Talanta}
{Gaseous HNO$_3$ is sampled by wet diffusion denuder and particulate NO$_3^-$ in parallel
by continuous aerosol sampler, both analysed online by continuous flow analysers with
chemiluminescence detection --- no preconcentration step, signal recorded at 1\,s
intervals. Detection limits (3\,S/N) are \textbf{5.1\,nM and 12.8\,nM in solution,
corresponding to 0.13\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ (49.7\,ppt) for HNO$_3$ and 0.10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ for
NO$_3^-$}. Applied to urban air in Brno across winter and summer campaigns 2022--2023 and
compared against filter sampling with NaF-coated dry denuders. The gas--particle
partitioning of nitrate is temperature- and humidity-driven on timescales of minutes, which
filter-based methods average away entirely and optical low-cost sensors cannot resolve at
all; a method at this time resolution is the reference any nitrate-aware correction scheme
would need. The abstract reports no field intercomparison statistics against the reference
method, only that the comparison was made.}
{10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130457}

\paperentry{Kafi et al.\ 2026}
{Evaluating the suitability of Sentinel-5P data for air quality monitoring in Tehran}
{Sci Total Environ}
{Six years (2019--2024) of TROPOMI Level-2 CO, NO$_2$, SO$_2$, O$_3$ and UV Aerosol Index
against 22 ground stations in a megacity with complex topography, assessed by Pearson and
Spearman correlation and Kling--Gupta Efficiency, plus a spatiotemporal trajectory
comparison. \textbf{Only NO$_2$ showed strong agreement; CO, SO$_2$ and O$_3$ were weak},
and the trajectory analysis diverged as well. The authors are explicit that UVAI represents
column aerosol loading rather than surface particulate concentration and warn against
reading it as PM --- a caveat routinely ignored in the satellite-PM literature and worth
citing for that reason alone. The diagnosis (coarse pixel size against a topographically
constrained urban basin) is plausible but not separated from the competing explanation of
station siting, and no vertical-profile or boundary-layer-height information is brought in
to test it.}
{10.1016/j.scitotenv.2026.182069}

\paperentry{Aparicio et al.\ 2026}
{Sensor system design and deployment for environmental and visitor monitoring in cultural heritage sites}
{Sensors (Basel)}
{Design, build and field validation of low-power low-cost nodes across five pilot sites in
four European countries under the EU ARGUS project, in two configurations: static nodes at
fixed positions and dynamic nodes carried by a quadruped robot or drone. Payload spans
thermal--humidity probes, volumetric water content, wind, pollution monitors and two
generations of visitor-counting devices, with Baltan\'as used as the iterative prototyping
site before wider deployment. Carried for the mobile-platform architecture --- a
sensor-on-robot deployment pattern that is directly transferable to indoor and
semi-enclosed PM mapping --- rather than for any air-quality result. \textbf{The abstract
reports no PM performance figures, no reference co-location and no calibration procedure},
which is the whole question for a low-cost node, so nothing here constrains data quality.
Tier C on that basis.}
{10.3390/s26154941}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 3 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Chebaicheb et al.\ 2026}
{A phenomenology of submicron organic aerosol components in urban France}
{Environ Int}
{The first nationwide harmonised organic-aerosol source apportionment for France:
time-resolved Aerosol Chemical Speciation Monitor measurements at 12 (sub)urban sites over
one to seven years each, with a single rolling PMF protocol applied under common
constraints and acceptance criteria across all datasets. Primary factors --- hydrocarbon-like,
biomass-burning and cooking-like OA --- account for \textbf{$\approx$33\% of OA mass};
oxygenated OA dominates at every site and splits into less- and more-oxidised components
everywhere except Poitiers and Strasbourg. Site-specific factors resolved a mixed
shipping/industrial component at Marseille Longchamp and an amine-related component at
Strasbourg and Creil. Wintertime LO-OOA correlates with BBOA, implicating residential
combustion in secondary as well as primary mass; summer LO-OOA tracks biogenic precursors.
\textbf{CHIMERE shows systematic component-level bias against the observations.} The
harmonisation is the contribution --- rolling PMF applied inconsistently is why the existing
European OA literature does not pool --- and the limitation is that (sub)urban sites only
cannot constrain the rural background these factors are transported through.}
{10.1016/j.envint.2026.110453}

\paperentry{Sui et al.\ 2026}
{Contrasting annual variation of organic and elemental carbon in the Pearl River Delta}
{J Environ Manage}
{Three years of continuous OC/EC observation (2019--2021) spanning the COVID-19 lockdown,
with PMF source apportionment. OC declined steadily, driven by suppressed secondary organic
carbon formation in the PRD's NOx-rich regime --- a relationship the authors report as
\emph{reversing} during lockdown when NOx fell sharply, which is the interesting result and
the one that identifies the regime. EC was flat or slightly rising, so fossil combustion
from industry and mobile sources has not responded to the control programme.
\textbf{Fossil-related emissions are $\approx$70\% of carbonaceous aerosol} and that share
did not fall over the period. A single-site three-year record cannot separate emission
change from meteorological variability, and no such adjustment is described; the lockdown
period is doing most of the causal work and is a one-off natural experiment with a
confounded meteorology.}
{10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.130708}

\paperentry{You et al.\ 2026}
{Satellite-based estimation of ambient ammonia and emission inventory uncertainty in South Korea}
{Environ Pollut}
{Monthly ground-level NH$_3$ at 15\,km resolution for 2013--2017, from Cross-track Infrared
Sounder retrievals refined by a two-stage framework: a linear mixed-effects model carrying
meteorology plus the Clean Air Policy Support System inventory, then a generalised additive
model over the LMM residuals. \textbf{Cross-validated $R^2$ = 0.73, MAE 0.15, RMSE 0.21}.
Concentrations peak in agricultural areas March--June; temperature is the dominant
meteorological control (\textbf{+18.9\% per +1\,$^{\circ}$C in June}), with relative
humidity and wind speed strongest in March. The GAM residual surface identifies
agricultural hotspots the inventory does not contain, which is the paper's actual claim ---
inventory gaps, not model skill. NH$_3$ matters here as the limiting precursor for ammonium
nitrate and sulfate, so a 15\,km monthly NH$_3$ field is a plausible predictor for
secondary PM$_{2.5}$ models, but the resolution is far coarser than the sources, and
validation against sparse ground NH$_3$ is circular with the sparsity that motivated the
work.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.128903}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 3 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Mahmutovic Persson et al.\ 2026}
{Chronic aerosolized PM$_{2.5}$, plasma proteins and coronary S1P response in rats}
{Environ Res}
{Well-characterised urban PM$_{2.5}$ was re-aerosolised and delivered to rats under two
regimes designed to separate an acute high dose from a chronic lower dose at comparable
cumulative burden, with coronary vasomotor tone measured ex vivo by wire myograph and
systemic effects read from plasma sphingosine-1-phosphate and endothelin-1 plus
immunohistochemistry. \textbf{Only the chronic regime moved anything}: altered S1P vasomotor
response in coronary arteries and significantly decreased plasma total protein and ET-1.
The design is the point --- most PM inhalation toxicology uses acute high-dose exposure
because it is tractable, and this says that route can miss the effect entirely. Re-aerosolised
collected PM is not fresh ambient aerosol (semivolatiles are lost, morphology changes), the
ET-1 \emph{decrease} is not obviously in the direction the vascular-dysfunction hypothesis
predicts and is not reconciled, and no n or dose figures appear in the abstract.}
{10.1016/j.envres.2026.125391}

\paperentry{Seymore et al.\ 2026}
{Gestational nanoparticle inhalation alters placental glucose transporters and increases glucose flux}
{Toxicol Appl Pharmacol}
{Pregnant Sprague Dawley rats exposed by whole-body inhalation to TiO$_2$ nanoparticles at
\textbf{9.8 $\pm$ 1.0\,mg/m$^3$}, an occupational-level concentration, from gestational day
5 to 19; GLUT1/3/4 assayed in term placenta at GD20 and ex vivo placental perfusion used as
a functional test. Glut3 mRNA and GLUT1 protein fell, but GLUT4 expression and membrane
localisation of both GLUT1 and GLUT4 rose, and perfusion confirmed \textbf{increased}
glucose flux (AUC 95\% CI 77.4--127.5 exposed vs 39.1--73.6 control). The authors state
plainly that this contradicts their hypothesis. Read as a compensatory adaptation rather
than a benign finding --- upregulated placental glucose delivery is not obviously protective
in a fetal-growth-restriction model. The dose is roughly three orders of magnitude above
ambient PM$_{2.5}$ and the particle is a single engineered material, so this constrains
occupational nanomaterial exposure and says little directly about ambient PM.}
{10.1016/j.taap.2026.117996}

\paperentry{Chen et al.\ 2026}
{Gut microbiota signatures reflect PM$_{2.5}$-exacerbated asthma in a murine model}
{Allergy}
{\textbf{Metadata-only: a research letter deposited without an abstract, so nothing below the
title is verifiable from the record.} Registered here because the gut--lung axis in
PM-exacerbated allergic airway disease is a live mechanistic question and because the author
group (Academia Sinica Air Quality Research Center with NTU clinical immunology) pairs
characterised ambient aerosol with a murine model, which is unusual. No finding, effect size
or exposure characterisation is claimed by this issue, and the record supports no figure or
statistic in the analytics. Flagged for retrieval once the full text is indexed.}
{10.1111/all.70485}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 3 records}{Teal}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026}
{Combined cold-spell and heavy-pollution exposure and circulatory mortality, Beijing--Tianjin--Hebei}
{Zhonghua Yu Fang Yi Xue Za Zhi}
{Daily mortality, PM$_{2.5}$ and meteorology from 70 districts and counties, cold seasons
(November--March) 2013--2018, mean daily PM$_{2.5}$ \textbf{98.58 $\pm$ 78.19\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}.
Three combined-exposure scenarios were constructed --- overlapping cold spell and heavy
pollution (A), a standalone pollution event within 7\,d of a cold spell ending (B), and a
standalone cold spell within 7\,d of a pollution event ending (C) --- with district-level
DLNMs pooled by random-effects meta-analysis. The overlapping event gave \textbf{CRR 1.35
(1.14--1.60)} against \textbf{1.14 (1.09--1.20)} for cold spell alone and \textbf{1.08
(1.00--1.16)} for heavy pollution alone. Supra-additivity is the substantive result, and it
is the case for joint rather than single-hazard alerting. Heavy pollution is defined by a
concentration threshold, not by composition, so the interaction could equally be a
composition effect: winter heating episodes in this region differ chemically from other
high-mass days, and the design cannot separate the two.}
{10.3760/cma.j.cn112150-20260105-00006}

\paperentry{Moro et al.\ 2026}
{Long-term PM$_{2.5}$, hypertension and metabolic syndrome in rural Ghana}
{Environ Res}
{Cross-sectional analysis of 1{,}833 AWI-Gen participants aged 40--60 in rural Ghana, with
2007--2015 mean PM$_{2.5}$ from a satellite-based model and MetS by harmonised criteria,
under sequentially adjusted multivariable logistic regression. \textbf{OR 1.06 (1.02--1.11)
per 1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ for hypertension}, a monotone quartile gradient ($p_{\text{trend}}$ =
0.003) with the top quartile at 50\% greater odds, and \textbf{PAF 18.6\%}. \textbf{No
association with MetS overall or with any other component} --- the null is as informative
as the positive, and the authors report it rather than burying it. Three limitations bound
the inference: the design is cross-sectional so temporality is not established; exposure is
modelled at satellite resolution over a region with almost no reference monitoring and no
local validation is reported; and household air pollution from solid-fuel cooking, which
dominates personal exposure in this setting, is not in the model at all.}
{10.1016/j.envres.2026.125484}

\paperentry{Ting 2026}
{Statistical fragility and methodological constraints in metabolomic mediation of air pollution and cardiovascular risk}
{JACC Adv}
{\textbf{Metadata-only: correspondence deposited without an abstract.} Registered because it
completes an exchange this watch is already carrying --- the authors' reply
(\texttt{10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103149}) surfaced in the 12 August window as an
Elsevier metadata-only record and remains unresolved. The subject, statistical fragility of
mediation estimates in air-pollution metabolomics, is directly relevant: mediation analysis
on high-dimensional metabolite panels with modest cohort sizes produces indirect effects
whose confidence intervals are dominated by the product-of-coefficients variance, and the
literature routinely reports them as though they were primary. No numeric claim is made from
this record. Retrieve both letters together once full text is available.}
{10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103150}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 3 records}{Coral}

\paperentry{Liu M.\ et al.\ 2026}
{Divergent near-surface ozone and NO$_2$ trends and ozone-attributable burden, Huaihe River Basin}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{Twelve years (2013--2024) of high-resolution satellite-derived surface MDA8 O$_3$ and
NO$_2$ across the Huaihe Basin, with pixel-wise OLS trends, the Hurst exponent as a
persistence diagnostic, and BenMAP-CE for attributable premature mortality and economic
loss. \textbf{O$_3$ rose at 2.792\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$\,yr$^{-1}$ (mean
104.15\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$) while NO$_2$ fell at $-$1.385\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$\,yr$^{-1}$ (mean
29.86)} --- the textbook VOC-limited response to NOx-only control, here quantified at basin
scale with an outward expansion of O$_3$-affected area and contraction of NO$_2$ hotspots.
Cardiovascular mortality was the largest cause-specific component, concentrated in Jiangsu
and Shandong. \textbf{No PM metric is estimated}, which for a basin whose PM$_{2.5}$ and
O$_3$ share precursors is a real gap; and the Hurst exponent applied to a 12-point annual
series is a weak persistence estimator being asked to carry a forward-looking claim.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15802-3}

\paperentry{Mou et al.\ 2026}
{Ship emissions and regional air quality in the post-DECA era, Yangtze River Basin}
{Environ Pollut}
{A bottom-up 2023 emission inventory for \textbf{18{,}478 vessels} built from
\textbf{$\approx$230 million AIS trajectories} on the world's busiest inland shipping
corridor, coupled to WRF-Chem for concentration attribution. SO$_2$ and PM$_{2.5}$ ship
emissions are substantially controlled post-DECA, but \textbf{NOx reached
156.41\,kt\,yr$^{-1}$, 82.0\% of major-pollutant mass}, with hotspots at
Yichang--Jingzhou, Wuhan and Nanjing--Shanghai. Ship contribution to NO$_2$ was
