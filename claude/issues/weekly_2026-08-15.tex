\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}The week in one paragraph}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}The measurement side gave ground this week --- 45\%
against 54\% --- and what replaced it was clinical-endpoint work in organ systems the
watch had not seen: kidney, thyroid, brain white matter, Parkinson's. The instrument
literature meanwhile turned from where a calibration fails to \emph{how long} a sensor
survives, which is the same question one level down.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Last week's finding was that four groups independently stopped publishing new low-cost
sensor correction equations and started publishing the failure boundaries of existing
ones. This week that inquiry got more specific and more uncomfortable.
\textbf{Benjamin et al.} (\textit{Sensors}) show that a low-cost CO and PM$_{2.5}$ unit
calibrated in one environmental regime does not extrapolate to another --- the failure is
in the environment, not the reference. \textbf{Balasubramanian et al.} fit a
PurpleAir correction across the whole United States, which is the opposite move and is
useful precisely as a contrast: a national model is a statement that regional structure
can be absorbed, and Benjamin's result is a statement that it cannot.
\textbf{Fawaz et al.} address a failure mode the correction literature does not touch at
all --- surface fouling of a SAW microparticle sensor in a cascade impactor, cleaned in
situ by SAW-induced droplet displacement --- and \textbf{Kirchhoff et al.} re-examine the
working fluid in condensation particle counters. Two of those four are about the sensor
surviving contact with real air, not about fitting its output.

On the health side the week's five tier-A clinical records land in five different organ
systems: \textbf{Mandal} (eGFR decline, 12{,}271 Indian adults), \textbf{Wang et al.}
(thyroid, $n=24{,}333$), \textbf{Sullivan} (childhood white-matter microstructure),
\textbf{Delaney} (first Parkinson's hospitalisation) and \textbf{Chow} (blood metabolomic
signatures linking air pollution to lung cancer). \textbf{Not one of the five measured the
exposure it analysed.} All five inherit a modelled surface --- satellite, land-use
regression or a spatiotemporal product --- and none reports the model's error at the
concentrations where the effect is claimed. \textbf{Across 134 records and 34 sensing
papers, the count of records that validated an instrument against a health endpoint is
again zero}, for the third consecutive weekly rollup.
\end{keybox}

% -------------------------------------------------------------- gaps & direction
\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Open gaps}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Teal}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Measurement--epidemiology coupling: zero for a third week.} Across 134
records and 34 sensing papers, \textbf{not one validated an instrument against a health
endpoint}. The nearest approach this week is Makacha et al.\ (14 August), which ran
minute-resolved \emph{personal} PM$_{2.5}$ monitoring on 343 women against facility
reference monitors and found ambient--personal concordance of \textbf{0.01--0.56 with
slopes below 1 in every setting} --- an exposure-misclassification measurement with no
health outcome attached. Put it beside the five tier-A clinical records above, none of
which measured its own exposure, and the shape of the gap is exact.

\item \textbf{Every large clinical result this week rests on an unvalidated exposure
surface.} Mandal (1\,km spatiotemporal model, Delhi annual means 118--130\,$\mu$g/m$^3$),
Moro (satellite model over rural Ghana), Atalell (SA2-level daily PM$_{2.5}$ at a
1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ contrast), Sullivan and Delaney (modelled residential surfaces).
\textbf{None reports out-of-sample model error at the exposure levels where the effect is
claimed.} This is not a criticism of any one paper --- it is the field convention --- but
at per-unit odds ratios of 1.04--1.06 the convention is now the binding constraint on
inference.

\item \textbf{Ultrafine and speciated metrics: worse than last week.} Of 134 records,
\textbf{exactly one} classifies as ultrafine/nanoscale and four as size-resolved or number
distribution, against 48 PM$_{2.5}$-only and 16 joint PM$_{2.5}$/PM$_{10}$. Composition
and speciation reached 8, and optical properties 7 --- both up, and both driven by the
\textit{Atmospheric Environment} deposit batch rather than by sustained interest. The
deposition physics that determines dose remains the most under-served measurement axis in
this corpus.

\item \textbf{Geographic coverage against attributable burden: unchanged and still
inverted.} China 31, North America 20, Europe 19 against \textbf{South Asia 6 and
sub-Saharan Africa 5}. South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa together carry a far larger share
of global PM-attributable DALYs than China, North America and Europe combined, and
contributed 11 of 134 records. Two of those 11 --- Mandal and Moro --- are this week's
strongest clinical records, which makes the scarcity more striking, not less.

\item \textbf{Unread records are 11\% of the corpus.} Fifteen records carry no abstract
from any checked source and were classified but not evaluated; a Crossref re-query during
this rollup resolved \textbf{0 of 15} abstracts, though it did resolve six authorships.
The nine \textit{Atmospheric Environment} records among them are exactly the
instrumentation and source-apportionment work this watch most wants to read.
\textbf{Absence of criticism in the register does not mean a record is sound.}

\item \textbf{Negative and null results: present, which is a good sign.} Moro reports a
clean null for metabolic syndrome and all its non-hypertension components alongside a
positive hypertension estimate; Hung reports no significant mediation through any measured
inflammatory cytokine, against the default assumption in that literature; Atalell reports
null prenatal and cumulative windows alongside a positive early-childhood window. A corpus
with no nulls is a reporting-bias signal. This one has them and they are load-bearing.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{3mm}
\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize\color{Deep}Where this is heading --- judgement, clearly
marked as such}\\[1.2mm]
\footnotesize
\textbf{The instrumentation literature is descending the reliability stack, and that is
the most useful thing it has done in three weeks.} Two weeks ago the question was whether
a correction fits. Last week it was where a correction stops working. This week the
questions were sensor surface fouling (Fawaz), working-fluid choice in a CPC (Kirchhoff),
environmental extrapolation limits (Benjamin) and deployment-scale correction structure
(Balasubramanian). Those are maintenance, drift and physical-integrity questions --- the
things that actually determine whether a five-year network deployment yields usable data,
and the things a held-out $R^2$ on a two-week co-location cannot address. Confidence:
moderate. Four papers over seven days is a thin base, and the same caution applies as
last week --- some of what looks like a turn is this pipeline's harvesting legs firing
differently.

\vspace{1.4mm}
\textbf{What would change the picture.} The same single paper as last week: a calibrated
low-cost network reporting an exposure--response against a clinical endpoint. Nothing in
the 21 registered trials will supply it. The likelier route is now visible in this week's
corpus --- a large linked-registry cohort (the kind producing the thyroid, renal and
Parkinson's results) retrofitting a dense sensor layer onto outcome ascertainment it
already has. That requires no new trial, only a cohort willing to instrument its
participants' addresses.

\vspace{1.4mm}
\textbf{What a distributed low-cost PM sensing programme should do differently.} Three
things this week's corpus argues for, and they are cheaper than the alternative.
\textbf{(1)} Budget for \emph{maintenance} as a measured quantity, not an operational
afterthought: Fawaz's in-situ cleaning and Benjamin's extrapolation limits both say node
performance degrades in ways a deployment-time calibration does not capture, so schedule
re-co-location and report performance as a function of time since it. \textbf{(2)} Report
exposure-surface error at the concentrations of interest, because every large clinical
result this week would have been strengthened by one number nobody supplied. \textbf{(3)}
Site the next network where the burden is, not where the reference monitors are --- South
Asia and sub-Saharan Africa produced this week's two best clinical results on the worst
exposure data in the corpus, and that is the highest-leverage measurement gap available.
\textbf{The week was quieter than the last two, and more clinically diverse than any week
so far.}
\end{keybox}
