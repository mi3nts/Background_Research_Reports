\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Three papers, three scales, one claim: particulate
exposure degrades the innate pulmonary defence against infection --- and today one of them
carries a rescue.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Zhang et al.\ (\textit{Environ Sci Technol}) followed 3{,}583 hospitalised patients with
severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome and found long-term pre-admission PM$_{2.5}$
associated with \textbf{HR 1.16 (95\% CI 1.04--1.30) per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} for
SFTS-attributable death, with viral load partially mediating; the paired mouse and cell
work shows PM$_{2.5}$ pre-exposure raising SFTSV replication and tissue injury, implicates
mitochondrial redox and ROS pathways, and \emph{reverses the effect with
N-acetylcysteine}. Masci et al.\ (\textit{Environ Toxicol Pharmacol}) reach the same
place from nutrition: in rats, chronic 20\% caloric restriction alone reproduced the
alveolar-macrophage phenotype that residual oil fly ash produced in well-nourished
animals --- reduced viability, lower Nrf2, higher TNF$\alpha$ --- and undernourished
macrophages then \textbf{failed to mount a response to a Poly(I:C) viral challenge
regardless of PM exposure}, i.e.\ the two insults are not additive because the first one
has already saturated the damage. Introna et al.\ (\textit{ACS EST Air}) supply the
dosimetric caveat that both need: exposing lung cells to subway-platform PM$_{2.5}$ at a
realistic \emph{in situ} air-liquid-interface dose of 0.12\,$\pm$\,0.07\,$\mu$g/cm$^2$
produced \textbf{no measurable change in viability or inflammatory markers at 24\,h},
while the same particles submerged at laboratory doses moved four cytokines. The
uncomfortable synthesis is that the epidemiology is real, the mechanism is plausible, and
the standard in-vitro dose is nowhere near the ambient one.
\end{keybox}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Six of thirteen records arrived without an abstract --- the highest
abstract-free share on record.} All six came through the Crossref by-ISSN sweep
(\textit{Atmos Environ}, \textit{Atmos Res}, \textit{Atmos Pollut Res},
\textit{Environ Pollut}), and Saturday's thin PubMed leg (2 records) meant the sweep
carried 8 of 13. They are shipped as \textit{Metadata only} with design, sample and
endpoint left unextracted rather than inferred. Two of them --- aerosol pH and preterm
birth, and ammonia-driven ROS generation in secondary organic aerosol --- are the day's
most interesting titles and the least characterisable records in it.

\item \textbf{A household-fuel transition with a measurable physiological dividend.}
In CHARLS, moving from solid to clean cooking fuel attenuated peak-expiratory-flow decline
by \textbf{11.0\,L/min (95\% CI 4.7--17.3)} over two years and was associated with
\textbf{OR 0.80 for chronic lung disease and 0.80 for heart disease} over the long term.
This is one of the few PM-relevant exposures where the intervention, the physiological
intermediate and the disease endpoint are all measured in the same cohort.

\item \textbf{An outdoor-activity framing of wildfire smoke, at a two-orders-of-magnitude
ratio.} A preprint on the July 2026 Gironde--Landes fires reports the footprint over the
EAQI ``Poor'' threshold (hourly PM$_{2.5}$ $>$ 50\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) at \textbf{149 times the
burned area}, extending roughly 370\,km downwind. The methodological point is the
correction the author makes against himself: the 10\,km CAMS analysis reproduces the
episode (daily $r = 0.83$) but underestimates peaks by a median factor of $\sim$2.2, so
the mapped heat--smoke relay is a conservative floor, not an estimate.

\item \textbf{Childhood eczema, with traffic proximity separated from the household
cluster.} A 1{,}227-child Porto survey isolates \textbf{aOR 1.32 (1.11--1.48) for
residence within 300\,m of a traffic zone} alongside \textbf{aOR 1.48 (1.26--1.73) for
postnatal secondhand smoke}, after adjustment that also absorbs family history
(aOR 3.53), income and maternal education. Cross-sectional and questionnaire-based ---
but the traffic term surviving that adjustment set is the part worth citing.

\item \textbf{Front-matter contamination in the newly promoted ES\&T feed, caught and
closed.} The first run with the widened ISSN list pulled \textbf{154 works from
\textit{Environ Sci Technol}, of which 142 were mastheads and issue-information stubs}
from volumes 42--52 being re-deposited in bulk. They carry DOIs and titles, so nothing
downstream would have rejected them. A title-pattern filter now drops them at the source.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 2 records}{Violet}

\paperentry{Introna et al.\ 2026}
{Novel approach to assess the toxicity of PM$_{2.5}$ on a subway platform using a mobile air-liquid interface system and submerged exposure}
{ACS EST Air}
{A mobile ALI exposure system operated \emph{on the platform itself}, with A549
monoculture and A549 + dTHP-1 coculture, benchmarked against conventional submerged
laboratory exposure to the same metal-rich mechanical-wear particles. At the realistic
in-situ ALI dose of \textbf{0.12 $\pm$ 0.07\,$\mu$g/cm$^2$ there was no change in
viability or inflammatory markers at 24\,h}. Submerged at higher dose, viability fell in
both cell types; A549 released IL-6 and TNF$\alpha$ at 100\,$\mu$g/mL, and dTHP-1
released IL-8 at the lowest dose tested (10\,$\mu$g/mL) with all four cytokines elevated
at 100. The authors' own reading is the right one --- longer in-situ exposures are needed
to reach comparable dose --- and the wider implication is that the submerged literature's
dose range is not the ambient one.}
{10.1021/acsestair.6c00018}

\paperentry{Masci et al.\ 2026}
{Air pollution and undernutrition induce rat alveolar macrophage functional dysregulation and compromise their response to a viral challenge}
{Environ Toxicol Pharmacol}
{Male Wistar rats, control vs.\ nutritional growth retardation (20\% caloric restriction,
4 weeks), each with intranasal residual oil fly ash or vehicle; isolated alveolar
macrophages assayed for viability, TNF$\alpha$, oxidative stress, Nrf2 and LC3, then
challenged with Poly(I:C). Undernutrition alone reproduced what ROFA did to well-nourished
animals. \textbf{ROFA added little or nothing on top of undernutrition} --- an attenuated
rather than additive response --- and after Poly(I:C), control macrophages mounted an
inflammatory response that prior ROFA \emph{amplified}, while undernourished macrophages
failed to respond at all regardless of exposure. The interaction structure, not the main
effect, is the finding: in a population that is already nutritionally compromised, the
marginal immunological cost of PM may be small because the defence is already gone.}
{10.1016/j.etap.2026.105138}

\band{Other clinical endpoints\hfill 1 record}{Slate}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026}
{PM$_{2.5}$ exposure and the prognosis of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{A cohort of \textbf{3{,}583 hospitalised SFTS patients} with long-term pre-admission
ambient PM$_{2.5}$: \textbf{HR 1.16 (95\% CI 1.04--1.30) per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$} for
SFTS-attributable death, with exploratory mediation implicating viral load. A
counterfactual burden assessment estimates \textbf{137 avoidable SFTS deaths in Xinyang,
2010--2022} had annual PM$_{2.5}$ met the Chinese national standard. The experimental arm
shows PM$_{2.5}$ pre-exposure increasing SFTSV replication and tissue injury;
transcriptomics implicate mitochondrial redox and ROS pathways, and ROS/MDA measurement
plus N-acetylcysteine intervention support oxidative priming as the upstream step.
Emerging-infection prognosis is a genuinely new endpoint class for this watch.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c03214}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 1 record}{Teal}

\paperentry{Liu et al.\ 2026b}
{Household clean cooking fuel transition and attenuated cardiopulmonary function decline in middle-aged and older adults}
{Indoor Air}
{CHARLS 2011--2020, with two independent subcohorts built from baseline solid-fuel users:
a short-term physiological cohort (n = 6{,}534; 27.4\% transitioned by 2013) and a
long-term disease cohort (n = 6{,}362; 33.5\% transitioned by 2015). Transition attenuated
peak-expiratory-flow decline by \textbf{11.0\,L/min (95\% CI 4.7--17.3)} and lowered
systolic blood pressure modestly ($\beta = -1.7$, $p = 0.017$). Long term:
\textbf{OR 0.80 (0.7--1.0, $p = 0.044$) for chronic lung disease} and \textbf{0.80
(0.7--1.0, $p = 0.028$) for heart disease}, with no association for hypertension;
protective effects were larger in non-smokers and non-drinkers. The two-subcohort design
is what lets the physiological intermediate and the disease endpoint be read together.}
{10.1155/ina/8898458}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 1 record}{Sage}

\paperentry{Hatem et al.\ 2026}
{Child characteristics, household and home environmental factors associated with eczema}
{Eur J Pediatr}
{1{,}227 children aged 5--11 from 20 primary schools in Porto (46.5\% response), analysed
with mixed-effects logistic regression with a school random intercept and multiple
imputation. Independently associated with higher odds: \textbf{residence within 300\,m of
a traffic zone, aOR 1.32 (1.11--1.48)}; \textbf{postnatal secondhand smoke, aOR 1.48
(1.26--1.73)}; family history 3.53; higher family income 1.40; all-purpose cleaner use
1.16. Lower odds: maternal education 0.68, detached housing 0.67, fur blanket use 0.85.
Household dampness interacted with residence type and winter ventilation frequency.
Cross-sectional and self-reported, so temporality is unavailable --- but the traffic term
surviving a socioeconomic adjustment set this dense is the citable part.}
{10.1007/s00431-026-07327-1}

\band{Reproductive \& developmental\hfill 1 record}{Sky}

\paperentry{Han et al.\ 2026}
{Aerosol pH associated with preterm birth risk: unmasking the hidden role of atmospheric acidity in maternal-fetal health}
{Environ Pollut}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} Surfaced through the Crossref by-ISSN sweep with no abstract
deposited; title, journal, authorship and DOI verified, nothing else extracted. Worth

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 2 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Fei et al.\ 2026}
{SHAP-interpretable machine learning integrating exposures and multi-omics in COPD-lung cancer comorbidity}
{Front Immunol}
{A sequential cross-scale framework: GBD epidemiology 1990--2021 across 204 countries,
exposome-wide random-forest classification with SHAP interpretation, immune
characterisation via CIBERSORT and single-cell RNA sequencing, candidate-gene
prioritisation by SMR and LASSO, then wet-lab validation including a macrophage-epithelial
Transwell co-culture. \textbf{SHAP identified smoking, particulate matter pollution and
residential radon as the major shared risk factors}; TREM1 and ODF2L emerged as shared hub
genes, downregulated in both diseases, and macrophage-specific silencing of either
attenuated macrophage-driven A549 migration and proliferation. The exposure side is
GBD-level and coarse --- this is not an exposure-assessment paper --- but it is a clean
worked example of putting an exposome variable through an interpretable-ML pipeline and
ending at a testable target.}
{10.3389/fimmu.2026.1875146}

\paperentry{Zeng et al.\ 2026}
{Aerosol vertical distributions determine the seasonal relationships between column AOD and surface PM$_{2.5}$ over Europe}
{Atmos Environ}
{\textbf{Metadata only.} No abstract deposited. The title states a result that matters
directly to satellite-based PM$_{2.5}$ estimation --- the AOD-to-surface conversion being
governed by vertical structure and therefore seasonally non-stationary --- so this is the
highest-priority record of the six for abstract retry or full-text retrieval on the next
run.}
{10.1016/j.atmosenv.2026.122305}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 5 records}{Deep}

\paperentry{Defrance 2026 (preprint)}
{Beyond the fire perimeter: the smoke footprint that constrains outdoor activity dwarfs the burned area of the July 2026 Gironde--Landes wildfire episode}
{Research Square}
{ERA5-Land wet-bulb globe temperature combined with CAMS analysis PM$_{2.5}$ at hourly
resolution over 25--31 July 2026, against 40 background monitoring stations. The two
fires burned 340\,km$^2$ and forced $\sim$220{,}000 evacuations; the footprint above the
European AQI ``Poor'' threshold (hourly PM$_{2.5}$ $>$ 50\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, where reduced
outdoor activity is advised) reached \textbf{149 times the burned area} and about
\textbf{370\,km downwind}, with 139$\times$ on the EU 2030 daily limit of 25\,$\mu$g/m$^3$
and at least 121$\times$ against the larger operational fire perimeter. Plume co-location
rose from 73\% hourly to 94\% daily. At the stations, a same-day heat--smoke relay
affected 28\% of affected station-days at 25\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ and 19\% at 50, against
0--2\% in the co-located grid cells --- \textbf{the 10\,km analysis smooths away the peaks
that drive the relay}, and reproduces the episode at daily $r = 0.83$ while
underestimating peaks by a median factor of $\sim$2.2. Preprint, single author, single
episode; the self-directed error analysis is unusually thorough for the format.}
{10.21203/rs.3.rs-10757961/v1}
