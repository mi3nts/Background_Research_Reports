\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Two records on opposite ends of the evidence
hierarchy make the same argument: the particle-mass concentration a monitor reports is
conditional on a co-exposure the monitor does not measure.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Hur et al.\ (\textit{Environ Pollut}) pooled \textbf{262{,}288} deaths across eight
Kyushu cities over 26 pollen seasons and fitted an explicit SPM\,$\times$\,pollen
interaction in a two-stage time-series. The marginal association of suspended particulate
matter with respiratory mortality at lag 1 is barely distinguishable from null
(\textbf{RR 1.010, 95\,\% CI 1.000--1.019}), but it separates cleanly on pollen load:
\textbf{1.021 (1.005--1.037)} above the 75th percentile against \textbf{1.003
(0.987--1.018)} below it, and the split is robust to the choice of cutoff. A mass-only
instrument in Fukuoka in March therefore returns a number whose health meaning changes
by a factor of seven depending on a biogenic co-pollutant it is blind to.
Çulpan et al.\ (\textit{Rev Environ Health}) reach the same conclusion by subtraction
across \textbf{21 studies and 990{,}157} children: the fine fraction is the only exposure
with a consistent blood-pressure association (\textbf{+0.59\,mmHg systolic per
10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}, 0.28--0.90), the coarse-fraction estimates do not survive sensitivity
analysis, and NO$_2$ is null --- but the authors' own stated limitation is that they could
not fit multipollutant models because the primary studies did not report the exposure
covariance. Both papers are asking the measurement side for the same thing: a
co-exposure-resolved record, not a scalar.
\end{keybox}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}What changed today}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The thinnest window this watch has run: 14 records against 26 yesterday
and 27 on 6 August.} This is a calendar artefact, not a signal --- 8 August is a Saturday
and PubMed's entry-date volume collapses at weekends. The PubMed connector returned
\textbf{9 hits on the health axis and zero on the instrumentation axis}, the third
consecutive empty sensing query from that leg, and the local harvester returned 4 and 0.
The corpus is held up almost entirely by \textbf{Crossref by ISSN, which supplied 6 of
the 7 sensing and exposure records}. Treat the daily counts for 8 August as a floor when
they enter the weekly trend line.

\item \textbf{Six of fourteen records --- and six of seven on the sensing axis --- carry no
abstract anywhere.} All six are \textit{Atmospheric Environment} / \textit{Atmospheric
Pollution Research} deposits made today; Crossref, Europe PMC and Semantic Scholar all
return metadata only, and Semantic Scholar has no record of the DOIs at all. They are
listed with design, metric and region but are \textbf{not summarised}, because a summary
written from a title is a fabrication. The highest-value pickups are Madalipay et al.\ on
provincial PM$_{2.5}$ conversion factors for South Korea and Cuerda Barcaiztegui et al.\
on a two-year classroom ventilation and IAQ field study in Madrid.

\item \textbf{Five effect estimates with intervals, down from sixteen yesterday}, and
three of them are the same Kyushu interaction reported at different pollen strata.
Two are co-pollutant (NO$_2$, indoor air nicotine). \textbf{No PM ratio estimate from a
cohort entered the corpus today.} Çulpan et al.'s meta-analytic estimates are mean
differences in mmHg and are deliberately excluded from the forest plot, which is a
log-ratio axis --- plotting 0.59 against a null of 1.0 would render a harmful effect as
protective. They are carried in the digest text and the register instead.

\item \textbf{The one high-temporal-resolution result is a co-pollutant one.} Shukla
et al.\ (\textit{BMJ Open}) fit a Poisson GAM/DLNM on \emph{hourly} NO$_2$ against
18{,}943 adult asthma admissions in Birmingham and Solihull, and locate the association
at a \textbf{3--6 hour lag} (RR 1.13, 1.01--1.26 at the 18\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ mean over
lag 0--24\,h, daytime). The lag structure is the transferable part: an exposure signal
resolvable at 3--6 hours is invisible to a 24-hour filter-based method and is exactly the
regime distributed low-cost sensing is built for. It is NO$_2$, not PM, so it enters as a
methodological rather than a substantive record.

\item \textbf{Nothing new on sensor calibration, drift or co-location.} Three Consensus
sweeps (calibration and drift, co-location against reference monitors, satellite--surface
fusion with humidity correction) returned 20 hits each, capped at 3 per sweep by the free
tier, and \textbf{every returned DOI resolved to 2019--2025 or to a record already in
\texttt{seen.json}}. OpenAlex returned HTTP 429 for the sixth consecutive run. The
calibration literature was not silent today; the available discovery paths simply cannot
see a single day of it.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase. Records
marked \textsc{metadata only} were surfaced without an abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or
Semantic Scholar and are listed, not summarised.\par}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 2 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{Hur et al.\ 2026}
{Pollen modifies the SPM--mortality association across eight Kyushu cities}
{Environ Pollut}
{Two-stage time-series over 262{,}288 all-cause deaths, eight cities, pollen seasons
(Feb--Apr) 1989--2014, with an explicit SPM\,$\times$\,pollen interaction term in stage one
and random-effects pooling in stage two. Respiratory mortality at lag 1:
\textbf{RR 1.010 (1.000--1.019)} overall, \textbf{1.021 (1.005--1.037)} on days above the
75th-percentile pollen concentration against \textbf{1.003 (0.987--1.018)} below,
stable across alternative cutoffs. The threat to inference is exposure misclassification
in the modifier, not the exposure: pollen is measured at a single station per city and
the interaction is fitted on a binary split of a highly skewed distribution, so the
contrast is a lower bound on a continuous effect. Suspended particulate matter is a
Japanese regulatory metric with a 100\,\% cut near 10\,$\mu$m and is not
interchangeable with PM$_{2.5}$. For distributed sensing the implication is concrete:
an optical counter with a fluorescence or coarse-mode channel can resolve the modifier and
the exposure on the same node, which no regulatory network in this study did.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.128933}

\paperentry{Shukla et al.\ 2026}
{Hourly NO$_2$ and emergency admissions for acute asthma, Birmingham and Solihull}
{BMJ Open}
{Poisson generalised additive model with a distributed-lag non-linear term on
\emph{hourly} counts --- 18{,}943 adult asthma admissions to four acute hospitals,
June 2016--May 2022, linked through the PIONEER data hub. The mean 18\,$\mu$g/m$^3$
NO$_2$ exposure over lag 0--24\,h carried \textbf{RR 1.13 (1.01--1.26)} for daytime
admissions, with the association concentrated at a \textbf{3--6 hour lag} and a steeper
gradient in the most deprived quintile. Exposure is assigned from fixed monitors to
residence, so the hourly resolution is nominal rather than personal --- time-activity
displacement over a 3--6\,h window is precisely the scale at which residential assignment
fails, which biases toward the null. The pollutant is NO$_2$, so this enters the watch on
method rather than on substance: it is the cleanest recent demonstration that the
health-relevant exposure signal lives below the 24-hour averaging period that
filter-based reference methods impose.}
{10.1136/bmjopen-2025-110612}

\band{Cardiovascular \& metabolic\hfill 1 record}{Teal}

\paperentry{Çulpan et al.\ 2026}
{Long-term air pollution and blood pressure in children: updated systematic review and meta-analysis}
{Rev Environ Health}
{PRISMA-guided update over prior reviews plus PubMed and Web of Science to 27 March 2025;
21 studies, 990{,}157 participants in the qualitative synthesis, with meta-analysis
restricted to the fine and coarse fractions and NO$_2$ because cohort data for SO$_2$,
O$_3$ and CO were too sparse. \textbf{Fine PM: +0.59\,mmHg systolic per 10\,$\mu$g/m$^3$
(0.28--0.90)}, the only estimate consistent across sensitivity analyses. The coarse
fraction gave nominally tighter systolic (+0.28, 0.25--0.31) and diastolic (+0.27,
0.24--0.30) associations that \emph{did not survive} sensitivity analysis --- an
implausibly narrow interval for a pooled estimate, which usually indicates a single
dominant study. NO$_2$ was null for both. Several pollutants raised the odds of
hypertension as a dichotomous outcome. Two limitations are load-bearing: exposure
increments and hypertension definitions were not harmonised across primary studies, and
no multipollutant model could be fitted because the primary studies did not report
exposure covariance, so the pollutant-specific attribution is weaker than the point
estimates suggest. Note also that the PubMed deposit strips subscripts from every
particle-size label, so fraction assignment here is inferred from the stated
meta-analytic scope.}
{10.1515/reveh-2026-0032}

\band{Occupational \& indoor exposure\hfill 2 records}{Clay}

\paperentry{Tillery et al.\ 2026}
{Indoor air nicotine, NO$_2$ and COVID-19 outcomes in a Great Plains tribal community}
{Indoor Air}
{COVID-19 Wayakta He? study: 290 multigenerational Cheyenne River Sioux Tribe households,
each pair of adults at least 9 years apart in age, with one week of passive air-nicotine
and NO$_2$ badges plus saliva serology. Geometric mean air nicotine
0.052\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ (detected in 40.1\,\% of homes); geometric mean NO$_2$ 10.5\,ppb,
range 0.65--138, consistent with propane cooking and heating. \textbf{OR 1.21
(1.01--1.51)} for prior infection with detectable air nicotine; \textbf{OR 3.69
(1.11--16.6)} for self-reported long COVID with NO$_2$ exposure. The second interval
spans a factor of fifteen and rests on a self-reported outcome in a cross-sectional
design with one week of exposure measurement standing in for a multi-year window ---
it is a direction, not a magnitude. The study's real contribution is that it measured
the microenvironment at all: no ambient network in the Northern Plains resolves
household combustion, and community-deployed passive samplers are the only instrument
that reached it.}
{10.1155/ina/7749725}

\paperentry{Cuerda Barcaiztegui et al.\ 2026}
{Classroom ventilation management and user activities vs.\ indoor air quality, Madrid primary schools}
{Atmos Pollut Res}
{\textsc{metadata only} --- no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar at
the time of this issue. A two-year classroom field study on ventilation management and
occupant activity is directly on this watch's deployment axis and is the single highest-priority abstract re-check for the next run.}
{10.1016/j.apr.2026.103170}

\band{Mechanistic toxicology\hfill 1 record}{Violet}

\paperentry{Yibcharoenporn et al.\ 2026}
{Metabolic profiling of PM-induced developmental toxicity in zebrafish embryos}
{ACS Omega}
{Zebrafish embryos exposed 4--50\,hpf to fine and coarse PM at 500 and
1000\,$\mu$g/mL, with untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics. The fine fraction alone produced
a \textbf{22\,h hatching delay} and disrupted pantothenate/CoA, retinol and riboflavin
metabolism; riboflavin fell significantly at the higher dose. Glutathione responses
diverged by size --- reduced glutathione depleted under the fine fraction but
\emph{elevated} under the coarse --- which is the most interesting result here because it
argues against a single shared oxidative mechanism scaled by mass. The dose is the
problem: 500--1000\,$\mu$g/mL of suspended PM is three to four orders of magnitude above
any inhalable ambient concentration, so this establishes a mechanism, not a risk. The
authors' closing suggestion of vitamin supplementation is not supported by anything in
the design.}
{10.1021/acsomega.6c00972}

\band{Burden, policy \& mitigation\hfill 3 records}{Coral}

\paperentry{Gu et al.\ 2026}
{Electro-ultrasonic coupled agglomeration for removal of fine particles from biomass combustion}
{J Hazard Mater}
{Segmented continuous-flow bench platform combining bipolar charging ($\pm$16\,kV), a
300\,V/400\,Hz AC field and 16\,kHz/142\,dB ultrasound, with particle-trajectory
simulation and SEM. The coupled field reached \textbf{86.4\,\% light transmittance},
against single-field results 33.1 and 67.2 percentage points lower, with the extinction
coefficient down to 0.9\,m$^{-1}$; simulated cumulative particle displacement rose
$>$445\,\% as linear trajectories became three-dimensional helical paths, and SEM
confirmed 15--20\,$\mu$m agglomerates. The performance metric is the weakness: light
transmittance and extinction coefficient are \emph{optical} proxies, not gravimetric or
number-resolved removal efficiency, so the headline ``removal efficiency'' is not
directly comparable with any filtration literature that reports mass penetration. The
submicron fraction claimed as the target is precisely the fraction an extinction
measurement is least sensitive to.}
{10.1016/j.jhazmat.2026.143130}

\paperentry{Wojcik et al.\ 2026}
{An air pollution curriculum for pulmonary and critical care fellows}
{ATS Sch}
{Kern six-step curriculum development across five domains --- composition, health effects,
\emph{monitoring}, risk mitigation and environmental justice --- with a 20-item knowledge
scale built by modified Delphi and delivered as pre/post assessment over one academic
year with spaced repetition. Knowledge rose in all five domains ($p<0.001$). Single
programme, no control arm, self-selected respondents and a knowledge rather than
behaviour endpoint, so the effect size is uninterpretable; it is included because the
explicit inclusion of \emph{monitoring} as a clinical competency domain is a small but
real indicator of where sensor-derived exposure data is expected to land clinically.}
{10.1093/atsscholar/aapag039}

\paperentry{Kokol et al.\ 2026}
{Artificial intelligence in the management of climate-change-related health issues in Africa}
{Res Sq (preprint)}
{Scoping review over Scopus to October 2025, VOSviewer clustering a global against an
African literature corpus. The divergence is the finding: global work clusters on
environmental modelling, air-pollution mortality tracking and exposome integration,
African work on supervised classification for infectious-disease triage and food
security, with a ``compute deficit'', data silos and power instability named as barriers.
This is bibliometrics, not evidence --- a VOSviewer map measures what Scopus indexed, and
Scopus under-indexes African journals, so the gap is partly an artefact of the
instrument. Preprint, not peer reviewed.}
{10.21203/rs.3.rs-9910414/v1}
