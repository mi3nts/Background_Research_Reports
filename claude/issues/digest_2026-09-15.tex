\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Signal of the day}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{2.5mm}

\begin{keybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\small\color{Deep}Per unit mass, wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ carries about
4.6 times the mortality hazard of ordinary urban PM$_{2.5}$ --- but the exposure contrast
behind that number is a thirtyfold difference in concentration, and that is the part to
read carefully.}\\[1.4mm]
\footnotesize
Su et al.\ (\textit{Lancet Oncol}) followed \textbf{414{,}016} SEER-Medicare patients aged
65 and over with histologically confirmed lung cancer through \textbf{1{,}211{,}490}
person-years (2008--2019), with zip-code wildfire and non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ from
validated models and a time-varying Cox model on 3-year moving averages. Per
1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$, the hazard of death was \textbf{HR 1.0782 (95\% CI 1.0641--1.0924)} for
wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ against \textbf{1.0171 (1.0149--1.0192)} for non-wildfire ---
non-overlapping intervals, and the sharpest population-scale statement yet that smoke mass
and urban mass are not the same exposure.\\[1.0mm]
The caution is arithmetic. Median wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ was \textbf{0.28\,$\mu$g/m$^3$}
(IQR 0.21--0.39) against \textbf{8.11} for non-wildfire, so a ``per 1\,$\mu$g/m$^3$''
wildfire coefficient is extrapolated several IQR-widths beyond anything observed, and on
the per-SD scale the ordering reverses (1.0229 versus 1.0384) because an SD of wildfire
PM$_{2.5}$ is a far smaller absolute dose. Both numbers are correct; only one is a
statement about the air people breathed. The monitoring implication survives either
reading: a network reporting mass alone cannot distinguish these two exposures.
\end{keybox}

\item \textbf{A dense day, and for once the epidemiology carries intervals.} Twenty-two

\textit{Burden, policy \& mitigation} is the sole empty one.

\item \textbf{The DOI gate removed three duplicates that title screening called new, and
one is a repeat offender.} The Consensus sweep returned ten low-cost-sensor calibration

title. Consensus keeps re-surfacing it and no title-normalised index will ever catch it.

\item \textbf{Two findings rest on weak arithmetic, and both are named in place.} The
Himawari-8 study of Javanese coal-plant plumes reports random forest and XGBoost scoring
\textbf{exactly 1.000} on every metric --- but the target is a deterministic threshold on

projection built on them is uninterpretable. The Korean agricultural analysis lifts

quantity does. A third belongs with them: the \textit{JECH} suicide meta-analysis pools
per-increment and high-versus-low categorical PM$_{2.5}$ contrasts under one label,
producing a 38-fold short-versus-long gap that is not a duration effect.

\item \textbf{Four in-scope records were verified and deliberately held.} The MoAE
low-cost sensor calibration framework (\textit{Atmos Environ}), a CTM--InMAP burden study
over Punjab, a SW-Europe dust and sea-salt compliance analysis (\textit{Atmos Res}) and an
office breathing-zone sensor-placement chamber study (\textit{Build Environ}) all have
resolving DOIs and no abstract in Crossref, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar, Consensus or
Scholar Gateway --- all four same-day Elsevier deposits, the failure mode logged on
14 September. They are kept \emph{out} of \texttt{seen.json}; the MoAE paper is the
tier-A pickup. Separately, \textbf{arXiv returned HTTP 406} and an empty body, a new
failure distinct from the \texttt{submittedDate} behaviour previously logged, and
OpenAlex returned HTTP 429 again.

\item \textbf{Evidence geography is tied, not led.} China and Global / multi-region sit at
\textbf{seven records each}. Sub-Saharan Africa's two matter disproportionately --- a
Nigerian cohort and South African pneumococcal surveillance, both built on exposure
surfaces with no in-country reference network.
\end{itemize}

\section*{\sffamily\bfseries\color{Deep}Paper-level digest}
\vspace{-1.5mm}{\color{Amber}\rule{\linewidth}{1.1pt}}\vspace{1mm}

{\fontsize{7.4}{9}\selectfont\color{Slate}Ordered by cluster. Each entry gives the
methodological core and the finding that matters, not an abstract paraphrase.\par}

\band{Exposure assessment \& modelling\hfill 8 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Han et al.\ 2026}
{Source apportionment of PM$_{2.5}$ oxidative potential in metropolitan Melbourne}
{Environ Pollut}
{Published PMF source contributions linked by multiple linear regression to oxidative
potential measured on \textbf{three complementary acellular assays} (DTT, AA, DCFH);
models explain \textbf{73--86\%} of OP variance. Combustion dominates OP$_v$-DTT at
52--53\% with biomass burning above traffic; OP$_v$-AA is driven by metal-rich sources
including diesel at 20--22\%; secondary nitrate contributes 28--33\% and secondary
sulphate 10\% at the second site. The assay-dependent ranking is the result --- a
single-assay OP metric would nominate a different mitigation target. Reusing an earlier
PMF solution propagates its rotational ambiguity, and no uncertainty is quoted on the
source-resolved shares. First Southern Hemisphere estimate of its kind.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.129159}

\paperentry{Liu et al.\ 2026}
{Decadal changes in PM$_{2.5}$ sources and secondary aerosol drivers, Shenzhen 2014--2024}
{J Environ Manage}
{Eleven years of speciated filter sampling with PMF apportionment and machine-learning
attribution of secondary formation drivers. Annual mean fell \textbf{37.7 to
17.3\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$}. Secondary sulphate was historically dominant and declined
fastest; vehicles became the largest single contributor after 2019; \textbf{secondary
nitrate barely moved and is now the binding constraint}. NO$_x$ and VOC cuts drove nitrate
and SOA declines but rising atmospheric oxidation capacity offset them, while sulphate
tracked oxidation and regional transport over local SO$_2$. Attributing a decadal trend to
oxidation capacity from a correlational model at one site cannot exclude meteorological
confounding, and the PMF factor series carry no uncertainty bounds.}
{10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.130913}

\paperentry{Zhang et al.\ 2026}
{Surface-based temperature inversion climatology across China and its relation to PM$_{2.5}$}
{Environ Pollut}
{Hourly microwave-radiometer profiles from \textbf{33 stations} over 2024--2025, validated
against radiosondes at 25 sites, used to build a surface-based inversion climatology and
relate it to PM$_{2.5}$ at hotspot stations. Winter SBI occurrence \textbf{39.2\%} against
\textbf{12.1\%} in summer, with a northwest-to-southeast gradient peaking in the Tarim
Basin and Hexi Corridor. SHAP and Spearman analysis place boundary-layer variables in the
top predictor tier monthly and daily, with effective mixing-layer height third among all
hourly predictors in every season. Two years is short for a climatology, the network is
sparse relative to the terrain it spans, and SHAP importance over correlated
meteorological predictors does not establish the causal pathway it is read as
establishing.}
{10.1016/j.envpol.2026.129172}

\paperentry{Kim et al.\ 2026}
{LASSO-based reduced-form CMAQ for ozone and PM$_{2.5}$ response to emission change}
{PLoS One}
{Surrogate for CMAQ fitted by LASSO over \textbf{118 emission scenarios} spanning seven
sectors and 17 provincial regions, with an adaptive logit transform to respect
concentration bounds. Trained on 100 simulations and tested on 18: mean RMSE
\textbf{0.1\,ppb} for ozone and \textbf{0.1\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} for PM$_{2.5}$ against the
parent model. The sparse set of retained sector--region terms is the practical output,
making the control-design problem interpretable rather than opaque. Error is quoted
against CMAQ and not against observations, so every CMAQ bias passes through undiagnosed;
118 scenarios is thin for a 119-dimensional predictor space and no out-of-envelope
extrapolation test is reported.}
{10.1371/journal.pone.0347073}

\paperentry{Aji et al.\ 2026}
{Fine-mode combustion aerosol events near Indonesian coal plants from AE-filtered Himawari-8}
{Environ Monit Assess}
{\textbf{41{,}119} daily Himawari-8 observations around 13 Java coal plants, 2015--2025,
with events defined as \r{A}ngstr\"om exponent $>1.2$ and AOD $>0.4$ (8.52\% of days).
Suralaya has the highest mean AOD at 1.01 with 51.4\% very-high-pollution days; Pacitan
the lowest at 0.46. Random forest and XGBoost return accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and
AUC-ROC all \textbf{exactly 1.000} --- on a target that is a deterministic threshold
function of the two inputs, so the models relearn the labelling rule rather than predict
anything, and the 2026--2060 Monte Carlo projection built on them is uninterpretable.
Column AOD without surface validation cannot separate plant plume from boundary-layer
aerosol.}
{10.1007/s10661-026-15804-1}

\paperentry{Ilies et al.\ 2026}
{Urban PM$_{10}$ and population exposure from satellite, meteorological and socio-spatial data}
{Front Public Health}
{MODIS-MAIAC AOD combined with four ground stations and meteorology in a regression for
PM$_{10}$ over Oradea, Romania, then folded with population density, built form, NDVI and
elevation into a composite exposure index. Cold-season maxima reach
\textbf{41.8\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} against an annual mean near 20.5 --- above WHO, inside EU
limits --- and \textbf{20.1\%} of the metropolitan population falls in
high-predisposition areas. The AOD--PM$_{10}$ relation alone is weak
(\textbf{R = 0.28}) and meteorology carries the model, which is the authors' own admission
and the standing reason satellite-only surfaces underperform at city scale. Four stations
cannot validate an intra-urban field, and the composite index is unweighted and never
checked against measured personal exposure.}
{10.3389/fpubh.2026.1940025}

\paperentry{Kim \& Park 2026}
{Loading-rate-based analysis of PM$_{2.5}$ and precursor gases in Korean agricultural areas}
{Atmosphere}
{547 days of hourly data from eight agricultural sites, comparing concentration-based with
loading-rate-based interpretation, where loading rate is concentration scaled by a
wind-speed-derived air exchange rate. Concentration correlations among PM$_{2.5}$,
NH$_3$, NO$_2$ and SO$_2$ are weak and inconsistent; loading-rate correlations are strong
and uniform at \textbf{$r \ge 0.816$}, and the diurnal shape flips from bimodal to
unimodal. The authors concede the improvement may reflect the shared wind-speed term ---
it almost certainly does, since dividing every series by one common quantity induces
correlation by construction. Recorded as a method to treat with suspicion: any dense
open-field network normalising by a common meteorological variable manufactures the same
result.}
{10.3390/atmos17090898}

\paperentry{Ahmad et al.\ 2026}
{Carbonaceous composition and multi-wavelength optical properties of PM in Lahore}
{Atmosphere}
{Six-week urban campaign in Lahore, 30 quartz filters collected, 25 usable for gravimetry
and optics, \textbf{only the first 15 analysed for OC/EC}. PM$_{2.5}$ ranged
\textbf{40--417\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} with a mean of \textbf{137 $\pm$ 84}. Organic carbon
was 76.6\% of total carbon against 23.4\% elemental. Mean absorption \r{A}ngstr\"om
exponent \textbf{1.17 $\pm$ 0.30}, with 56\% of samples in 1.0--1.5, 32\% below 1.0 and
12\% above 1.5. The authors are appropriately careful that AAE intervals describe spectral
behaviour rather than attribute sources. Fifteen OC/EC filters in one pre-monsoon season
cannot characterise Lahore's annual cycle, and the gravimetric series has no co-located
reference for traceability. Directly relevant to optical low-cost sensors, whose response
depends on exactly this absorption behaviour.}
{10.3390/atmos17090899}

\band{Sensing, forecasting \& instrumentation\hfill 4 records}{Amber}

\paperentry{Vance et al.\ 2026}
{Evaluation of the Partector 2 Pro for ultrafine particles from 3D printing}
{J Occup Environ Hyg}
{Wind-tunnel comparison of a portable diffusion-charging monitor against SMPS and CPC
during controlled printing of PLA, PETG, TPU and nylon. Number-concentration agreement is
strong across all four filaments --- regression slopes \textbf{0.88--1.05}, Pearson
$r > 0.96$, mean deviations \textbf{$-$9.6\% to $+$6.2\%}, inside the $\pm$30\%
manufacturer specification. Sizing is systematically biased low (slopes 0.82--0.90) and
worst at the fine end: \textbf{$-$27.0\% to $+$7.5\% at D10} and $-$28.3\% to $-$7.3\%
across D25--D75. That pattern is the operational point for any distributed ultrafine
network --- the instrument is fit for concentration surveillance but its size distribution
must not be used to infer deposition fraction. Wind tunnel only, with no humidity or
co-pollutant interference testing.}
{10.1080/15459624.2026.2724020}

\paperentry{Wathore et al.\ 2026}
{Environmental and temporal performance of ML calibration for a low-cost PM$_{2.5}$ sensor}
{Aerosol Air Qual Res}
{Long-term co-location in Vishakhapatnam comparing Random Forest and XGBoost against
linear regression, adding temperature, relative humidity, hour-of-day and month-of-year
stepwise, plus a hybrid ensemble. The best single model cuts RMSE \textbf{58\%} against
the linear baseline and the hybrid reaches \textbf{63\%}, meeting US EPA low-cost
PM$_{2.5}$ performance criteria. The contribution is the evaluation design rather than the
models: performance is reported binned across environmental and temporal strata and across
AQI categories, which exposes where a calibration degrades instead of burying it in a
pooled $R^2$. No absolute RMSE in $\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ is given, and single-site training
leaves transferability untested --- the same limitation already flagged for the Sofia
calibration-transfer work in this corpus.
\textit{Connector-sourced; outside the 15 September entry window.}}
{10.1007/s44408-026-00094-6}

\paperentry{Komiljon et al.\ 2026}
{Non-cumulative, size-specific calibration of low-cost PM sensors under simulated drilling}
{Atmosphere}
{Controlled laboratory evaluation of PMS5003 and Sniffer4D under simulated concrete-slab
drilling, with six correction models --- linear, polynomial, Random Forest, XGBoost, ANN,
Kalman --- fitted independently per size fraction. Random Forest and ANN perform best,
\textbf{$R^2 > 0.89$ for PM$_1$} and \textbf{$> 0.87$ for PM$_{2.5}$} at 50\,s averaging.
The premise is the useful part: reporting non-cumulative rather than cumulative bins,
since cumulative output conflates fractions with different deposition behaviour and
different regulatory meaning. Against that, a single drilling source in a chamber produces
a mineral-dust size distribution that will not transfer to combustion aerosol, and
PM$_{10}$ performance is not reported alongside the finer fractions.
\textit{Connector-sourced; Crossref resolves only the preprint of record.}}
{10.20944/preprints202604.1260.v1}

\paperentry{Wang et al.\ 2026}
{14-day PM$_{2.5}$ and O$_3$ forecasting over eastern China with a CNN--Transformer}
{Environ Sci Technol}
{Hybrid architecture combining multilevel regional meteorological fields with emission
inventories at a 14-day lead. Relative $R^2$ gains over baseline are \textbf{40\% for
O$_3$} (0.58 to 0.81) and \textbf{18\% for PM$_{2.5}$} (0.44 to 0.52); 850\,hPa fields add
5\% for O$_3$ and emissions add 9.8\% for PM$_{2.5}$; 66--83\% of O$_3$ episodes are
captured in the north. Read honestly, PM$_{2.5}$ skill remains poor --- $R^2$ 0.52 at
14 days is not decision-grade, and the paper's own framing concedes O$_3$ is the stronger
case. No episode-capture rate is reported for PM$_{2.5}$, which is the number a forecast
user would act on, and gridded reanalysis truth hides station-scale error.}
{10.1021/acs.est.6c05923}

\band{Respiratory \& allergic\hfill 3 records}{Sage}

\paperentry{Kremer et al.\ 2026}
{Pneumococcal population structure modifies the effect of air pollution on invasive disease}
{Nat Microbiol}
{Nineteen years of South African national surveillance --- \textbf{59{,}017} invasive
pneumococcal disease cases with \textbf{4{,}350} sequenced isolates --- in Bayesian
spatiotemporal models linking environment to pathogen diversity. PM$_{2.5}$ is positively
associated with IPD, but \textbf{the lag structure varies by serotype}: only serotypes 4,
8 and 23F confer immediate risk, and high prevalence of lineage GPSC21 (serotype 19F)
shifts the population-level response to immediate. Cumulative 8-week effects for moderate
humidity and cold minimum temperature are $+5\%$ and $+4\%$. No confidence interval
accompanies the headline pollution association and the serotype-stratified lag models
present a large multiple-comparison surface. Still the first demonstration that pathogen
genotype modifies an air-quality exposure--response, and a direct argument for pairing
dense PM monitoring with pathogen surveillance.}
{10.1038/s41564-026-02458-5}

\paperentry{Zhu et al.\ 2026}
{Extreme cold events and paediatric asthma visits, modified by ambient CO and SO$_2$}
{J Asthma Allergy}
{\textbf{62{,}167} paediatric asthma outpatient visits at one tertiary hospital in Nanjing,
2019--2024, quasi-Poisson DLNM over lag 0--14. Extreme cold events show \textbf{no overall
association}, but cumulative RR reaches \textbf{2.60 (95\% CI 1.13--5.95)} in the high-CO
stratum and \textbf{3.41 (1.15--10.11)} in the high-SO$_2$ stratum. The intervals are very
wide and the strata are subsets of an already null main effect, so this is
hypothesis-generating; notably PM$_{2.5}$ and PM$_{10}$ did not emerge as modifiers.
Single-hospital catchment with city-average exposure. Relevant here mainly as a reminder
that pollutant-by-meteorology interaction terms demand far denser exposure data than one
municipal station provides.}
{10.2147/jaa.s483101}

\paperentry{Martinez \& Nadeau 2026}
{Early-life exposure to air pollutants and childhood asthma}
{Curr Allergy Asthma Rep}
{Narrative synthesis of recent cohort and mechanistic work on the prenatal and early
postnatal window. Reports epigenetic modification in airway epithelium, a convergence
point shared between tobacco smoke exposure and genetic asthma predisposition, new serum
biomarkers of exposure, and a murine result in which asthma-predisposing information
passes from exposed dam to offspring \textbf{via NK cells}. No pooled estimate, no effect
size, and no stated search strategy or inclusion criteria, so study selection is
unauditable and the piece cannot be weighted against the systematic reviews in this
window. Useful as a pointer to the NK-cell transmission mechanism and the biomarker
candidates, and for nothing quantitative.}
{10.1007/s11882-026-01293-2}

\band{Other clinical endpoints\hfill 2 records}{Slate}

\paperentry{Su et al.\ 2026}
{Wildfire-related PM$_{2.5}$ and overall survival of older lung cancer patients}
{Lancet Oncol}
{\textbf{414{,}016} SEER-Medicare patients aged 65+, \textbf{1{,}211{,}490} person-years,
2008--2019, time-varying Cox on 3-year moving averages of zip-code wildfire and
non-wildfire PM$_{2.5}$. Per 1\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$: \textbf{HR 1.0782 (95\% CI
1.0641--1.0924)} for wildfire against \textbf{1.0171 (1.0149--1.0192)} for non-wildfire,
a 4.6-fold difference in per-unit potency. The exposure contrast is the weakness --- median
wildfire PM$_{2.5}$ was 0.28\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$ (IQR 0.21--0.39) against 8.11 for
non-wildfire, so the per-unit hazard is extrapolated far outside the observed range, and
the per-SD estimates (1.0229 versus 1.0384) reverse the ordering. Zip-code assignment with
no residential history. Species-resolved rather than mass-only monitoring is the direct
implication.}
{10.1016/s1470-2045(26)00295-0}

\paperentry{Mtenga et al.\ 2026}
{Ambient PM$_{2.5}$ exposure and cancer incidence in Nigerian adults}
{Cancer Epidemiol}
{First prospective sub-Saharan African cohort on this question. 5{,}621 adults, median
follow-up 4.7 years, 147 incident cancers confirmed histopathologically, mean PM$_{2.5}$
\textbf{50.4\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$} --- tenfold the WHO guideline. Each 10\,$\mu$g\,m$^{-3}$
gives \textbf{HR 1.12 (95\% CI 1.06--1.19)} for all-cancer incidence and the top quartile
\textbf{1.82 (1.29--2.57)}; mediation analysis attributes \textbf{41.3\%} to oxidative
stress and inflammation. Site-specific signals for lung, breast and haematopoietic cancer
rest on very small strata at 147 total events, and biomarkers measured once in a subsample

\paperentry{Garner et al.\ 2026}

{Reprod Toxicol}
{Pregnant Sprague Dawley rats exposed by whole-body inhalation to nano-TiO$_2$ at
